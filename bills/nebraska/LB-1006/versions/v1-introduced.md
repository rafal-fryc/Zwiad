Section 1. Sections 1 to 6 of this act shall be known and may be cited as the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act. Sec. 2. The Legislature finds and declares that: (1) It is the policy of Nebraska to protect consumers from predatory pricing schemes in whatever iteration they appear or evolve; (2) Advancements in algorithms, in artificial intelligence, and in electronic shelving technology have created commercial and retail environments where it is possible to set pricing for a product or service based on personal or protected data; (3) Current law does not sufficiently prohibit companies from using personal or protected data to set prices; (4) The use of personal or protected data to set prices presents a distinct potential for companies to violate consumer privacy rights and may result in discrimination; (5) While offering goods at different prices due to customer demand is  not  new,  there  are  subtle  and  important  differences  between  price optimization and dynamic and surveillance pricing; (6) It is in the interest of the state and its consumers to regulate technologies or business models which can impact consumers or competition by monetizing consumers' personal information; (7) Business models in dynamic and surveillance pricing have results that  will  lead  to  the  elimination  of  numerous  jobs,  the  existence  of which are relied upon by the communities of the state for the livelihood of their residents and by the state directly for economic growth; (8) Protection of these jobs will further protect consumers who are part of this economic ecosystem and will add to the growth of the economy and job market of Nebraska; and (9) By enacting laws regulating dynamic, surveillance, and personalized  algorithmic  pricing,  Nebraska  can  protect  and  promote  the growth of jobs in the future.

Surveillance pricing includes the use of technological methods, systems, or tools, including, but not limited to,  sensors,  cameras,  device  tracking,  biometric  monitoring,  or  other forms  of  observation  or  data  collection  that  are  capable  of  gathering information  about  the  behavior,  characteristics,  location,  or  other personal  attributes  of  a  consumer,  whether  in  physical  or  digital environments. Sec.  4. (1)  Any  business  that  knowingly  advertises,  promotes, labels, or publishes a statement, display, image, offer, or announcement that uses personalized algorithmic pricing using consumer data specific to a particular consumer is required to provide a clear and conspicuous disclosure to consumers that the price was set by an algorithm using the personal data of the consumer. (2) A grocery store larger than ten thousand square feet shall not use electronic shelving labels or any digital shelf display technology, and shall use a nondigital presentation of price. This prohibition does not  limit  the  ability  of  any  grocery  store  or  business  to  provide  a consumer a discount, promotional price, or loyalty program benefit based on the consumer's previous purchase history. (3) No business shall use electronic shelving labels or any digital shelf display technology to display personalized algorithmic pricing or surveillance pricing. (4)  No  business  shall  use  personalized  algorithmic  pricing  or surveillance pricing for any reason. (5)  No  business  shall  collect  or  use  the  data  of  an  individual younger than seventeen years of age for targeted advertising or personalized algorithmic pricing under any circumstances. Sec.  5. The  Protecting  Consumers  and  Jobs  from  Predatory  Pricing Act shall not apply to any: (1) Person providing financial services, including, but not limited to,  financial  institutions,  financial  institution  affiliates,  brokerdealers, registered investment advisors, and persons that provide consumer  credit  products  such  as  credit  cards,  personal  loans,  and mortgages; or (2)  Insurer  licensed,  regulated,  or  otherwise  authorized  to  do business in Nebraska, including any person, agent, or affiliate acting on behalf of such insurer. Sec.  6. (1)  Whenever  a  potential  violation  of  the  Protecting Consumers  and  Jobs  from  Predatory  Pricing  Act  occurs,  the  Attorney General may bring an action in the name of the State of Nebraska in a court of competent jurisdiction to enjoin and restrain such violation and its continuance. (2)  In  connection  with  any  such  action,  the  Attorney  General  is authorized to take evidence, make a determination of the relevant facts, and to issue subpoenas. (3)  If  it  appears  to  the  satisfaction  of  the  court  that  the defendant has, in fact, violated the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act, the court may issue an injunction, enjoining and restraining  such  violation  and  its  continuance  without  requiring  proof that any person has, in fact, been injured or damaged by the violation.
