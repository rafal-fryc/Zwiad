# Georgia SB 540 — Conversational AI Services Regulation for Minors

**Jurisdiction:** Georgia
**Status:** Signed into law (May 11, 2026)
**Effective Date:** July 1, 2027
**Official Text:** [Georgia General Assembly – SB 540](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/73452)

## Summary

Georgia SB 540 regulates operators of AI companion chatbots — conversational AI systems designed to simulate sustained human-like relationships through persistent memory of past interactions. The bill passed both chambers of the Georgia General Assembly with bipartisan support and was signed by Governor Brian Kemp on May 11, 2026. It takes effect July 1, 2027.

The law requires chatbot operators to disclose that users are interacting with an AI system, implement parental controls and privacy tools for minor users, and establish response protocols when users express suicidal ideation or self-harm intent. Enforcement authority rests with the Georgia Attorney General, who may bring civil actions with penalties of up to $10,000 per knowing violation.

## Key Provisions

- **AI Disclosure:** Operators must notify users that they are interacting with an AI system, not a human.
- **Minor Protections:** Operators must provide tools for parents or minors to manage screen time, privacy settings, notifications, safety settings, and relationship-simulation features.
- **Age Assurance:** Before enabling features that may generate sexually explicit content, operators must use commercially reasonable age assurance methods.
- **Self-Harm Protocols:** Operators must adopt a protocol governing how an AI companion chatbot responds to users expressing suicidal ideation or self-harm intent.
- **No Embedded-Platform Carve-Out:** Unlike some comparable state laws, SB 540 contains no exemption for chatbots embedded within larger platforms, meaning operators such as Meta and Google are subject to the law's requirements if they operate qualifying AI companion chatbots in Georgia.
- **Exclusions:** Internal business tools, customer service chatbots, educational tools, and AI companion chatbots operated by licensed mental health or behavioral health professionals are excluded from coverage.

## Enforcement

The Georgia Attorney General may bring civil enforcement actions. Penalties reach $10,000 per knowing violation, plus compensatory damages and attorney's fees. A 30-day notice and opportunity-to-cure provision applies to first-time, non-malicious violations.

## Update 2026-05-19

**Status change: passed-first-chamber → signed**

Governor Brian Kemp signed Georgia SB 540 into law on May 11, 2026, making Georgia the latest state to enact a chatbot safety statute. The law targets operators of AI companion chatbots — systems that simulate sustained human-like relationships through persistent memory — and takes effect July 1, 2027. Key obligations include mandatory AI-identity disclosure to users, parental controls for minors, commercially reasonable age assurance before enabling sexually explicit content features, and formal response protocols for users expressing suicidal ideation or self-harm intent. Uniquely, SB 540 contains no carve-out for chatbots embedded within larger platforms, meaning major technology companies operating qualifying AI companion chatbots in Georgia are covered. The Georgia Attorney General may bring civil enforcement actions with penalties up to $10,000 per knowing violation; first-time non-malicious violations receive a 30-day notice-and-cure period.
