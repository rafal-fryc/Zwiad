# Oregon Enacts AI Companion Chatbot Law SB 1546 With Private Right of Action

**Jurisdiction:** Oregon | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Oregon Governor Tina Kotek signed [SB 1546](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/SB1546) into law, making Oregon the first state to enact an AI companion chatbot safety law with a private right of action and statutory damages. The law, which takes effect January 1, 2027, requires operators of AI companion systems to implement suicide and self-harm detection protocols, provide mandatory disclosures, and comply with heightened protections for minors. Violations carry statutory damages of $1,000 per violation, enforceable exclusively through private litigation rather than Attorney General action.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- SB 1546 passed the Oregon Senate 26-1 and the House 52-0 on March 5, 2026, and was [signed into law by Governor Kotek](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/oregon-lawmakers-pass-major-chatbot-bill-in-significant-win-for-kids-and-ai-safety), with an effective date of January 1, 2027 ([OLIS](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2026R1/Measures/Overview/SB1546)).
- The law defines "artificial intelligence companions" as AI systems that simulate sustained human-like relationships with users and retain contextual information across interactions to personalize engagement ([Miller Nash LLP](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/oregons-new-ai-companion-law-what-you-need-to-know)).
- All operators must disclose that users are interacting with AI (not a human), detect expressions of suicidal ideation or self-harm, interrupt conversations when necessary, and refer users to crisis resources including the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline ([Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/03/oregon-legislature-passes-bill-regulating-consumer-facing-interactive-ai-with-private-right-of-action/)).
- For users known or reasonably believed to be minors, additional requirements apply: prohibition on sexually explicit content, "take a break" prompts at least every three hours, and restrictions on engagement-maximizing tactics such as reward systems designed to prolong engagement ([Miller Nash LLP](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/oregons-new-ai-companion-law-what-you-need-to-know)).
- SB 1546 provides a private right of action -- not AG enforcement -- for users who suffer "ascertainable loss of money or property or other injury in fact," with statutory damages of $1,000 per violation, injunctive relief, and attorney fees ([Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102mmmi/oregon-sb-1546-the-first-chatbot-law-with-real-teeth)).
- Oregon's law is part of a broader West Coast trend: Washington Governor Ferguson signed [HB 2225](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/double-win-washington-gov-ferguson-signs-two-major-ai-safety-bills-into-law) on March 24, 2026, enacting similar AI chatbot protections, and both build on California's SB 243 framework ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/resource/the-rest-of-the-west-oregon-and-washington-build-on-california-chatbot-law/)).
- Unlike California's SB 243 and Washington's HB 2225, which include AG enforcement, Oregon's SB 1546 relies exclusively on private litigation, making it the first chatbot safety law with "real teeth" for individual plaintiffs ([Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102mmmi/oregon-sb-1546-the-first-chatbot-law-with-real-teeth)).

## Action Items

- **By January 1, 2027:** Operators of AI companion systems accessible to Oregon users must implement suicide/self-harm detection and crisis referral protocols, add prominent disclosures that the system is AI-generated, and deploy age-gating or minor-detection mechanisms with corresponding content and engagement restrictions.
- **Assess litigation exposure:** Unlike peer states, SB 1546's exclusive enforcement mechanism is private lawsuits with $1,000-per-violation statutory damages and attorney fees. Evaluate the potential class action risk given the per-violation damages structure.
- **Review product design:** Audit engagement-maximizing features (reward systems, streak mechanics, notification patterns) that could trigger liability under the minor-protection provisions.
- **Monitor related state laws:** Track Washington HB 2225 (effective date TBD) and California SB 243 for compliance harmonization opportunities across the three West Coast jurisdictions.
