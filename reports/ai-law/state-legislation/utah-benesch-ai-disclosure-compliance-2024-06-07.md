# Utah Generative AI Disclosure Law: Benesch Compliance Analysis for Consumer-Facing Businesses

**Jurisdiction:** Utah | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** June 7, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Utah's [Artificial Intelligence Policy Act (UAIPA), enacted as SB 149](https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/static/SB0149.html) and signed March 13, 2024, became effective May 1, 2024 — making Utah the first US state to impose binding disclosure obligations on entities using generative AI in consumer-facing interactions. As analyzed by [Benesch, Friedlander, Coplan & Aronoff LLP](https://www.beneschlaw.com/resources/utah-generative-ai-disclosure-law-takes-effect-as-part-of-consumer-protection-legislation.html) in June 2024, the law is notable for its extraterritorial scope: any business interacting with Utah residents — regardless of where the business is organized or located — is subject to the UAIPA's disclosure obligations. The Act creates a two-tier framework: all covered entities must disclose AI use on request, while entities providing services in regulated occupations must disclose proactively at the start of any interaction. Enforcement rests with the Utah Division of Consumer Protection with fines up to $2,500 per violation, and the Utah Attorney General may pursue injunctive relief and disgorgement. Benesch's analysis emphasizes that businesses should audit consumer-facing tools and implement both on-demand and proactive disclosure mechanisms to achieve compliance.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Utah's First-Mover Status in Generative AI Regulation

[Senate Bill 149](https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/static/SB0149.html), introduced during Utah's 2024 legislative session as "Artificial Intelligence Amendments," was signed by Governor Spencer Cox on March 13, 2024. It amended Utah's existing [Consumer Sales Practices Act (CSPA)](https://le.utah.gov/~2024/bills/static/SB0149.html) rather than creating a standalone regulatory framework, deliberately grafting AI disclosure obligations onto proven consumer protection enforcement infrastructure. The law took effect May 1, 2024 — giving businesses a compliance window of fewer than seven weeks from signing.

At the time of enactment, no other US state had adopted legislation specifically regulating private-sector use of generative AI. As [Greenberg Traurig noted](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/4/utah-enacts-first-ai-focused-consumer-protection-legislation-in-us), Utah's UAIPA was the "first AI-focused consumer protection legislation in the US." The law predated Colorado's broader AI Act (SB 24-205, signed May 2024), California's AI Transparency Act (SB 942, signed September 2024), and any federal AI disclosure statute.

### Relationship to Utah's Consumer Protection Framework

The UAIPA does not stand alone — it operates within the Division of Consumer Protection's (UDCP) existing authority over Utah's consumer-protection statutes. The law adds AI-specific disclosure obligations and anti-fraud provisions to the list of behaviors regulated under the Consumer Sales Practices Act. Violations of AI-specific obligations are treated as deceptive acts or practices under the CSPA, activating the UDCP's full enforcement toolkit including administrative fines, injunctions, and disgorgement. Per [Orrick's April 2024 analysis](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/04/Utah-AI-Laws-Require-Consumer-Facing-Disclosures-Starting-May-1), Utah also simultaneously enacted HB 452 (the "Artificial Intelligence Learning" bill), which established the Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy and the AI Learning Laboratory regulatory sandbox.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope of Coverage: Extraterritorial Application

A key feature emphasized by [Benesch's June 2024 analysis](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/utah-generative-ai-disclosure-law-takes-4253678/) is the UAIPA's extraterritorial reach. The law covers any "person" who "uses, prompts, or otherwise causes" generative AI to interact with individuals "in connection with any act administered and enforced by" the UDCP. This formulation captures businesses headquartered outside Utah, provided they interact with Utah residents. Benesch specifically notes that "all businesses interacting with Utah residents are subject to the UAIPA and UDCP, regardless of where the business itself is organized or located."

The covered activities are tied to the UDCP's regulatory jurisdiction, which includes: the Consumer Sales Practices Act, the Utah Consumer Privacy Act, the Utah Social Media Regulation Act, and the laws governing regulated occupations certified by the Department of Commerce.

### Two-Tier Disclosure Framework

The UAIPA establishes two categories of disclosure obligation based on the nature of the service being provided:

**Tier 1 — On-Request Disclosure (All Covered Entities)**

Any person using generative AI to interact with a consumer in connection with a UDCP-administered activity must clearly and conspicuously disclose that the interaction involves generative AI — but only when asked or prompted by the consumer. The key compliance challenge, as [Benesch identifies](https://www.beneschlaw.com/resources/utah-generative-ai-disclosure-law-takes-effect-as-part-of-consumer-protection-legislation.html), is that inquiries may be posed in numerous forms ("Am I talking to a bot?", "Is this AI?") and may be directed at the AI system itself rather than a human operator. Businesses must therefore configure their generative AI tools to recognize and respond to such inquiries with a "clear and conspicuous" disclosure.

**Tier 2 — Proactive Disclosure (Regulated Occupations)**

Entities providing services through "regulated occupations" — a category covering any occupation requiring a state license or certificate — must proactively disclose the use of generative AI at the outset of any interaction, without waiting to be asked. The list of regulated occupations is extensive, spanning more than 30 healthcare professions (physicians, nurses, mental health counselors, dentists), attorneys, accountants, engineers, pest control operators, and barber shop operators. As [FPF's analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/chatbots-in-check-utahs-latest-ai-legislation/) notes, the proactive obligation applies both to verbal exchanges (disclosure at the start of the interaction) and electronic messaging (disclosure prior to written exchanges beginning).

### Definition of Generative AI

The UAIPA defines generative AI as an "artificial system" that: (a) is trained on data; (b) interacts with a person using text, audio, or visual communication; and (c) generates non-scripted outputs similar to outputs a human could create, with limited or no human oversight. As explained by [Regulations.AI's SB 149 summary](https://regulations.ai/regulations/utah-sb-149-ai-policy-act-2024), this definition is intentionally technology-neutral and broad, capturing current large language models, image generators, voice synthesis systems, and similar AI tools deployed in consumer interactions.

### Anti-Fraud Provision

Separate from the disclosure requirements, the UAIPA makes it a deceptive trade practice to use generative AI to generate content with intent to defraud, deceive, or manipulate consumers. This provision is enforced directly under the CSPA. Significantly, the "AI-made-me-do-it" defense is foreclosed: [Skadden's April 2024 analysis](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2024/04/utah-becomes-first-state) confirms that businesses may not attribute consumer protection violations to outputs generated by AI tools — human accountability for AI-generated content in commercial interactions is expressly preserved.

### Enforcement Mechanisms

Enforcement is vested in the Utah Division of Consumer Protection and the Utah Attorney General:

- **Administrative Fines**: The UDCP may impose fines up to $2,500 per violation. Where a court or administrative order has already been issued, subsequent violations can incur fines up to $5,000 per violation.
- **Civil Enforcement**: The Utah AG may bring actions for declaratory and injunctive relief, and may seek disgorgement of any money received in violation of the UAIPA.
- **Private Right of Action**: The UAIPA does not create a private right of action. Only the UDCP and AG may initiate enforcement.
- **Per-Transaction Exposure**: Each transaction can constitute a separate violation, meaning that scaled deployment of non-compliant AI tools across many consumers creates compounding penalty exposure.

### Regulatory Sandbox

In parallel with the disclosure requirements, Utah established the "AI Learning Laboratory Program" — a voluntary regulatory sandbox administered by the newly created Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy. Businesses may apply to participate and, if accepted, enter into a regulatory mitigation agreement allowing them to reduce regulatory responsibilities related to AI system testing for a limited period. The sandbox is designed to encourage AI innovation without full regulatory exposure during development and testing phases.

### 2025 Amendments (SB 226 and SB 332)

As reported by [Davis Polk in March 2025](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/utah-scales-back-reach-generative-ai-consumer-protection-law), the Utah legislature amended the UAIPA through SB 226 and SB 332 in early 2025, with amendments effective May 7, 2025. Key changes include:

- **Narrowed Disclosure Triggers**: Proactive disclosure is now required only for "high-risk" AI interactions — defined as interactions involving the collection of sensitive personal information (financial, health, or biometric data) combined with personalized recommendations that could reasonably be relied upon for significant personal decisions.
- **Safe Harbor**: A person is not subject to enforcement if the generative AI itself clearly and conspicuously discloses its AI nature at the outset of and throughout any interaction.
- **Narrowed Definition**: "AI system" is now defined to require the system be "designed to simulate human conversation," excluding routine automated communications such as appointment reminders.
- **Sunset Extended**: The UAIPA's repeal date was extended from May 2025 to July 1, 2027.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Entities and Industries

The UAIPA's broad coverage creates compliance obligations across virtually all industries deploying consumer-facing generative AI tools:

- **Healthcare and Licensed Professions**: The heaviest compliance obligations fall on physicians, nurses, mental health counselors, dentists, attorneys, accountants, and engineers — all subject to Tier 2 proactive disclosure. Any telehealth platform, legal tech chatbot, or AI-assisted professional service tool used with Utah residents requires proactive disclosure protocols.
- **Retail and E-Commerce**: Customer service chatbots, product recommendation tools, and virtual shopping assistants fall under Tier 1 on-demand disclosure obligations.
- **Financial Services**: Robo-advisors, AI-powered financial planning tools, and AI-assisted banking services face both Tier 1 and potentially Tier 2 disclosure requirements.
- **Technology and SaaS Platforms**: Any consumer-facing SaaS platform using generative AI in interactions with Utah-resident users is subject to coverage, regardless of headquarters location.

### Compliance Timeline and Recommended Steps

The Benesch analysis emphasizes that the law became effective May 1, 2024, meaning businesses were already potentially in violation as of the finding date (June 7, 2024). Key compliance steps identified by Benesch and other law firm analyses include:

1. **Inventory consumer-facing AI tools**: Identify all generative AI systems that interact with consumers in any UDCP-regulated activity or regulated occupation context.
2. **Configure on-demand disclosure**: Reprogram AI systems to recognize questions about AI identity in varied forms and respond clearly. The AI tool itself must be capable of self-identification.
3. **Implement proactive disclosure for regulated occupations**: Build disclosure scripts into the initial stages of any interaction conducted via generative AI in a licensed profession context.
4. **Review extraterritorial reach**: Even if the business is not based in Utah, assess whether it serves Utah residents; if so, the UAIPA applies.
5. **Update content policies**: Ensure AI-generated consumer content complies with the anti-fraud provision; do not use AI to obscure the AI origin of materials in ways that could deceive consumers.

### Enforcement Outlook

As of June 2024, no enforcement actions had been publicly reported under the UAIPA. The [IAPP noted](https://iapp.org/news/a/utah-brings-gen-ai-into-consumer-protection-realm-with-bill-passage) that the UDCP has not issued formal guidance on the law, creating interpretive uncertainty around what constitutes a "clearly and conspicuously" compliant disclosure. The 2025 amendments, while narrowing scope, also introduced a safe harbor that provides a clearer compliance pathway for businesses that build disclosure functionality directly into their AI tools.

### Comparison to Other State AI Disclosure Laws

Utah's UAIPA was the first mover in this regulatory space, but by mid-2024, other states were moving in similar directions:

- **Colorado** (SB 24-205, effective February 2026): Broader scope covering algorithmic discrimination in consequential decisions; risk-based rather than disclosure-focused; more comprehensive obligations for developers and deployers.
- **California** (SB 942, effective January 2026): Targets generative AI providers with over 1 million monthly users; requires AI content detection tools and manifest/hidden watermarking.
- **Tennessee** (ELVIS Act, 2024): Focuses on AI-generated voice and likeness protections rather than general disclosure requirements.

As [Davis+Gilbert's comparative analysis](https://www.dglaw.com/utah-colorado-and-other-states-lead-groundbreaking-ai-legislation-in-u-s/) observes, Utah's disclosure-focused approach contrasts with Colorado's risk-management framework — reflecting different legislative priorities: Utah prioritizes consumer transparency at the point of interaction, while Colorado prioritizes preventing algorithmic bias in high-stakes decisions.

## Action Items

- Review all consumer-facing AI deployments for Utah resident interactions and map them to Tier 1 or Tier 2 disclosure obligations under the UAIPA.
- Configure generative AI tools to self-identify when asked by consumers, in any form of inquiry, including direct questions to the AI system.
- For any licensed or regulated occupation services delivered to Utah residents via AI, implement mandatory proactive disclosure at the start of each interaction.
- Update privacy policies and terms of service to reflect generative AI use in consumer interactions.
- Assess whether post-May 2025 amendments (SB 226, SB 332) change the compliance posture — particularly whether the "high-risk" AI interaction threshold applies to your use cases and whether the safe harbor can be adopted.
- Monitor UDCP enforcement activity for initial enforcement actions and interpretive guidance on the "clearly and conspicuously" standard.
- For businesses in multiple states, track Colorado (effective February 2026) and California (effective January 2026) AI disclosure laws for alignment or divergence with the Utah approach.
