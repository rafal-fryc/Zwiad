# Federal Court Stays Enforcement of Colorado AI Act as xAI and DOJ Mount Constitutional Challenge

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-05-08

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 27, 2026, a federal magistrate judge in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado approved a joint motion by xAI and the Colorado Attorney General to stay enforcement of the Colorado AI Act ([SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)), placing the country's first comprehensive state AI law entirely on hold while constitutional litigation proceeds. The case — *x.AI LLC v. Weiser*, No. 1:26-cv-01515 — was filed April 9, 2026 by xAI, which challenges the law on First Amendment, Commerce Clause, due process, and equal protection grounds; the U.S. Department of Justice intervened on April 24, 2026, adding a separate Fourteenth Amendment equal protection challenge. The court's order bars Colorado from initiating any enforcement actions or investigations under the Act while the stay remains in place, and conditions further litigation milestones on Colorado completing either rulemaking or replacing the law with new legislation.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Case:** [*x.AI LLC v. Weiser*, No. 1:26-cv-01515](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73171074/x-ai-llc-v-weiser/) (D. Colo.) — filed April 9, 2026 by xAI (developer of the Grok large language model) against Colorado Attorney General Philip J. Weiser ([Colorado Sun, April 24, 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/04/24/doj-joins-lawsuit-colorado-ai-law-federal-court/)).
- **Stay order date:** April 27, 2026, granted by Magistrate Judge Cyrus Y. Chung of the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado ([HR Dive, April 28, 2026](https://www.hrdive.com/news/colorado-ai-bias-law-unconstitutional-elon-musks-xai/817258/)).
- **Scope of stay:** Colorado will not initiate investigations or enforcement actions under SB 24-205 for violations occurring on or before the 14th day after the court's ruling on any anticipated preliminary injunction motion ([Baker McKenzie / Connect on Tech](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/colorado-two-step-already-facing-potential-amendments-a-federal-court-pauses-enforcement-of-colorados-forthcoming-ai-law/)).
- **Litigation trigger:** xAI must file a motion for preliminary injunction or amended complaint within 28 days of Colorado completing rulemaking or enacting new legislation to replace or amend SB 24-205 ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/colorado-ai-act-hits-wall-litigation-legislative-uncertainty-and-enforcement)).
- **DOJ intervention:** On April 24, 2026, the Department of Justice filed a Complaint in Intervention, making this the Trump administration's first litigation action to curtail state AI regulation authority — a move previewed in Trump's December 2025 executive order directing the DOJ to establish an AI Litigation Task Force ([DOJ press release, April 24, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-intervenes-xai-lawsuit-challenging-colorados-algorithmic-discrimination)).
- **xAI's constitutional claims:** xAI pleads six counts targeting four constitutional objections: (1) First Amendment challenges to SB 24-205's substantive obligations and mandated disclosures (arguing that designing, training, and deploying AI models is protected expressive activity); (2) Commerce Clause and extraterritoriality challenges; (3) a due-process vagueness claim; and (4) an equal protection challenge to the law's diversity carve-out ([Jenner & Block client alert, April 2026](https://www.jenner.com/en/news-insights/client-alerts/doj-joins-xai-in-lawsuit-challenging-colorado-ai-act)).
- **DOJ's distinct claim:** The DOJ's complaint in intervention focuses exclusively on equal protection, arguing that SB 24-205 unlawfully requires AI companies to suppress disparate racial and sex impacts in their outputs while simultaneously exempting differential treatment designed to advance "diversity" — creating an impermissible racial and sex classification under the Fourteenth Amendment ([DOJ Complaint in Intervention, April 24, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/crt/media/1437846/dl)).
- **AG Weiser's position:** Colorado's Attorney General — who agreed to the joint stay motion — co-signed a letter with Governor Polis and Senate Majority Leader Robert Rodriguez stating that a "state-by-state patchwork of regulation poses significant challenges to the cultivation of a strong technology sector" ([Colorado Politics, June 2024](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/news/colorado-governor-attorney-general-vow-to-revise-recently-enacted-ai-law/article_53aa96b8-2a7e-11ef-9d99-d791f3cce317.html)).
- **The law being challenged:** SB 24-205 was signed by Governor Polis on May 17, 2024, making Colorado the first U.S. state to enact comprehensive high-risk AI regulation. It imposes risk management, impact assessment, bias mitigation, and disclosure obligations on developers and deployers of "high-risk AI systems" used in consequential decisions (employment, housing, credit, education, healthcare). Its effective date had already been delayed once — from February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2026 — after the Colorado legislature passed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) during a special session in August 2025, which Governor Polis signed on August 28, 2025 ([Colorado General Assembly SB 24-205 page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)).

## Broader Significance [HIGH confidence]

The *xAI v. Weiser* case is the first federal constitutional challenge to a state comprehensive AI law to reach the stay stage, and the DOJ's intervention transforms it from a private dispute into a direct test of the Trump administration's strategy to limit state AI regulation through litigation. The DOJ AI Litigation Task Force — created pursuant to President Trump's [December 11, 2025 executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) — identified state laws imposing AI-specific algorithmic discrimination obligations as priority targets.

The constitutional questions raised — whether AI model design and training constitute protected speech, whether state disparate-impact mandates violate equal protection, and whether extraterritorial application of state AI rules conflicts with the Commerce Clause — have no direct precedent. A ruling either way could have cascading effects on AI regulation in Connecticut, New York, Illinois, and other states advancing similar frameworks in 2026.

Attorney General Weiser's decision to join the stay request rather than contest it, combined with the Colorado legislature's active work on a major rewrite (Senate Bill 189, introduced May 2026), signals that the law faces near-simultaneous pressure from all three branches at the state and federal levels.

## Action Items

- **Pause SB 24-205 compliance investments:** Developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems operating in Colorado should hold SB 24-205-specific compliance buildouts pending the outcome of the preliminary injunction motion and any legislative replacement. The enforcement stay means no investigations or penalties can be initiated while the case is active.
- **Watch the 28-day clock:** If Colorado enacts new legislation or completes rulemaking — most likely following adjournment of the legislative session on May 13, 2026 — xAI's motion for a preliminary injunction must be filed within 28 days. A ruling on that motion will be the next major litigation milestone.
- **Monitor SB 189 concurrently:** Colorado Senate Bill 189, which proposes to repeal and replace SB 24-205, was advancing through the legislature as of early May 2026. If enacted, SB 189 resets compliance obligations (see [related report below](#related-reports)). The final shape of the replacement statute will determine whether the constitutional challenge proceeds, narrows, or is mooted.
- **Assess exposure in other states:** The DOJ's equal protection theory, if adopted, would implicate any state AI law with a diversity carve-out or disparate-impact framework. Compliance teams should flag analogous provisions in Connecticut SB 5, New York AI bills, and other state high-risk AI statutes.
- **Preserve existing documentation:** Despite the enforcement pause, companies that built SB 24-205 compliance programs should retain their risk assessments and impact documentation. This material may directly support compliance with any successor statute or with requirements in other jurisdictions.
