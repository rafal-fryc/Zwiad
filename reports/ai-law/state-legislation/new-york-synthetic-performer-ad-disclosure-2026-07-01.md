# New York AI Synthetic Performer Advertising Disclosure Law Now in Effect

**Jurisdiction:** New York | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-07-01

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

New York's first-in-the-nation law requiring disclosure when advertisements feature AI-generated "synthetic performers" took effect on June 9, 2026. The underlying development was reported via a July 1, 2026 Lexology digest item and was subsequently picked up and researched during this platform's July 17, 2026 scan cycle (finding ID `SCAN-20260717-071`); this report's `date`/`first_reported` fields reflect the July 1, 2026 date on which the development was originally reported in the source digest, not the later scan date encoded in the finding ID. The law amends [General Business Law § 396-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/396-B) and requires any person who produces or creates a covered advertisement to conspicuously disclose the use of a synthetic performer whenever they have actual knowledge of its inclusion, backed by escalating civil penalties. Governor Kathy Hochul signed the underlying bills, [S.8420-A/A.8887-B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8420/amendment/A), on December 11, 2025, and formally announced the law's effectiveness via [a press release](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-first-nation-law-requiring-disclosure-when-advertisements-include-ai) confirming the June 9, 2026 effective date. <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [NEEDS REVIEW]

- The law amends [General Business Law § 396-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/396-B) and requires conspicuous disclosure whenever a commercial advertisement includes a "synthetic performer" — defined as a digitally created, reproduced, or modified asset generated via AI or a software algorithm that creates the impression of a human performer but is not recognizable as any actual identifiable person, per the [Governor's press release](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-first-nation-law-requiring-disclosure-when-advertisements-include-ai) and [Cooley's client alert](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai). <!-- verified -->
- Governor Hochul signed the enabling bills, [S.8420-A (Senate)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8420/amendment/A) and A.8887-B (Assembly), on December 11, 2025; the disclosure obligation became effective **June 9, 2026** — six months after enactment — per [McDermott Will & Emery's analysis](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-yorks-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-what-advertisers-need-to-know/) and confirmed in the Governor's own announcement of the law's effectiveness. <!-- verified -->
- Covered advertising is broad and not limited to television: it extends to digital advertising, social media campaigns, online video, sponsored content, and streaming advertisements reaching New York consumers, regardless of where the advertiser is headquartered, per [Crowell & Moring's client alert](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/synthetic-performers-real-consequences-implications-of-trailblazing-new-york-ai-ad-law) and [All About Advertising Law](https://www.allaboutadvertisinglaw.com/2026/06/new-york-ai-advertising-disclosure-requirement-now-in-effect.html). <!-- verified -->
- Statutory exemptions cover "expressive works" (films, TV programs, streaming content, documentaries, video games, and similar audiovisual productions), audio-only advertisements, and advertisements where AI is used solely for translation, per [Governor's press release](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-first-nation-law-requiring-disclosure-when-advertisements-include-ai) and [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-29-new-york-enacts-synthetic-performer-disclosure-law-for-advertisements-including-those-using-generative-ai). <!-- verified -->
- The statute does not prescribe specific disclosure wording or placement, requiring only that the notice be "conspicuous" to consumers; illustrative language offered by an advocacy organization includes phrasing such as "This advertisement contains one or more synthetic performers generated using artificial intelligence," per [Transparency Coalition's coverage](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/using-ai-in-an-ad-youve-got-to-disclose-it-starting-this-month-in-new-york). This specific example wording could not be independently corroborated against a second source as of this writing; the broader point that the statute itself does not prescribe wording is confirmed by multiple other sources. <!-- unverifiable: source unavailable (WebFetch permission denied across rounds 1-2; example quote not independently corroborated via WebSearch; broader no-prescribed-wording claim is verified) -->
- Civil penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each subsequent violation, per the [Governor's press release](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-first-nation-law-requiring-disclosure-when-advertisements-include-ai) and [Lowenstein Sandler's analysis](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/articles/new-york-legislation-requires-disclosure-on-ai-generated-performers-in-advertising-and-strengthens-post-mortem-publicity-rights-savare-sterba-tijani). <!-- verified -->
- A companion law signed the same day, [S.8391/A.8882](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8391) (amending [NY Civil Rights Law § 50-f](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVR/50-F)), strengthens New York's post-mortem digital replica and right-of-publicity protections for deceased performers — a related but legally distinct statute from GBL § 396-b, not part of the same legislative package. It requires consent from a deceased individual's heirs or successors-in-interest before their digital replica or likeness can be used commercially or in an expressive audiovisual work/sound recording, and became effective immediately upon enactment on December 11, 2025. Confirmed via [Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/articles/new-york-legislation-requires-disclosure-on-ai-generated-performers-in-advertising-and-strengthens-post-mortem-publicity-rights-savare-sterba-tijani), [Skadden's alert on the two newly enacted laws](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/01/two-newly-enacted-new-york-laws-will-regulate), and [Debevoise & Plimpton's summary](https://www.debevoisedatablog.com/2025/12/17/new-york-enacts-landmark-ai-right-of-publicity-laws/), each of which describes the advertising-disclosure law and the posthumous-publicity-rights law as two distinct enactments signed at the same event rather than a single package. <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- Advertisers and agencies running campaigns reaching New York consumers should audit current and planned creative for AI-generated or AI-assisted synthetic performers and confirm conspicuous disclosure language is in place now that the June 9, 2026 effective date has passed.
- Legal and compliance teams should distinguish "synthetic performers" (covered) from expressive-work content, audio-only ads, and AI-assisted translation (exempted) when scoping compliance obligations.
- Given the $1,000/$5,000 escalating penalty structure, build a documented disclosure review step into ad approval workflows, since penalties apply per violation.
- Monitor for New York Attorney General or Department of State guidance on acceptable disclosure formats, since the statute itself does not specify required wording or placement.
- Track the separate, companion posthumous digital-replica/publicity-rights law (S.8391/A.8882, amending Civil Rights Law § 50-f), signed the same day, which creates additional consent obligations for advertisers using likenesses of deceased performers.
