# AI Summer Roundup: Colorado and the EU Establish Parallel Comprehensive AI Frameworks

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado, EU | **Category:** AI Law / Privacy | **Date:** 2024-08-19

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

During the summer of 2024, two major comprehensive AI regulatory frameworks emerged simultaneously on opposite sides of the Atlantic. Colorado became the first US state to enact comprehensive AI legislation when Governor Jared Polis signed [Senate Bill 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205) on May 17, 2024 — a law requiring developers and deployers of "high-risk" AI systems to use reasonable care to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination. At the EU level, [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng), the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, entered into force on August 1, 2024, after publication in the Official Journal on July 12, 2024. Both regimes share a risk-based architecture focused on protecting individuals from discrimination and opaque algorithmic decision-making, though their scope, enforcement mechanisms, and timelines differ substantially. Organizations operating across jurisdictions face overlapping but non-identical compliance obligations that require immediate planning attention.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Colorado: The Road to SB 24-205

Colorado's AI legislation emerged from years of concern over algorithmic discrimination in consequential decision-making. The bill was carried by Senator Robert Rodriguez and passed through both chambers of the Colorado General Assembly in spring 2024. Governor Polis signed [SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205) on May 17, 2024, while expressing reservations in an accompanying [signing letter](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_205_signed.pdf) urging federal preemption and noting the bill may need amendments before its effective date.

Colorado's comprehensive consumer privacy statute, the Colorado Privacy Act (CPA), provided the existing regulatory backdrop. SB 24-205 addresses AI-specific risk that goes beyond the CPA's data rights framework, focusing specifically on how AI systems interact with protected characteristics when making consequential decisions. The [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-july/colorado-enacts-law-regulating-high-risk-artificial-intelligence-systems/) noted in its July 2024 analysis that SB 24-205 is modeled in part on Colorado's existing consumer protection framework, with the Colorado AG holding exclusive enforcement authority.

### EU: Adoption of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

The EU Artificial Intelligence Act represents the culmination of more than three years of legislative work following the Commission's initial proposal in April 2021. After the European Parliament voted to adopt the final text in March 2024, the regulation was published in the [Official Journal of the European Union](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ:L_202401689) on July 12, 2024, entering into force on August 1, 2024. The Act spans 180 recitals and 113 articles, establishing the world's first comprehensive horizontal regulatory framework for AI systems. As documented by [EU digital strategy resources](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), the framework's risk-tiered approach was explicitly designed to accommodate future technological change while providing legal certainty for operators and developers.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Colorado SB 24-205: Key Provisions

**Scope and Definitions**

SB 24-205 applies to any "developer" or "deployer" of a "high-risk artificial intelligence system" doing business in Colorado. Per the [Colorado General Assembly's enrolled text](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_205_signed.pdf), a "high-risk AI system" is one that makes, or is a substantial factor in making, a consequential decision — defined as a decision with a material legal or similarly significant effect on the provision or denial of: education enrollment or opportunities; employment or employment opportunities; financial or lending services; essential government services; health-care services; housing; insurance; or legal services. The statute expressly excludes AI systems used solely for narrow, specified purposes such as anti-fraud detection, network security, and certain research functions.

**Developer Obligations**

Developers — those who create or intentionally and substantially modify high-risk AI systems — must, per the [National Association of Attorneys General analysis](https://www.naag.org/attorney-general-journal/a-deep-dive-into-colorados-artificial-intelligence-act/):

- Use reasonable care to protect consumers from known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination.
- Disclose to the Colorado AG and known deployers any known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination within 90 days of discovery.
- Provide deployers with documentation including: training data summaries; data governance measures; evaluation documentation for algorithmic discrimination; intended use cases; foreseeable limitations; technical capabilities; and artifacts such as model cards, dataset cards, or impact assessments.
- Make available to deployers the information necessary for deployers to complete their own impact assessments.

**Deployer Obligations**

Deployers — those who deploy high-risk AI systems — must use reasonable care to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination. A rebuttable presumption of compliance arises if a deployer, per [TrustArc's compliance analysis](https://trustarc.com/resource/colorado-ai-law-sb24-205-compliance-guide/):

- Implements a risk management policy incorporating principles, processes, and personnel for identifying and mitigating discrimination risks.
- Completes impact assessments of each high-risk system, and re-performs them within 90 days of any significant modification.
- Annually reviews each high-risk deployment for algorithmic discrimination.
- Notifies consumers when a high-risk AI system makes or will be a substantial factor in making a consequential decision affecting them.
- Provides consumers an opportunity to correct incorrect personal data used in such decisions.
- Provides consumers an opportunity to appeal, via human review (if technically feasible), any adverse consequential decision.
- Publishes a publicly accessible statement describing types of high-risk AI systems deployed and the nature of consequential decisions made.

**Small Business Exemption**

Per the [CDT's FAQ analysis](https://cdt.org/insights/faq-on-colorados-consumer-artificial-intelligence-act-sb-24-205/), deployers with fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees are exempt from the requirements to publish a website statement, conduct independent impact assessments, and implement a risk management policy — but only if: the deployer does not train the AI system on its own data; the deployer uses the system only for its intended purpose as specified by the developer; and the deployer provides consumers with any impact assessment furnished by the developer. Notably, this exemption does not apply to developers regardless of size.

**Enforcement**

The Colorado Attorney General holds exclusive enforcement authority. Violations are treated as unfair trade practices under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act and are subject to enforcement actions. There is no private right of action under SB 24-205.

**Effective Date — Post-Signing Developments**

As of the August 2024 article by [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.eyeonprivacy.com/2024/08/ai-summer-roundup-eu-and-colorado-celebrate-summer-with-ai-legislation/), the law was set to take effect February 1, 2026. However, subsequent developments have modified this: Governor Polis signed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205) in August 2025 following a failed special legislative session, delaying the effective date to **June 30, 2026**. The 2026 regular legislative session was expected to consider substantive amendments.

### EU AI Act: Key Provisions

**Risk-Based Architecture**

The [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng) organizes AI systems into four risk tiers. Systems posing "unacceptable risk" (e.g., social scoring by public authorities, real-time biometric identification in public spaces with limited exceptions, systems exploiting psychological vulnerabilities) are flatly prohibited. "High-risk" systems — covering AI in biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, access to essential services, law enforcement, immigration, and administration of justice — face the most demanding compliance obligations. "Limited-risk" systems must meet transparency requirements. "Minimal-risk" systems face no mandatory requirements.

**High-Risk System Obligations**

Providers (developers) of high-risk AI systems must, per the [EU digital-strategy resource portal](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) and the [Lowenstein Sandler client alert](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/the-eu-artificial-intelligence-act-of-2024-what-you-need-to-know-privacy):

- Establish quality and risk management systems.
- Ensure data governance and data quality for training, validation, and testing data.
- Maintain technical documentation.
- Implement logging and automatic record-keeping.
- Provide transparency documentation to deployers.
- Enable human oversight.
- Achieve specified accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity standards.
- Register the AI system in the EU database.

Deployers of high-risk AI systems must use systems in accordance with provider instructions, ensure human oversight by competent individuals, ensure input data is relevant and sufficiently representative, conduct fundamental rights impact assessments in specified contexts, provide explanations of decisions to affected individuals, and monitor operation while reporting unacceptable risks.

**General-Purpose AI (GPAI) Models**

A notable EU-specific category involves GPAI models (e.g., large language models). From [August 2, 2025](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/steptechtoe-blog/eu-ai-act-obligations-for-gpai-models-now-applicable.html), providers of GPAI models must: maintain and publish technical documentation; provide summaries of training data; comply with EU copyright law; and share information with regulators and downstream deployers. Models posing "systemic risk" (presumed when trained on more than 10²⁵ FLOPs of cumulative computation) face additional obligations: adversarial testing, incident reporting, and enhanced cybersecurity.

**Enforcement Structure**

The [European AI Office](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), established within the Commission, oversees GPAI model compliance and coordinates enforcement across member states. National competent authorities handle enforcement of high-risk system obligations at the member state level. Penalties for prohibited practices are up to EUR 35 million or 7% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher); penalties for high-risk system violations are up to EUR 15 million or 3% of global turnover.

### Comparative Analysis

| Feature | Colorado SB 24-205 | EU AI Act |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | Reasonable-care standard + rebuttable presumption | Mandatory requirements + conformity assessments |
| Scope trigger | "Consequential decision" in 8 categories | Risk tier classification across broader set of use cases |
| Key concern | Algorithmic discrimination | Fundamental rights, safety, and transparency |
| Enforcement | Colorado AG (exclusive) | EU AI Office + national authorities |
| Private right of action | None | None (member states may supplement) |
| Small business carve-out | Yes (deployers < 50 FTE) | Risk-proportionate; no blanket SME exemption |
| Developer obligations | Disclosure, documentation | Comprehensive conformity + registration |
| Effective date (as of 2024) | February 1, 2026 (later delayed to June 30, 2026) | Phased: prohibited practices Feb 2025; GPAI Aug 2025; high-risk Aug 2026 |

Both frameworks are notably silent on federal preemption in the US context; Colorado's legislation explicitly anticipated (and invited) federal action.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Industries and Organizations

Both regimes target overlapping sectors: employment decision-making (hiring, promotion, termination), financial services (credit, lending, insurance underwriting), healthcare (clinical decision support, diagnostic AI), education (admissions, grading), and housing. Companies deploying AI in any of these sectors and serving Colorado consumers or EU residents face dual compliance exposure.

The [Sheppard Mullin analysis](https://www.eyeonprivacy.com/2024/08/ai-summer-roundup-eu-and-colorado-celebrate-summer-with-ai-legislation/) emphasized that both regimes particularly target algorithmic discrimination — the risk that AI systems produce disparate outcomes based on protected characteristics — as a top compliance concern for developers and deployers alike. Organizations without existing bias testing and impact assessment workflows face significant build-out requirements.

### Compliance Obligations and Timeline

**Immediate (August 2024 onward):**
- EU AI Act in force; prohibited practices not yet enforceable (enforcement begins February 2025).
- Organizations should begin mapping AI systems against EU risk categories.
- Begin Colorado SB 24-205 compliance planning with an eye toward the February 2026 (later June 2026) effective date.

**February 2, 2025:**
- EU: Prohibitions on unacceptable-risk AI practices are enforceable. Penalties up to EUR 35 million or 7% of global turnover apply.
- AI literacy obligations for providers and deployers take effect.

**August 2, 2025:**
- EU: GPAI model obligations become applicable; affected providers must have technical documentation, training data summaries, and copyright compliance programs in place.

**June 30, 2026:**
- Colorado SB 24-205: Effective date for all developer and deployer obligations (as amended by SB 25B-004).

**August 2, 2026:**
- EU: High-risk AI system obligations fully applicable. Commission enforcement powers for GPAI models commence.

### Enforcement Outlook

Colorado's exclusive AG enforcement model, combined with the absence of a private right of action, suggests an enforcement environment where significant resources will focus on the most visible deployers (large employers, financial institutions, healthcare systems). The EU's multi-layered enforcement — national authorities for high-risk systems, EU AI Office for GPAI — combined with substantial penalty ceilings, creates far more distributed enforcement risk for global organizations.

Both regimes are heavily disclosure-dependent: enforcement is expected to concentrate on organizations that fail to maintain documentation, conduct impact assessments, or provide required consumer disclosures, since those failures leave paper trails.

## Action Items

- **Inventory AI systems now.** Map all AI tools used in employment, lending, insurance, healthcare, housing, or education against both the Colorado "consequential decision" definition and the EU Annex III high-risk categories. Do not wait for an effective date to begin this work.
- **Assess EU exposure first.** The EU AI Act's phased rollout began in February 2025; organizations with EU operations need to assess whether any deployed systems fall in the prohibited-practices category. Engage EU counsel to evaluate GPAI obligations if the organization deploys or builds on large language models.
- **Build developer-to-deployer documentation flows.** Both regimes require developers to furnish deployers with specific information. Organizations operating as both developers and deployers (e.g., companies that fine-tune foundation models for internal use) need internal documentation protocols treating the two roles as distinct compliance tracks.
- **Prepare impact assessments.** Colorado requires deployers to complete impact assessments and re-perform them within 90 days of significant modification. The EU requires fundamental rights impact assessments in specified high-risk contexts. A unified impact assessment methodology that satisfies both frameworks is achievable and advisable for multi-jurisdiction operators.
- **Monitor Colorado amendments.** Governor Polis explicitly invited the legislature to revisit SB 24-205 before its effective date. Track the 2026 regular session for substantive changes to the small business exemption, scope definitions, or enforcement mechanism before finalizing compliance programs.
- **Track federal preemption developments.** Colorado's law and similar state bills create pressure for federal AI legislation. If Congress enacts preemptive federal law, state compliance obligations may shift substantially.
