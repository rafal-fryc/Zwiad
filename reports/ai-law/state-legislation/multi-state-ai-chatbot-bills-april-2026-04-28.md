# State AI Legislation Wave: Connecticut SB 5 Passes Legislature, Colorado HB 1263 Enacted, California Chatbot Bills Advance

**Jurisdiction:** Connecticut, Colorado, California, Florida | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-28

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The week of April 27, 2026 marked a turning point in state AI regulation. Connecticut's Senate Bill 5 — now styled the [AI Responsibility and Transparency Act](https://www.cga.ct.gov/asp/CGABillStatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&bill_num=SB5) — passed both chambers by wide bipartisan margins (Senate 32-4, House 131-17) and heads to Governor Ned Lamont, who has indicated he will sign it. Colorado's [HB 1263](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263) governing conversational AI service operators has been enacted and takes effect January 1, 2027. In California, four companion chatbot and AI disclosure bills cleared key committee hurdles. In Florida, a House leadership decision killed Governor DeSantis's "AI Bill of Rights" for the second time despite a 35-2 regular session Senate vote on SB 482 and a 37-1 special session Senate vote on the refiled SB-2D.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

**Connecticut SB 5 (AI Responsibility and Transparency Act)**

- Senate passed SB 5 on April 21, 2026 by a vote of 32-4 after extensive floor debate, per [CT Mirror](https://ctmirror.org/2026/04/21/artificial-intelligence-regulation-senate-ct/).
- House passed the bill on May 1, 2026 by a vote of 131-17, per [CT Mirror House passage report](https://ctmirror.org/2026/05/01/artificial-intelligence-house-regulation-passage-ct/).
- Governor Lamont has publicly indicated he intends to sign the bill, per [CT News Junkie](https://ctnewsjunkie.com/2026/05/02/bill-regulating-ai-heads-to-lamonts-desk-after-bipartisan-house-passage/).
- **Automated employment decisions:** Employers deploying automated tools in recruiting or personnel decisions must provide disclosures and pre-decision notices; using such tools is not a defense to discrimination claims, per [Employment Law Letter](https://www.employmentlawletter.com/2026/05/connecticuts-ai-responsibility-and-transparency-act-key-impacts-on-the-workplace/).
- **Frontier developer obligations:** Persons who train a foundation model using at least 10^26 floating-point operations and do business in Connecticut must maintain internal processes to address catastrophic risks and are prohibited from retaliating against employees who report safety concerns.
- **Companion chatbot safety:** Operators of AI companion chatbots must implement evidence-based detection of suicidal ideation, refer users to the 988 crisis hotline, and limit outputs encouraging self-harm or sexualized interactions with minors.
- **Synthetic content provenance:** AI-generated content must be detectable as such; subscription-based AI providers must disclose key terms to consumers.
- **WARN notice addition:** Connecticut employers must now include AI-related disclosures in WARN notices filed with the state Department of Labor.
- **Effective dates:** Core employer obligations and frontier developer provisions take effect October 1, 2026; interactive disclosure and pre-decision notice requirements for automated employment decisions apply to deployments on or after October 1, 2027, per [Employment Law Letter](https://www.employmentlawletter.com/2026/05/connecticuts-ai-responsibility-and-transparency-act-key-impacts-on-the-workplace/).

**Colorado HB 1263 (Conversational AI Service Operator Requirements)**

- Passed the Colorado House by a vote of 40-24, per [Colorado Politics](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/04/12/bipartisan-colorado-bill-targeting-ai-chatbot-risks-advances/).
- Enacted into law; effective date January 1, 2027, per [Colorado General Assembly bill page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263).
- Defines "conversational AI service" as a publicly accessible AI system primarily simulating human conversation through text, visual, or audio communications.
- **Disclosure:** Operators must clearly disclose at the start of interactions, at regular intervals, and upon user request that the user is interacting with an AI — not a human.
- **Minor protections:** Operators may not provide points or rewards to minors to encourage chatbot engagement; may not generate or display harmful content including self-harm material or sexually explicit content to minor users.
- **Mental health protocols:** Operators must implement a protocol for user prompts regarding suicidal ideation or self-harm and report annually to the Attorney General.
- **Professional misrepresentation ban:** Operators may not imply chatbot output is provided by, endorsed by, or equivalent to services from licensed professionals (e.g., lawyers, physicians, therapists).
- **Civil penalty:** $1,000 per violation, per [Troutman Pepper Locke analysis](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-27-2026/).

**California AI Bills (Committee Stage)**

- **AB 1988 (PAUSE Act):** Passed Assembly Health Committee on April 22, 2026; now in Appropriations. Requires companion chatbots to detect "credible crisis expressions" (suicidal ideation, violent intent), prompt users to seek human support, and impose a mandatory 20-minute crisis interruption pause upon escalation or repeat expressions, per [California Legislative Information](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1988).
- **AB 2023 / SB 1119 (Companion Chatbots: Children's Safety):** AB 2023 passed Assembly Privacy and Consumer Protection Committee 13-2; SB 1119 passed Senate Privacy Committee 7-0 and Judiciary Committee 13-0. Companion bills require annual child-safety risk assessments, prohibit harmful and sexually explicit content for minors, mandate parental controls and time limits, and establish crisis response protocols, per [California Senator Steve Padilla's office](https://sd18.senate.ca.gov/news/nations-strongest-chatbot-protections-children-advance-california-legislature).
- **AB 1609 (Customer Service Chatbots):** Passed committee; pending Assembly Appropriations hearing May 6, 2026. Would require large businesses (annual gross revenue over $500 million) to disclose AI chatbot use, provide access to a human agent within 15 minutes of request, and post telephone numbers conspicuously on websites. Penalties up to $10,000 per repeated violation, per [California Legislative Information](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB1609).

**Florida Special Session — AI Blocked**

- Florida's House Speaker Daniel Perez declared on the first day of the special session (April 28, 2026) that the House would only address congressional redistricting, blocking Governor DeSantis's "AI Bill of Rights" for the second time in two months, per [Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/04/28/florida-speaker-kills-desantis-ai-regulation-vaccine-repeal-bills-on-first-day-of-special-session/).
- The Florida Senate had approved SB 482 (the AI Bill of Rights) 35-2 during the March 2026 regular session, per [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-03-06/florida-senate-approves-ai-bill-of-rights-remains-halted-in-house) and the [Florida Senate bill page](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/482), and a refiled version, SB-2D, 37-1 during the April 2026 special session, but no companion bills were filed in the House for either, per [Bay News 9](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/04/29/florida-lawmakers-won-t-address-possible-ai-regulations-during-special-session-).
- The AI Bill of Rights would have banned companion chatbots from interacting with minors without parental consent and required bots to disclose their non-human nature, per [Florida Phoenix background](https://floridaphoenix.com/2026/04/20/floridas-ai-bill-of-rights-what-happened-whats-in-it-and-whats-next/).

## Action Items

- **Connecticut employers (immediate):** Audit all automated tools used in recruiting and personnel decisions — disclosures, pre-decision notices, and anti-discrimination compliance obligations take effect October 1, 2026. Begin mapping which tools meet the "developer/deployer" classification.
- **Frontier AI developers operating in Connecticut:** Assess whether training compute exceeds 10^26 FLOP threshold triggering the frontier developer obligations (catastrophic risk protocols, whistleblower protections). Effective October 1, 2026.
- **Colorado chatbot operators:** HB 1263 is enacted with a January 1, 2027 effective date. Operators must implement human-disclosure, minor-protection protocols, and AG annual reporting mechanisms before that date.
- **California-facing businesses:** Monitor AB 1988, AB 2023/SB 1119, and AB 1609 through California's Appropriations and floor stages. AB 1609's Appropriations hearing is scheduled May 6, 2026 — passage there would signal strong momentum toward floor vote.
- **Large businesses (>$500M revenue):** Begin planning for AB 1609's potential human-access-within-15-minutes requirement even before enactment given the operational complexity of compliance.
- **Florida watchers:** DeSantis is expected to continue pushing AI regulation; monitor for a renewed legislative push in the 2027 regular session or another special session.
- **Federal preemption risk:** All state AI laws remain subject to federal preemption risk under the Trump administration's executive order framework directing Commerce and DOJ to challenge state AI laws. Track DOJ AI Litigation Task Force activity.
