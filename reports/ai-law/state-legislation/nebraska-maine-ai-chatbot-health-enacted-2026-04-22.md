# Nebraska Chatbot Disclosure Law and Maine AI Mental Health Ban Enacted; State AI Legislative Wave Accelerates

**Jurisdiction:** Nebraska, Maine, and Multiple States | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** April 22, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Nebraska Governor Jim Pillen signed [LB 525](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/bills/view_bill.php?DocumentID=59601) on April 14, 2026, making Nebraska the fourth state in 2026 to enact a chatbot disclosure law. On April 13, 2026, Maine Governor Janet Mills signed [LD 2082](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?snum=132&ld=2082) (now Chapter 687), prohibiting any person or AI system from independently providing therapy or psychotherapy services without a licensed professional. Both laws reflect a rapidly expanding state regulatory movement: more than a dozen additional AI bills advanced in state legislatures during the same week, and Connecticut's Senate passed a sweeping frontier-AI bill on April 21, 2026.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

### Nebraska LB 525 — Conversational AI Safety Act

- **Signed:** April 14, 2026 by Governor Jim Pillen; passed the unicameral legislature 49-0 on April 10, 2026 ([Nebraska Legislature](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/bills/view_bill.php?DocumentID=59601))
- **Dual-purpose bill:** LB 525 adopts both the Agricultural Data Privacy Act and the Conversational Artificial Intelligence Safety Act — the AI provisions originated from LB 1185 ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/))
- **Chatbot disclosure to minors:** Operators of conversational AI services must disclose to minors that they are interacting with an AI, not a human ([Nebraska Unicameral Update](https://update.legislature.ne.gov/?p=40890))
- **General disclosure obligation:** Operators must also disclose to any user that the service is not human when a reasonable person would not otherwise understand this ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/))
- **Content safeguards for minors:** Operators must adopt measures to prevent the AI service from producing sexually explicit content or statements simulating romantic or sexual innuendo with minors ([Nebraska Unicameral Update](https://update.legislature.ne.gov/?p=40890))
- **Mental health safety protocol:** Operators must adopt a protocol to respond to user prompts regarding suicidal ideation or self-harm that refers users to crisis service providers ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/))
- **No AI mental health services:** The chatbot service cannot represent or state that it is designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/))
- **Enforcement:** Nebraska Attorney General may bring civil action; penalties range from $1,000 per violation to $500,000 per operator, plus declaratory relief and actual damages ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/))
- **National context:** Nebraska is the fourth state to enact a chatbot law in 2026, following California (SB 243, effective January 1, 2026), Oregon (SB 1546), and Washington (HB 2225) ([Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/2026-chatbot-legislation-tracker/))

### Maine LD 2082 — AI in Mental Health Services

- **Signed:** April 13, 2026 by Governor Janet Mills; enacted as [Chapter 687](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?snum=132&ld=2082) ([Maine Legislature](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?snum=132&ld=2082))
- **Operative prohibition:** No person may provide, advertise, or offer therapy or psychotherapy services through AI unless the services are delivered by a licensed professional ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/))
- **Administrative AI use permitted:** Licensed mental health professionals may use AI for administrative tasks (scheduling, billing, note-taking, progress tracking) but must maintain full responsibility for all AI outputs ([TrackBill / Maine Legislature](https://trackbill.com/bill/maine-legislative-document-2082-an-act-to-regulate-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-providing-certain-mental-health-services/2762640/))
- **Therapeutic AI prohibited:** AI is prohibited from making independent therapeutic decisions, directly conducting therapeutic communication with clients, or generating treatment plans without licensed professional review and approval ([TrackBill / Maine Legislature](https://trackbill.com/bill/maine-legislative-document-2082-an-act-to-regulate-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-providing-certain-mental-health-services/2762640/))
- **Consent and disclosure:** Clients must receive written disclosure and provide explicit consent before AI may be used when therapy sessions are recorded or transcribed, including information about data storage, retention, and deletion ([TrackBill / Maine Legislature](https://trackbill.com/bill/maine-legislative-document-2082-an-act-to-regulate-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-providing-certain-mental-health-services/2762640/))
- **Penalties:** Civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation, enforced by the Maine Department of Professional and Financial Regulation following administrative hearing ([TrackBill / Maine Legislature](https://trackbill.com/bill/maine-legislative-document-2082-an-act-to-regulate-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-providing-certain-mental-health-services/2762640/))

### Additional State AI Legislative Developments (Week of April 14–20, 2026) [MEDIUM confidence]

The following state bills advanced materially during the same reporting period, per [Troutman Pepper Locke's April 20 update](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-ai-law-update-april-20-2026/):

- **Iowa SF 2417 (Chatbot Disclosure):** House passed amended bill and returned to Senate for concurrence; would require conversational AI to disclose to minors that it is not human or a mental health professional
- **Tennessee SB 1700 and SB 837:** Both bills passed the full Senate
- **Oklahoma SB 1521 and HB 3544:** Both bills, having passed their chamber of origin, advanced out of opposite-chamber committees with "do pass" recommendations
- **Hawaii HB 1782 and SB 3001:** Both bills passed their respective chambers as amended, with each chamber subsequently disagreeing with the other's amendments and returning the bills for further concurrence
- **Connecticut SB 5 (Comprehensive AI):** Connecticut's Senate passed an amended comprehensive AI bill on April 21, 2026, by a vote of 32-4; the bill regulates developers of "frontier" AI models, creates an AI regulatory sandbox, governs youth social media and AI chatbot use, and provides whistleblower protections for frontier AI developers ([CT Mirror](https://ctmirror.org/2026/04/21/artificial-intelligence-regulation-senate-ct/))

## Action Items

- **Audit Nebraska chatbot deployments:** If your organization operates a conversational AI service accessible to Nebraska users — particularly minors — evaluate whether the service complies with LB 525's disclosure, content safeguard, and mental health protocol requirements. The law is in effect as of signing (April 14, 2026); confirm the applicable effective date with Nebraska counsel.
- **Review Maine AI therapy products immediately:** Any product or service that uses AI to conduct therapeutic or psychotherapy interactions with clients in Maine must either (a) cease the independent AI-delivered therapy component or (b) ensure a licensed professional supervises and approves all AI outputs. The $10,000-per-violation penalty structure creates significant exposure for non-compliance.
- **Audit AI-scripted mental health content broadly:** Both Nebraska LB 525 (prohibition on representing a chatbot as able to provide professional mental health care) and Maine LD 2082 (prohibition on AI-delivered therapy) signal a national trend. Assess any chatbot product that touches mental health topics — crisis response scripts, wellness apps, and therapy-adjacent features are all potentially in scope.
- **Track Iowa, Tennessee, Oklahoma, Hawaii bills to enrollment:** All four state chatbot/AI bills advanced in the same week and are on near-term paths to enactment. Subscribe to bill-status alerts for Iowa SF 2417, Tennessee SB 1700/SB 837, Oklahoma SB 1521/HB 3544, and Hawaii HB 1782/SB 3001.
- **Monitor Connecticut SB 5:** Now passed by the Senate, Connecticut SB 5 moves to the House. If enacted, its frontier-model requirements and chatbot regulations would create significant new obligations for AI developers operating in Connecticut.
- **Map multi-state chatbot compliance:** With California, Oregon, Washington, Nebraska, and potentially Idaho, Georgia, Iowa, Tennessee, Oklahoma, and Hawaii all enacting or advancing chatbot laws in 2026, compliance teams should build a unified chatbot disclosure matrix across all enacted state laws. The FPF's [2026 Chatbot Legislation Tracker](https://fpf.org/2026-chatbot-legislation-tracker/) is a reliable starting resource.
