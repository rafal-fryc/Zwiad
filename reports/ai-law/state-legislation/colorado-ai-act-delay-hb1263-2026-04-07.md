# Colorado AI Act Enforcement Delayed Until June 30, 2026; Chatbot Safety Bill HB 1263 Advances

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Governor Jared Polis signed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) on August 28, 2025, <!-- verified --> delaying enforcement of the Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act ([SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)) from February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2026. <!-- verified --> The delay followed a failed special legislative session where lawmakers could not agree on substantive amendments to the nation's first comprehensive state AI law. <!-- verified --> Separately, Colorado [HB 26-1263](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263), a bipartisan chatbot safety bill targeting minors' interactions with conversational AI, cleared the House Business Affairs and Labor Committee on March 26, 2026, <!-- verified --> and was pending House floor action as of early April 2026. <!-- verified -->

## Background [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

The Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act ([SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)), signed into law in May 2024, <!-- verified --> is the first comprehensive state law in the United States regulating high-risk artificial intelligence systems. <!-- verified --> Modeled in part on the EU AI Act, it imposes obligations on both "developers" and "deployers" of high-risk AI systems used in "consequential decisions" -- decisions that have material legal or similarly significant effects on consumers in areas such as employment, education, housing, insurance, and financial services ([American Bar Association analysis](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-july/colorado-enacts-law-regulating-high-risk-artificial-intelligence-systems/)). <!-- verified -->

Developers must disclose information about training data, known risks of algorithmic discrimination, and system limitations to deployers. <!-- verified --> Deployers must implement risk management programs, conduct impact assessments, provide consumer notifications when AI is used in consequential decisions, and offer mechanisms to correct data and appeal adverse outcomes ([Akin Gump analysis](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/colorado-postpones-implementation-of-colorado-ai-act-sb-24-205)). <!-- verified --> Both developers and deployers must exercise "reasonable care" to protect consumers from known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination ([NAAG deep dive](https://www.naag.org/attorney-general-journal/a-deep-dive-into-colorados-artificial-intelligence-act/)). <!-- verified -->

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

### Enforcement Delay: SB 25B-004

Governor Polis called a special legislative session in August 2025 to address concerns that the AI Act would impose excessive costs on state and local governments and covered businesses. <!-- verified --> Senate Majority Leader Robert Rodriguez <!-- verified --> initially proposed the "AI Sunshine Act" (SB 4), <!-- verified --> which would have narrowed the original law while maintaining key consumer protections. When negotiations collapsed, Rodriguez amended his proposal to simply delay the effective date from February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2026 ([Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2025/08/25/colorado-ai-law-tweak-dies/)). <!-- verified --> The bill passed and the Governor signed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) on August 28, 2025. <!-- verified -->

Critically, SB 25B-004 does not alter any substantive requirements of the AI Act. <!-- verified --> The obligations that deployers and developers will face on June 30, 2026, are identical to those originally set for February 1 ([Clark Hill analysis](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/colorados-ai-law-delayed-until-june-2026-what-the-latest-setback-means-for-businesses/)). <!-- verified --> Colorado lawmakers have signaled that further amendments may be considered during the 2026 regular session, but as of April 2026, no bill modifying SB 24-205's core requirements has been enacted. <!-- verified -->

### Chatbot Safety Bill: HB 26-1263

[HB 26-1263](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263), introduced on February 19, 2026, <!-- verified --> would establish requirements for operators of "conversational artificial intelligence services" -- AI systems accessible to the general public that primarily simulate human conversation. <!-- verified --> The bill, sponsored by Representatives Sean Camacho and Javier Mabrey, <!-- verified --> would take effect January 1, 2027, <!-- verified --> and includes the following key provisions ([Colorado Newsline](https://coloradonewsline.com/2026/03/27/colorado-lawmakers-advance-bill-to-regulate-ai-chatbot-platforms/); [CPR News](https://www.cpr.org/2026/02/26/ai-kids-prevent-suicides-bipartisan-bill/)):

- **Disclosure requirement:** Operators must clearly and conspicuously notify minors that they are interacting with AI, not a human.
- **Anti-manipulation provisions:** Operators are prohibited from offering rewards to increase engagement with children and must prevent chatbots from creating emotional dependence through false claims of being human.
- **Content restrictions:** Operators must take reasonable measures to prevent AI chatbots from generating sexually explicit content when a minor is the user, and must prevent romantic or sexual role-playing interactions with minors.
- **Parental controls:** Required if chatbots are accessible to children under age 13.
- **Suicide prevention:** Operators must provide suicide-prevention resources for users who express suicidal thoughts or interest in self-harm.

Violations constitute a deceptive trade practice enforceable by the Attorney General under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act, with civil penalties of $1,000 per violation. <!-- verified -->

The bill passed the House Business Affairs and Labor Committee 10-3 on March 26, 2026, <!-- verified --> and was referred to the Committee of the Whole ([Colorado House Democrats](https://www.cohousedems.com/news/ai-chatbot-protections-bill-passes-committee)). <!-- verified --> As of March 31, 2026, it was listed at House Second Reading, laid over daily. <!-- verified --> No confirmed House floor vote was found in available sources as of April 7, 2026. <!-- verified -->

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

**AI deployers and developers** operating in Colorado face a firm June 30, 2026 deadline to comply with the AI Act's requirements for high-risk systems. Because no substantive amendments have been enacted despite ongoing legislative discussion, organizations should prepare for the law as written in SB 24-205. Key compliance obligations include implementing risk management programs, completing impact assessments, establishing consumer notification and appeal mechanisms, and conducting annual reviews for algorithmic discrimination.

**Conversational AI operators** should monitor HB 26-1263's progress. If enacted, it would impose additional child-safety obligations effective January 1, 2027, including disclosure, content moderation, and parental control requirements. The $1,000-per-violation civil penalty structure and Attorney General enforcement mechanism represent meaningful enforcement risk for platforms with large user bases.

**Broader signal:** Colorado continues to position itself as a leading state jurisdiction for AI regulation, with both its comprehensive AI Act and targeted chatbot legislation moving forward. Other states considering AI legislation are likely watching Colorado's approach.

## Action Items

- **By June 30, 2026:** Deployers of high-risk AI systems must have risk management policies, impact assessments, consumer notification mechanisms, and appeal processes in place under SB 24-205.
- **Developers** must prepare disclosure documentation, training data summaries, and algorithmic discrimination risk disclosures for deployers by the same deadline.
- **Monitor HB 26-1263** through the 2026 Colorado legislative session for House floor vote and potential Senate action. If enacted, begin compliance planning for January 1, 2027 effective date.
- **Track potential SB 24-205 amendments** during the 2026 regular session that could modify core AI Act requirements before the June 30 enforcement date.
