# Texas AI Law TRAIGA 2.0 Now in Effect: Enforcement Ramp-Up Begins

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-06-15

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Texas's Responsible AI Governance Act (TRAIGA 2.0), enacted as [House Bill 149](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/HB00149F.htm) and signed by Governor Greg Abbott on June 22, 2025, has been in effect since January 1, 2026, making Texas one of the first states in the country with a live, operative AI governance law. Six months in, the Texas Attorney General has not yet filed a formal enforcement action — the complaint portal required by statute is still under construction and must be operational by September 1, 2026, at which point enforcement activity is likely to accelerate. Organizations doing business in Texas should treat this window as a final compliance opportunity, not a grace period.

**Clarification note:** The original scanner finding described TRAIGA 2.0 as requiring "impact assessments, consumer notifications, and opt-out rights for automated decision-making." This characterization reflects the original 2024 proposal (HB 1709), not the enacted law. The final HB 149 eliminated all those requirements for private-sector entities. The operative TRAIGA 2.0 imposes no private-sector impact assessment mandate and no consumer opt-out right for automated decisions. See the Detailed Analysis below for what the law actually requires.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Law in force:** TRAIGA 2.0 (HB 149) became effective January 1, 2026. Signed by Governor Abbott on [June 22, 2025](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?LegSess=89R&Bill=HB149).
- **Enforcement status:** As of May 2026, the Texas AG has filed no formal enforcement actions and has issued no civil investigative demands. The [TRAIGA enforcement tracker](https://traiga.news/traiga-enforcement-what-the-texas-ag-has-and-has-not-done-and-what-comes-next/) confirms the complaint portal is still being built.
- **Complaint portal deadline:** The statute requires the AG's complaint portal to be operational by September 1, 2026, after which consumer complaints can be formally received and trigger the enforcement sequence. [Source: Duane Morris analysis](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/texas_new_ai_law_is_now_in_effect_what_employers_need_to_know_about_traiga2_0526.html).
- **Who is covered:** Any person or entity that develops or deploys an AI system in Texas, produces a product or service used by Texas residents, or promotes, advertises, or conducts business in Texas. No revenue or data-processing threshold applies — geographic nexus to Texas is sufficient. [Source: K&L Gates analysis](https://www.klgates.com/Pared-Back-Version-of-the-Texas-Responsible-Artificial-Intelligence-Governance-Act-Signed-Into-Law-6-24-2025).
- **Private sector obligations (limited):** The enacted law imposes no impact assessment mandate, no consumer notification requirement, and no opt-out right for private-sector entities. The primary private-sector obligation is avoiding the statute's categorical prohibitions on intentional AI misuse. [Source: Littler Mendelson analysis](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/texas-joins-fray-and-enacts-ai-legislation).
- **Government entity obligations (more extensive):** State and local government agencies must (1) disclose to individuals when they are interacting with an AI system, before or at the time of the interaction; (2) refrain from AI-based social scoring; and (3) ensure AI use does not violate constitutional rights. [Source: Greenberg Traurig analysis](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/6/traiga-key-provisions-of-texas-new-artificial-intelligence-governance-act).
- **Penalties:** Civil penalties of $10,000–$200,000 per violation and up to $40,000 per day for continuing violations. The AG must provide 60 days' written notice and a cure period before seeking penalties for curable violations. [Source: Wiley Law alert](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Texas-Responsible-AI-Governance-Act-Enacted).
- **No private right of action:** Enforcement is exclusively by the Texas AG. Individuals cannot sue under TRAIGA.

## What TRAIGA 2.0 Actually Prohibits [HIGH confidence]

The enacted law uses an **intent-based framework** focused on specific harmful uses rather than broad sector-based AI risk categories. All entities — public and private — are prohibited from:

1. Developing or deploying AI systems **with the intent to unlawfully discriminate** against a protected class under federal or Texas state law. Note: disparate impact alone (an AI system producing statistically unequal outcomes without discriminatory design) does **not** constitute a violation under this standard. This is a materially lower bar than the Colorado AI Act's "reasonable care" standard.
2. Using AI to **manipulate human behavior** in a way designed to incite self-harm, violence, or criminal activity.
3. Using AI to **generate child sexual abuse material** (CSAM) or unlawful deepfake pornography.
4. Developing or deploying AI systems with the **sole intent of impairing constitutional rights** of any person.
5. Using **biometric data from publicly available sources** to uniquely identify individuals without consent.

Government entities face an additional prohibition: AI-based **social scoring** systems that assign scores tied to personal characteristics for use in determining access to public services.

[Source: Wikipedia TRAIGA entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TRAIGA); [Perkins Coie analysis](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/texas-enacts-responsible-artificial-intelligence-governance-act).

## Safe Harbors [MEDIUM confidence]

TRAIGA provides three significant safe harbors that reduce litigation risk for good-faith actors:

- **Third-party misuse:** A developer or deployer is not liable for violations caused by a third party's unauthorized use of the AI system.
- **Audit and remediation:** If a violation is discovered through internal testing or a good-faith audit and promptly remediated, no penalty applies.
- **NIST AI RMF compliance:** Organizations that substantially comply with the [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2023/01/26/AI%20RMF%201.0.pdf) qualify for a safe harbor. This creates a de facto compliance standard even though NIST RMF adherence is not mandated.

[Source: Ropes & Gray analysis](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/06/navigating-traiga-texas-new-ai-compliance-framework).

## Regulatory Sandbox and Advisory Council [MEDIUM confidence]

TRAIGA establishes two institutional structures:

- **Regulatory Sandbox (36 months):** Administered by the Texas Department of Information Resources (DIR). Approved participants may test experimental AI applications with reduced regulatory burden. The sandbox does not exempt participants from TRAIGA's core prohibitions (CSAM, discriminatory intent, behavior manipulation). No applications have been publicly reported as approved as of this writing.
- **AI Advisory Council (7 members):** Appointed by the governor, lieutenant governor, and speaker of the house. The council has no rulemaking authority; it is advisory only. Its mandate includes conducting AI training programs for state agencies, recommending AI policy, and issuing reports on AI ethics, data privacy, and legal compliance.

[Source: Goodwin Law analysis](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/alerts-practices-dpc-with-traiga-lone-star-state-leans); [Latham & Watkins alert](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/texas-signs-responsible-ai-governance-act-into-law).

## Action Items

- **Government entities in Texas:** If not already compliant, immediately implement pre-interaction AI disclosure notices for all public-facing AI systems. The January 1, 2026, effective date has passed; continued non-compliance is a live enforcement risk once the AG's complaint portal opens in September 2026.
- **Private employers and businesses:** Audit AI-assisted tools used in hiring, employment decisions, and customer-facing applications to confirm they were not developed or deployed with discriminatory intent. Obtain vendor representations on discriminatory design and retain documentation.
- **All in-scope entities:** Evaluate adoption of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework as a proactive safe harbor strategy. Even partial implementation provides documented risk management evidence that could be decisive in an enforcement context.
- **Healthcare providers:** Note that companion legislation [SB 1188](https://www.hchlawyers.com/blog/2026/february/new-texas-laws-require-ai-disclosure-in-healthca/) imposes separate AI disclosure obligations in medical diagnosis and treatment contexts; TRAIGA alone is not the complete Texas healthcare AI compliance picture.
- **Monitor September 2026:** The AG's complaint portal is required to be live by September 1, 2026. Treat this as the operational start of TRAIGA enforcement, not the statutory effective date of January 1, 2026.
- **Track federal preemption:** The Trump Administration's AI Executive Order (December 2025) directed federal agencies to preempt inconsistent state AI laws. Whether TRAIGA's government disclosure mandates conflict with federal policy remains unresolved.
