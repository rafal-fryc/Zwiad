# Connecticut AI Responsibility and Transparency Act (SB 5) Enacted as Public Act No. 26-15

**Jurisdiction:** Connecticut | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** May 1, 2026

> **Title note:** The bill's official enrolled title is *An Act Concerning Online Safety* (the name under which it appears in the [Connecticut General Assembly bill tracker](https://www.cga.ct.gov/asp/CGABillStatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&bill_num=SB5) and on bill-tracking services such as [TrackBill](https://trackbill.com/bill/connecticut-senate-bill-5-an-act-concerning-online-safety/2804715/)). "AI Responsibility and Transparency Act" is the commonly used branded short title widely adopted by the press and stakeholders; it does not appear in the enrolled text. The enacted law is formally designated **Public Act No. 26-15**.

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Connecticut's [Senate Bill 5](https://www.cga.ct.gov/asp/CGABillStatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&bill_num=SB5) — officially enrolled as *An Act Concerning Online Safety*, commonly known as the Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act, and now enacted as [**Public Act No. 26-15**](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf) — has been signed into law by Governor Ned Lamont. The Senate voted 32-4 on April 21, 2026, and the House voted 131-17 on May 1, 2026. Connecticut is now home to one of the nation's most comprehensive state AI laws, imposing obligations across employment decision-making, frontier model safety, synthetic content provenance, youth social media protections, and state workforce development.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Legislative passage:** The Senate voted 32-4 on April 21, 2026, per [CT Mirror (April 21)](https://ctmirror.org/2026/04/21/artificial-intelligence-regulation-senate-ct/), and the House voted 131-17 on May 1, 2026, giving the bill final passage and sending it to the governor, per [CT Mirror (May 1)](https://ctmirror.org/2026/05/01/artificial-intelligence-house-regulation-passage-ct/) and [Hartford Business Journal](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/connecticut-passes-ai-regulations-after-years-in-development/).
- **Enactment:** Governor Lamont signed SB 5 into law; the Connecticut General Assembly has published the official [Public Act No. 26-15 PDF](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf), confirming enactment. The precise signature date has not been publicly announced by the Governor's office as of the time of writing; the bill was transmitted to the Governor on May 14, 2026, per [FastDemocracy](https://fastdemocracy.com/bill-search/ct/2026/bills/CTB00033095/).
- **Official Public Act text:** The enacted law is at [cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf). The enrolled bill text (R04) remains available at [cga.ct.gov/2026/TOB/S/PDF/2026SB-00005-R04-SB.PDF](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/TOB/S/PDF/2026SB-00005-R04-SB.PDF).
- **Enforcement authority:** The Attorney General is the exclusive enforcement authority for nearly all provisions; there is no private right of action, per [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/05/unpacking-connecticuts-new-ai-law).
- **Cure period:** An optional 60-day cure period applies to violations occurring on or before December 31, 2027, per [Cozen O'Connor / JDSupra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/connecticut-ai-and-youth-social-media-3702701/).

## Provisions by Subject Area [HIGH confidence]

### Automated Employment Decision Technology (AEDT)

Effective **October 1, 2026**, developers of AI tools used as a "substantial factor" in hiring, promotion, discipline, or discharge must provide deployers with compliance-related information. Effective **October 1, 2027**, deployers must notify affected employees and applicants of the technology's use, its purpose, the categories of data processed, and its data sources. SB 5 also amends Connecticut's anti-discrimination statutes to codify that automated decision-making is not a defense to a discrimination claim — courts may consider proactive anti-bias testing as a mitigating factor, per [Shipman & Goodwin](https://www.shipmangoodwin.com/insights/connecticuts-ai-responsibility-and-transparency-act-key-impacts-on-the-workplace.html) and [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/connecticut-poised-to-enact-one-of-the-nations-most-comprehensive-ai-laws-102mrpv).

### WARN Act AI Disclosure

Effective **October 1, 2026**, any employer filing a federal WARN Act notice with the Connecticut Department of Labor must additionally disclose whether the layoffs are related to the employer's use of artificial intelligence or another technological change. Connecticut is among the first U.S. jurisdictions to require this disclosure, per [Shipman & Goodwin](https://www.shipmangoodwin.com/insights/connecticuts-ai-responsibility-and-transparency-act-key-impacts-on-the-workplace.html).

### Frontier Model Developer Whistleblower Protections

Effective **October 1, 2026**, developers training foundation models using more than 10²⁶ computing operations must protect employees who internally report concerns that the developer or model may contribute to a catastrophic risk. Large frontier developers — those with over $500 million annual revenue — must establish an anonymous internal reporting process by **January 1, 2027**, per [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/connecticut-poised-to-enact-one-of-the-nations-most-comprehensive-ai-laws-102mrpv). By **May 1, 2027**, and every three months thereafter, large frontier developers must also prepare and submit quarterly reports to their officers and directors disclosing all anonymously submitted concerns from the preceding quarter, along with the status of investigations and remedial actions (with an exception where the report implicates a specific officer or director), per the [CT OLR Bill Analysis](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/BA/PDF/2026SB-00005-R000338-BA.PDF).

### Synthetic Content Provenance [HIGH confidence]

Public Act No. 26-15 creates a two-tiered structure for synthetic content obligations, with different scopes and effective dates:

**Tier 1 — Large Generative AI Providers (effective October 1, 2026)**

Large generative AI providers — those with more than one million monthly users — must embed provenance data (consistent with standards such as C2PA) into any audio, image, or video content they generate or materially alter, and must take reasonable steps to make that provenance data resistant to removal or tampering. This obligation targets high-volume consumer-facing providers and requires machine-readable records of origin at the point of generation, per [AI Policy Desk](https://www.aipolicydesk.com/blog/connecticut-ai-law-2026-compliance-checklist), [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/05/ct-ai-transparency-safety-consumer-protection-law), and [Transparency Coalition](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/tcai-bill-guide-sb-5-connecticuts-omnibus-ai-and-online-safety-bill). The official Public Act text at [cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf) is the authoritative source; the October 1, 2026 date for this tier is confirmed by multiple independent law firm and policy analyses.

**Tier 2 — All Developers of AI Systems Capable of Generating Synthetic Content (effective October 1, 2027)**

Beginning October 1, 2027, developers of AI systems or general-purpose AI models capable of producing or manipulating synthetic digital content — defined as any digital content (including audio, image, text, or video) produced or manipulated by such a system — must ensure that outputs are marked and detectable as synthetic by the time consumers first encounter or interact with them, in a manner accessible to persons with disabilities. Technical solutions must be effective, interoperable, robust, and reliable, taking into account different types of synthetic content, implementation costs, and the state of the art. Exemptions apply to text-only content on matters of public interest unlikely to mislead a reasonable consumer, systems used for standard editing without substantially altering input data, and systems used to detect, prevent, investigate, or prosecute crime, per [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/05/unpacking-connecticuts-new-ai-law) and the second-tier analysis confirmed by [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ai-regulatory-roundup-recent-developments-in-colorado-connecticut-and-california).

### Youth Social Media Protections

Effective **January 1, 2028**, social media platforms must obtain parental consent before exposing minors to personalized algorithmic feeds. Default settings must include a one-hour daily time limit, no notifications outside 8 AM–9 PM ET, and blocking of sensitive content. A Surgeon General-style warning label occupying 75% of the screen for 30 seconds on first daily access is also required, per the [Kelley Drye AI regulatory roundup](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ai-regulatory-roundup-recent-developments-in-colorado-connecticut-and-california).

### Workforce Development and State Programs

The law creates a Connecticut AI Academy, requires state agency AI inventories and impact assessments, adds AI to K-12 computer science curricula, and directs the Department of Economic and Community Development to design a regulatory sandbox program for AI products (report due January 1, 2028), per [CBIA](https://www.cbia.com/news/issues-policies/sweeping-ai-online-safety-bill).

## Action Items

- **Employers (immediate):** Map all AI tools used in hiring, promotion, discipline, or discharge decisions now; begin vendor due-diligence to confirm AEDT developer obligations can be met by October 1, 2026.
- **Employers (October 1, 2026):** Implement WARN Act AI-disclosure workflows for Connecticut layoff notices; confirm HR and legal are briefed on the new disclosure form and process.
- **Employers (October 1, 2027):** Stand up AEDT pre-decision notification systems for all affected applicants and employees, including disclosures of data categories and data sources.
- **Frontier model developers (October 1, 2026):** Establish internal whistleblower protocols if training runs exceed 10²⁶ FLOPs; large developers (>$500M revenue) must have anonymous reporting in place by January 1, 2027.
- **Large frontier developers (May 1, 2027):** Implement quarterly board-level reporting cadence to officers and directors covering all anonymously submitted employee concerns and the status of related investigations.
- **Large generative AI providers — >1M monthly users (by October 1, 2026):** Implement C2PA-compatible content provenance embedding for all generated audio, image, and video outputs; audit tamper-resistance capabilities. This deadline is October 1, 2026, not 2027.
- **All AI developers capable of generating synthetic content (by October 1, 2027):** Ensure all synthetic outputs are marked and detectable as synthetic at the point of consumer encounter; assess technical feasibility of interoperable marking solutions; review applicable exemptions.
- **Social media platforms (by January 1, 2028):** Update minor-user account defaults, parental consent flows, and first-session warning label display to satisfy Connecticut's youth protection requirements.
- **All covered entities:** Begin full compliance planning immediately; track implementation guidance or rulemaking from the Attorney General's office. The law is enacted — October 1, 2026 deadlines are binding.
