# Colorado Legislature Approves AI Bill Targeting "High-Risk" Systems and AI Labeling (SB 24-205)

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** May 20, 2024

> **Note to readers:** A comprehensive companion report covering SB 24-205 in full depth — including legislative history, detailed statutory analysis, exemptions, enforcement, and industry impact — exists in this knowledge base at [reports/ai-law/state-legislation/colorado-ai-act-sb24-205-passage-2024-05-14.md](reports/ai-law/state-legislation/colorado-ai-act-sb24-205-passage-2024-05-14.md). This memo supplements that report with the Holland & Knight analysis perspective, emphasizing the law's AI labeling provisions and the dual-obligation framework for developers and deployers, as reported by Holland & Knight LLP on Lexology (May 20, 2024).

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Colorado's [Senate Bill 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205), signed into law by Governor Jared Polis on May 17, 2024, is the first comprehensive state AI regulation in the United States. The law — formally titled "Consumer Protections for Artificial Intelligence" — imposes duties on both **developers** and **deployers** of "high-risk" artificial intelligence systems to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination in consequential decisions. Distinctively, SB 24-205 also mandates broad **AI labeling** requirements that apply to any business deploying an AI system to interact with consumers, not only high-risk systems. Enforcement rests exclusively with the Colorado Attorney General; civil penalties may reach $20,000 per violation with no private right of action. The law's effective date has since been delayed from February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2026, following a failed special legislative session in August 2025.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### First Comprehensive State AI Law

Prior to SB 24-205, no U.S. state had enacted a comprehensive cross-sector AI regulatory framework. Existing state AI legislation addressed narrow subjects — chatbot disclosure, employment AI auditing, or deepfake prohibitions — without creating a general duty of care applicable to the AI supply chain. [Holland & Knight LLP](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/05/colorado-legislature-approves-ai-bill-targeting-highrisk-systems-and) characterized SB 24-205 as the "first law in the U.S. to impose specific requirements intended to mitigate risk of 'algorithmic discrimination' on both developers and deployers of artificial intelligence."

The bill was introduced by Senator Robert Rodriguez and cleared the Colorado Senate on May 3, 2024, and the House on May 8, 2024. Governor Polis signed the bill on May 17, 2024, accompanied by a letter expressing reservations and urging the legislature to improve the law before the February 1, 2026 effective date. He simultaneously called on Congress to pass preemptive federal AI legislation. Official bill text is available at the [Colorado General Assembly bill page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205) and as a [signed PDF](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_205_signed.pdf).

### EU AI Act Influence

SB 24-205 borrows the EU AI Act's risk-tiered structure, focusing regulatory obligations on "high-risk" systems defined by their use in consequential decisions. Unlike the EU Act, Colorado's approach does not categorically ban any class of AI and does not impose product-safety-style conformity assessments. Instead, it adopts a negligence-based "reasonable care" standard — a distinctly American legal framework that ties liability to outcomes rather than pre-market authorization.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope: High-Risk AI Systems

SB 24-205 defines a **high-risk artificial intelligence system** as any system that makes, or is a "substantial factor" in making, a **consequential decision** — one with a material legal or similarly significant effect on a consumer's access to, or the cost or terms of:

- Employment, employment opportunities, or employment-related benefits
- Education enrollment or opportunity
- Financial or lending services
- Essential government services
- Health care services
- Housing
- Insurance
- Legal services

**Algorithmic discrimination** — the harm the law is designed to prevent — means any condition where an AI system results in unlawful differential treatment or impact on the basis of race, color, ethnicity, sex, religion, age, national origin, limited English proficiency, disability, veteran status, genetic information, or reproductive health.

### Developer Obligations [HIGH confidence]

Under [SB 24-205](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_205_signed.pdf), a **developer** (a person doing business in Colorado who develops or intentionally and substantially modifies a high-risk AI system commercially) must:

1. **Provide deployer documentation** — Developers must supply deployers with materials describing the AI system's intended uses, known harmful uses, training data evaluation for bias, and risk mitigation measures taken.
2. **Publish a public summary** — Developers must maintain a publicly accessible statement describing the types of high-risk AI systems they have developed and how they manage foreseeable algorithmic discrimination risks.
3. **Report discrimination incidents** — Within 90 days of discovering or receiving a credible report of algorithmic discrimination, developers must notify the Colorado Attorney General and all known Colorado deployers.

### Deployer Obligations [HIGH confidence]

A **deployer** (a person doing business in Colorado who deploys a high-risk AI system to make consequential decisions about Colorado consumers) must:

1. **Implement a risk management policy** — Using a recognized framework (NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, or substantially equivalent).
2. **Conduct impact assessments** — Prior to deployment, annually, and within 90 days of any intentional and substantial modification.
3. **Notify consumers** — When a high-risk AI system makes or substantially influences a consequential decision, the deployer must notify the affected consumer, explain the decision, and describe how the consumer may correct inaccurate data.
4. **Provide human review and appeal** — Consumers must have a meaningful opportunity to appeal adverse consequential decisions and to request human review.
5. **Publish a summary** — Deployers must publish a summary of all high-risk AI systems deployed.

A deployer obtains a **rebuttable presumption of reasonable care** by satisfying these obligations. A **safe harbor** also applies to organizations that discover and cure violations through their own voluntary review before any complaint is filed.

### AI Labeling: The Broader Disclosure Requirement [HIGH confidence]

A provision that distinguishes SB 24-205 from a pure "high-risk AI" framework is its **general AI labeling requirement**. As analyzed by [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/05/colorado-legislature-approves-ai-bill-targeting-highrisk-systems-and) and confirmed in the [JD Supra republication](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/colorado-legislature-approves-ai-bill-5675109/):

> *Any person doing business in Colorado that deploys or makes available an artificial intelligence system **intended to interact with consumers** must ensure disclosure to each consumer that the consumer is interacting with an artificial intelligence system.*

This notice requirement applies **regardless** of whether the AI system is "high-risk." It covers any consumer-facing AI — customer service chatbots, virtual assistants, AI-powered recommendation systems, and similar tools. The notice obligation requires businesses to clearly communicate to consumers at the point of interaction that they are engaging with AI, not a human.

This breadth makes the AI labeling obligation one of the most broadly applicable provisions of SB 24-205 — reaching companies that might otherwise conclude they do not deploy "high-risk" AI because their AI tools do not make consequential decisions.

### Enforcement [HIGH confidence]

The Colorado Attorney General has **exclusive** enforcement authority. There is no private right of action. Violations constitute **unfair or deceptive trade practices** under the [Colorado Consumer Protection Act](https://coag.gov/resources/consumer-protection/), with civil penalties of up to **$20,000 per violation**. The AG may also pursue injunctive relief.

The [Colorado AG's official AI page](https://coag.gov/ai/) serves as the enforcement and rulemaking hub, including any guidance issued before the June 30, 2026 effective date.

### Governor's Signing Statement

Governor Polis signed the bill while simultaneously expressing reservations about potential compliance burdens on innovators and smaller businesses. His formal letter called on the legislature to "significantly improve" the law and urged Congress to enact federal AI legislation that would preempt Colorado's approach. This ambivalence helped spur the 2025 special session, which collapsed without substantive changes, producing only the enforcement delay ([SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004), signed August 28, 2025).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Must Comply with AI Labeling

Because the general AI disclosure requirement applies to **any consumer-facing AI system**, not just high-risk deployments, the practical scope is extremely broad:

- Retailers and e-commerce sites using AI chatbots for customer service
- Financial services firms using AI for customer communications
- Healthcare portals using AI assistants for patient intake
- Any business using AI to respond to consumer inquiries

Companies that currently conclude SB 24-205's high-risk requirements do not apply to their business should nonetheless audit whether they deploy any consumer-facing AI system — and plan for the general disclosure requirement by June 30, 2026.

### Developer-Deployer Supply Chain Obligations

[Holland & Knight's analysis](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/05/colorado-legislature-approves-ai-bill-targeting-highrisk-systems-and) and the [National Association of Attorneys General deep dive](https://www.naag.org/attorney-general-journal/a-deep-dive-into-colorados-artificial-intelligence-act/) both emphasize that the developer-deployer dual obligation creates a compliance chain: AI vendors (developers) must prepare documentation packages for enterprise customers (deployers), whose compliance in turn depends on the quality of that documentation. A developer's failure to adequately document risks flows downstream into deployer liability exposure.

### Key Exemptions

Several categories are exempt or partially exempt:
- AI systems **approved or cleared by federal agencies** (FDA, FAA, Federal Housing Finance Agency)
- Systems deployed under certain **federal government contracts**
- **Banks and credit unions** subject to AI examination under qualifying prudential-regulator guidance
- **Insurance companies** and their developers under Colorado's algorithmic-insurance statutes
- **HIPAA-covered entities** under qualifying health-care AI guidance

### Enforcement Outlook and Federal Preemption Risk

As of April 2026, enforcement is scheduled for June 30, 2026. Colorado AG Phil Weiser has publicly defended the law. However, President Trump's December 11, 2025 executive order — [Eliminating State Law Obstruction of National Artificial Intelligence Policy](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) — directed the Commerce Department to compile a list of "onerous" state AI laws and the DOJ to develop litigation strategies. Colorado's AI Act is widely expected to be a primary target. The executive order cannot itself preempt state law but signals a potential federal challenge before the enforcement date.

## Action Items

- **All consumer-facing AI deployments:** Audit whether any AI system interacts with consumers in Colorado. If so, implement the general AI disclosure/labeling requirement regardless of whether the system qualifies as "high-risk." Target: June 30, 2026.
- **High-risk AI deployers:** Implement risk management programs aligned with NIST AI RMF or ISO 42001, conduct pre-deployment impact assessments, establish consumer notification workflows, and create human review and appeal channels. Target: June 30, 2026.
- **AI developers:** Prepare developer documentation packages for deployer customers — intended uses, harmful uses, training data bias evaluation, risk mitigation. Establish a 90-day incident-reporting protocol for the Colorado AG.
- **Legal and compliance teams:** Map which AI systems in current use qualify as "high-risk" under the consequential decisions definition; determine which AI systems interact with consumers and fall under the labeling requirement.
- **Monitor federal preemption developments:** Track the Commerce Department list expected under Trump's AI EO and any resulting DOJ enforcement action that could affect SB 24-205 enforcement before June 30, 2026.
- **Track 2026 legislative session amendments:** The Colorado General Assembly may modify SB 24-205's core requirements during 2026; monitor for bills that could alter compliance obligations before the effective date.
