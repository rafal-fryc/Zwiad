# Colorado Enacts First-in-the-Nation Comprehensive AI Guardrails (SB 24-205): DLA Piper Analysis

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** June 5, 2024

> **Note to readers:** This report presents the DLA Piper analytical perspective on Colorado SB 24-205, published June 5, 2024. For the comprehensive primary enactment analysis, see the companion report at [reports/ai-law/state-legislation/colorado-ai-act-sb24-205-passage-2024-05-14.md](reports/ai-law/state-legislation/colorado-ai-act-sb24-205-passage-2024-05-14.md). For enforcement delay developments through 2026, see [reports/ai-law/state-legislation/colorado-ai-act-enforcement-delayed-2026-04-13.md](reports/ai-law/state-legislation/colorado-ai-act-enforcement-delayed-2026-04-13.md).

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 17, 2024, Colorado Governor Jared Polis signed [Senate Bill 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205), making Colorado the first US state to enact a comprehensive regulatory framework governing the development and use of artificial intelligence. As analyzed by [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2024/05/colorado-enacts-first-in-the-nation-comprehensive-ai-guardrails), the law imposes differentiated obligations on both **developers** and **deployers** of "high-risk" AI systems, drawing heavily on the EU AI Act's risk-tiered architecture while adopting a distinctly American "reasonable care" negligence standard rather than the EU's prescriptive product-safety regime. The law targets **algorithmic discrimination** in consequential decisions affecting employment, education, healthcare, housing, finance, insurance, legal services, and essential government services. Governor Polis signed with explicit reservations, calling for federal preemptive legislation. The law's original effective date of February 1, 2026 was subsequently delayed to June 30, 2026 following a failed 2025 special legislative session. As of March 2026, Colorado's AI Policy Work Group has unanimously proposed replacing SB 24-205 entirely with a new Automated Decision-Making Technology (ADMT) framework, placing the long-term future of the law in significant doubt.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### First Comprehensive State AI Framework

By mid-2024, the United States had no federal comprehensive AI regulation. Congress had debated AI legislation but enacted nothing equivalent to a national framework. The Biden administration's October 2023 executive order on AI directed agencies to develop standards and guidance but imposed no direct private-sector legal duties on AI developers or deployers. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, January 2023) offered voluntary guidance, but compliance was entirely discretionary.

Prior state AI legislation addressed only narrow use cases: chatbot disclosure (California AB 13, 2019), employment screening audits (New York City Local Law 144, 2023), or deepfake prohibitions (various state laws). No state had enacted a cross-sector framework imposing affirmative duties of care on the entire AI development and deployment supply chain.

[DLA Piper's analysis](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2024/05/colorado-enacts-first-in-the-nation-comprehensive-ai-guardrails) placed Colorado's law in this context, characterizing it as a significant step into regulatory territory the federal government had not yet entered.

### EU AI Act as Blueprint

Colorado SB 24-205's structure closely mirrors the [EU AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689), which the European Parliament approved on March 13, 2024, the EU Council formally adopted on May 21, 2024, and which entered into force on August 1, 2024. Both instruments:

- Focus regulatory obligations on AI systems used in high-stakes, consequential contexts
- Distinguish between developers (who build AI systems) and deployers (who use AI systems to make decisions)
- Require risk management programs and impact assessments
- Mandate transparency to affected individuals

The Colorado law differs from the EU AI Act in critical respects. The EU Act imposes prescriptive product-safety-style conformity assessments before high-risk AI systems may be placed on the market. Colorado adopts a **negligence-based "reasonable care" standard** — a liability framework aligned with US tort law — which imposes duties ex-post rather than requiring pre-market authorization. As DLA Piper noted, Colorado's approach is "less stringent and more narrowly drawn" than the EU regime: it focuses specifically on predictive AI that makes or influences decisions, rather than capturing generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT) that produces content.

### Legislative History

Senator Robert Rodriguez introduced SB 24-205 on April 10, 2024. Additional Senate sponsors included Senators Cutter, Michaelson Jenet, Priola, and Winter F.; House sponsors were Representatives Brianna Titone, Manny Rutinel, and Duran. The [Colorado Senate passed the bill on May 3, 2024; the House passed it on May 8, 2024](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205). Governor Polis signed it on May 17, 2024.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope: High-Risk AI Systems and Consequential Decisions

SB 24-205 applies to **high-risk artificial intelligence systems** — defined as AI systems that make, or are a "substantial factor" in making, a **consequential decision**. A consequential decision is one that has a material legal or similarly significant effect on a consumer's access to, or the cost or terms of:

- Employment, employment opportunities, or employment-related benefits
- Education enrollment or opportunity
- Financial or lending services
- Essential government services
- Health care services
- Housing
- Insurance
- Legal services

**Algorithmic discrimination** — the harm SB 24-205 seeks to prevent — means any condition in which an AI system produces unlawful differential treatment or impact that disfavors an individual or group based on actual or perceived race, color, ethnicity, sex, religion, age, national origin, limited English proficiency, disability, veteran status, genetic information, or reproductive health.

Per [DLA Piper's framing](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2024/05/colorado-enacts-first-in-the-nation-comprehensive-ai-guardrails), the law affects **predictive AI** that makes decisions, not generative AI that creates content — a scoping distinction significant for companies whose AI deployments are primarily content-generation tools rather than decision-making systems.

### Developer Obligations [HIGH confidence]

A **developer** is a person doing business in Colorado who develops or intentionally and substantially modifies a high-risk AI system offered commercially. Developer obligations include:

1. **Use reasonable care** to protect Colorado consumers from known or reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination in the high-risk AI system.
2. **Provide deployer documentation** — supply each deployer with a statement describing the system's intended uses, known harmful uses, how training data was evaluated for bias, and what risk mitigation steps were taken.
3. **Publish a public summary** — maintain a publicly accessible disclosure summarizing the types of high-risk AI systems developed and how the developer manages foreseeable algorithmic discrimination risks.
4. **Report discrimination incidents** — upon discovering or receiving credible notification of algorithmic discrimination, notify the Colorado Attorney General and all known Colorado deployers within 90 days.

### Deployer Obligations [HIGH confidence]

A **deployer** is a person doing business in Colorado who deploys a high-risk AI system to make, or be a substantial factor in making, consequential decisions about Colorado consumers. Deployer obligations include:

1. **Implement a risk management policy and program** aligned with a recognized framework — the [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/ai-risk-management-framework), ISO 42001, or a substantially equivalent standard.
2. **Conduct impact assessments** — prior to deployment, annually, and within 90 days of any intentional and substantial modification. Impact assessments must evaluate foreseeable algorithmic discrimination risks.
3. **Provide consumer notification** — when a high-risk AI system makes or substantially influences a consequential decision about a consumer, notify the consumer of the decision, explain the basis, and describe how the consumer may correct inaccurate data.
4. **Provide human review and appeal** — consumers must have a meaningful opportunity to appeal adverse consequential decisions and to request human review where technically feasible.
5. **Publish a summary** of all high-risk AI systems deployed.

A deployer that satisfies these obligations receives a **rebuttable presumption of reasonable care**. A **safe harbor** also protects organizations that discover and cure violations through their own voluntary compliance review before any complaint is filed.

### General AI Interaction Disclosure [HIGH confidence]

Beyond the high-risk framework, SB 24-205 includes a general disclosure requirement that extends to any business deploying an AI system intended to interact with consumers — regardless of whether the system qualifies as "high-risk." Any such deployment must include a clear disclosure that the consumer is interacting with an AI, not a human, unless it would be obvious to a reasonable person. This provision has broad reach into retail, customer service, and HR chatbot applications that might not otherwise trigger the high-risk compliance track.

### Exemptions [HIGH confidence]

Several categories of AI are exempt or receive partial compliance credit:

- **Federally regulated AI:** Systems approved or cleared by the FDA, FAA, or Federal Housing Finance Agency; systems deployed under qualifying federal government contracts
- **Financial institutions:** Banks, credit unions, and affiliates subject to AI examination by state or federal prudential regulators under qualifying published guidance
- **Insurance:** Insurers and developers for insurers subject to Colorado's algorithmic-insurance statutes
- **HIPAA-covered entities:** Healthcare entities in compliance with qualifying HIPAA AI-related guidance

### Enforcement [HIGH confidence]

The Colorado Attorney General has **exclusive** enforcement authority. There is no private right of action. Violations constitute **unfair or deceptive trade practices** under the [Colorado Consumer Protection Act](https://coag.gov/resources/consumer-protection/), with civil penalties up to **$20,000 per violation**. The AG may also seek injunctive relief and restitution. The [Colorado AG's AI page](https://coag.gov/ai/) serves as the enforcement and rulemaking hub.

### Governor's Signing Statement [HIGH confidence]

Governor Polis signed SB 24-205 while simultaneously issuing a [formal letter](https://www.dwt.com/-/media/files/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2024/05/sb24205-signing-statement.pdf) expressing significant reservations. Key concerns:

- The law "could inadvertently stifle innovation" and create undue compliance burdens, particularly on smaller AI developers and deployers
- The law creates a "complex compliance regime" that could produce a confusing state-by-state patchwork if other states adopt similar measures
- Federal preemptive legislation — providing "a strong national policy governing AI consumer protection that supersedes state law" — would be "the best course of action"
- He called on the bill's sponsors and the legislature to "significantly improve" the law before the February 1, 2026 effective date

These reservations proved prescient. The 2025 regular session produced no substantive amendments, and the August 2025 special legislative session called by Polis himself produced only SB 25B-004 — an enforcement delay — without any substantive amendments to the law's core requirements.

## Post-Enactment Developments (2025-2026) [HIGH confidence]

### Enforcement Delay: SB 25B-004

Following the failed 2025 special session, the Colorado General Assembly passed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004), signed by Governor Polis on August 28, 2025. This measure delayed enforcement of SB 24-205 from February 1, 2026 to **June 30, 2026**, providing additional time for either legislative improvements or replacement.

### AI Policy Work Group Proposes Replacement Framework (March 2026)

In a significant development, Colorado's AI Policy Work Group — established by Governor Polis — unanimously approved a proposal in March 2026 to replace SB 24-205 entirely. As reported by [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-colorado-ai-policy-work-group-proposes-an-updated-framework-to-replace-the-colorado-ai-act) and confirmed by the [Governor's Office](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/colorado-artificial-intelligence-policy-workgroup-delivers-unanimous-support-revised-policy), the proposed **Automated Decision-Making Technology (ADMT) Framework** would:

- Drop the complex risk-management, impact-assessment, and notification obligations from SB 24-205 that applied to small businesses
- Retain the core principle of protecting consumers from discriminatory automated decisions
- Preserve exclusive enforcement by the AG's Office with civil penalties and injunctions; no private right of action
- Add a **90-day cure period** for developers and deployers to remedy violations before incurring penalties
- Target a new effective date of **January 1, 2027**

If passed during the 2026 legislative session, the ADMT Framework would supersede SB 24-205 in its entirety — meaning companies that have built compliance programs around the current law's requirements may need to recalibrate.

### Federal Preemption Pressure

President Trump's December 11, 2025 executive order, [Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/), directed the Commerce Department to identify "onerous" state AI laws and the DOJ to develop litigation strategies. Colorado's CAIA is widely regarded as a primary target. The EO cannot itself preempt state law but signals a potential federal challenge before Colorado's June 30, 2026 enforcement date.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected by SB 24-205 (as Currently Written)

**Employers and HR Tech:** Any Colorado employer using AI tools in hiring, promotion, performance evaluation, compensation, or termination decisions is a "deployer" of a high-risk AI system. This includes automated resume screening, performance scoring, or scheduling systems. The impact assessment and human-review requirements impose compliance infrastructure obligations broadly analogous to GDPR's Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) process.

**AI Developers and SaaS Vendors:** AI vendors whose products are used in high-risk employment, lending, healthcare, or other covered contexts — whether or not they control the deployment — are "developers" with documentation, disclosure, and incident-reporting obligations to their enterprise customer-deployers. This creates a contractual compliance chain running from model providers through integration partners to end-user enterprises.

**Consumer-Facing AI Deployments (Broad):** The general AI interaction disclosure requirement applies to all consumer-facing AI systems, regardless of high-risk classification. Retailers, financial institutions, and healthcare portals using any AI chatbot or virtual assistant must audit these systems for the disclosure requirement.

**Smaller Businesses:** The U.S. Chamber of Commerce, Chamber of Progress, and Consumer Technology Association collectively urged Governor Polis to veto SB 24-205, citing disproportionate compliance burdens on smaller AI users who neither develop AI systems nor control the systems' algorithmic outputs. The proposed ADMT Framework's removal of the most burdensome compliance requirements reflects these concerns.

### EU AI Act vs. Colorado SB 24-205: Key Contrasts

| Dimension | EU AI Act | Colorado SB 24-205 |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Risk tiers: unacceptable, high, limited, minimal | High-risk / non-high-risk binary |
| Liability standard | Product safety / conformity assessments | Negligence / reasonable care |
| Coverage | Broad (generative AI included in some tiers) | Predictive AI making decisions; generative AI generally excluded |
| Pre-market authorization | Yes (Notified Body conformity assessment for high-risk) | No |
| Enforcement | Multi-agency (national market surveillance authorities, AI Office) | Exclusive state AG authority |
| Private right of action | Yes (under AI Liability Directive proposals; national tort law) | No |

### Compliance Timeline (as of April 2026)

- **June 30, 2026:** Current SB 24-205 enforcement date (per SB 25B-004 delay)
- **2026 legislative session:** Colorado General Assembly expected to consider ADMT Framework replacement legislation
- **January 1, 2027:** Proposed effective date for the ADMT Framework if the replacement bill passes

## Action Items

- **Consumer-facing AI operators:** Audit whether any AI system interacts with Colorado consumers. If so, implement the general AI interaction disclosure regardless of whether the system is "high-risk." Target: June 30, 2026 (or when any replacement law takes effect).
- **High-risk AI deployers:** Maintain parallel compliance tracks — continue SB 24-205 compliance preparation (NIST AI RMF alignment, impact assessments, consumer notification workflows) while monitoring whether the ADMT Framework passes during the 2026 session. Either law requires documented governance of automated decision-making.
- **AI developers/vendors:** Prepare developer documentation packages for deployer-customers as required under SB 24-205; determine whether equivalent obligations exist under the proposed ADMT Framework.
- **Legal and compliance teams:** Map which AI systems qualify as "high-risk" under the consequential decisions definition; determine which AI systems interact with consumers and fall under the general disclosure requirement; model the compliance gap between SB 24-205 requirements and proposed ADMT Framework requirements.
- **Monitor 2026 Colorado legislative session:** Track whether the ADMT Framework replacement bill advances and when it takes effect. If passed, recalibrate compliance programs accordingly.
- **Track federal preemption developments:** Monitor Commerce Department activities under the Trump AI EO and any resulting DOJ litigation that could affect SB 24-205 or any replacement law before the enforcement date.
