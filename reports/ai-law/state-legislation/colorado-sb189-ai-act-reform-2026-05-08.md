# Colorado SB 189 Signed: AI Act Repealed and Replaced with Disclosure-Based ADMT Framework, Employer Obligations Substantially Reduced

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-05-08 | **Last Updated:** 2026-06-01

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Colorado Senate Bill 189 (SB26-189), signed into law by Governor Jared Polis on May 14, 2026, repeals and replaces the landmark Colorado AI Act ([SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)) with a narrower "automated decision-making technology" (ADMT) transparency framework. The signed law takes effect January 1, 2027, and substantially reduces obligations on employers and other deployers of AI systems used in consequential decisions. Where SB 24-205 imported the conceptual architecture of the EU AI Act — risk-based duties of care, mandatory impact assessments, bias audits, and algorithmic discrimination reporting — SB 189 dismantles that framework and replaces it with disclosure and notice requirements, consumer rights to human review, and three-year recordkeeping obligations. The pivot reflects two years of political pressure from the White House, business groups, a federal court stay, and a DOJ intervention, condensed into fewer than two weeks of legislative action before the session's May 13, 2026 adjournment ([Carpe Datum Law, May 2026](https://www.carpedatumlaw.com/2026/05/colorados-ai-reset-two-weeks-a-white-house-callout-and-a-pivot-away-from-the-eu-model/); [Seyfarth Shaw, May 2026](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/colorado-enacts-artificial-intelligence-replacement-law.html)).

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Official bill:** [SB26-189 Automated Decision-Making Technology](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189), Colorado General Assembly — introduced by Senate Majority Leader Robert Rodriguez (D-Denver), the original author of SB 24-205, who described SB 189 as "more of a notice bill" compared to its predecessor ([Colorado Sun, May 1, 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/05/01/colorado-ai-law-change-bill-introduced/)).
- **Signed:** Governor Jared Polis signed SB 189 on May 14, 2026 — less than seven weeks before SB 24-205's June 30, 2026 effective date.
- **Effective date:** January 1, 2027 (replacing SB 24-205's June 30, 2026 date).
- **Core disclosure change:** Under SB 24-205, developers and deployers must proactively disclose detailed information — including how AI systems evaluate information and what personal data categories are processed — before or at the point of consequential decisions. SB 189 removes the upfront "nature, source and extent" disclosure and replaces it with a simple notice that ADMT is being used; full details are only required within 30 days if a consumer requests them following an adverse outcome ([CPR News, May 4, 2026](https://www.cpr.org/2026/05/04/changes-colorado-ai-law-bill/)).
- **Scope change — "high-risk" to "covered ADMT":** SB 24-205 targets "high-risk AI systems." SB 189 reframes the regulated category as an "automated decision-making technology" that "materially influences" a consequential decision, dropping requirements for mandatory bias audits, impact assessments, and risk management programs ([Mayer Brown, March 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-colorado-ai-policy-work-group-proposes-an-updated-framework-to-replace-the-colorado-ai-act/)).
- **Consequential decisions still covered:** Employment, education, housing, financial/lending services, insurance, healthcare, and essential government services remain within scope ([LegiScan bill summary, 2026](https://legiscan.com/CO/bill/SB189/2026)).
- **Consumer rights retained:** Consumers retain the right to request personal data used by a covered ADMT, correct factually incorrect data, and request meaningful human review following an adverse consequential decision ([LegiScan bill summary, 2026](https://legiscan.com/CO/bill/SB189/2026)).
- **Enforcement preserved under CCPA:** The Colorado Attorney General retains exclusive enforcement authority through the Colorado Consumer Protection Act; violations are treated as deceptive trade practices; a 60-day notice-and-cure period is required before any enforcement action ([Colorado SB26-189 bill text, leg.colorado.gov](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/115623/download)).
- **Record retention:** Developers and deployers must retain records for at least three years ([Colorado SB26-189 bill text](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/115623/download)).
- **Legislative vote:** House passed 57-6 on May 9, 2026; Senate concurred in House amendments 34-1 on May 12, 2026 (final session day).

## Legal and Regulatory Context [HIGH confidence]

### Origin: The Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205)

Colorado's [SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205), signed by Governor Jared Polis in May 2024, was the first comprehensive state AI consumer protection law in the United States. Modeled in part on the EU AI Act, it imposed duties of care, bias impact assessments, risk management programs, and transparency obligations on developers and deployers of "high-risk" AI systems used in consequential decisions. Governor Polis signed the bill with a reluctance letter urging the legislature to refine it before the effective date.

The law's original effective date of February 1, 2026, was delayed to June 30, 2026, when the legislature failed to agree on amendments during a 2025 special session, and Governor Polis signed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) in August 2025 solely to extend the timeline.

### Federal Court Stay — xAI Lawsuit

On April 9, 2026, Elon Musk's xAI filed suit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado challenging SB 24-205. The complaint pleads six constitutional counts organized around four broad objections: (1a) First Amendment — SB 24-205 unconstitutionally compels xAI to alter Grok's training, fine-tuning, system prompts, and outputs; (1b) First Amendment — the law unconstitutionally compels content- and viewpoint-based disclosures about bias-mitigation practices; (2) Dormant Commerce Clause — because SB 24-205 applies whenever a Colorado resident is affected by an AI system regardless of where the interaction occurs, it unconstitutionally regulates transactions taking place entirely outside Colorado (xAI is organized under Nevada law and headquartered in Palo Alto, California); (3) Due Process — key operative terms such as "high-risk artificial intelligence system" and "algorithmic discrimination" are unconstitutionally vague; (4) Equal Protection — the law's carve-out permitting discriminatory algorithms aimed at "expanding diversity or redressing historical discrimination" is unconstitutional ([Colorado Sun, April 10, 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/04/10/elon-musk-colorado-ai-law-federal-court-lawsuit/); [Baker Botts analysis](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102mpre/xai-sues-to-enjoin-colorados-ai-act-before-june-30-effective-date); [JURIST, April 2026](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/04/xai-sues-colorado-over-constitutional-violations-in-new-ai-bill/)).

On April 24, 2026, the [U.S. Department of Justice intervened](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-intervenes-xai-lawsuit-challenging-colorados-algorithmic-discrimination) in support of xAI, arguing SB 24-205 compels AI developers and deployers to discriminate based on race, sex, religion, and other protected characteristics in violation of the Equal Protection Clause. The DOJ's intervention is notable as a direct assertion of federal authority over state AI regulation.

On April 27, 2026, U.S. Magistrate Judge Cyrus Y. Chung of the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado granted a joint motion — agreed to by both xAI and Colorado Attorney General Philip Weiser — to stay enforcement of SB 24-205 pending further legislative developments. This was a consensual arrangement, not a contested ruling: both parties agreed to the stay terms. Under the court's order, Colorado agreed not to initiate investigations or enforcement actions until 14 days after the court rules on xAI's motion for a preliminary injunction ([HR Dive, April 2026](https://www.hrdive.com/news/colorado-ai-bias-law-unconstitutional-elon-musks-xai/817258/); [Colorado Politics, April 28, 2026](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/04/28/colorados-unprecedented-ai-law-cant-be-enforced-yet-judge-rules/)). The court order provides that xAI must file a motion for preliminary injunction or amended complaint within 28 days of Colorado completing the rulemaking required to implement SB 189 — meaning the litigation clock does not restart on legislative enactment alone but upon AG rulemaking completion.

### The Policy Working Group Framework

In March 2026, the Colorado AI Policy Work Group — convened by Governor Polis with participation from consumer groups, hospitals, school districts, technology companies, and industry associations — [unanimously agreed on a framework](https://governorsoffice.colorado.gov/governor/news/colorado-artificial-intelligence-policy-workgroup-delivers-unanimous-support-revised-policy) to replace SB 24-205 with an ADMT-focused approach emphasizing transparency, recordkeeping, and consumer rights over the original law's structural compliance requirements. SB 189 is the legislative vehicle for that working group's recommendations ([Mayer Brown, March 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-colorado-ai-policy-work-group-proposes-an-updated-framework-to-replace-the-colorado-ai-act/)).

## What Changes Under SB 189 vs. SB 24-205 [HIGH confidence]

| Requirement | SB 24-205 | SB 189 |
|---|---|---|
| Regulated category | "High-risk AI systems" | "Covered ADMT" materially influencing a consequential decision |
| Upfront disclosure | Detailed description of AI purpose, personal data categories, nature and extent of processing | Simple notice that ADMT is being used |
| Post-decision disclosure | Disclosure required on a specific adverse outcome | Plain language description within 30 days after adverse outcome — only if consumer requests |
| Bias audits / impact assessments | Required annually | Eliminated |
| Risk management program | Required | Eliminated |
| Algorithmic discrimination reporting | Required to AG within 90 days of discovery | Eliminated |
| Duty of care to prevent discrimination | Yes | Eliminated |
| Effective date | June 30, 2026 | January 1, 2027 |
| Consumer human review right | Yes | Yes (retained) |
| Record retention | Not specified in the same terms | 3 years |
| Enforcement | AG through Colorado Consumer Protection Act | AG through Colorado Consumer Protection Act (unchanged) |

## Update (May 2026): Enacted — Employer Obligations and EU Model Pivot [HIGH confidence]

Governor Polis signed SB 189 on May 14, 2026, making Colorado the first state to both enact and then repeal-and-replace a comprehensive risk-based AI law. The signed law marks a deliberate pivot away from the EU AI Act model that informed SB 24-205, driven by White House pressure (President Trump's December 2025 executive order identified SB 24-205 as "excessive State regulation"), DOJ intervention in the xAI lawsuit, and sustained business coalition opposition ([Carpe Datum Law, May 2026](https://www.carpedatumlaw.com/2026/05/colorados-ai-reset-two-weeks-a-white-house-callout-and-a-pivot-away-from-the-eu-model/); [Seyfarth Shaw, May 2026](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/colorado-enacts-artificial-intelligence-replacement-law.html)).

### Employer-Specific Obligations Under the Signed Law

The signed SB 189 imposes a tiered notice-and-response framework on employers using ADMT to materially influence employment decisions. "Materially influence" is defined to mean that an ADMT output is a non-de minimis factor used in making a consequential decision — encompassing outputs that constrain, rank, score, recommend, classify, or otherwise meaningfully alter how a decision is made. Incidental, trivial, or clerical uses are excluded. Covered employment decisions include hiring, termination, promotion, compensation, and scheduling ([Littler, May 2026](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/colorado-amends-its-artificial-intelligence-law-substantially-reducing); [Ogletree, May 2026](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/colorados-new-ai-act-targets-automated-decision-making-for-consequential-decisions/)).

**Obligations eliminated (compared to SB 24-205):**
- Duty of care aimed at preventing algorithmic discrimination
- Annual bias impact assessments
- Documented risk management programs
- Proactive reporting of discriminatory outcomes to the Colorado AG within 90 days
- Upfront detailed disclosure of how AI systems evaluate information and what personal data categories are processed

**Obligations that remain (effective January 1, 2027):**

1. **Pre-use notice:** Before using covered ADMT, employers must provide a "clear and conspicuous" notice that automated decision-making technology is being used to materially influence a consequential decision and how the individual may obtain additional information. A "prominent public notice that is reasonably accessible at points of consumer interaction" satisfies this standard — for example, a notice in the online job application process ([Epstein Becker Green, May 2026](https://www.workforcebulletin.com/inside-colorados-senate-bill-26-189-impacts-and-implications-for-employers)).

2. **Post-adverse-outcome disclosure (within 30 days, only if requested):** After an adverse employment decision materially influenced by ADMT, employers must provide:
   - A plain language description of the consequential decision and the role the ADMT played
   - Instructions to request additional information, including the ADMT's name, version number, developer, and types/categories/sources of personal data used
   - An explanation of the right to correct inaccurate data and request human review, and how to exercise those rights
   ([Ogletree Multistage Notices Analysis, May 2026](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/multistage-notices-under-colorados-revamped-ai-act/); [HR Dive, May 2026](https://www.hrdive.com/news/revamped-colorado-ai-law-targets-consequential-hr-decisions/820425/))

3. **Meaningful human review:** Upon request following an adverse outcome, employers must provide review by a person who: has actual authority to override the decision; considers relevant evidence; is trained for the role; and does not simply ratify the system's output. A recruiter who approves an AI-generated ranking without genuine deliberation does not meet this standard ([Littler, May 2026](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/colorado-amends-its-artificial-intelligence-law-substantially-reducing)).

4. **Recordkeeping:** Employers must retain records demonstrating compliance — including ADMT version identifiers, changelogs, and documentation of material system changes — for at least three years after the date of a consequential decision ([Colorado SB26-189 bill text](https://leg.colorado.gov/bill_files/115623/download)).

5. **Developer documentation obligations:** Developers of covered ADMT must provide deployers (e.g., employers) with technical documentation describing the system's intended uses, categories of training data, known limitations, and instructions for appropriate use and human review — shifting a portion of the compliance burden upstream to AI vendors ([Wilson Sonsini, May 2026](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/colorado-legislature-repeals-and-replaces-colorado-ai-act-what-sb-189-means-for-your-business.html)).

### Companion Legislation Status

During the same session, the Colorado legislature passed two companion AI bills that address specific deployment contexts:

- **HB 26-1263 (Conversational AI Service Operator Requirements):** Signed by Governor Polis on May 29, 2026. Effective January 1, 2027. Requires operators of conversational AI services (AI chatbots accessible to the general public) to estimate user age and regularly disclose AI status to users identified as minors; bans sexually explicit content and gamification tactics designed to drive engagement for minors; mandates crisis response protocols when users express suicidal ideation; prohibits chatbots from presenting as licensed mental health professionals ([Denver Gazette, May 29, 2026](https://www.denvergazette.com/2026/05/29/gov-polis-signs-a-bipartisan-bill-regulating-ai-chatbots-for-colorado-minors/); [Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263)).

- **HB 26-1139 (Use of Artificial Intelligence in Health Care):** Passed the legislature before the May 13 adjournment. As of June 1, 2026, HB 1139 remains unsigned by Governor Polis. The bill would prohibit health insurance carriers from basing coverage decisions solely on group data collected by AI systems, require licensed clinician review of any AI-recommended coverage denial, and mandate disclosure to state insurance and health agencies of all utilization review functions for which AI is used ([Colorado General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1139); [Ropes & Gray, May 2026](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/05/colorado-scales-back-ai-law-with-targeted-implications-for-health-care)).

## Industry and Stakeholder Reaction [MEDIUM confidence]

The bill's unusually wide reception reflects the political balance Rodriguez has sought. Business groups and industry associations praised SB 189 as a workable replacement that reduces compliance burdens without eliminating consumer protections. Consumer advocacy groups, while noting the bill is less comprehensive than the original, acknowledged the notice and appeal rights as meaningful protections ([The Sum and Substance](https://tsscolorado.com/rodriguez-introduces-new-ai-bill-drawing-kudos-from-business-and-consumer-groups/); [Axios Denver, May 3, 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/denver/2026/05/03/colorado-ai-artificial-intellligence-legislation)).

Rodriguez himself described the tradeoff plainly: "This one is more of a notice bill," acknowledging the 2024 law he authored was more comprehensive. The 5-0 and 7-0 committee votes suggest the working group's consensus translated into legislative momentum. The BSA (Business Software Alliance) noted residual concerns, particularly around scope ambiguity in the "materially influence" standard ([BSA, 2026](https://www.bsa.org/news-events/news/bsa-co-ai-law-revisions-raise-significant-concerns)).

## Action Items

- **Audit which AI tools trigger coverage:** The shift from "high-risk AI system" to "covered ADMT" changes the threshold. Organizations should map all AI-assisted employment workflows — hiring (resume scoring, interview scheduling, candidate ranking), performance management, compensation modeling, scheduling — and assess which outputs "materially influence" a consequential decision under the new standard. Incidental, trivial, and clerical uses are excluded.
- **Design pre-use notices:** Develop a "clear and conspicuous" ADMT notice for each employment touchpoint where covered ADMT is used. Placement in the online job application process is explicitly recognized as a compliant method. Notice should identify (a) that ADMT is used to materially influence a consequential decision and (b) how to obtain additional information.
- **Build post-adverse-outcome disclosure workflows:** Establish a process to deliver 30-day post-adverse-outcome disclosures on request, including: plain language description of the decision and the ADMT's role; ADMT name, version, developer, and data categories; and instructions for exercising correction and human review rights.
- **Define the "meaningful human review" process:** Document reviewer qualifications, override authority, deliberation protocols, and training requirements. A reviewer who ratifies ADMT output without genuine deliberation does not satisfy the statutory standard.
- **Establish three-year record retention:** Implement recordkeeping for ADMT version identifiers, changelogs, and compliance documentation covering all consequential decisions. Records must be retained for at least three years from the date of each decision.
- **Audit AI vendor contracts:** The law imposes documentation obligations on ADMT developers; ensure vendor agreements require delivery of technical documentation — intended uses, training data categories, known limitations, instructions for appropriate use and human review — prior to January 1, 2027.
- **Monitor AG rulemaking:** SB 189 directs the Attorney General's office to develop disclosure requirements. Rulemaking will flesh out what ADMT notices and post-decision disclosures must contain by January 1, 2027. Subscribe to AG rulemaking announcements; any rulemaking completion also restarts xAI's 28-day window to file a preliminary injunction motion.
- **Track HB 1139:** Governor Polis has not signed HB 1139 (AI in health care coverage decisions). Healthcare and health insurance employers should monitor its status; if signed, it imposes independent AI governance requirements on coverage determinations.

## Update 2026-05-19

**Status change: signed into law (was: pending)**

Governor Jared Polis signed [SB 26-189](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189) into law on May 14, 2026, formally repealing and replacing the Colorado AI Act (SB 24-205) with the new "Automated Decision-Making Technology" framework. The bill cleared both chambers with strong bipartisan margins: the House passed it 57-6 on May 9, 2026, and the Senate concurred in the House amendments 34-1 on May 12, 2026, the final day of the legislative session ([Colorado Sun, May 12, 2026](https://coloradosun.com/2026/05/12/colorado-ai-law-rewrite-passes/); [Troutman Privacy, May 2026](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/colorado-legislature-passes-bill-to-repeal-and-replace-colorado-ai-act/)). The signed law takes effect January 1, 2027, superseding the June 30, 2026 effective date of SB 24-205. During the same session, the Colorado legislature also passed two companion AI bills: HB 1263, which establishes guardrails for AI chatbots interacting with minors, and HB 1139, which imposes transparency requirements for AI use in healthcare. Both bills passed the legislature but, as of May 19, 2026, HB 1263 had not been confirmed as signed by Governor Polis, and HB 1139's signing status remained unconfirmed — legislative passage and gubernatorial enactment are distinct steps ([Morrison Foerster, May 15, 2026](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260515-colorado-hits-reset-ai-regulation); [Holland & Knight, May 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/colorado-governor-signs-sb-189)). With SB 189 now enacted, the xAI v. Colorado AG litigation clock is poised to restart — but under the April 27, 2026 court order, xAI's 28-day deadline to file a preliminary injunction motion or amended complaint runs from the AG's completion of the rulemaking required to implement SB 189, not from the date of legislative enactment alone ([HR Dive, April 2026](https://www.hrdive.com/news/colorado-ai-bias-law-unconstitutional-elon-musks-xai/817258/); [CPR News, May 12, 2026](https://www.cpr.org/2026/05/12/ai-artificial-intelligence-disclosure-bill-colorado/)).
