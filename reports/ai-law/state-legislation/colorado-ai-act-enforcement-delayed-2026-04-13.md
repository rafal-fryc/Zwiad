# Colorado AI Act Enforcement Delayed to June 30, 2026; Chatbot Safety Bill Advances Amid Federal Preemption Pressure

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Governor Jared Polis signed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) on August 28, 2025, delaying enforcement of the Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act ([SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)) from February 1, 2026, to **June 30, 2026**. The delay followed a failed special legislative session where lawmakers could not agree on substantive amendments to the nation's first comprehensive state AI law. No substantive requirements were altered — the obligations developers and deployers face on June 30, 2026, are identical to those originally set for February 1. The delay now intersects with President Trump's [December 11, 2025 executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/), which targets "onerous" state AI laws for federal preemption challenge.

Separately, Colorado [HB 26-1263](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263), a bipartisan chatbot safety bill targeting minors' interactions with conversational AI, cleared the House Business Affairs and Labor Committee on March 26, 2026, and was pending House floor action as of early April 2026.

## Background [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

The Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act ([SB 24-205](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205)), signed into law in May 2024, is the first comprehensive state law in the United States regulating high-risk artificial intelligence systems. Modeled in part on the EU AI Act, it imposes obligations on both "developers" and "deployers" of high-risk AI systems used in "consequential decisions" — decisions that have material legal or similarly significant effects on consumers in areas such as employment, education, housing, insurance, and financial services.

Governor Polis signed the law with a reluctance letter asking the legislature to refine it before the effective date. Multiple amendment bills were introduced during the 2025 regular session but none advanced, leading to the August 2025 special session.

## Key Requirements of the Colorado AI Act [HIGH confidence]

SB 24-205 creates a duty of reasonable care for developers and deployers of "high-risk" AI systems — systems that make, or are a substantial factor in making, "consequential decisions" affecting education, employment, financial/lending services, essential government services, health care, housing, insurance, or legal services.

**Developer duties:**
- Documentation to deployers (intended uses, harmful uses, training data summaries, risk mitigation, evaluation outputs)
- Public statement summarizing high-risk systems
- Disclosure of algorithmic discrimination risks to the Colorado AG and deployers within 90 days of discovery

**Deployer duties:**
- Risk management program (NIST AI RMF or equivalent)
- Annual impact assessments
- Consumer notice when high-risk AI is used in a consequential decision
- Right to correct data and appeal to human review
- Public summary of deployed high-risk systems

**Enforcement:** The Colorado Attorney General has **exclusive** enforcement authority; no private right of action. Violations are unfair trade practices under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act — civil penalties **up to $20,000 per violation**.

## Detailed Analysis

### Enforcement Delay: SB 25B-004 [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Governor Polis called a special legislative session in August 2025 to address concerns that the AI Act would impose excessive costs on state and local governments and covered businesses. Senate Majority Leader Robert Rodriguez initially proposed the "AI Sunshine Act" (SB 4), which would have narrowed the original law while maintaining key consumer protections. When negotiations collapsed, Rodriguez amended his proposal to simply delay the effective date from February 1, 2026, to June 30, 2026. The bill passed and the Governor signed [SB 25B-004](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25b-004) on August 28, 2025.

SB 25B-004 does not alter any substantive requirements of the AI Act. Colorado lawmakers have signaled that further amendments may be considered during the 2026 regular session, but as of April 2026, no bill modifying SB 24-205's core requirements has been enacted. A working group was expected to release repeal-and-replace consensus language around March 2026.

### Chatbot Safety Bill: HB 26-1263 [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

[HB 26-1263](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1263), introduced on February 19, 2026, would establish requirements for operators of "conversational artificial intelligence services" — AI systems accessible to the general public that primarily simulate human conversation. The bill, sponsored by Representatives Sean Camacho and Javier Mabrey, would take effect January 1, 2027, and includes the following key provisions:

- **Disclosure requirement:** Operators must clearly and conspicuously notify minors that they are interacting with AI, not a human.
- **Anti-manipulation provisions:** Operators are prohibited from offering rewards to increase engagement with children and must prevent chatbots from creating emotional dependence through false claims of being human.
- **Content restrictions:** Operators must take reasonable measures to prevent AI chatbots from generating sexually explicit content when a minor is the user, and must prevent romantic or sexual role-playing interactions with minors.
- **Parental controls:** Required if chatbots are accessible to children under age 13.
- **Suicide prevention:** Operators must provide suicide-prevention resources for users who express suicidal thoughts or interest in self-harm.

Violations constitute a deceptive trade practice enforceable by the Attorney General under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act, with civil penalties of $1,000 per violation.

The bill passed the House Business Affairs and Labor Committee 10-3 on March 26, 2026, and was referred to the Committee of the Whole. As of March 31, 2026, it was listed at House Second Reading, laid over daily. No confirmed House floor vote was found in available sources as of April 7, 2026.

## Federal Preemption Concerns [HIGH confidence]

On December 11, 2025, President Trump signed **"Eliminating State Law Obstruction of National Artificial Intelligence Policy"**. Key directives bearing on Colorado:

- **Commerce Department list (~March 11, 2026):** Evaluation identifying "onerous" state AI laws conflicting with federal minimally-burdensome policy. Colorado's Act is widely expected to appear.
- **DOJ AI Litigation Task Force:** Challenges on Commerce Clause, preemption, or other constitutional grounds.
- **Federal funding conditions:** Potential BEAD broadband funding conditions tied to state AI-law posture.
- **FTC/FCC actions:** FTC policy statement on FTC Act application to AI (potentially preempting state UDAP-based AI enforcement) and an FCC AI reporting/disclosure proceeding.

Commentators agree the EO itself cannot preempt state law — preemption requires a federal statute or validly promulgated regulation — but it signals an aggressive federal litigation posture. Colorado AG Phil Weiser has defended the Act, making a federal-state confrontation plausible before June 30, 2026.

## Compliance Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

1. **Do not pause compliance work.** June 30, 2026 is real; substantive obligations are unchanged.
2. **Track the repeal-and-replace draft.** Any substantive changes will arrive on a tight pre-effective-date window.
3. **Monitor the Commerce Department list.** Inclusion likely triggers DOJ litigation and possible preliminary injunctions.
4. **Preserve documentation optionality.** Developer/deployer artifacts align with NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act — value retained under most scenarios.
5. **Watch FTC preemption statement.** Because enforcement runs through the Colorado Consumer Protection Act, an FTC policy statement could indirectly weaken the $20,000-per-violation hook.
6. **Monitor HB 26-1263.** If enacted, conversational AI operators face additional child-safety obligations effective January 1, 2027, with $1,000-per-violation enforcement risk for platforms with large user bases.

## Action Items

- **By June 30, 2026:** Deployers of high-risk AI systems must have risk management policies, impact assessments, consumer notification mechanisms, and appeal processes in place under SB 24-205.
- **Developers** must prepare disclosure documentation, training data summaries, and algorithmic discrimination risk disclosures for deployers by the same deadline.
- **Monitor HB 26-1263** through the 2026 Colorado legislative session for House floor vote and potential Senate action. If enacted, begin compliance planning for January 1, 2027 effective date.
- **Track potential SB 24-205 amendments** during the 2026 regular session that could modify core AI Act requirements before the June 30 enforcement date.
- **Track Commerce Department 90-day list** for inclusion of Colorado's AI Act and any resulting DOJ litigation that could affect enforcement before the effective date.
