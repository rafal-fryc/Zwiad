# Texas House Speaker Creates Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2024-04-17

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 2, 2024, Texas House Speaker Dade Phelan (R-Beaumont) formally established the [House Select Committee on Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies](https://house.texas.gov/committees/committee/035), appointing Rep. Giovanni Capriglione (R-Southlake) as chair. The five-member committee was charged with conducting a comprehensive review of AI advancements and their economic, ethical, and societal implications across Texas. The committee held multiple hearings, produced an [interim report](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/committees/reports/interim/88interim/House-Select-Committee-on-Artificial-Intelligence-and-Emerging-Technologies-Interim-Report-2024.pdf) with policy recommendations, and directly shaped significant AI legislation in the 89th Legislative Session, including HB 149, which established a comprehensive AI regulatory framework effective January 1, 2026.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

Texas had already taken preliminary steps toward AI governance before the committee's creation. In 2023, the 88th Legislature established the Artificial Intelligence Advisory Council, with Rep. Capriglione serving as Co-Chair, to study AI applications in state government. The rapid acceleration of generative AI capabilities throughout 2023 and early 2024 prompted Speaker Phelan to take more decisive legislative action.

The creation of the Select Committee followed a broader national trend of state legislatures establishing dedicated bodies to study AI. By early 2024, multiple states had formed AI task forces or committees, but Texas distinguished itself by creating a select committee with a specific mandate to produce actionable legislative recommendations on an accelerated timeline. The [Texas Association of Business praised the committee's creation](https://www.txbiz.org/2024/04/02/tab-applauds-speaker-phelan-on-creation-of-house-select-committee-on-artificial-intelligence-and-emerging-technology/), reflecting strong private-sector interest in shaping Texas AI policy.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Committee Formation and Mandate

Speaker Phelan's [proclamation](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/speaker/PROC-Artificial-Intelligence-and-Emerging-Technologies.pdf) established the committee with four core charges:

1. **Study the current state of AI/ET** and its uses across sectors
2. **Determine the impact of AI/ET** on various sectors of society
3. **Identify policy considerations** for the responsible use of AI/ET in the state's public and private sectors
4. **Recommend legislative, policy, regulatory, and remedial actions** to address the challenges and opportunities presented by AI/ET

The committee was required to submit an initial report to the House by May 16, 2024, with authority to submit additional reports as needed. This compressed timeline signaled the urgency Speaker Phelan placed on the issue.

### Committee Membership

The five-member committee comprised a bipartisan group:

- **Chair: Rep. Giovanni Capriglione** (R-Southlake) -- Co-Chair of the AI Advisory Council and the House's leading voice on technology policy
- **Rep. Jeff Leach** (R-Plano)
- **Rep. Oscar Longoria** (D-Mission)
- **Rep. Angelia Orr** (R-Celina)
- **Rep. Armando Walle** (D-Houston)

The committee held its [first meeting on May 3, 2024](https://www.kvue.com/article/news/politics/texas-legislature/texas-lawmakers-impact-artificial-intelligence-ai/269-77afed1b-04be-4f30-884f-f3b2bb9c33f8), and conducted multiple hearings throughout the interim period, receiving testimony from technology companies, academics, government agencies, and public interest groups.

### Interim Report and Key Recommendations

The committee produced a comprehensive [interim report](https://www.house.texas.gov/pdfs/committees/reports/interim/88interim/House-Select-Committee-on-Artificial-Intelligence-and-Emerging-Technologies-Interim-Report-2024.pdf) with recommendations spanning several policy areas:

**Public Agency AI Management:** The committee recommended establishing a comprehensive AI inventory for public agencies, requiring all Texas state agencies to conduct annual audits of AI systems currently deployed, identifying their functions, data sources, and potential risks of bias or misuse. It also recommended developing AI-specific training for public sector employees focused on responsible AI use, data privacy, and bias mitigation.

**Innovation and Accountability:** Recommendations included creating a regulatory sandbox to allow innovation outside standard regulations, requiring disclosure of AI use to consumers in high-risk interactions, and mandating that AI systems include measures to reduce bias and prevent algorithmic discrimination.

**Digital Threats:** The committee recommended strengthening laws to address emerging digital threats including deepfake technology, election interference, revenge pornography, and AI-generated child sexual abuse material.

**Data Privacy:** The report recommended adjustments to the [Texas Data Privacy and Security Act](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?LegSess=88R&Bill=HB4) to incorporate AI and emerging technologies.

### Legislative Outcomes in the 89th Session

The committee's work directly informed major legislation filed in the [89th Legislative Session](https://www.texaspolicyresearch.com/texas-ai-regulation-and-the-case-for-federal-preemption/) (which began January 14, 2025):

**HB 149 (Capriglione):** The most significant outcome was [House Bill 149](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/HB00149F.pdf), which created a comprehensive AI regulatory regime for the private sector. The bill included oversight of biometric data, mandates for AI developers and deployers, and the creation of the Texas Artificial Intelligence Council. HB 149 passed overwhelmingly and takes full effect on January 1, 2026.

**HB 2818 (Capriglione):** [House Bill 2818](https://www.texaspolicyresearch.com/texas-lawmakers-tackle-ai-regulatory-overreach-or-responsible-oversight/) proposed creating a new AI Division within the Department of Information Resources to modernize legacy systems with generative AI. The bill authorized nine new employees and imposed an $8.1 million cost through 2027 with approximately $5 million in annual recurring costs. HB 2818 passed and went into effect.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

The committee's work and resulting legislation affect a broad range of stakeholders in Texas:

- **State agencies** must now inventory and audit AI systems annually and develop employee training programs
- **Technology companies** operating in Texas face new developer and deployer mandates under HB 149, effective January 1, 2026
- **Businesses using AI** in consumer-facing applications must prepare for disclosure requirements in high-risk interactions
- **Political campaigns and media organizations** face new restrictions on deepfake technology and AI-generated content

### National Significance

Texas's approach is notable for several reasons. As the second-largest state by population and GDP, Texas's AI regulatory decisions carry outsized influence on the national landscape. The committee's work produced legislation (HB 149) that represents one of the most comprehensive state-level AI regulatory frameworks in the country, alongside Colorado's AI Act. The [debate over federal preemption](https://www.texaspolicyresearch.com/texas-ai-regulation-and-the-case-for-federal-preemption/) of state AI laws adds additional complexity, as any federal AI framework could potentially override Texas's regulatory approach.

## Action Items

- **For organizations operating in Texas:** Begin compliance assessment for HB 149 requirements well ahead of the January 1, 2026 effective date, particularly regarding AI disclosure obligations and bias mitigation measures
- **For Texas state agencies:** Prepare for mandatory AI system inventories and annual audits; develop employee AI training programs
- **For AI developers and deployers:** Review HB 149's developer and deployer mandate provisions to understand specific obligations around biometric data, consumer disclosure, and algorithmic discrimination prevention
- **Monitor:** Track implementation guidance from the Texas Artificial Intelligence Council created under HB 149, which will shape the practical compliance landscape
