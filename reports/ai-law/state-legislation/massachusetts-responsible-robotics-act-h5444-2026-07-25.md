# Massachusetts Advances Framework for Responsible Use of Robotics

**Jurisdiction:** Massachusetts | **Category:** AI Law / State Legislation | **Date:** July 24, 2026

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

Massachusetts is on the verge of enacting the nation's first comprehensive state law regulating the responsible use of robotic devices. The bill, commonly called the "Responsible Robotics Act" and formally captioned *An Act relative to civil rights and technology* (House docket 5444, engrossed from the original filing, House docket 1978, "An Act to ensure the responsible use of certain advanced robotic technologies"), bans the manufacture, sale, transfer, possession, or operation of robotic devices mounted with weapons, and separately bars using robots to threaten, criminally harass, or physically restrain people, subject to narrow carve-outs for the military, law enforcement, and Attorney General-approved research and safety testing waivers ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use), [Mondaq reprint](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/terrorism-homeland-security-defence/1821546/massachusetts-advances-framework-for-responsible-use-of-robotics)). The bill passed the House 155-1 in May 2026 and, as of this writing, sits before the Senate Committee on Ways and Means, with Massachusetts' formal legislative session set to end July 31, 2026 -- creating a narrow window for enactment ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use)). The measure has drawn an unusual coalition of support spanning the ACLU of Massachusetts and robotics manufacturer Boston Dynamics ([ACLU of Massachusetts](https://www.aclum.org/press-releases/lawmakers-file-bill-ensure-responsible-use-robots/), [Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-supports-ma-bill-on-responsible-use-of-robots/)). It arrives alongside -- and may ultimately be absorbed into -- a much larger $575.4 million Senate economic development package (S.3178) that separately imposes new safety-framework and audit obligations on "large frontier" AI developers and funds AI and robotics R&D, meaning the state's "robotics framework" is really two converging legislative tracks rather than one bill ([Massachusetts Senate Press Room](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1445), [Daily Hampshire Gazette](https://gazettenet.com/2026/07/24/senators-pad-575-million-economic-development-bill/)).

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

The legislation traces to a bill first filed by Representative Lindsay Sabadosa (D-Northampton) and then-co-petitioner Patricia Duffy in the 2023-2024 session addressing weaponized robots, following widely reported incidents of hobbyists mounting firearms on commercially available quadruped robots ([TechCrunch, Dec. 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/21/massachusetts-lawmakers-mull-killer-robot-bill/)). The bill was refiled for the 2025-2026 session as House docket 1978, "An Act to ensure the responsible use of certain advanced robotic technologies," sponsored by Rep. Sabadosa and Sen. Michael Moore ([search summary of malegislature.gov Bill H.1978](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H1978)). Massachusetts framed the effort as closing a regulatory gap that emerged as advanced mobile robots -- most notably those built by Boston Dynamics, headquartered in Waltham, Massachusetts -- moved from research labs into commercial and public deployment ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use)).

The bill built a broad advocacy coalition prior to House passage: the ACLU of Massachusetts (through its Technology for Liberty program director Kade Crockford), MassRobotics, the Association for Uncrewed Vehicle Systems International (AUVSI), and Boston Dynamics itself all publicly backed the measure, an alignment of civil-liberties and industry interests that is unusual for technology regulation ([ACLU of Massachusetts](https://www.aclum.org/press-releases/lawmakers-file-bill-ensure-responsible-use-robots/), [MassRobotics](https://www.massrobotics.org/massrobotics-supports-state-legislation-to-ensure-the-safe-use-of-robots/), [Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-supports-ma-bill-on-responsible-use-of-robots/)). Boston Dynamics has previously been a signatory, along with Agility Robotics, ANYbotics, Clearpath Robotics, Open Robotics, and Unitree, to a 2022 open letter pledging not to weaponize general-purpose mobile robots, and industry framed the Massachusetts bill as codifying that voluntary commitment into law ([Boston Dynamics](https://bostondynamics.com/news/boston-dynamics-supports-ma-bill-on-responsible-use-of-robots/)). The bill (redesignated H.5444 as it advanced through the House) passed by a 155-1 vote in May 2026 and was referred to the Senate Committee on Ways and Means, where it remained pending as of late July 2026 ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use)).

Separately, and running on a parallel track, the Massachusetts House and Senate have been negotiating a large economic development bond bill (House version passed July 9, 2026 as a roughly $561 million package; Senate version, S.3178, passed July 23, 2026 as a $575.4 million package) that funds AI and robotics commercialization ($75 million for AI development and application, $25 million for robotics R&D, $100 million for the defense sector, and $100-200 million in research bridge funding for public higher education) while also creating new AI safety obligations for "large frontier" AI developers and, per at least one industry-trade account, incorporating restrictions on weaponized robots similar to those in H.5444 ([WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/07/09/massachusetts-house-economic-development-bill-policies), [Massachusetts Senate Press Room, S.3178 Fact Sheet](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1445), [Daily Hampshire Gazette](https://gazettenet.com/2026/07/24/senators-pad-575-million-economic-development-bill/), [Automate.org](https://www.automate.org/robotics/industry-insights/massachusetts-is-betting-that-robotics-and-ai-will-drive-its-next-economy)). Reporting from the Boston Globe indicates the AI-safety provisions of S.3178 have become contested between OpenAI (which wants Massachusetts to align with the audit regime recently enacted in Illinois and with California/New York frameworks) and Anthropic (which is pushing an amendment from Sen. Mike Rush for more stringent third-party safety evaluations) ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/07/23/newsletters/massachusetts-senate-ai-openai-anthropic-the-scrum/)).

## Detailed Analysis [LOW confidence]

**Official text could not be retrieved at time of writing.** WebFetch access to malegislature.gov was unavailable throughout this research session (permission errors on all attempted URLs), so the provisions below are drawn from secondary reporting (law firm client alerts, an ACLU press release, a manufacturer statement, and trade press) rather than the enrolled bill text. The expected primary sources for manual verification are:
- House bill (current form): [malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H5444](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H5444)
- House bill (original filing): [malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H1978](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/H1978)
- Senate economic development bill: [malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S3178](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S3178)

Based on the corroborating secondary sources, the Responsible Robotics Act's core prohibitions are:

1. **Weaponization ban.** It is unlawful to manufacture, modify, sell, transfer, possess, or operate a robotic device equipped or mounted with a weapon, defined to include firearms, explosives, chemical agents, and weaponized lasers ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/terrorism-homeland-security-defence/1821546/massachusetts-advances-framework-for-responsible-use-of-robotics), [ACLU of Massachusetts](https://www.aclum.org/press-releases/lawmakers-file-bill-ensure-responsible-use-robots/)).
2. **Coercive-use ban.** It is separately unlawful to use a robotic device to threaten to commit a crime, to criminally harass a person, or to physically restrain or attempt to restrain a person ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/terrorism-homeland-security-defence/1821546/massachusetts-advances-framework-for-responsible-use-of-robotics)).
3. **Government-use oversight.** Law enforcement agencies must obtain a warrant before deploying robotic devices in circumstances that would otherwise trigger constitutional protections, and agencies must submit periodic public reporting on their use of robotic systems ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use)).
4. **Exemptions and waivers.** The prohibitions do not apply to the U.S. military and its contractors, or to law enforcement personnel disposing of explosives using robotic devices. Private companies developing anti-weaponization countermeasures (for example, robots designed to auto-disable upon detecting gunfire) may seek case-by-case waivers from the Massachusetts Attorney General ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/terrorism-homeland-security-defence/1821546/massachusetts-advances-framework-for-responsible-use-of-robotics)).
5. **Enforcement.** Violations carry civil fines reportedly ranging from $5,000 to $25,000 per violation, in addition to any penalties available under other applicable law, alongside criminal penalties and a private right of action allowing individuals harmed by a violation to seek damages or injunctive relief ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/terrorism-homeland-security-defence/1821546/massachusetts-advances-framework-for-responsible-use-of-robotics)).

Because these figures and mechanisms come from press coverage rather than verified statutory text, and because the bill may still be amended in the Senate or merged into S.3178 before enactment, readers should treat the specific dollar thresholds and mechanics as provisional pending confirmation against the enrolled bill or, if enacted, the session law (expected to be published in the Acts of 2026 and codified in Massachusetts General Laws).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

If enacted substantially as passed by the House, the law would be the first state-level statute in the country specifically targeting weaponized robotic devices and imposing use-based restrictions and government oversight obligations on robotics deployment generally ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use), [The Robotage](https://therobotage.com/news/massachusetts-first-robot-weapons-law)). Robotics manufacturers, integrators, and end-users operating in Massachusetts -- including logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, public safety, and defense-adjacent firms -- should expect compliance obligations covering: (1) product design and sale restrictions on weapon-mountable hardware or accessories; (2) contractual and end-use monitoring to avoid facilitating prohibited uses by customers; and (3) for law enforcement and public agencies, new warrant and reporting requirements before and after deploying robots in the field ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2166/2026-07-20-massachusetts-advances-framework-responsible-use)).

The measure is also notable for arriving alongside a much larger state investment in the sector: the pending economic development package separately allocates $75 million for AI development, $25 million for robotics R&D, and $100 million for defense-sector economic growth, while creating new statutory safety-framework and independent-audit obligations for "large frontier" AI developers, enforceable by the Attorney General ([Massachusetts Senate Press Room](https://malegislature.gov/PressRoom/Detail?pressReleaseId=1445), [Daily Hampshire Gazette](https://gazettenet.com/2026/07/24/senators-pad-575-million-economic-development-bill/)). Frontier AI developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic are actively lobbying over the stringency of those audit provisions, which could affect any company building or deploying large-scale AI models used in or from Massachusetts, including those with robotics applications ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/07/23/newsletters/massachusetts-senate-ai-openai-anthropic-the-scrum/)). Given the compressed legislative calendar (formal session ends July 31, 2026), affected organizations face significant uncertainty about final bill language, whether the robotics and AI-safety provisions will be enacted as standalone measures or merged, and the ultimate effective date.

## Action Items

- Monitor the Senate Committee on Ways and Means and full Senate floor action on H.5444 before the July 31, 2026 end of the formal session; track whether its provisions are folded into S.3178.
- Robotics manufacturers and integrators with Massachusetts operations should inventory any weapon-mountable hardware, accessories, or after-market modification kits offered for sale, and review distribution/reseller agreements for prohibited end-use scenarios.
- Public safety agencies and municipalities using or piloting robotic devices in Massachusetts should begin preparing warrant-request protocols and public reporting processes in anticipation of the oversight requirements.
- Companies developing anti-weaponization or safety-shutdown technology for robots should track the Attorney General's forthcoming waiver process once the law (if enacted) takes effect.
- AI developers operating "large frontier" models with Massachusetts nexus should monitor the S.3178 conference process, particularly the disputed third-party audit and safety-framework provisions.
- Verify final statutory citations and effective date against the enrolled bill text at malegislature.gov once the session concludes, since the provisions summarized here rely on secondary reporting rather than confirmed official text.
