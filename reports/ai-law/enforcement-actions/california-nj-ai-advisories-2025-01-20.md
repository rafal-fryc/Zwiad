# California and New Jersey Release Coordinated AI Advisories on Existing Law Applicability

**Jurisdiction:** California, New Jersey | **Category:** AI Law / Enforcement Guidance | **Date:** January 2025

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

In early January 2025, the attorneys general of California and New Jersey each issued significant AI-focused legal guidance, signaling a coordinated state-level assertion that existing law — without new AI-specific legislation — fully reaches artificial intelligence developers, vendors, and deployers. California AG Rob Bonta issued two legal advisories on January 13, 2025: one covering general consumer protection, civil rights, competition, and data privacy obligations for any entity developing, selling, or using AI; and a second addressing healthcare-specific obligations under California law. New Jersey AG Matthew Platkin and the Division on Civil Rights (DCR) issued guidance on January 9, 2025 clarifying that the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination (NJLAD) prohibits "algorithmic discrimination" — discrimination enabled by automated decision-making tools — and launched a new Civil Rights Innovation Lab to support AI-related enforcement. Both actions reinforce the emerging multistate posture that AI is not a regulatory gap but rather a new application domain for decades of established law.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The January 2025 advisories from California and New Jersey arrived against a backdrop of intense state-level AI legislative activity. In 2024, several states, including California, Colorado, and Utah, passed or considered substantive AI-specific legislation. California Governor Gavin Newsom vetoed the high-profile [SB 1047](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB1047) — which would have imposed safety and testing obligations on large-scale AI model developers — but signed a slate of more targeted AI bills. Among those with provisions taking effect on January 1, 2025:

- **[SB 1120](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB1120)** — Requires licensed physicians to supervise AI tools used by health insurers in utilization review and management; prohibits denial of care based solely on AI algorithms.
- **[AB 2013](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2013)** — Mandates developers of generative AI models publicly post information about training data used (compliance deadline January 1, 2026).

**Note on SB 942:** [SB 942 (California AI Transparency Act)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB942) — which requires covered AI providers to make free, accessible tools available to detect if content was generated by AI — was signed in 2024 but its operative date is January 1, 2026. AB 853 (signed October 2025) subsequently extended that operative date to August 2, 2026. No SB 942 requirements were in effect in January 2025.

New Jersey had no AI-specific legislation equivalent to California's 2024 package; instead, its January 2025 guidance relied entirely on the existing NJLAD, [N.J.S.A. § 10:5-1 et seq.](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-10/chapter-10-5/), which has prohibited discrimination in employment, housing, public accommodations, credit, and contracting since 1945.

The Massachusetts AG had issued a comparable advisory in April 2024 applying that state's consumer protection, civil rights, and data privacy laws to AI, establishing the multistate pattern that the California and New Jersey guidance follows. See the [Massachusetts AG AI Advisory (April 2024)](reports/ai-law/enforcement-actions/massachusetts-ag-ai-advisory-2024-04-22.md).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### California: Two-Pronged Advisory

**General AI Advisory (January 13, 2025)**

The [California Department of Justice's first advisory](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/Legal%20Advisory%20-%20Application%20of%20Existing%20CA%20Laws%20to%20Artificial%20Intelligence.pdf) covers the full range of entities that develop, sell, or use AI systems. It identifies the following existing California legal frameworks as applicable:

- **Unfair Competition Law (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200)** — Prohibits unlawful, unfair, or fraudulent business practices. AI-generated misrepresentations (deepfakes, misleading chatbots, deceptive marketing) fall within this statute.
- **False Advertising Law (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17500)** — Prohibits false or misleading claims about AI tools' capabilities or accuracy.
- **California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)** — Governs collection, use, and sale of personal information, including AI training data. AI systems that output personal information must honor CCPA transparency and individual rights obligations; data processing must be "reasonably necessary and proportionate."
- **California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA)** — AI systems trained on, or that process, private electronic communications without consent may violate CIPA's restrictions on wiretapping, eavesdropping, and recording.
- **Civil Rights Laws** — AI that produces discriminatory outcomes based on protected characteristics violates California's civil rights framework.
- **Competition Laws** — AI-enabled price fixing, collusion, or other anticompetitive practices remain actionable.

The advisory also highlighted new laws effective January 1, 2025, including disclosure requirements for AI training datasets, disclosure requirements relating to AI use in election materials, prohibitions on deepfake misuse, and AI-generated telemarketing disclosure rules.

**Healthcare-Specific Advisory (January 13, 2025)**

The [second advisory](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-issues-legal-advisories-application-california-law-ai) targets healthcare providers, insurers, vendors, investors, and others that develop, sell, or use AI in healthcare settings. The advisory identified the following as potential unlawful practices under existing California law:

- Denying health insurance claims using AI in a manner that overrides physicians' determinations about necessary treatment.
- Applying predictive tools that use past claims data in ways that disadvantage certain patient populations.
- Double-booking patient appointments because AI predicts a patient is likely to miss — a form of discriminatory administrative barrier.
- Conducting cost-benefit analyses of treatments for patients with disabilities using AI that embeds stereotypes undervaluing their lives.
- Using AI to override licensed physicians' clinical judgments, which may violate California's corporate practice of medicine doctrine (Bus. & Prof. Code § 2400 et seq.) — among the most comprehensive such prohibitions in the country.

Healthcare entities must also comply with the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA) and HIPAA when AI systems access or disclose patient data.

### New Jersey: NJLAD Algorithmic Discrimination Guidance

On January 9, 2025, [AG Platkin and the DCR issued guidance](https://www.njoag.gov/attorney-general-platkin-and-division-on-civil-rights-announce-new-guidance-on-algorithmic-discrimination-creation-of-civil-rights-innovation-lab/) clarifying that the NJLAD prohibits algorithmic discrimination across three theories of liability:

1. **Disparate Treatment** — Intentional discrimination enabled through algorithmic tools targeting or excluding protected groups.
2. **Disparate Impact** — Facially neutral tools that produce discriminatory outcomes based on protected characteristics; intent to discriminate is not required.
3. **Failure to Accommodate** — Tools that fail to account for reasonable accommodations for individuals with disabilities.

The guidance emphasizes that covered entities — employers, housing providers, places of public accommodation, credit providers, and contractors — cannot escape NJLAD liability merely because a third party developed the discriminatory tool. Employers who deploy a vendor-supplied hiring algorithm are liable for discriminatory outputs even without knowledge of the tool's inner workings.

The guidance identified three stages where algorithmic discrimination most commonly arises:
- **Design**: Predictive tools trained on historically biased hiring data may replicate and amplify past discrimination.
- **Training**: Tools not trained on data inclusive of individuals who require accommodations may penalize those individuals.
- **Deployment**: Selective application of a tool — e.g., screening only candidates from one demographic group — constitutes discriminatory deployment.

The DCR simultaneously announced the [Civil Rights Innovation Lab](https://www.njoag.gov/attorney-general-platkin-and-division-on-civil-rights-announce-new-guidance-on-algorithmic-discrimination-creation-of-civil-rights-innovation-lab/), a new initiative to deploy AI tools for complaint processing, discrimination detection, enforcement expansion, and public education. The Lab signals that the DCR intends to use AI both as a subject of enforcement and as an enforcement tool.

The full guidance document is available in PDF form from the [NJ Office of Attorney General](https://www.nj.gov/oag/newsreleases25/2025-0108_DCR-Guidance-on-Algorithmic-Discrimination.pdf).

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

**Who is Affected**

The California advisories reach any entity that develops, sells, or uses AI in connection with California residents — a category that includes technology companies, healthcare systems, insurers, financial institutions, retailers, and employers. The healthcare advisory has particular implications for health plan utilization management functions now subject to SB 1120's physician supervision requirement.

The New Jersey guidance applies to all covered entities under the NJLAD — employers, housing providers, places of public accommodation, credit providers, and contractors — that deploy automated decision-making tools affecting New Jersey residents.

**Compliance Implications**

For California-connected entities:
- AI systems that process personal information of California residents must be inventoried for CCPA compliance.
- AI-generated content and marketing must be reviewed under the UCL and False Advertising Law for deceptive claims.
- Healthcare AI vendors and providers must document physician supervision of any utilization review or clinical decision-support tools, consistent with SB 1120.
- Training data for AI systems used in California must be assessed for CMIA and HIPAA compliance where patient data is involved.

For New Jersey-connected entities:
- AI tools used in hiring, promotion, housing screening, or lending decisions must be audited for disparate treatment and disparate impact on NJLAD-protected classes.
- Third-party vendor contracts should be reviewed to ensure vendors bear indemnification obligations for discriminatory algorithmic outputs.
- Reasonable accommodation processes should be built into, or checked against, any automated decision-making tool affecting individuals who may need accommodations.

**Enforcement Outlook**

California AG Bonta characterized the advisories as preliminary steps, stating: "The fifth-largest economy in the world is not the wild west; existing California laws apply to both the development and use of AI." The advisory expressly notes that the AG has enforcement authority under the UCL and other cited statutes, signaling prospective enforcement action. California's CCPA enforcement program already has a track record of significant settlements (e.g., the $1.55 million Healthline.com settlement (announced July 2025)).

New Jersey's DCR, through the Civil Rights Innovation Lab, is developing internal AI capacity to detect and remedy discrimination — a direct signal of heightened enforcement attention. The December 2025 adoption of [disparate impact rules under the NJLAD](https://www.njoag.gov/ag-platkin-announces-division-on-civil-rights-adopts-landmark-rules-on-disparate-impact-discrimination-under-new-jersey-law/) (effective December 15, 2025) further codifies the framework previewed in the January 2025 guidance, suggesting a multi-year enforcement build-up.

## Action Items

- **Inventory AI use cases** — Compile a list of all AI systems used for consumer-facing, employment, healthcare, or credit decisions affecting California or New Jersey residents.
- **CCPA gap assessment** — Evaluate whether AI systems processing California residents' personal information meet transparency, consent, and data minimization requirements.
- **Healthcare AI review (California)** — Verify that any AI deployed in utilization management or clinical decision-support functions has documented physician oversight meeting SB 1120 requirements.
- **Employment AI audit (New Jersey)** — Audit vendor-supplied and internally developed hiring, promotion, and performance tools for disparate impact on NJLAD-protected classes; obtain representations and warranties from vendors regarding bias testing.
- **Third-party contract review** — Ensure AI vendor agreements include indemnification for discriminatory outputs and representations of CCPA/NJLAD compliance.
- **Monitor enforcement developments** — Track DCR Civil Rights Innovation Lab announcements and California AG enforcement actions under the UCL for early signals of AI-specific enforcement targeting.
- **Revisit in Q4 2025** — New Jersey's disparate impact rules took effect December 15, 2025; confirm compliance posture at that point.
