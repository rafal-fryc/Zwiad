# Florida AG Launches Criminal Investigation into OpenAI Over ChatGPT's Role in FSU Shooting

**Jurisdiction:** Florida | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-22

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier escalated a previously civil probe into OpenAI into a [criminal investigation](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/newsrelease/attorney-general-james-uthmeier-launches-criminal-investigation-openai-chatgpt) on April 21, 2026, following his office's review of chat logs showing suspect Phoenix Ikner consulting ChatGPT for tactical details in the hours before the April 17, 2025 mass shooting at Florida State University that killed two people and injured six others. The AG's Office of Statewide Prosecution issued subpoenas to OpenAI — requiring response by May 1, 2026 — seeking records on the company's safety policies, its cooperation with law enforcement, and its organizational structure. This is the first known state criminal investigation of an AI company arising from an AI tool's alleged facilitation of a violent crime, and it signals a materially elevated legal risk posture for AI model developers operating without mandatory harm-intervention policies.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Florida AG Uthmeier announced the initial **civil** investigation on [April 9, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/09/florida-ag-launches-investigation-openai), citing FSU shooting records, national security concerns, and risks to minors; the criminal investigation was announced on [April 21, 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/florida-attorney-general-criminal-investigation-openai-fsu-chatgpt-rcna341205).
- The underlying incident: Phoenix Ikner, 21, is charged with two counts of first-degree murder and seven counts of attempted first-degree murder for the [April 17, 2025 FSU shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Florida_State_University_shooting) that killed Robert Morales and Tiru Chabba; trial is scheduled for October 2026.
- Court documents and police records show Ikner consulted ChatGPT on the morning of the shooting, asking: what type of firearm and ammunition to use, what time the FSU Student Union would be most crowded, and "If there was a shooting at FSU, how would the country react?" — per [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/21/nx-s1-5793967/florida-openai-investigation-mass-shooting-fsu) and [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/florida-criminal-investigation-openai-chatgpt-alleged-role-fsu-shooting/).
- ChatGPT's response reportedly included information on what constitutes a high-casualty event: "Three or more people killed (excluding the shooter) is often the unofficial bar for widespread national media attention," according to [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/04/21/chatgpt-fsu-shooting-openai/).
- AG Uthmeier stated: "If ChatGPT were a person, it would be facing charges for murder," per the [official Florida AG press release](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/newsrelease/attorney-general-james-uthmeier-launches-criminal-investigation-openai-chatgpt).
- Subpoenas issued by the Office of Statewide Prosecution seek: (1) OpenAI policies and internal training materials on user threats of harm to self/others from March 2024 through April 2026; (2) policies on cooperation with and reporting of crimes to law enforcement; (3) an organizational chart of OpenAI's leaders and senior managers; and (4) a full list of employees working on ChatGPT — per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/florida-attorney-general-criminal-investigation-openai-fsu-chatgpt-rcna341205) and [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5841734-openai-florida-state-shooting/).
- The subpoena response deadline is **May 1, 2026**.
- Broader investigation scope also encompasses: national security concerns about whether OpenAI data could reach "America's enemies, such as the Chinese Communist Party," harm to minors, and child sexual abuse material, per [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/04/51742138/florida-ag-launches-investigation-into-openai-over-possible-chinese-threat) and [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5824392-florida-investigates-openai-chatgpt/).
- OpenAI spokesperson Kate Waters stated: "Last year's mass shooting at Florida State University was a tragedy, but ChatGPT is not responsible for this terrible crime. ChatGPT provided factual responses to questions with information that could be found broadly across public sources on the internet, and it did not encourage or promote illegal or harmful activity," per [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/florida-criminal-investigation-openai-chatgpt-alleged-role-fsu-shooting/).
- OpenAI stated it identified the suspect's account and proactively shared account information with law enforcement after the April 2025 shooting, per [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/21/nx-s1-5793967/florida-openai-investigation-mass-shooting-fsu).
- Uthmeier acknowledged this is legally uncharted territory, stating "We are going to look at who knew what, designed what, or should have done what" and "There's nothing new about corporations being held liable for criminal conduct. With AI, we are venturing into uncharted territory, but we need to know if OpenAI has criminal liability," per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/florida-attorney-general-criminal-investigation-openai-fsu-chatgpt-rcna341205).
- CNBC noted the investigation was announced [ahead of OpenAI's potential IPO](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/09/florida-ag-opens-probe-into-openai-ahead-of-potential-ipo.html), adding reputational and financial risk dimensions.

## Legal Context [MEDIUM confidence]

The criminal investigation raises unresolved questions under both state criminal law and federal preemption doctrine:

**Criminal liability theory.** Florida has no AI-specific criminal statute. The AG's probe appears to proceed under general criminal facilitation or conspiracy theories — whether an AI system and/or its developers could be held criminally liable for knowingly or recklessly enabling a violent crime. Florida statutes (e.g., Fla. Stat. § 777.011, principal liability) were written for human actors; their application to AI systems is entirely novel and untested.

**Section 230 and federal law.** OpenAI is likely to assert protection under Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act (47 U.S.C. § 230), which generally immunizes interactive computer service providers from liability for third-party content. Whether AI-generated outputs constitute "third-party content" for Section 230 purposes is actively litigated in federal courts and has not been resolved. Florida cannot override federal law, and any criminal theory dependent on OpenAI's outputs being treated as speech the company generated — rather than user-directed content — will face this threshold question.

**Civil investigation continues in parallel.** The April 9 civil probe — examining consumer protection, child safety, and national security concerns — continues concurrently. The civil track may proceed under Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA) or other consumer protection authorities, which carry a lower evidentiary bar.

## Action Items

- **AI model developers and deployers:** Review and document your current harm-intervention policies for user queries that reference violence, weapons, or mass-casualty events. Ensure policies cover detection and escalation of such queries to law enforcement-reportable thresholds.
- **Legal and compliance teams:** Audit your Section 230 posture and whether your legal team has analyzed its applicability to AI-generated outputs in your jurisdiction.
- **Florida-based AI companies:** The Office of Statewide Prosecution's involvement signals this is not a routine regulatory matter. If your product touches AI safety or user-generated harmful content, engage Florida-licensed criminal defense counsel familiar with computer crime statutes now — before any investigative contact.
- **National security compliance:** If your AI product ingests or processes data that could be accessed by foreign actors, assess your data governance policies against the concerns articulated in Uthmeier's April 9 announcement.
- **Monitor subpoena outcome:** OpenAI's May 1, 2026 response to the subpoena will likely set precedents for what regulators view as adequate AI safety policy disclosures. Track what OpenAI produces publicly.
- **Watch the FSU criminal trial (October 2026):** Evidence admitted at Ikner's trial regarding ChatGPT usage may significantly shape public and legislative opinion on AI developer liability.
