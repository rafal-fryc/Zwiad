# DOJ Civil Rights Division Convenes Federal Agencies to Coordinate AI Civil Rights Enforcement

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2024-10-18

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On October 9, 2024, the Department of Justice's Civil Rights Division convened the heads of civil rights offices from more than a dozen federal agencies to align enforcement strategy against AI-related civil rights harms. Attendees — drawn from the EEOC, CFPB, FTC, HHS, DHS, HUD, DOL, and agencies spanning Agriculture, Commerce, Education, Energy, Interior, Transportation, and Treasury — collectively pledged to continue collaboration and coordinate external stakeholder engagement on AI equity. The convening was held pursuant to responsibilities assigned by [Executive Order 14110 on the Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title3-vol1/pdf/CFR-2024-title3-vol1-eo14110.pdf) (October 30, 2023), which tasked the Civil Rights Division with coordinating agencies to prevent and address unlawful discrimination arising from AI systems. The Civil Rights Division also introduced its new Chief Technologist, Dr. Laura Edelson, signaling an intent to systematically scale AI enforcement capacity. Although EO 14110 was revoked on January 20, 2025 by [EO 14148 ("Initial Rescissions of Harmful Executive Orders and Actions")](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/28/2025-01901/initial-rescissions-of-harmful-executive-orders-and-actions), the underlying statutory civil rights obligations enforced by each participating agency remain in force; the interagency coordination architecture it created is now effectively dissolved.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Executive Order 14110 and the Civil Rights Mandate

On October 30, 2023, President Biden signed [Executive Order 14110](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title3-vol1/pdf/CFR-2024-title3-vol1-eo14110.pdf), titled "Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence." Section 7.1(b) of the order directed the Attorney General to coordinate federal civil rights and consumer protection enforcement in connection with AI, specifically tasking the Civil Rights Division with convening agency civil rights offices, developing policy guidance, and ensuring that existing federal anti-discrimination law applies fully to AI-assisted decisions in areas including housing, employment, education, healthcare, credit, and federal benefits programs. (The [Federal Register's public comment notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/25/2024-08818/request-for-input-from-the-public-on-section-71b-of-executive-order-14110-safe-secure-and) for implementation activities under EO 14110 is expressly captioned "Section 7.1(b)" — confirming that provision as the operative mandate for the Civil Rights Division's interagency role.)

The order established a baseline principle that an organization's use of AI does not excuse it from its existing legal obligations. It directed agencies to clarify that consumer protection laws and anti-discrimination statutes apply regardless of whether a discriminatory outcome is produced by a human employee or an algorithmic system.

### The April 2024 Agency Pledge

The October 2024 convening was the second major interagency milestone under EO 14110. The first occurred on April 4, 2024, when the [DOJ announced that five new cabinet-level agencies had joined a pledge to enforce civil rights laws in AI](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/five-new-federal-agencies-join-justice-department-pledge-enforce-civil-rights-laws): the Departments of Education, Health and Human Services, Homeland Security, Housing and Urban Development, and Labor. The joint statement — originally signed by the DOJ, EEOC, CFPB, and FTC in April 2023 — grew with each convening cycle, reflecting the Biden administration's attempt to build a durable cross-agency AI enforcement coalition.

### DOJ Civil Rights Division AI Program

The Civil Rights Division maintains a dedicated [Artificial Intelligence and Civil Rights page](https://www.justice.gov/archives/crt/ai) centralizing its AI-related enforcement work, guidance, and public resources. By October 2024, the Division had compiled a portfolio of AI-adjacent enforcement actions — including a settlement with a Virginia IT firm whose AI-generated job advertisements required H-1B, OPT, or H-4 visa status, effectively [excluding U.S. workers in violation of the Immigration and Nationality Act](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/civil-rights-division-obtains-settlement-company-used-ai-generated-advertisements-excluded). The Division also held an [October 2, 2024 public symposium on AI](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202410/us-justice-developing-ai-use-guidelines-for-law-enforcement-civil-rights) at which enforcement strategy was publicly discussed.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope and Participants at the October 9 Convening

The October 9, 2024 interagency convening, described in [DOJ's official readout](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/readout-justice-departments-interagency-convening-advancing-equity-artificial-intelligence), brought together civil rights office heads and senior officials from the following agencies:

- **Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)** — Chair Charlotte Burrows
- **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)** — Director Rohit Chopra
- **Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)** — Director of Civil Rights Melanie Fontes Rainer
- **Department of Homeland Security (DHS)** — Officer for Civil Rights and Civil Liberties Shoba Sivaprasad Wadhia
- **Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)** — Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary Diane Shelley
- Additional agency representatives from the Departments of Agriculture, Commerce, Education, Energy, Interior, Labor, Transportation, and Treasury; the Federal Trade Commission; and the Social Security Administration

The breadth of representation — covering sectors from consumer credit to agricultural lending to transportation — underscores the scope of domains in which AI algorithmic decision-making intersects with federal civil rights protections.

### Commitments and Outcomes

All participants [pledged to continue collaboration](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/readout-justice-departments-interagency-convening-advancing-equity-ai) and agreed to partner on external stakeholder engagement around their collective efforts to advance equity and civil rights in AI. Agencies provided updates on obligations assigned to them under EO 14110, including developing and issuing:

- Policy guidance on how existing federal civil rights and consumer protection law applies to AI in education, employment, healthcare, housing, and credit
- Public education materials on how AI systems can violate federal protections
- Holistic remediation frameworks for addressing systemic algorithmic bias

The convening also highlighted a theme of technical capacity-building: each agency was expected to develop internal expertise to evaluate algorithmic systems and to conduct enforcement investigations that require understanding AI decision logic, training data sources, and audit methodologies.

### Introduction of the Chief Technologist

The Civil Rights Division introduced Dr. Laura Edelson as its new [Chief Technologist](https://x.com/LauraEdelson2/status/1828449159902294176) — a former Northeastern University faculty member and platform accountability researcher. Dr. Edelson was tasked with systematically expanding the division's AI enforcement capacity, increasing internal operational efficiency through technological modernization, and developing an audit-centered framework for detecting and remedying algorithmic bias. At the October 2, 2024 symposium, she outlined three strategic pillars: soliciting public complaints of AI-related discrimination, encouraging AI whistleblowers, and developing a government-side methodology for auditing algorithmic systems in enforcement investigations. The hiring of a Chief Technologist signals the Division's intent to build direct technical capacity rather than rely solely on third-party expert witnesses in litigation.

### Legal Basis: Existing Anti-Discrimination Statutes Applied to AI

A core theme at the convening was the principle that existing federal statutes already reach AI-facilitated discrimination — no new AI-specific legislation is required to bring enforcement actions. The participating agencies enforce a broad array of statutes that collectively cover the deployment of AI:

- **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act** (employment discrimination) — enforced by the EEOC and DOJ
- **Fair Housing Act** — enforced by HUD and DOJ
- **Equal Credit Opportunity Act** — enforced by the CFPB and DOJ
- **Title VI of the Civil Rights Act** (discrimination in federally funded programs) — enforced by DOJ and multiple agency civil rights offices
- **Americans with Disabilities Act** and **Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act** — relevant to AI systems that create access barriers
- **Immigration and Nationality Act** — enforced by DOJ in the employment discrimination context

[Gibson Dunn's analysis of the agency pledge cycle](https://www.gibsondunn.com/us-agencies-issue-pledge-to-investigate-ai-development-and-use/) concluded that the Biden administration's strategy was to create a "whole of government" enforcement posture: each agency would use its existing authority to regulate AI within its domain, with the DOJ Civil Rights Division serving as a coordinating hub to share investigative techniques, identify cross-sector patterns, and develop shared guidance.

### Role of Algorithmic Auditing

Dr. Edelson's remarks — both at the October 2 symposium and at the October 9 convening — centered on algorithmic auditing as a core enforcement methodology. She discussed how audits can be used to prevent, investigate, monitor, and remedy algorithmic bias. This signals that enforcement investigations will increasingly involve requests for technical documentation of AI systems, including training data composition, model validation protocols, disparity testing results, and internal bias audit reports. Organizations deploying AI in high-stakes decisions should expect that such records will be discoverable in future enforcement proceedings.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Current Operational Status Following EO 14110 Revocation

On January 20, 2025, President Trump signed [EO 14148 ("Initial Rescissions of Harmful Executive Orders and Actions")](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/28/2025-01901/initial-rescissions-of-harmful-executive-orders-and-actions), which explicitly revoked EO 14110 along with dozens of other Biden-era executive orders. Three days later, on January 23, 2025, Trump separately signed [EO 14179 ("Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence")](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/31/2025-02172/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence) as the affirmative replacement AI policy order. EO 14179 emphasized removing regulatory impediments to AI development and directed agencies to revise or rescind policies derived from EO 14110. In practice, the EEOC removed AI-specific guidance documents from its website in January 2025; the interagency coordination mechanism described in this report has no successor structure under the current administration.

However, the underlying civil rights statutes enforced by each participating agency remain in force. The Trump administration has not indicated it will cease enforcement under Title VII, the Fair Housing Act, or other statutes when AI is the mechanism of discrimination. The enforcement risk for organizations deploying algorithmic decision-making tools in employment, lending, housing, or healthcare has not disappeared — the risk profile has shifted, but the legal exposure under existing law remains.

### Affected Industries and Use Cases

Organizations most directly in scope for the enforcement posture developed at this convening include:

- **Employers** using AI for hiring, performance review, or termination decisions (EEOC, DOJ enforcement)
- **Mortgage lenders and credit card issuers** using AI for underwriting or credit limit decisions (CFPB, DOJ enforcement)
- **Landlords and property management companies** using algorithmic tenant screening (HUD, DOJ enforcement)
- **Healthcare providers and insurers** using AI for prior authorization, diagnosis support, or benefits administration (HHS, DOJ enforcement)
- **Federal contractors** and recipients of federal funding who deploy AI in program administration (Title VI exposure across multiple agencies)
- **IT staffing and recruiting firms** whose AI sourcing tools embed citizenship status or national origin criteria (DOJ INA enforcement)

### The Algorithmic Audit Disclosure Risk

The Chief Technologist's emphasis on algorithmic auditing has a concrete compliance implication: organizations that conduct internal AI bias audits generate documentation that may be discoverable in regulatory investigations. Companies should develop a legal strategy for audit records before deploying AI in high-stakes decisions — including whether audits are privileged, what their scope should be, and how findings of bias will be remediated and documented.

## Action Items

- **Review AI deployment in Title VII, Fair Housing Act, and ECOA contexts**: Even without the EO 14110 coordination architecture, agency enforcement of existing statutes against algorithmic discrimination remains a live risk. Audit AI systems used in hiring, lending, housing, and benefits decisions for disparate impact against protected classes.
- **Assess the discoverability of existing AI audit records**: If your organization has conducted internal or third-party algorithmic bias audits, evaluate whether those records are protected by attorney-client privilege or work product doctrine in the context of a potential regulatory investigation.
- **Monitor successor interagency activity under the Trump administration**: Watch for whether the DOJ, FTC, or CFPB initiates individual enforcement actions against AI-enabled discrimination independent of the interagency coordination structure. The CFPB under Director Chopra (who attended the October 9 convening) pursued active AI enforcement through early 2025; successor leadership appointments may shift agency posture.
- **Track AI whistleblower risk**: Dr. Edelson's public call for AI whistleblowers to come forward — delivered at the October 2, 2024 symposium — suggests that enforcement leads may originate from internal company sources. Implement internal escalation paths so that employee concerns about algorithmic bias are addressed before reaching regulators.
- **Watch for AI-specific guidance from surviving agency civil rights offices**: HHS Office of Civil Rights and HUD's civil rights office continue to operate independent of the now-revoked EO 14110. Guidance they issued in 2024 may remain operative unless specifically withdrawn.
