# xAI v. Bonta: Ninth Circuit Appeal Advances Constitutional Challenge to California AI Training Data Transparency Law

**Jurisdiction:** California | Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** April 19, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Elon Musk's xAI LLC is pursuing a federal constitutional challenge to California's [AB 2013 Generative AI Training Data Transparency Act](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2013), which requires AI developers to publicly disclose summaries of their training datasets. On March 4–5, 2026, U.S. District Judge Jesus G. Bernal denied xAI's motion for a preliminary injunction in [X.AI LLC v. Bonta, 2:25-cv-12295](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72086154/xai-llc-v-bonta/) (C.D. Cal.), finding xAI had not demonstrated a likelihood of success on any of its four constitutional claims. xAI has since appealed to the Ninth Circuit ([Case No. 26-1591](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca9/26-1591)), where the interlocutory appeal of the injunction denial is pending. The IAPP warns that stakeholders should "expect similar lawsuits against other AI transparency laws," marking this case as a likely national precedent-setter.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **The law at issue:** California [AB 2013](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2013) (effective January 1, 2026) requires developers of generative AI systems available to Californians to publicly post a "high-level summary" covering twelve categories of training data, including data volume, IP status, personal information, and synthetic data use, [per the National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/unmaking-grok-elon-musks-xai-sues-california-attorney-general-over-ai-training).
- **Filing date and counsel:** xAI filed its complaint on December 29, 2025, two days before the law took effect. xAI retained [Clement & Murphy](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2025/12/31/elon-musks-xai-represented-by-clement-murphy-firm-sues-california-law-ab-2013-the-generative-ai-training-data-transparency-act/), the appellate boutique co-founded by former U.S. Solicitor General Paul Clement, signaling aggressive constitutional litigation strategy.
- **Four constitutional counts:** The complaint alleges (1) per se Takings Clause violation (Fifth Amendment); (2) regulatory Takings Clause violation (Fifth Amendment); (3) compelled speech in violation of the First Amendment; and (4) unconstitutional vagueness under the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, [per court docket records at CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72086154/xai-llc-v-bonta/).
- **xAI's core theory:** xAI characterizes AB 2013 as an "unconstitutional trade-secrets-destroying disclosure regime" that compels disclosure of training data composition worth billions in competitive value without compensation, per [the Institute for Law & AI's analysis](https://law-ai.org/xais-challenge-to-californias-ai-training-data-transparency-law-ab2013/).
- **Preliminary injunction denied:** Judge Bernal found xAI's trade secret claims "generalized" and "abstract" — xAI failed to specifically demonstrate that its Grok model's datasets are distinct from competitors' datasets in legally protectable ways. On the First Amendment claim, the court found xAI had not shown the disclosure requirement was likely to violate free speech at the preliminary injunction stage, [per Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/court-upholds-california-ai-transparency-law).
- **AG's defense:** California AG Rob Bonta argued that training dataset composition is not a trade secret warranting constitutional protection, citing *Ruckelshaus v. Monsanto Co.* for the proposition that investment-backed expectations are diminished in areas subject to longstanding public-interest regulation. Bonta further argued AB 2013 requires only an "ingredient list," not the underlying "recipe," [per Goodwin Procter's analysis](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/alerts-otherindustries-xai-challenges-californias-training-data).
- **Ninth Circuit appeal filed:** xAI filed an interlocutory appeal of the preliminary injunction denial in the Ninth Circuit (No. 26-1591), where it was docketed by March 17, 2026, [per Justia docket records](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca9/26-1591).
- **Industry compliance posture:** OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google each published training data disclosures by January 1, 2026, without filing suit. Both OpenAI and Anthropic used high-level categorical descriptions rather than dataset-specific enumeration, establishing market practice that complicates xAI's "disclosure is too burdensome" argument, [per PYMNTS reporting](https://www.pymnts.com/cpi-posts/ai-developers-avoid-details-in-initial-training-data-disclosures-under-california-statute/).
- **Parallel AG enforcement activity:** Separately, AG Bonta issued a cease-and-desist letter to xAI regarding Grok-generated deepfakes in February 2026, suggesting the AG intends to use multiple enforcement vectors against xAI, [per the California Department of Justice press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-sends-cease-and-desist-letter-xai-demands-it-halt-illegal).
- **National precedent signal:** [IAPP analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/xai-v-bonta-a-constitutional-clash-for-training-data-transparency) notes that the constitutional theories raised — particularly on compelled commercial speech and trade secret takings — will apply to analogous training data transparency laws in other states, making Ninth Circuit treatment of the appeal critical for AI governance nationally.

## Action Items

- **Monitor Ninth Circuit Case No. 26-1591** for briefing deadlines and oral argument scheduling. A Ninth Circuit ruling on the preliminary injunction standard will signal whether xAI's First Amendment or Takings theories have traction and could inform enforcement risk assessments for AB 2013 compliance programs.
- **Do not delay AB 2013 compliance** in reliance on xAI's litigation. The preliminary injunction was denied; the law remains fully operative. Developers who have not yet published training data disclosures addressing all twelve statutory categories face UCL enforcement exposure from both the California AG and private plaintiffs.
- **Review disclosure granularity** against emerging market practice: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google adopted categorical descriptions rather than dataset-specific enumeration. Document the rationale for the granularity level chosen in anticipation of a future enforcement inquiry or discovery request in copyright litigation.
- **Coordinate with IP counsel** on AB 2013 disclosures if your organization has asserted or may assert trade secret protections over training data. xAI's Takings Clause theory — while currently unsuccessful at the preliminary injunction stage — may influence how courts assess the scope of disclosure obligations if the case proceeds on the merits.
- **Track federal preemption developments** alongside this case. If Congress enacts AI preemption legislation in 2026, AB 2013 obligations may be displaced, but until then AB 2013 is controlling California law.
- **Assess other state training data transparency bills** in light of xAI's constitutional theories: similar First Amendment compelled speech and vagueness challenges are likely to follow if other states enact comparable requirements. Build this litigation risk into state-by-state regulatory mapping.
