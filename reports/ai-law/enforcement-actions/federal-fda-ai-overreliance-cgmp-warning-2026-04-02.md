# FDA Issues First cGMP Warning Letter Citing AI Overreliance in Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law — Enforcement | **Date:** April 2, 2026

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 2, 2026, the FDA issued a [warning letter to Purolea Cosmetics Lab](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/purolea-cosmetics-lab-722591-04022026) (Livonia, Michigan), making it the first enforcement action in US pharmaceutical regulatory history to cite AI overreliance as a standalone current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) violation. The warning letter followed a facility inspection in October 2025 and contains a dedicated section titled "Inappropriate Use of Artificial Intelligence in Pharmaceutical Manufacturing." The company used AI agents to generate drug product specifications, standard operating procedures, and master production records without subjecting those outputs to quality unit review — a failure the FDA characterized as a violation of 21 CFR 211.22(c). A second critical deficiency arose because the firm failed to conduct process validation prior to distribution, relying on an AI agent that never flagged the requirement; the FDA cited this as a violation of 21 CFR 211.100. The enforcement action signals that the FDA will apply existing cGMP obligations — not new AI-specific rules — to hold manufacturers accountable for unchecked AI use, and that quality unit accountability cannot be delegated to automated systems.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### FDA's Regulatory Framework for Drug Manufacturing

The [Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act), Section 501(a)(2)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2022-title21/pdf/USCODE-2022-title21-chap9-subchapV-partA-sec351.pdf) deems a drug to be adulterated if the methods used in its manufacture, processing, packing, or holding do not conform to or are not operated or administered in conformity with current good manufacturing practice. Regulations implementing this requirement are codified at [21 CFR Parts 210 and 211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211), which collectively establish comprehensive requirements for facilities, quality control, record-keeping, and process validation.

A cornerstone of the cGMP framework is the quality unit (QU): under [21 CFR 211.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-C/section-211.22), the QU is responsible for approving or rejecting all components, drug product containers, closures, in-process materials, packaging materials, labeling, and drug products, and for reviewing production and control records to assure that no errors have occurred. This authority is non-delegable under the existing regulatory structure.

### FDA's Prior AI Guidance and Engagement

Before this enforcement action, the FDA had taken a collaborative, guidance-based approach to AI in manufacturing. In March 2023, the FDA published a discussion paper titled ["Artificial Intelligence in Drug Manufacturing"](https://www.fda.gov/media/165743/download) to solicit stakeholder input and signal its intent to develop a regulatory framework for AI adoption in production operations. That paper received over 800 comments and informed subsequent regulatory activity.

In January 2025, the FDA published a draft guidance, ["Considerations for the Use of Artificial Intelligence to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products"](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/07/2024-31542/considerations-for-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-to-support-regulatory-decision-making-for-drug), laying out a risk-based credibility assessment framework for AI/ML models used to generate data in support of regulatory submissions. In January 2026, the FDA also released its "Guiding Principles of Good AI Practice in Drug Development," reinforcing human oversight as a foundational expectation.

Despite this guidance activity, the Purolea case demonstrates that the FDA was not waiting for AI-specific rules to bring enforcement action — it applied the existing cGMP framework directly.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The Warning Letter: What Purolea Did

[Purolea Cosmetics Lab](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/purolea-cosmetics-lab-722591-04022026) is a Michigan-based contract drug manufacturer. During the October 2025 inspection, the firm disclosed to investigators that it had employed AI agents as its primary tool for generating core compliance documentation: drug product specifications, standard operating procedures, master production records, and related cGMP records. The stated rationale was that the AI would assist the company in meeting FDA requirements.

The problem, as cited in the warning letter, was twofold. First, the firm's quality unit did not review the AI-generated documents to verify accuracy or cGMP compliance before those documents were incorporated into manufacturing operations. Second, when FDA investigators raised the absence of process validation, the company's stated response was that it had not been aware process validation was legally required — because the AI agent had not flagged that requirement.

### Violations Cited

The warning letter cites two primary cGMP violations related to AI use:

**Violation 1 — 21 CFR 211.22(c): Failure of quality unit to review AI-generated documents.** Under this provision, the QU must review production records and approve or reject batches. The FDA's position, as stated in the warning letter and confirmed by [multiple law firm analyses](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-warning-letter-highlights-risks-of-using-ai-in-drug-manufacturing), is that any AI output used in cGMP activities must be reviewed and cleared by an authorized human representative of the QU before being entered into the quality system. Using AI-generated documentation without that review constitutes a failure of QU oversight.

**Violation 2 — 21 CFR 211.100: Failure to conduct process validation.** [21 CFR 211.100](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-211/subpart-F/section-211.100) requires that written procedures for production and process control be designed to assure that the drug products have the identity, strength, quality, and purity they purport to possess. Process validation is a core component of this requirement, and distributing a drug product without completing validation is a fundamental cGMP failure. The company's explanation — ignorance traceable to the AI system's omission — did not constitute a legal defense.

### FDA's Characterization of AI Overreliance

Importantly, the FDA framed the warning letter not as opposition to AI adoption but as an application of existing accountability principles. As [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/fda-warns-firm-for-inappropriate-use-of-ai-in-drug-manufacturing.html) and [Clarskton Consulting](https://clarkstonconsulting.com/insights/fda-warning-letter-for-inappropriate-ai-use/) note, the agency expressly stated that it does not prohibit the use of AI in cGMP activities. What is prohibited is using AI as a replacement for human judgment and quality unit review. The warning letter section dedicated to "Inappropriate Use of Artificial Intelligence" is significant precisely because it treats AI overreliance as a named, standalone cGMP category — not merely a side note to another violation.

[Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/asprescribed/2026/04/fdas-warning-letter-suggests-growing-scrutiny-of-ai-overreliance) characterized the action as signaling that the FDA expects companies to maintain appropriate human oversight and verification processes when relying on AI in regulated activities, and as an early marker of FDA's broader governance expectations for AI in pharmaceutical manufacturing.

### Novel Aspects and Industry Significance

Several aspects of this enforcement action distinguish it from prior FDA warning letters:

1. **First AI-specific cGMP violation category.** Prior warning letters addressed AI use only tangentially (e.g., as part of broader documentation deficiencies). This is the first to create a discrete, labeled violation section for AI misuse, per [Manufacturing Chemist](https://manufacturingchemist.com/fda-issues-first-cgmp-warning-letter-citing-ai) and [ECA Academy / GMP Compliance](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/use-of-ai-agents-leads-to-the-first-fda-warning-letter-relating-to-ai).

2. **Application of existing law, not new rulemaking.** The FDA used FD&C Act Section 501(a)(2)(B) and 21 CFR Parts 210/211 — statutes enacted and regulations finalized decades before AI existed — to reach conduct enabled by modern AI agents. This means all existing cGMP-regulated manufacturers are currently exposed to equivalent enforcement risk, regardless of pending AI-specific guidance.

3. **AI ignorance is not a regulatory defense.** The firm's statement that it did not know process validation was required because "the AI never told it" was rejected outright. Manufacturers bear full legal responsibility for the outputs of AI tools they employ, including any errors, omissions, or gaps in coverage, per [DLA Piper's analysis](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-warning-letter-highlights-risks-of-using-ai-in-drug-manufacturing).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected

Every pharmaceutical drug manufacturer regulated under 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 is directly affected. This includes:

- Domestic and foreign finished pharmaceutical manufacturers
- Contract Development and Manufacturing Organizations (CDMOs)
- Contract testing laboratories
- Generic drug manufacturers
- Over-the-counter drug product manufacturers

As [DLA Piper notes](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/04/fda-warning-letter-highlights-risks-of-using-ai-in-drug-manufacturing), CDMOs and contract testing labs face particular exposure because the FDA treats contractors as extensions of the sponsor manufacturer, meaning AI governance failures at a contract facility translate directly into compliance risk for the brand sponsor. Quality agreements that do not address AI use, permitted applications, human oversight requirements, and audit rights are now considered incomplete.

### Compliance Requirements and Timelines

No new regulations or deadlines are created by this warning letter. The enforcement action applies existing cGMP obligations. However, the practical compliance implications are immediate:

- **Quality unit oversight must extend to AI-generated outputs.** Any AI output used in a cGMP context — specifications, SOPs, batch records, validation protocols — must be reviewed and approved by an authorized QU representative before use.
- **Process knowledge requirements cannot be delegated to AI.** Manufacturers are responsible for ensuring their personnel (or designated QU oversight) understand all applicable regulatory requirements, independent of what any AI tool surfaces.
- **AI governance documentation should be established.** While not yet legally mandated by a specific AI regulation, [ProPharma Group](https://www.propharmagroup.com/thought-leadership/ai-cgmp-fdas-1st-warning-letter-non-compliant-manufacturing) and [Clarkston Consulting](https://clarkstonconsulting.com/insights/fda-warning-letter-for-inappropriate-ai-use/) both recommend that manufacturers formalize AI governance programs covering: permitted AI use cases, prohibited uses, required human review steps, validation/qualification of AI tools, and training.

### Potential Consequences and Enforcement Outlook

A warning letter requires the recipient to respond within 15 business days with corrective actions. Failure to remediate can result in injunction, seizure, or import alert for foreign manufacturers. Purolea has ceased drug production.

Looking forward, this action is likely to be the first of many. The FDA's CDER has [signaled](https://www.raps.org/news-and-articles/news-articles/2026/2/fda-s-cder-agenda-includes-new-guidance-on-digital) that new guidance on AI in manufacturing is on its 2026 agenda. The decision to use the existing cGMP framework — rather than waiting for AI-specific rules — signals that the FDA considers all current AI use in regulated manufacturing to be immediately subject to full compliance scrutiny.

## Action Items

- Review all AI tools currently used in any cGMP context (document generation, specifications, SOPs, batch records, validation protocols) and determine whether quality unit review and approval is documented for every AI output.
- Update standard operating procedures to require explicit QU review and approval of AI-generated outputs before they enter the quality system.
- Audit quality agreements with CDMOs, contract labs, and outsourced manufacturers to add provisions governing AI use, permitted applications, human oversight requirements, and audit rights — provisions the FDA now considers a basic requirement of a complete quality agreement.
- Conduct regulatory knowledge training for all personnel who rely on AI tools in regulated activities; AI ignorance of a requirement is not a defense.
- Monitor FDA's CDER guidance agenda for forthcoming AI-specific guidance, anticipated in 2026, and begin gap analysis against draft guidance once published.
- Consider whether AI tools used in manufacturing have been qualified or validated under existing FDA computer system and software validation expectations (see 21 CFR 211.68).
