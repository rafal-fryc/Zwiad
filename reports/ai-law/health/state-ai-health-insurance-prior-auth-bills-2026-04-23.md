# State Wave: AI Oversight in Health Insurance Prior Authorization — Indiana and Alabama Enact Laws; Seven More States Have Bills Pending

**Jurisdiction:** Indiana, Alabama, California, Connecticut, Illinois, Maryland, Missouri, Oregon, Washington (Multi-state) | **Category:** AI Law — Health | **Date:** 2026-04-23

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Indiana and Alabama have enacted laws in early 2026 restricting health insurers' use of AI in prior authorization and claims adjudication, joining a growing number of states that prohibit AI-only coverage denials and require licensed physician review of adverse determinations. [Indiana HB 1271](https://iga.in.gov/legislative/2026/bills/house/1271) was signed on March 4, 2026 (effective July 1, 2026), and [Alabama SB 63](https://legiscan.com/AL/bill/SB63/2026) was signed on April 17, 2026. Bills with materially similar prohibitions are pending in California, Connecticut, Illinois, Maryland, Missouri, and Oregon. Health insurers and managed care organizations operating in these states must audit their AI-assisted utilization management workflows now, as enforcement obligations in several jurisdictions are either already effective or imminent.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

### Enacted Laws

**Indiana HB 1271 (signed March 4, 2026; effective July 1, 2026)**

- Prohibits insurers from using any automated process, system, or tool — including AI — as the **sole basis to downcode a claim** on medical necessity grounds unless a human employee or contractor has first reviewed the covered individual's medical record. ([Indiana General Assembly — HB 1271 enrolled text](https://iga.in.gov/pdf-documents/124/2026/house/bills/HB1271/HB1271.07.ENRS.pdf))
- Imposes a **parallel obligation on providers**: prohibits use of AI to submit a health benefits claim without review by a provider or other person involved in developing the claim. ([LegiScan — IN HB1271](https://legiscan.com/IN/bill/HB1271/2026))
- Requires insurers to **disclose**, in an easily accessible and readable manner, whenever AI is used to make an adverse determination on a prior authorization request or to downcode a claim. ([National Law Review — State Oversight on AI in Health Insurance Decisions](https://natlawreview.com/article/state-legislatures-consider-oversight-artificial-intelligence-health-insurance))
- Establishes a new chapter of Indiana code (IC 27-1-52) governing the downcoding of health benefits claims. ([Indiana General Assembly — HB 1271 bill page](https://iga.in.gov/legislative/2026/bills/house/1271))
- The substantive provisions take effect **July 1, 2026** — the law was signed March 4, 2026 but obligations are not operative until that date. ([APTA Indiana — HB1271 Announcement](https://inapta.org/2026/03/25/hb1271-announcement/))

**Alabama SB 63 (signed April 17, 2026)**

- Prohibits health insurance companies from using AI **exclusively** to make coverage determinations; a human must make the final decision to deny or reduce coverage. ([Alabama Reflector — What passed in the Alabama Legislature: April 7-9, 2026](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/04/10/what-passed-in-the-alabama-legislature-april-7-9-2026/))
- Requires **prominent written disclosure** to affected parties (both group plan sponsors and individual enrollees) if AI is used at any point during the coverage determination process. ([Governor Ivey's office — SB 63 signing announcement](https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/04/governor-ivey-signs-bills-to-enhance-alabamians-health-and-healthcare-delivery/))
- Establishes **standards for AI training data** used in coverage tools, including data quality and anti-discrimination requirements. ([Engrossed text — Alabama SB 63](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/SB63-eng.pdf))
- Mandates the Alabama Department of Insurance to take **disciplinary action** if an insurer fails to re-evaluate an AI-issued coverage denial or repeatedly fails to disclose AI use. ([Becker's Payer Issues — Alabama bill would restrict insurers' use of AI](https://www.beckerspayer.com/healthcare-information-technology/alabama-bill-would-restrict-insurers-use-of-ai-in-prior-authorization-decisions/))
- Passed the House 102-0 and the Senate concurred 33-0. ([Alabama SB 63 — LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/AL/bill/SB63/2026))

### Enacted in Adjacent Wave (2025–2026)

**California SB 1120 (effective January 1, 2025)** — prohibits health plans from basing medical necessity determinations solely on AI or automated algorithms; requires human clinical review; mandates AI systems be open for audit and based on individual clinical circumstances. ([Simbo AI — SB 1120 analysis](https://www.simbo.ai/blog/exploring-the-impact-of-california-s-sb-1120-and-ab-3030-on-ai-integration-in-healthcare-practices-2785941/))

**Maryland HB 820 (effective October 1, 2025)** — requires AI tools used in utilization review to incorporate medical history, individual circumstances, and clinical information; mandates specialist parity review (final decision by a physician in the same specialty); requires AI systems to be available for state audit. ([HealtheSystems — Maryland Enacts AI Bill for Health Insurance Utilization](https://healthesystems.com/regulatory/maryland-enacts-ai-bill-for-health-insurance-utilization/))

**Washington E2SSB 5395 (signed March 23, 2026; effective June 11, 2026)** — prohibits private insurers from using AI as the **only means to deny, delay, or modify** health care services; AI may be used to approve but not to deny without human clinical review; prohibits over-reliance on group population datasets for individual determinations; requires insurers to report by October 1, 2026 the percentage of total denials aided by AI. Chapter 157, 2026 Laws. ([WSMA — Prior Authorization Reform Bill Signed](https://www.wsma.org/Shared_Content/News/advocacy-report/2026/April%203/wsmas-latest-prior-authorization-reform-bill-signed-into-law); [Washington Legislature — E2SSB 5395 Bill Summary](https://app.leg.wa.gov/billsummary?BillNumber=5395&Year=2025&Initiative=false))

### Pending Bills (as of April 2026) [MEDIUM confidence]

The following states have introduced bills in the 2026 legislative session targeting AI in prior authorization and utilization management. Specific bill numbers and status may have changed after the source publication date.

- **California** — Additional legislation introduced in 2026 to build on SB 1120, targeting insurer disclosure obligations and auditing requirements for AI tools used in downcoding. ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/state-legislatures-consider-oversight-artificial-intelligence-health-insurance))
- **Connecticut** — A separate Connecticut bill targeting AI by health insurers in coverage determinations was introduced by Senator Saud Anwar (a companion to SB 447 from the 2025 session, which did not advance). Connecticut SB 5 — the 2026 AI omnibus that passed the Senate 32-4 on April 21, 2026 — is a broad AI regulatory bill covering frontier AI models, youth social media, synthetic content, and companion chatbots; it does not contain health insurance prior authorization restrictions. The status of a standalone health insurance AI bill for 2026 could not be confirmed at time of writing; practitioners should monitor the Connecticut Insurance and Real Estate Committee. ([CT Mirror — CT lawmakers consider AI regulation, online safety bills](https://www.wshu.org/connecticut-news/2026-03-05/ct-ai-regulation-online-safety-bills); [CT Mirror — Amended AI bill passed by CT Senate](https://ctmirror.org/2026/04/21/artificial-intelligence-regulation-senate-ct/))
- **Illinois** — **Illinois HB 4054** did not receive a full House vote and remained in the Information Management & Technology Committee; it is considered stalled. The active Illinois vehicle is **SB3114, the Transparency in Downcoding Act**, which passed the Senate Insurance Committee on March 11, 2026 and was placed on the Senate floor calendar for third reading on March 24, 2026. However, the current status of SB3114 as of late April 2026 is uncertain: an April 3, 2026 [Transparency Coalition legislative update](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-april3-2026) described the bill as still stalled in Senate Insurance Committee, and [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1959835) showed only 25% progression as of April 17, 2026, with a Rule 2-10 Third Reading Deadline of May 8, 2026 — a deadline that would be superfluous if the bill had already cleared committee and reached the floor. Whether the bill successfully advanced to the full Senate floor as of late April 2026 cannot be confirmed from available sources. SB3114 would ban payers from using automated tools — including AI — to downcode physician claims unless the downcoding decision is made by a licensed physician in the same or similar specialty as the treating physician; it also prohibits targeted or discriminatory downcoding patterns against physicians treating complex or chronic conditions. Practitioners should monitor the [Illinois General Assembly bill status page](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=3114&GAID=18&DocTypeID=SB&SessionID=114&GA=104) for current status. ([LegiScan — Illinois SB3114](https://legiscan.com/IL/bill/SB3114/2025); [TrackBill — Illinois SB3114](https://trackbill.com/bill/illinois-senate-bill-3114-transparency-in-downcoding-act/2804368/); [WANDTV — Downcoding transparency bill heads to Illinois Senate floor](https://www.wandtv.com/news/statehouse/downcoding-transparency-bill-heads-to-il-senate-floor/article_8bd55932-4a53-4cb3-a1fe-b4e622c25f88.html))
- **Maryland** — Follow-on 2026 bill proposed after HB 820's enactment to add additional audit and transparency obligations on AI tools used in utilization review. ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/state-legislatures-consider-oversight-artificial-intelligence-health-insurance))
- **Missouri** — Bill introduced targeting AI-only coverage denials; provisions modeled on prohibition framework used in Indiana and Alabama. ([JDSupra — Sheppard Mullin analysis](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/state-legislatures-consider-oversight-5000321/))
- **Oregon** — Bill introduced to prohibit AI as sole basis for prior authorization denials and require human review. ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/state-legislatures-consider-oversight-artificial-intelligence-health-insurance))

### Federal Context [HIGH confidence]

CMS issued a proposed rule on April 14, 2026 (CMS-0062-P) extending prior authorization API and electronic transparency requirements to prescription drug prior authorizations for Medicare Advantage, Medicaid managed care, CHIP managed care, and QHP issuers on the federally-facilitated exchanges. The proposed rule does not directly restrict AI use in denial decisions, but its transparency and decision-timeline requirements overlay state AI restrictions for insurers operating in regulated programs. Public comment closes June 15, 2026. ([Federal Register — CMS-0062-P](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/14/2026-07205/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-patient-protection-and-affordable-care-act-interoperability-standards))

### Key Policy Patterns Across States [HIGH confidence]

Across all enacted and pending legislation, three core requirements emerge consistently:

1. **Prohibition on AI-only adverse determinations**: No state permits AI to be the sole basis for denying, reducing, or delaying coverage. Every enacted law requires a qualified human professional — in most cases a licensed physician — to review and finalize any adverse determination.

2. **Physician specialty parity**: Several states (Maryland, Washington) require the reviewing physician to have clinical experience in the relevant specialty, not merely any licensed clinician.

3. **Mandatory disclosure**: All enacted laws require insurers to disclose to enrollees or plan sponsors when AI was used in the coverage determination process, with varying specificity as to format and timing.

4. **Data quality and bias standards**: Alabama SB 63 goes further by imposing standards on the training data used in AI tools, incorporating anti-discrimination guardrails directly into the operative statute.

5. **Audit and oversight access**: California SB 1120, Maryland HB 820, and Washington E2SSB 5395 each require AI tools to be open for inspection or audit by state regulators.

## Action Items

- **Immediate inventory**: Map all AI or algorithmic tools used in prior authorization, utilization review, and claims downcoding workflows. Identify the states where each tool is deployed.
- **Indiana compliance (effective July 1, 2026)**: Prepare now — obligations take effect July 1, 2026, not upon the March 4, 2026 signing date. Before that date, ensure no AI tool can generate a final downcode without a documented human review of the individual's medical record, and implement disclosure notices for any AI-assisted adverse determination or downcode.
- **Alabama compliance (effective upon signing, April 17, 2026)**: Update enrollment communications to include prominent written disclosure when AI is used at any point in coverage determination. Audit AI training datasets against Alabama's data quality and anti-discrimination standards. Establish a re-evaluation process for AI-issued denials that triggers before Department of Insurance enforcement.
- **California SB 1120 (effective January 1, 2025)**: Confirm AI utilization review tools are individually based (not population-level algorithms) and subject to audit access. This law is already in force.
- **Maryland HB 820 (effective October 1, 2025)**: Confirm specialty-parity physician review is in place for denied prior authorizations.
- **Washington E2SSB 5395 (effective June 11, 2026)**: Configure reporting systems to track the percentage of denials aided by AI; first report due October 1, 2026. Obligations are operative as of June 11, 2026.
- **Multi-state monitoring**: Track pending bills in Connecticut, Missouri, and Oregon. For Illinois, SB3114 (Transparency in Downcoding Act) advanced to the Senate floor calendar in March 2026 but its current status as of late April 2026 is uncertain — monitor the [Illinois General Assembly bill status page](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=3114&GAID=18&DocTypeID=SB&SessionID=114&GA=104) directly; the Rule 2-10 Third Reading Deadline of May 8, 2026 is a near-term inflection point.
- **Federal overlay**: Monitor CMS-0062-P rulemaking through the June 15, 2026 comment deadline; assess interaction with state AI disclosure obligations on electronic prior authorization for drugs.
- **Governance documentation**: Establish written policies and audit trails documenting human review of every AI-assisted adverse determination, with physician credentials and specialty recorded where required.
