# Former Pastor Sues OpenAI and Sam Altman Over ChatGPT's Medical Advice in Pulmonary Embolism Case

**Jurisdiction:** California (San Francisco County Superior Court; plaintiff resides in Florida) | **Category:** AI Law / Product Liability | **Date:** July 25, 2026

## Action Items

- **Healthcare, life-sciences, and consumer-health-tech companies deploying LLM chatbots** should audit whether their products offer diagnostic-sounding assessments or "personalized recovery plans" that could be characterized as practicing medicine without a license, and confirm disclaimers are prominent and conversation flows escalate (not discourage) users reporting acute or worsening symptoms to seek in-person care.
- **In-house and outside counsel for AI companies operating in California** should monitor this case for early motion practice (e.g., anticipated Section 230 and product-vs-service arguments, and any removal to federal court, as occurred in Florida's parallel OpenAI suit) since resolution of the "product" vs. "service"/speech characterization will affect strict-liability exposure industry-wide. Counsel should also track whether Winters is coordinated with other California product-liability suits against OpenAI given reporting on a possible JCCP-style consolidation proceeding.
- **Compliance teams at OpenAI competitors offering health-adjacent chatbot features** (e.g., integrations with patient portals or wearables) should track whether this suit's injunction request against "ChatGPT Health" succeeds, since a preliminary injunction here could be persuasive authority against similar features elsewhere.
- **Track for updates**: watch for OpenAI's answer/motion to dismiss, any case-management conference date, and whether the case is coordinated with other pending ChatGPT liability suits (mental health, wrongful death) given overlapping counsel (Social Media Victims Law Center, Tech Justice Law).
