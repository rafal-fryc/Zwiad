# FTC and Commerce Miss March 11 EO Deadlines: Federal AI Preemption Strategy Stalls

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-04-29

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Two key March 11, 2026 deadlines established by President Trump's [December 11, 2025 executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) on national AI policy have passed without compliance, according to [Axios reporting](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/trump-missed-ai-deadlines) confirmed by [Silicon Report](https://www.siliconreport.com/trump-administration-pushes-states-on-ai-while-key-federal-deadlines-slip-0210291aa864185a). The Federal Trade Commission did not issue its required policy statement on AI output preemption, and the Department of Commerce did not publish its evaluation of state AI laws or refer any to the DOJ's AI Litigation Task Force. The failures materially undercut the administration's near-term capacity to preempt state AI regulation: without the Commerce evaluation, the DOJ Task Force has no referral list, and without the FTC statement, the administration lacks a preemption theory to take to court.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **March 11, 2026** was the 90-day deadline from the December 11, 2025 executive order ([EO 14365, Federal Register Doc. 2025-23092](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/16/2025-23092/ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence)) for both the FTC and Commerce deliverables.
- The **FTC** was required to issue a policy statement explaining the circumstances under which state laws that require AI models to alter their "truthful outputs" are preempted by the FTC Act's prohibition on unfair or deceptive practices — a novel legal theory with no judicial precedent, per [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102mirs/march-2026-federal-deadlines-that-will-reshape-the-ai-regulatory-landscape) and [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/examining-the-landscape-and-limitations-of-the-federal-push-to-override-state-ai-regulation).
- The **Department of Commerce** was required to publish a comprehensive review of state AI laws, identify "onerous" laws that conflict with federal policy, and refer them to the DOJ's AI Litigation Task Force — thus providing the enforcement pipeline's first trigger, per [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/trump-executive-order-targets-state-ai-laws-raising-questions-and-risks).
- Neither deliverable was completed or publicly announced by the deadline — or in the weeks following it — per [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/trump-missed-ai-deadlines) and [Silicon Report](https://www.siliconreport.com/trump-administration-pushes-states-on-ai-while-key-federal-deadlines-slip-0210291aa864185a).
- Without the Commerce evaluation, the DOJ AI Litigation Task Force — formally established January 9, 2026 — has no official referral list of state laws to challenge, per [Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/unpacking-the-december-11-2025-executive-order).
- The Commerce Department was also required to issue a BEAD broadband funding policy notice by March 11 conditioning non-deployment BEAD funds on states' compliance with the EO's AI policy; that notice has also not been published, per [Benton Institute](https://www.benton.org/blog/trump-executive-orders-shape-federal-ai-regulation-and-override-state-actions).
- White House legislative recommendations titled the [National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf) were released on March 20, 2026 — a separate deliverable from the same EO that was completed on time — but as a non-binding document it imposes no legal obligations on states.
- **State AI laws remain fully operative.** Colorado SB 24-205 takes effect June 30, 2026; Texas RAIGA (TRAIGA) has been effective since January 1, 2026; over 600 state AI bills were introduced in Q1 2026, per [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-04-24-state-ai-laws-where-are-they-now).

## Why the Missed Deadlines Matter [MEDIUM confidence]

The EO's preemption strategy was architecturally dependent on the sequential completion of its agency directives:

1. Commerce publishes list of "onerous" state laws → 2. DOJ Task Force challenges those laws in court → 3. FTC policy statement provides preemption theory for DOJ litigation → 4. FCC considers federal disclosure standard that supersedes conflicting state requirements.

With steps 1 and 3 incomplete, the enforcement cascade cannot begin. [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/examining-the-landscape-and-limitations-of-the-federal-push-to-override-state-ai-regulation) and [Goodwin Procter](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/alerts-otherindustries-trumps-ai-preemption-executive-order) have both noted that the EO itself lacks the force of law to preempt state statutes — only congressional legislation or surviving judicial challenges can establish binding preemption. The missed deadlines compound this legal limitation by removing the near-term administrative mechanisms through which the administration intended to signal and advance its preemption position.

The BEAD funding leverage — potentially conditioning $42 billion in broadband infrastructure funding on state AI law compliance — also requires the unreleased Commerce Policy Notice as its operative trigger. Until that notice issues, states face no concrete funding risk, per [Benton Institute analysis of the BEAD strategy's legal hurdles](https://www.benton.org/headlines/ai-preemption-executive-order%E2%80%99s-bead-strategy-faces-steep-legal-hurdles).

## Action Items

- **Do not reduce state AI compliance investments.** The missed deadlines confirm that federal preemption is not imminent. Organizations building compliance programs for Colorado SB 24-205 (effective June 30, 2026), Texas RAIGA, California's suite of AI statutes, and other enacted state laws should proceed on current timelines.
- **Monitor FTC and Commerce for late deliverables.** The deadlines were missed, not cancelled. Either agency could release its required deliverable at any time; an FTC policy statement on AI output preemption would immediately change the legal landscape for bias-audit and algorithmic-fairness compliance programs.
- **Track DOJ AI Litigation Task Force activity independently.** The Task Force may initiate litigation against specific state AI laws without waiting for a formal Commerce referral if it identifies legally vulnerable targets on its own initiative — particularly laws that the administration has publicly identified, such as Colorado's SB 24-205.
- **Assess BEAD funding dependency.** Organizations receiving or expecting BEAD non-deployment funds should monitor whether Commerce issues a retroactive or delayed Policy Notice conditionality rule. States with enacted AI laws that qualify as "onerous" under the EO's criteria remain at potential funding risk.
- **Track congressional action.** The White House's March 20, 2026 legislative recommendations represent the administration's preferred outcome — broad federal preemption through statute. The GUARDRAILS Act (H.R. 8031 / S. 4216), introduced the same day, represents the opposing congressional response. Neither has advanced, but floor votes would immediately shift compliance calculus.
