# Congress and the Trump Administration Target AI Data Center Energy Demands

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2025-03-13

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

By early 2025, the intersection of AI expansion and US energy infrastructure had become a major policy flashpoint in Washington. The House Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Energy held a hearing on March 5, 2025 titled "Scaling For Growth: Meeting The Demand For Reliable, Affordable Electricity," where expert witnesses attributed historic increases in electricity demand partly to AI data center growth and called for permitting reform. Simultaneously, the Trump administration took sweeping executive action — including declaring a national energy emergency and unveiling the $500 billion Project Stargate — to accelerate energy production and data center construction. The January 2025 emergence of DeepSeek, a Chinese AI model with reportedly lower energy requirements than US counterparts, introduced uncertainty into energy demand projections but did not materially slow either congressional or executive momentum. No comprehensive AI-energy legislation was enacted as of the date of this report; the policy landscape remained split between executive-branch acceleration and fragmented congressional proposals.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

AI data centers are extraordinarily energy-intensive facilities. The International Energy Agency (IEA) has estimated that US data center energy demand will increase by approximately 130% by 2030 compared to 2024 levels. Bloomberg Intelligence projected that AI energy demand could grow up to four times by 2032, even accounting for efficiency gains.

The rapid scaling of large language models and AI inference infrastructure in 2023 and 2024 — driven by companies including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon — accelerated already-rising projections for grid load growth. Utility companies and grid operators began raising alarms about whether the US grid could absorb this demand without reliability risks or ratepayer cost increases.

On January 20, 2025, within hours of his inauguration, President Trump signed an executive order [declaring a "national energy emergency"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/declaring-a-national-energy-emergency/) under the National Emergencies Act — one of a series of energy-related executive orders signed that day. The declaration authorized broad emergency powers to increase domestic energy supply, empowered the Department of Energy to exercise emergency authority over energy production and distribution, and required agency coordination to expedite permitting for projects on federal land. This executive action signaled the administration's intent to treat energy production for AI infrastructure as a national security and economic priority.

On January 21, 2025, President Trump announced at a White House press conference the creation of [Project Stargate](https://openai.com/index/announcing-the-stargate-project/), a joint venture between OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, and investment firm MGX. The venture committed an initial $100 billion and up to $500 billion over four years to build 20 new US data centers, with SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son as chairman. This announcement framed AI infrastructure investment as a pillar of US economic and national security strategy.

On January 23, 2025, President Trump signed [Executive Order 14179](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/31/2025-02172/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence), "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence." The order revoked Biden-era AI policies, directed the development of a 180-day AI action plan focused on human flourishing, economic competitiveness, and national security, and stated a goal of maintaining US global AI dominance.

In January 2025, the Chinese AI company DeepSeek released its R1 model, which demonstrated capabilities comparable to leading US models. DeepSeek-V3, the base model underlying R1, was reportedly trained using only 2,048 NVIDIA H800 GPU chips — far fewer than the estimated 20,000 A100 GPUs (an unverified media estimate) used to train GPT-4. R1 itself was developed via reinforcement learning on top of V3, not trained from scratch at that compute scale. This development created significant market uncertainty (the so-called "DeepSeek shock") about whether massive infrastructure investment was necessary to achieve AI performance gains.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### The March 5, 2025 House Hearing

The [House Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Energy](https://energycommerce.house.gov/events/energy-subcommittee-scaling-for-growth-meeting-the-demand-for-reliable-affordable-electricity) held its hearing "Scaling For Growth: Meeting The Demand For Reliable, Affordable Electricity" on March 5, 2025. Subcommittee Chair Bob Latta (R-OH) opened by attributing "historic increases in electricity demand" to "energy intensive AI models and domestic manufacturing."

Four witnesses testified:

- **Todd Brickhouse**, CEO and General Manager of an electric power cooperative
- **Asim Z. Haque**, Senior Vice President for Governmental and Member Services of PJM Interconnection, a regional transmission organization (RTO)
- **Noel W. Black**, Senior VP of Regulatory Affairs of an electric utility company
- **Tyler H. Norris**, James B. Duke Fellow, Duke University

All four witnesses addressed two cross-cutting themes: (1) the scale of anticipated energy demand growth attributable to data centers and AI, and (2) the inadequacy of current permitting and grid interconnection processes to meet that demand.

Witness Black testified that electricity demand growth is "driven by data centers and artificial intelligence." Witness Norris acknowledged that DeepSeek introduces "uncertainty" about precise energy quantities needed but forecast that "data centers are expected to account for the single largest growth segment" in the energy space, adding "up to 44% of US electricity load growth through 2028." All witnesses called for permitting reform to accelerate interconnection queue processing and infrastructure buildout.

According to analysis from [Mintz / ML Strategies](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2025-03-07-congress-holds-hearing-energy-and-ai-data-centers-ai), the hearing represented an early but significant congressional acknowledgment that AI energy infrastructure is a near-term grid reliability challenge. However, the hearing produced no immediate legislative output and no bills were voted out of committee in connection with the hearing.

### DeepSeek's Effect on the Policy Debate

The [DeepSeek R1 model](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/potential-impacts-of-deepseek-on-datacenters-and-energy-demand) demonstrated that AI model capabilities could be achieved at substantially lower compute — and therefore energy — cost than previously assumed by US AI labs and policymakers. Some analysts initially speculated this would reduce forecasted data center energy demand.

However, most analysts and policymakers concluded the opposite: more efficient AI models tend to lower the cost per AI inference, driving broader adoption (the "Jevons Paradox" effect), which increases aggregate demand. [Brookings Institution analysis](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/why-ai-demand-for-energy-will-continue-to-increase/) concluded that AI energy demand would continue rising despite efficiency gains. [Bloomberg Intelligence](https://enkiai.com/ai-market-intelligence/ai-energy-demand-2026-why-deep-seek-fuels-a-power-surge/) forecast AI energy demand growing 4x by 2032 even factoring in DeepSeek-style efficiency improvements.

Neither party in Congress altered its policy positions in response to DeepSeek. The Trump administration continued pursuing energy expansion and data center permitting acceleration.

### Executive Branch Actions

The Trump administration's executive actions on AI energy infrastructure in the first quarter of 2025 were substantial:

1. **National Energy Emergency (Jan. 20)**: Declared under the National Emergencies Act; authorized DOE emergency authority over energy production; required expedited permitting on federal land. [Official declaration](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/declaring-a-national-energy-emergency/).

2. **Project Stargate Announcement (Jan. 21)**: $500 billion joint venture (OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, MGX); 20 planned US data centers; framed as a US-China competitiveness initiative.

3. **EO 14179 — AI Leadership (Jan. 23)**: Revoked Biden-era AI policies; mandated a 180-day AI action plan; directed rescission of policies conflicting with AI dominance goals. [Federal Register text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/31/2025-02172/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence).

Later in 2025, the administration expanded these efforts through [EO 14318 — Accelerating Federal Permitting of Data Center Infrastructure (July 23, 2025)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/accelerating-federal-permitting-of-data-center-infrastructure/), which defined "Qualifying Projects" as data centers requiring over 100 megawatts of new AI load, directed Commerce to launch financial support (loans, grants, tax incentives), and directed DOI, DOE, and DOD to authorize data center construction on federal lands. Four DOE sites were subsequently announced: Idaho National Laboratory, Oak Ridge Reservation, Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant, and Savannah River Site.

### Congressional Legislation

As of March 2025, no comprehensive AI-energy legislation had passed Congress. Legislative proposals remained fragmented and in early stages:

- **Clean Cloud Act of 2025 (S. 1475)**: Would establish an emissions performance standard and fee system for data centers and cryptocurrency mining facilities under the Clean Air Act, with fees escalating toward zero-carbon compliance by 2035. Annual emissions reporting to DOE and EPA is a component of the bill, but the primary mechanism is the emissions standard and associated fee structure.
- **Securing Reliable Power for Advanced Technologies Act (H.R. 5927)**: Would amend the Defense Production Act to accelerate energy infrastructure for designated "critical AI infrastructure" projects.
- **Data Center Water and Energy Transparency Act** (Sen. Durbin): Would require data centers to publicly disclose energy and water usage.
- **Permitting reform bills**: Multiple bills — the SPEED Act (H.R. 4776), ePermit Act (H.R. 4503), and PERMIT Act (H.R. 3898) — addressed federal permitting streamlining with applicability to energy infrastructure.

As noted by [Mintz analysis](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2025-03-07-congress-holds-hearing-energy-and-ai-data-centers-ai), neither party had proposed a comprehensive AI regulatory or energy roadmap specifically addressing data centers. Existing bills were narrow in scope, with no floor votes scheduled as of the finding date.

Note: The Power for the People Act (S. 3682, introduced by Senators Van Hollen and Booker) was introduced in January 2026 and is therefore outside the scope of this March 2025 legislative snapshot.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Energy and Utility Sector

Electric utilities, grid operators, and rural electric cooperatives face near-term pressure to absorb unprecedented new load from data centers while managing interconnection queues, transmission constraints, and reliability obligations. The Senate Energy and Natural Resources Committee and FERC have both begun engaging with rulemaking and oversight processes related to large-load interconnection, according to [Bipartisan Policy Center analysis](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/explainer/strategic-federal-actions-aim-to-strengthen-ai-and-energy-infrastructure/).

### Technology and AI Companies

Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) and AI-native companies (OpenAI) building large data centers face a permitting and interconnection environment that, while moving toward greater executive facilitation, remains complex and subject to environmental review requirements that the Trump administration sought to reduce through emergency declarations and executive orders. Project Stargate participants (OpenAI, Oracle, SoftBank) face direct engagement with the federal permitting apparatus.

### Ratepayers and Consumer Advocates

Senator Elizabeth Warren and other Senate Democrats opened an investigation into whether big tech data center energy costs are being passed to residential ratepayers, according to [Sen. Warren's office press release](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/senator-warren-lawmakers-open-investigation-into-big-tech-data-centers-role-in-driving-up-families-utility-costs). This created a Democratic legislative counternarrative focused on cost allocation rather than supply expansion.

### Compliance and Regulatory Outlook

No mandatory compliance obligations arose directly from the March 2025 hearing or the executive orders in place as of that date. However, companies planning large-scale data center buildout should monitor:

- FERC large-load interconnection rulemaking (initiated by Secretary of Energy Wright)
- Environmental permitting exemptions under the national energy emergency declaration
- Federal land site solicitations from DOE (Idaho, Oak Ridge, Paducah, Savannah River)
- The Clean Cloud Act and transparency legislation if enacted

## Action Items

- **Data center developers**: Monitor FERC interconnection rulemaking; assess whether planned facilities qualify as "Qualifying Projects" under the administration's framework (100+ MW AI load threshold).
- **Energy/utility companies**: Engage with the interconnection queue reform process; submit comments on any FERC rulemakings related to large-load interconnection.
- **AI companies**: Assess Project Stargate participation or related federal land site solicitations; track DOE site-specific solicitations expected through end of 2025.
- **Compliance teams**: Monitor the Clean Cloud Act (S. 1475) — including its emissions performance standard, fee schedule, and reporting requirements — and the Data Center Water and Energy Transparency Act for progress; these would create new obligations if enacted.
- **Policy and government affairs teams**: Track the SPEED Act (H.R. 4776) and other permitting reform bills moving through the 119th Congress; watch for Senate action following House passage.
- **Ratepayer-focused entities**: Track the Warren investigation findings, which may produce legislative proposals addressing cost allocation for data center grid impacts.
