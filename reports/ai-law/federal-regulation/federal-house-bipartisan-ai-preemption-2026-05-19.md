# US House Bipartisan AI Talks on Preemptive Federal Regulation

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-05-19

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

House members from both parties are engaged in bipartisan negotiations on AI legislation that would establish federal baseline standards for AI safety, transparency, and algorithmic accountability while preempting the growing patchwork of state AI laws. The negotiations follow the White House's [March 2026 National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf) and a wave of competing House bills introduced in April 2026. The key sticking points remain the scope of federal preemption over state AI laws and what enforcement mechanisms will accompany any new federal standards — with Democrats generally opposing broad preemption absent robust federal safeguards.

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence]

- On April 27, 2026, Reps. Ted Lieu (D-CA) and Jay Obernolte (R-CA) introduced the [American Leadership in AI Act (H.R. 8516)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr8516ih), a bipartisan package consolidating over 20 AI legislative proposals drawn from the [2024 Bipartisan House AI Task Force report](https://lieu.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/reps-lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-bipartisan-bill-advance-american), covering standards development, research infrastructure, federal AI governance, worker protections, deepfake deterrence, and AI education — but explicitly not a state preemption vehicle, according to [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-consolidated-ai-bill-package/413134/).
- On September 16, 2025, Rep. Michael Baumgartner (R-WA) introduced [H.R. 5388, the American Artificial Intelligence Leadership and Uniformity Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/5388), which would establish a five-year moratorium on new state and local AI mandates; the bill was referred to the House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology and to its Subcommittee on Oversight and Investigations on December 19, 2025, per [GovTrack](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/hr5388).
- Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) released a discussion draft of the [TRUMP AMERICA AI Act](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/3/technology/blackburn-releases-discussion-draft-of-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence/) in March 2026 — the leading Senate vehicle for comprehensive federal AI regulation with preemption provisions — with [announced growing momentum](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/4/ai/what-they-are-saying-blackburn-announces-growing-momentum-for-trump-america-ai-act) from industry and advocacy groups in April 2026; no House companion bill has been publicly identified.
- A prior attempt at federal preemption failed: a 10-year moratorium on state AI laws passed the House as part of the budget reconciliation bill (29–24 in committee) but [the Senate voted 99–1 to strip the provision](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2025/july/senate-strikes-ai-moratorium) before President Trump signed the bill in July 2025.
- The White House [National Policy Framework](https://rollcall.com/2026/03/20/white-house-ai-framework-calls-for-preemption-of-state-laws/), released March 20, 2026, calls for Congress to preempt state laws regulating AI model development and bar developer liability for third-party misuse — while preserving state authority over consumer protection, child safety, fraud, and procurement.
- Democrats who have pushed back on preemption include Reps. Yvette Clarke (D-NY) and Don Beyer (D-VA) and Sen. Brian Schatz (D-HI); on March 20, 2026 a group of House Democrats introduced the [GUARDRAILS Act (H.R. 8031)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8031/text/ih) to repeal the underlying Trump executive order, per [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/white-house-ai-framework-puts-federal-preemption-at-the-center-of-the-debate).
- As of May 2026, over 600 state AI bills were introduced in Q1 2026 alone, per [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260323-white-house-releases-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence), reinforcing the urgency of the federal preemption debate.

## Action Items

- **Monitor H.R. 8516 closely**: The Lieu-Obernolte American Leadership in AI Act represents the most likely bipartisan vehicle for near-term AI legislation; track committee referral and markup schedule as it advances through the 119th Congress.
- **Assess exposure to state AI law patchwork**: Until Congress acts, existing state AI laws remain fully operative — including Colorado, Texas, Utah, and Connecticut frameworks — requiring continued multi-state compliance monitoring.
- **Engage on preemption scope**: Organizations with significant AI development or deployment operations should engage with the scope of preemption being debated; the critical variable is whether preemption covers only AI model development or extends to deployer and high-risk AI use obligations under state law.
- **Watch the Senate track**: The TRUMP AMERICA AI Act (Blackburn draft) is the primary Senate vehicle; any House companion bill or merged legislative text would be a major signal that floor votes are approaching.
- **Contingency planning for no federal action**: The July 2025 Senate 99–1 vote against a moratorium demonstrates that broad preemption remains difficult to achieve — organizations should maintain state-by-state compliance readiness as the baseline scenario.
