# FTC Seeks Comment on Proposed Policy Statement Addressing AI Output Accuracy Under Section 5

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-07-02

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On July 1, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission issued a [proposed policy statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-trade-commissions-proposed-policy-statement-concerning-suppression-accuracy-artificial) titled "Suppression of Accuracy in Artificial Intelligence Systems," describing how AI companies that deliberately steer their systems' outputs toward undisclosed ideological or other objectives — including to comply with state laws mandating bias mitigation or output alteration — may be committing a deceptive act or practice in violation of [Section 5 of the FTC Act](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems). The FTC is accepting public comments through **July 31, 2026**. The statement was issued pursuant to [Executive Order 14365](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy), "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence," signed by President Trump on December 11, 2025, which directed the FTC to clarify how the FTC Act's deception framework applies to AI models and to address conflicts between federal deception law and state laws requiring alteration of AI outputs.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The FTC published the proposed policy statement on its website on July 1, 2026, and it was entered in the [Federal Register on July 7, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems) (Document No. 2026-13628, 91 Fed. Reg. 41638-41642).
- The statement explains that under the FTC's established three-part deception framework, a representation, omission, or practice is deceptive if it is likely to mislead a reasonable consumer in a materially important way. The FTC's position is that AI companies which intentionally design systems to pursue undisclosed objectives — different from what users reasonably expect the system's output to reflect — may be engaging in deception under [Section 5 of the FTC Act](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-trade-commissions-proposed-policy-statement-concerning-suppression-accuracy-artificial).
- Critically, the FTC states that this deception theory applies "even if the deceptive steering is done in an effort to comply with State laws" — positioning the policy statement as a vehicle to argue that state AI bias-mitigation or content-alteration mandates can conflict with, and be preempted by, federal deception law ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/ftc-proposes-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy-under-state-ai-laws.html); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/consumer-protection/ftc-seeks-comment-on-proposed-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy-and-output-steering/)).
- The policy statement implements a directive in [Executive Order 14365](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13628/policy-statement-concerning-the-suppression-of-accuracy-in-artificial-intelligence-systems) (signed December 11, 2025), which ordered the FTC to issue guidance clarifying how Section 5 applies to AI models and, specifically, how state laws mandating alteration of "truthful" AI outputs may conflict with the FTC Act. Zwiad previously reported that the FTC missed the EO's original March 11, 2026 internal deadline for this statement (see Related Reports).
- Comments must be submitted on or before **Friday, July 31, 2026**, filed online at [regulations.gov, Docket No. FTC-2026-0859](https://www.regulations.gov/docket/FTC-2026-0859), referencing "AI Policy Statement; Matter No. P264200." Paper comments may be mailed to the FTC's Office of the Secretary, 600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Mail Stop H-144 (Annex P), Washington, DC 20580 ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-seeks-public-comment-policy-statement-addressing-ai-accuracy)).
- Law firm analyses (Steptoe, Covington, Spencer Fane, Reed Smith) independently corroborate the core facts: the deception theory targets "output steering" for undisclosed ideological objectives, and the statement is explicitly framed as a potential basis for challenging state AI regulation as conflicting with federal law ([Spencer Fane](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/ftc-proposes-new-policy-on-ai-accuracy-hiding-how-an-ai-system-is-steered-may-violate-federal-law/); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102n7u7/ftc-proposes-policy-prohibiting-deceptive-steering-of-ai-products-and-services/)).

## Action Items

- AI companies whose systems apply undisclosed content moderation, bias mitigation, or "ideological" filtering to outputs should assess whether current practices are adequately disclosed to users, given the FTC's deception theory.
- Companies operating in states with AI bias-mitigation, content-alteration, or "truthful AI" mandates (e.g., laws requiring adjustment of model outputs to correct perceived bias) should evaluate potential tension between state compliance obligations and this federal deception theory, and consider flagging the conflict in comments.
- Interested parties (AI developers, deployers, civil society, state regulators) should submit comments to [Docket No. FTC-2026-0859](https://www.regulations.gov/docket/FTC-2026-0859) by **July 31, 2026** if they wish to influence the final policy statement.
- Monitor for the FTC's final policy statement and any subsequent enforcement actions invoking this theory, as well as related litigation testing federal preemption of state AI-accuracy/bias laws.
