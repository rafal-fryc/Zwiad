# White House Releases National Policy Framework for AI, Urging Congress to Preempt State AI Laws

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-03-20

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On March 20, 2026, the White House released its [National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/), the deliverable required by President Trump's December 11, 2025 executive order "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence." The Framework urges Congress to broadly preempt state AI laws that impose "undue burdens" on AI development, while preserving state authority over generally applicable laws, child protection, state procurement, and zoning. The release has triggered bipartisan pushback, including the [GUARDRAILS Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8031/text/ih) introduced the same day to repeal the underlying executive order, even as states continue enacting AI laws — with over 600 AI bills introduced at the state level in Q1 2026 alone.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The Framework was released March 20, 2026 and is a non-binding set of legislative recommendations, not a rule or self-executing policy, per [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/March/White-House-Releases-National-Policy-Framework-AI) and [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260323-white-house-releases-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence).
- The Framework recommends Congress preempt state laws regulating AI model development and bar states from imposing liability on AI developers for third-party misuse of their systems, according to [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/white-house-national-ai-policy-framework-calls-for-preempting-state-laws-protecting-children) and [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/the-white-house-legislative-recommendations-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-an).
- Preserved state authorities include generally applicable consumer-protection and fraud laws, child-safety laws (including AI-generated CSAM prohibitions), state procurement and public-sector AI use, and zoning — per [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/white-house-releases-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial) and [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/White-House-Releases-National-AI-Policy-Framework-3-24-2026).
- Substantive policy proposals include minor-protection standards, IP clarifications (including copyright and digital-replica issues), free-speech protections against compelled alteration of truthful AI outputs, and worker safeguards, per [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/toward-a-national-ai-policy-the-trump-administration-releases-proposed-framework-for-federal-legislation/).
- The underlying [December 11, 2025 executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) directed DOJ to form an AI Litigation Task Force, directed Commerce to condition BEAD broadband funds on state AI-law alignment, and directed the FTC to issue a preemption policy statement on state laws altering truthful AI outputs, as analyzed by [Sidley Austin](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/unpacking-the-december-11-2025-executive-order) and [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/president-trump-signs-executive-order-challenging-state-ai-laws).
- The GUARDRAILS Act ([H.R. 8031](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8031/text/ih) / [S. 4216](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4216/text/is)) — the "Guaranteeing and Upholding Americans' Right to Decide Responsible AI Laws and Standards" Act — was introduced March 20, 2026 to repeal the executive order and prevent the withholding of federal funds from states that enact AI laws, per [Rep. Beyer's release](https://beyer.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=9009) and [Sen. Schatz's release](https://www.schatz.senate.gov/news/press-releases/schatz-colleagues-introduce-legislation-to-repeal-trumps-ai-moratorium).
- States introduced over 600 AI bills with private-sector requirements in Q1 2026, with continued focus on companion chatbots, digital replicas/deepfakes, and AI transparency; Washington passed HB 1170 (disclosure), HB 2225 (chatbot safety for minors), and SB 5395 (AI in health insurance) in March 2026, per [Inside Global Tech](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/04/06/u-s-tech-legislative-regulatory-update-first-quarter-2026/) and [Transparency Coalition](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-april10-2026).
- Constitutional commentators including [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/12/11/nx-s1-5638562/trump-ai-david-sacks-executive-order) and the [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/president-trumps-ai-national-policy-executive-order-is-an-unambiguous-threat-to-states-beyond-just-ai/) have questioned whether the executive order's preemption and funding-conditioning mechanisms are legally sustainable without congressional action.

## Action Items

- **AI developers and deployers:** Do not rely on the Framework as a safe harbor — it is non-binding and does not alter any state-law compliance obligations currently in force. Continue building compliance programs around enacted state laws (CO AI Act, CA AB 2013/SB 942, NY AI companion rules, TX TRAIGA, WA HB 1170/2225).
- **Multi-state compliance teams:** Track the FTC's forthcoming preemption policy statement on truthful-output alterations (90-day deadline from the December 2025 EO) and the Commerce/BEAD eligibility policy notice, both of which could create immediate operational conflicts with state disclosure and bias-audit regimes.
- **Government-affairs functions:** Monitor the GUARDRAILS Act (H.R. 8031 / S. 4216), the TRUMP AMERICA AI Act (Sen. Blackburn), NO FAKES Act, and GUARD Act for movement — these define the outer edges of the 2026 federal preemption debate.
- **State-regulated entities:** Assume the near-term risk environment is dominated by state law and private litigation, not federal preemption; federal preemption requires congressional action that remains uncertain in timing and scope.
- **Litigation exposure:** Companies named in actions by the DOJ AI Litigation Task Force or affected by BEAD funding conditions should coordinate early with outside counsel on intervention and constitutional challenges.
