# Senate Commerce Committee Advances 10 AI Bills for Full Senate Consideration (July 31, 2024)

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** privacy / ai-law | **Date:** 2024-08-19

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

On July 31, 2024, the [Senate Commerce Committee](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/2024/7/executive-session) held a full-committee executive session and approved ten AI-related bills for consideration by the full Senate. The package, assembled by Chair Maria Cantwell (D-WA) and Ranking Member Ted Cruz (R-TX), represents the most substantial bipartisan AI legislative output by a Senate committee in the 118th Congress. The bills collectively address AI safety standards and testbeds (housed at NIST), a national AI research infrastructure, AI education, small business readiness, content authentication, and a federal ban on non-consensual AI-generated intimate imagery. All ten measures advanced before the Senate's August recess, narrowing the window for floor consideration to the September–December 2024 period ahead of the 119th Congress. Most bills did not pass the full Senate before the end of the 118th Congress, though the Take It Down Act was ultimately enacted in 2025 after reintroduction.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Senate AI Policy Context

The Senate's approach to AI regulation in 2024 proceeded along two tracks. First, a bipartisan "Gang of Four" AI Working Group led by Senate Majority Leader Schumer released a [roadmap for AI policy in May 2024](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/senate-gang-of-four-releases-long-anticipated-ai-whitepaper), calling for $32 billion in annual AI research and development investment and recommending sector-by-sector AI legislation rather than a comprehensive horizontal statute. Second, individual committees developed sector-specific and cross-cutting legislation within their jurisdictions.

The Senate Commerce Committee, which has jurisdiction over NIST, the National Science Foundation (NSF), and consumer protection, emerged as the principal legislative vehicle. Chair Cantwell had been working to assemble a bipartisan package throughout 2024, and the July 31 executive session marked the first full-committee markup on AI in over a year, [according to Akin Gump's analysis](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/key-senate-committees-advance-ai-legislation).

### Companion Activity: Senate Homeland Security Committee

On the same day — July 31, 2024 — the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee separately advanced the [PREPARED for AI Act (S. 4495)](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/key-senate-committees-advance-ai-legislation), which would require federal agencies to assess and address AI risks prior to procurement and deployment. That bill passed 8-3. The parallel committee action underscored bipartisan momentum on AI governance across multiple Senate panels.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### The Ten Bills

The [Senate Commerce Committee](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/2024/7/commerce-committee-passes-bipartisan-bill-to-ensure-u-s-leads-global-ai-innovation/) approved the following ten measures:

---

**1. Future of Artificial Intelligence Innovation Act of 2024 (S. 4178)**
[Congress.gov page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/4178) | Passed by voice vote.

This is the anchor bill of the package, sponsored by Senators Cantwell, Young, Hickenlooper, and Blackburn. It formally codifies the [AI Safety Institute (AISI) at NIST](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/dem/release/commerce-committee-passes-bipartisan-bill-to-ensure-u-s-leads-global-ai-innovation/), which was established administratively by the Biden administration. Key provisions include:
- Directing NIST to develop voluntary guidelines, measurement science, and rigorous testing protocols for advanced AI models
- Creating testbed programs between NIST, NSF, and the Department of Energy (DOE) to evaluate foundation models
- Establishing a Foundation for Standards and Metrology (a nonprofit corporation), funded at $500,000–$1.25 million annually through FY 2027
- Directing the Office of Science and Technology Policy to create prize competitions for AI research challenges
- Requiring the Comptroller General to identify regulatory impediments to AI innovation

---

**2. Creating Resources for Every American to Experiment with Artificial Intelligence Act (CREATE AI Act) (S. 2714)**
[Congress.gov page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/2714/text) | Passed by roll call vote, 19-7.

The CREATE AI Act authorizes the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR), a cloud computing and data infrastructure to democratize access to AI research tools for students, researchers, and small businesses. The NAIRR would be overseen by NSF in coordination with a Steering Subcommittee, with day-to-day operations run by an independent non-governmental entity. Governance funding would be drawn from existing NSF appropriations under the National AI Initiative Act (P.L. 116-283). The CREATE AI Act passed committee with the most contested vote of the session (19-7), reflecting some Republican skepticism about federal research spending.

---

**3. Artificial Intelligence Research, Innovation, and Accountability Act of 2024 (S. 3312)**
[Congress.gov page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/3312) | Passed by voice vote.

This bill would establish a regulatory framework requiring developers and deployers of "high-impact" and "critical-impact" AI systems to:
- Register with and report to the Department of Commerce
- Comply with AI test, evaluation, validation, and verification (TEVV) standards issued by Commerce
- Conduct AI risk management assessments consistent with the NIST AI RMF
- Display transparency disclosures when deploying high-impact AI systems (those affecting housing, employment, credit, education, healthcare, government services, or insurance)

The bill directs NIST to develop content provenance and authenticity standards for online content, and allows the Secretary of Commerce to impose civil penalties for noncompliance.

---

**4. TEST AI Act of 2023 (S. 3162)**
Passed by voice vote.

The Testbeds to Evaluate and Strengthen Trustworthy Artificial Intelligence Act would direct NIST to coordinate with DOE to establish testbeds for testing and evaluation of trustworthy AI systems, improving interagency coordination and advancing AI tools, capabilities, and workforce development.

---

**5. Promoting United States Leadership in Standards Act of 2024 (S. 3849)**
Passed by voice vote.

This bill tasks the NIST Director to brief Congress on opportunities for supporting US industry involvement in the development of international technical standards for artificial intelligence. It authorizes NIST to conduct pilot programs and other activities to promote US participation in AI standards-setting bodies.

---

**6. National Science Foundation Artificial Intelligence Education Act of 2024 (S. 4394)**
Passed by voice vote.

The bill authorizes NSF to award scholarships at the undergraduate and graduate level in AI, quantum computing, and quantum-hybrid fields, including scholarships focused on AI applications in agriculture, education, and advanced manufacturing. It would also create professional development fellowships for the STEM and education workforce.

---

**7. Small Business Artificial Intelligence Training Act of 2024 (S. 4487)**
Passed by voice vote.

Directs the Department of Commerce and Small Business Administration (SBA) to develop and distribute AI training materials specifically targeted at small businesses, addressing practical AI adoption challenges.

---

**8. Validation and Evaluation for Trustworthy (VET) Artificial Intelligence Act (S. 4769)**
Passed by voice vote.

The VET AI Act directs NIST to collaborate with federal agencies, industry, academia, and civil society to develop detailed specifications and guidelines for evaluating and validating the trustworthiness of AI systems.

---

**9. Artificial Intelligence Public Awareness and Education Campaign Act (S. 4596)**
Passed by voice vote.

This bill would fund a public awareness campaign about AI, directed at consumers and the general public, to foster understanding of how AI systems work, their limitations, and their risks.

---

**10. TAKE IT DOWN Act (S. 4569)**
[Congress.gov text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/4569/text) | Passed by voice vote.

The Taking Action on Non-Consensual Explicit Image Sharing and DEEPFAKES Act would criminalize the non-consensual publication of intimate visual depictions — including AI-generated "deepfakes" — and would require online platforms to establish notice-and-removal mechanisms for victims. This bill has the broadest bipartisan and bicameral appeal of the ten measures. (See **Outcome** section below for enacted status.)

---

### Structural Theme

As [DLA Piper analyzed](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/ai-outlook/2024/major-ai-legislation-advances-in-senate-key-points), the ten bills collectively pursue a NIST-centric model of AI governance: rather than establishing a new regulatory agency or imposing direct mandates on private AI developers, the package primarily directs NIST to develop voluntary standards, testbeds, and guidelines, with mandatory requirements confined to the AI Research, Innovation, and Accountability Act's framework for high-impact and critical-impact systems.

### Procedural Context

The executive session was convened by Chair Cantwell and proceeded on a generally bipartisan basis. The most contentious vote was the CREATE AI Act's 19-7 roll call, which [Senator Young praised](https://www.young.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/young-praises-commerce-committee-passage-of-artificial-intelligence-bills/) as reflecting strong bipartisan support despite reservations about spending levels. The [Commerce Committee press release](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/2024/7/commerce-committee-passes-bipartisan-cantwell-moran-bills-to-boost-ai-education-help-small-businesses-leverage-ai-tools) highlighted the education-focused bills as a particularly strong area of bipartisan agreement.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Legislative Prospects at Time of Advance (August 2024)

The timing of the markup — immediately before the Senate's August recess — significantly constrained the likelihood of floor passage in the 118th Congress. As [Mintz's "AI: The Washington Report"](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2024-08-14-senate-commerce-committee-advances-10-ai-bills-ai) noted at the time, Senate Majority Leader Schumer had publicly discussed opportunities for floor consideration in September and December 2024, and provisions from the bills could potentially be incorporated into the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) as amendments.

The narrow post-recess legislative calendar, combined with November 2024 election-year dynamics, meant that standalone floor passage was unlikely for most of the bills. None of the ten bills passed the full Senate as standalone measures during the 118th Congress.

### The Take It Down Act: Legislative Trajectory

The TAKE IT DOWN Act represents the most significant standalone outcome from the July 2024 markup. After the 118th Congress ended, Senators Cruz and Klobuchar reintroduced the measure as S. 146 in the 119th Congress. [The Senate passed S. 146 unanimously](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/rep/release/senate-unanimously-passes-cruz-klobuchar-bill-stopping-ai-revenge-porn-2024-12/), and President Trump signed it into law on approximately May 19, 2025, making it the first federal AI-specific criminal statute targeting intimate imagery deepfakes. Covered platforms have until May 19, 2026 to establish the required notice-and-removal process.

### NIST AI Safety Institute

The Future of Artificial Intelligence Innovation Act's codification of AISI was significant because the Institute was created administratively by the Biden administration and had no statutory basis. Congressional codification would entrench the Institute against executive-branch restructuring. As of 2026, the AISI's status and staffing remain subject to policy developments in the new administration.

### Industry Impact

Organizations deploying high-impact AI systems should track the AI Research, Innovation, and Accountability Act (S. 3312) as a bellwether for the scope of future federal AI regulation. Although the bill did not advance to the Senate floor in the 118th Congress, its framework — mandatory TEVV compliance, risk assessments, and transparency disclosures for AI affecting housing, employment, credit, education, healthcare, and government services — reflects the emerging compliance architecture being contemplated by federal legislators and aligns with parallel state-level requirements already in force in states like Colorado.

## Action Items

- Monitor the 119th Congress for reintroduction of the Future of Artificial Intelligence Innovation Act, CREATE AI Act, and AI Research, Innovation, and Accountability Act, particularly in the context of NDAA amendments.
- Assess whether your organization deploys "high-impact AI systems" as defined in S. 3312 (AI affecting housing, employment, credit, education, healthcare, government services, or insurance) — this definition is likely to resurface in future federal proposals and mirrors existing state law frameworks.
- If your organization operates covered platforms under the TAKE IT DOWN Act (now enacted as law in May 2025), begin preparing notice-and-removal processes for non-consensual intimate imagery before the May 19, 2026 compliance deadline.
- Track NIST AI Safety Institute activities — voluntary guidelines developed by AISI may be adopted as mandatory standards in future legislation and should be incorporated into internal AI governance frameworks proactively.
- Engage with the NAIRR pilot program (already operating prior to the CREATE AI Act's passage) if your organization conducts AI research and could benefit from access to shared computing infrastructure.
