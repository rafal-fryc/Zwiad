# Senate Judiciary Committee Unanimously Advances NO FAKES Act to Protect Voice and Likeness from AI Deepfakes

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** June 18, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On June 18, 2026, the [Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously advanced S. 4591](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/6/senate-judiciary-advances-no-fakes-act-on-unanimous-vote-103007539), the Nurture Originals, Foster Art, and Keep Entertainment Safe (NO FAKES) Act of 2026, by voice vote. The bill creates a new federal intellectual property right giving every individual exclusive control over their voice and visual likeness in AI-generated digital replicas, with platform penalties up to $750,000 per unauthorized embodiment. The bill now awaits a full Senate floor vote, representing the strongest congressional momentum the legislation has achieved since its introduction.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The Senate Judiciary Committee voted unanimously to advance [S. 4591](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4591/text) on June 18, 2026; three Republican senators — Mike Lee (R-UT), Ted Cruz (R-TX), and Eric Schmitt (R-MO) — raised [First Amendment concerns](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/18/ai-deepfakes-bill-advanced-by-senate-judiciary-committee/) without opposing the bill's passage.
- The bill creates a federal right for every person — not just public figures or celebrities — to authorize use of their voice or visual likeness in any AI-generated, highly realistic digital replica, enforceable against individuals and platforms alike. ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/18/ai-deepfakes-bill-advanced-by-senate-judiciary-committee/))
- Platform liability for knowingly hosting or distributing unauthorized replicas ranges from **$5,000 per work** for individuals to **$750,000 per work** for non-compliant online services that willfully fail to remove reported content. ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/6/senate-judiciary-advances-no-fakes-act-on-unanimous-vote-103007539))
- The right survives death and may be transferred to heirs, executors, and estates, but terminates no later than 70 years after the individual's death. ([Congress.gov, S. 4591 text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4591/text))
- The bill establishes a DMCA-style notice-and-takedown framework with a counter-notification procedure — a major revision from earlier drafts that lacked any mechanism for the poster to contest removal. ([Manatt, Phelps & Phillips](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/congress-reintroduces-the-no-fakes-act-what-s-new-in-the-2026-bill))
- Exemptions cover First Amendment-protected speech, news and commentary, parody, and nonprofit libraries and accredited educational institutions acting without commercial advantage. ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/congress-reintroduces-the-no-fakes-act-what-s-new-in-the-2026-bill))
- The bill preempts **future** state digital replica laws but expressly preserves existing state statutes and common law causes of action in place as of January 2, 2025 — meaning Tennessee's ELVIS Act and California's existing right-of-publicity statutes remain in force. ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/senate-judiciary-committee-advances-legislation-to-protect-name))
- Lead Senate sponsors are Marsha Blackburn (R-TN), Chris Coons (D-DE), Thom Tillis (R-NC), and Amy Klobuchar (D-MN); the 2026 House companion is [H.R. 8915](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8915/text/ih), introduced May 20, 2026, by Representatives Maria Salazar (R-FL), Madeleine Dean (D-PA), Nathaniel Moran (R-TX), and Becca Balint (D-VT). (Note: H.R. 2794, the NO FAKES Act of 2025, was the April 2025 predecessor bill — also in the 119th Congress — and is not a companion to the current 2026 legislation.) ([Senator Blackburn press release](https://www.blackburn.senate.gov/2026/5/technology/blackburn-coons-salazar-dean-colleagues-introduce-revised-version-of-no-fakes-act))
- Supporters include [RIAA](https://www.riaa.com/unanimous-senate-action-advances-no-fakes-act-as-broad-consensus-pushes-landmark-voice-likeness-bill-toward-passage/), SAG-AFTRA, MPA, Walt Disney, Universal Music Group, Warner Music Group, Sony Music, Getty Images, OpenAI, Google/YouTube, IBM, and TikTok, among more than 30 organizations.
- Opponents include the Electronic Frontier Foundation, Center for Democracy and Technology, Computer & Communications Industry Association, NetChoice, and the Entertainment Software Association (video game industry), who argue the bill's takedown mechanisms risk suppressing lawful speech. ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/6/senate-judiciary-advances-no-fakes-act-on-unanimous-vote-103007539))

## Action Items

- **For online platforms hosting user-generated content:** Audit existing content moderation workflows for capacity to process NO FAKES-style takedown notices. The bill's $750,000-per-embodiment penalty for willful non-compliance creates severe financial exposure if the bill is enacted without operational readiness.
- **For AI developers building voice or image synthesis products:** Review whether your product could be characterized as "primarily designed" to create unauthorized replicas — the bill's primary-design test is the key threshold for provider liability.
- **For entertainment, media, and talent management organizations:** Assess existing contract language governing voice and likeness rights. License terms between living individuals and licensees will need to address the bill's 10-year renewable cap on license duration.
- **For legal and compliance teams:** Map your organization's existing exposure under state right-of-publicity statutes (California, Tennessee ELVIS Act, New York, Texas, Illinois) — the NO FAKES Act will layer a federal cause of action on top of these, not replace them.
- **For estate and trust practitioners:** Evaluate existing talent estate agreements. The bill's 70-year post-mortem right and the ability of heirs and estates to license are new considerations that may require updating existing testamentary or trust instruments.
- **Monitor Senate floor scheduling:** No floor vote has been scheduled as of June 28, 2026. Track the bill's progress at [GovTrack S. 4591](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s4591).
