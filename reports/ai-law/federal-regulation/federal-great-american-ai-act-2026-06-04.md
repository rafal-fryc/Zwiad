# Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026: First Comprehensive Federal AI Governance Framework Proposed

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** June 4, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a 269-page bipartisan discussion draft of the [Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026 (GAAIA)](https://obernolte.house.gov/media/press-releases/obernolte-trahan-release-discussion-draft-great-american-ai-act) on June 4, 2026 — the first attempt to create a comprehensive federal AI governance framework in the United States. The draft imposes binding compliance obligations on large frontier AI developers (those with over $500 million in annual revenue), establishes an independent audit regime, and would preempt all state laws specifically regulating AI model *development* for three years. The draft is open for public comment before formal introduction; feedback may be submitted to GAAIA@mail.house.gov.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Bipartisan sponsorship:** Lead sponsors are Rep. Obernolte (R-CA) and Rep. Trahan (D-MA), with additional support from Reps. Scott Franklin (R-FL), Suhas Subramanyam (D-VA), Erin Houchin (R-IN), and Scott Peters (D-CA), per the [official House press release](https://obernolte.house.gov/media/press-releases/obernolte-trahan-release-discussion-draft-great-american-ai-act).

- **Discussion draft stage only:** As of June 15, 2026, the bill has not been formally introduced in Congress. Sponsors released the draft specifically to solicit public feedback before introduction, per [FedScoop coverage](https://fedscoop.com/bipartisan-great-american-ai-act-draft-proposes-new-federal-ai-governance-framework/).

- **Four-title structure:** The draft organizes federal AI governance under four titles: (I) Frontier AI Governance; (II) Workforce; (III) Cybersecurity; and (IV) Research, Development, and International Cooperation, per [DLA Piper's analysis](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/06/unpacking-the-great-american-ai-act).

- **"Large frontier developer" threshold:** Title I places binding obligations exclusively on companies with over $500 million in annual gross revenue that have trained a frontier AI model — targeting the largest AI labs while exempting smaller developers and deployers, per [McDonald Hopkins analysis](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-great-american-ai-act-what-businesses-need-to-know).

- **Frontier AI Framework requirement:** Covered developers must publish a detailed safety plan (a "frontier AI framework") documenting how they identify and manage the most severe risks their models could pose, reviewed at least annually with material modifications disclosed within 30 days, per [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/06/unpacking-the-great-american-ai-act).

- **Transparency reporting:** Frontier developers must publish transparency reports covering release dates, supported languages, output modalities, intended uses, restrictions, risk assessments, third-party involvement, and mitigation steps, per [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-great-american-ai-act-what-businesses-need-to-know).

- **Independent Verification Organizations (IVOs):** The Center for Artificial Intelligence Standards and Innovation (CAISI) director would license IVOs that large frontier developers must retain to perform ongoing audits, evaluations, and testing — embedding compliance oversight directly in frontier labs, per [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/congress-proposes-first-comprehensive-federal-ai-framework).

- **Critical safety incident reporting:** Covered developers must report "critical safety incidents" to the CAISI director, per [FPF analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/frontier-ai-goes-federal-how-the-great-american-ai-act-compares-to-state-laws/).

- **Whistleblower protections:** The bill prohibits retaliation against employees or independent contractors who report AI violations (defined broadly as violations of federal law related to AI development, deployment, or operation). Remedies include reinstatement, double back pay with interest, compensatory damages, and attorney's fees, per [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/congress-proposes-first-comprehensive-federal-ai-framework).

- **State preemption (three-year):** No state or political subdivision may establish, continue in effect, or enforce any law or regulation *specifically regulating the development* of any AI model during a three-year window. Laws regulating AI *use* or *deployment* within a state's borders are explicitly preserved, per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/04/bipartisan-ai-draft-proposes-three-year-preemption-of-state-laws/).

- **CAISI authorization:** The bill formally authorizes the Center for AI Standards and Innovation within the Commerce Department, with $100 million appropriated per fiscal year from 2027 through 2029 for voluntary AI standards and guidelines, per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317903/20260606/federal-ai-regulation-bill-freezes-state-consumer-protections-three-years-sparks-revolt.htm).

- **Cybersecurity title:** Title III would reauthorize the Cybersecurity Act of 2015 through 2035 and authorize CISA (in consultation with CAISI) to award grants to maintainers of critical open-source software to enhance cybersecurity, per [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/06/unpacking-the-great-american-ai-act).

- **Workforce title:** Title II directs NIST and the National Science Foundation to establish grants and prizes for AI education and workforce development, and requires the Census Bureau and Bureau of Labor Statistics to revise federal surveys to capture AI adoption data, per [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/what-hr-needs-to-know-about-great-american-ai-act-of-2026).

## Stakeholder Reactions [HIGH confidence]

**Industry support:** The Business Software Alliance (BSA) and the Information Technology Industry Council (ITIC) expressed support for the draft, per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/04/bipartisan-ai-draft-proposes-three-year-preemption-of-state-laws/).

**Civil society opposition:** The preemption provision drew immediate and sharp criticism:

- [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/news/obernolte-trahan-bill-strips-states-authority-to-protect-consumers-workers-and-children/) stated: "This is a disastrous proposal that Big Tech is celebrating. This bill strips states of their authority to respond to real harms consumers are experiencing." Public Knowledge and the AFL-CIO also opposed the bill.

- Brad Carson, president of Americans for Responsible Innovation, described the preemption provision as "a generational mistake," arguing the bill "takes the current floor on state AI legislation and turns it into a federal ceiling, preventing state lawmakers from addressing emerging AI harms," per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317903/20260606/federal-ai-regulation-bill-freezes-state-consumer-protections-three-years-sparks-revolt.htm).

**Scope debate:** The [FPF analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/frontier-ai-goes-federal-how-the-great-american-ai-act-compares-to-state-laws/) notes a meaningful distinction: preemption applies only to laws regulating AI *development*, not AI *use* — meaning state consumer protection laws, civil rights laws, and sector-specific AI deployment rules would survive the three-year freeze.

## Action Items

- **Large frontier AI developers (>$500M revenue):** Begin gap analysis now against the draft's frontier AI framework, transparency reporting, and IVO audit requirements — even though formal introduction has not yet occurred. The compliance architecture described in the draft is detailed enough to guide internal readiness planning.

- **All organizations deploying AI:** Track the preemption provision closely. If enacted, state AI development regulations would be frozen for three years but state AI *use* laws (e.g., automated decision-making disclosure requirements) would remain in effect.

- **State-regulated industries:** Do not assume federal preemption will eliminate state compliance obligations. The bill explicitly carves out state laws on AI use and deployment, meaning laws like Colorado SB 189 (disclosure-based ADMT framework) and Connecticut SB 5 (employment AI) would continue to apply.

- **Submit public comment:** The sponsors are soliciting stakeholder feedback before formal introduction. Send comments to GAAIA@mail.house.gov. The comment window's end date has not been publicly announced — monitor the [Obernolte House website](https://obernolte.house.gov/media/press-releases/obernolte-trahan-release-discussion-draft-great-american-ai-act) for updates.

- **Monitor for formal introduction:** Track congress.gov for the bill's HR number assignment, committee referral, and hearing schedule. The House Science, Space, and Technology Committee and House Energy and Commerce Committee are likely venues given the bill's scope.

- **HR and employment teams:** Review Title II workforce provisions. The Census Bureau and BLS survey revisions could affect how companies report AI adoption; NIST workforce development programs may create relevant training obligations or opportunities, per [SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/what-hr-needs-to-know-about-great-american-ai-act-of-2026).
