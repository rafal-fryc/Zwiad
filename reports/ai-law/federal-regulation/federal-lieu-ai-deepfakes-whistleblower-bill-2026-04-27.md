# Lieu and Obernolte Introduce Bipartisan American Leadership in AI Act: Deepfake Penalties, Whistleblower Protections, and AI Standards

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** April 27, 2026

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

Reps. Ted Lieu (D-Calif.) and Jay Obernolte (R-Calif.) — former Co-Chair and Chair, respectively, of the Bipartisan House Task Force on Artificial Intelligence — introduced the **American Leadership in AI Act** on April 27, 2026, a sweeping omnibus package consolidating over 20 prior bipartisan legislative proposals. The bill targets deepfake distribution with strengthened criminal penalties and enhanced civil remedies, protects industry whistleblowers who disclose AI safety risks or violations, mandates U.S. participation in international AI standards bodies, and establishes a prize competition for AI research and development. The legislation represents the most comprehensive bipartisan federal AI bill introduced in the 119th Congress and directly builds on the December 2024 House AI Task Force final report.

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence]

- **Bill name:** The American Leadership in AI Act, introduced jointly by [Rep. Ted Lieu (D-Calif.) and Rep. Jay Obernolte (R-Calif.)](https://lieu.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/reps-lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-bipartisan-bill-advance-american) on April 27, 2026. The specific H.R. bill number had not been assigned on Congress.gov at time of writing; official text could not be retrieved from congress.gov. The expected URL for manual verification is: `https://www.congress.gov/search?q=%22American+Leadership+in+AI+Act%22&congress=119`.

- **Six legislative titles:** According to [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-consolidated-ai-bill-package/413134/) and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/27/ai-legislation-deepfakes-whistleblowers.html), the bill is organized into six titles: (1) strengthening AI standards, testing, and evaluations; (2) building research infrastructure and spurring R&D; (3) modernizing federal AI governance, procurement, and security; (4) protecting workers and empowering small businesses; (5) safeguarding Americans and deterring harmful deepfakes; and (6) expanding AI education, literacy, and inclusion.

- **Deepfake provisions (Title V):** The bill enhances legal remedies for deepfake victims and increases criminal penalties for AI-enabled fraud and impersonation, going beyond the 2025 TAKE IT DOWN Act by expanding the scope of covered harms to include fraud and impersonation, not just nonconsensual intimate imagery, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/27/ai-legislation-deepfakes-whistleblowers.html).

- **Whistleblower protections:** The bill incorporates and expands provisions similar to the standalone [H.R. 3460, AI Whistleblower Protection Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/3460) (introduced by Obernolte and Lieu in May 2025), which prohibits employment discrimination against employees or contractors who report AI security vulnerabilities or violations of federal AI law, according to [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/3460/text/ih?format=txt).

- **International AI standards mandate:** The bill requires U.S. participation in international organizations that develop technical standards for AI — a provision that links to the [Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI)](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/obernolte-plans-to-introduce-bill-to-permanently-establish-caisi/) at the Department of Commerce, according to [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-consolidated-ai-bill-package/413134/).

- **AI research prize competition:** The bill establishes a prize competition for groundbreaking AI research and development, consolidated from a prior legislative proposal. The specific source bill for this provision could not be independently confirmed at time of writing; the attribution to [H.R. 2385, the CREATE AI Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2385), which primarily focused on establishing the National AI Research Resource (NAIRR), has not been verified in that bill's text. The prize competition provision is consistent with grand challenge mechanisms included in other bills consolidated into this package, such as the Future of AI Innovation Act. This section should be treated as LOW confidence pending review of the official American Leadership in AI Act text when assigned an H.R. number.

- **Consolidation of 20+ prior proposals:** Lieu described the bill as "rooted in bipartisan legislation that colleagues have proposed, as well as the recommendations from the bipartisan House AI Task Force," per [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-consolidated-ai-bill-package/413134/).

- **Obernolte's separate forthcoming package:** Obernolte endorsed the Lieu bill but is simultaneously preparing his own separate AI legislative package — the ["Great American AI Act"](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/obernolte-plans-to-introduce-bill-to-permanently-establish-caisi/) — which would formally codify CAISI at the Department of Commerce. He expects to release it later in 2026, according to [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-consolidated-ai-bill-package/413134/).

## Relationship to the TAKE IT DOWN Act [HIGH confidence]

The **TAKE IT DOWN Act** (S. 146, 119th Congress) was signed into law by President Trump on May 19, 2025. It criminalizes the nonconsensual publication of intimate images and AI-generated "digital forgeries" (deepfakes), imposes a mandatory 48-hour takedown obligation on covered online platforms, and authorizes FTC enforcement. Criminal penalties are tiered: up to two years imprisonment for offenses involving adults and up to three years for offenses involving minors. Platforms had until May 19, 2026 to comply with notice-and-removal obligations, per [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146) and [Skadden analysis](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/06/take-it-down-act).

The American Leadership in AI Act's Title V **expands beyond** the TAKE IT DOWN Act in at least two dimensions: (1) it addresses AI-enabled **fraud and impersonation** — not only nonconsensual intimate imagery — providing new criminal and civil remedies for a wider category of AI-generated harms; and (2) it pairs those expansions with affirmative whistleblower protections for industry insiders who expose AI safety violations, creating a reporting pathway that the TAKE IT DOWN Act does not include. The two statutes, if enacted together, would form layered coverage — the TAKE IT DOWN Act governing platform takedown obligations for intimate images and the American Leadership in AI Act adding penalties for broader AI-enabled deception and an industry accountability mechanism.

## Political Context [MEDIUM confidence]

The bill has genuine bipartisan backing. Obernolte (Republican) served as Chair and Lieu (Democrat) served as Co-Chair of the House Bipartisan AI Task Force, whose [December 2024 final report](https://obernolte.house.gov/AITFReport) generated the policy recommendations this bill implements. The legislation consolidates over 20 prior bills from members of both parties, according to [Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/lieu-and-obernolte-introduce-consolidated-ai-bill-package/413134/).

The bill's introduction comes amid an active federal AI legislative environment in 2026. Key parallel developments include:
- The [White House National AI Legislative Framework](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/03/president-donald-j-trump-unveils-national-ai-legislative-framework/) (released March 2026), which urges Congress to preempt state AI laws.
- Obernolte's own forthcoming Great American AI Act, which would codify CAISI — signaling that the American Leadership in AI Act is one of multiple complementary packages being advanced simultaneously.
- The May 2026 compliance deadline for the TAKE IT DOWN Act's platform takedown obligations, making this a high-attention moment for AI content regulation.

Despite the bipartisan sponsorship, the bill faces a crowded legislative calendar. The 119th Congress has seen numerous AI bills introduced without progressing to floor votes, and the omnibus nature of the American Leadership in AI Act — spanning six distinct policy areas — may face committee jurisdiction challenges across the House Judiciary, Commerce, Science, and Education Committees.

## Action Items

- **Technology and AI companies:** Monitor H.R. bill number assignment on [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/) for the American Leadership in AI Act; review Title V provisions when legislative text becomes available to assess exposure under expanded deepfake fraud and impersonation liability.

- **HR and compliance teams:** The standalone AI Whistleblower Protection Act (H.R. 3460) is already in committee. If consolidated into the American Leadership in AI Act, it would prohibit retaliation against employees who report AI security vulnerabilities or violations of federal AI law — begin reviewing internal AI incident reporting policies now.

- **Platform operators:** The TAKE IT DOWN Act's notice-and-removal compliance deadline is May 19, 2026. Ensure 48-hour takedown processes are operational for intimate image removal requests; the new Lieu-Obernolte bill would add further obligations for AI-enabled fraud and impersonation content if enacted.

- **Policy and government affairs teams:** Track the parallel development of Obernolte's Great American AI Act (CAISI codification) and the White House national AI framework — the combined trajectory of these three tracks will define the federal AI regulatory landscape through 2026-2027.
