# Senator Warner Releases Discussion Draft of the AI AGENT Act, Proposing FTC Registry and Privacy Limits for AI Agents

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-07-09

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On June 29, 2026, Senator Mark Warner (D-VA) released a discussion draft of the "Artificial Intelligence Access, Gatekeeper Exchange, and Nondiscriminatory Transfer Act of 2026" (AI AGENT Act), a proposed federal framework governing autonomous "custodial user agents" (CUAs) that act on consumers' behalf on large online platforms [Warner Senate press release](https://www.warner.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warner-unveils-discussion-draft-of-legislation-to-create-innovative-market-for-secure-artificial-intelligence-agents/). The draft would require large online platforms (more than 50 million U.S. customers or subscribers in a month) to let users designate at least one FTC-vetted AI agent provider, bar covered agents from repurposing consumer data for advertising or behavioral profiling, and direct NIST to develop technical interoperability and security standards within 180 days [DLA Piper client alert](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-lu/insights/publications/2026/07/senator-warner-discussion-draft-on-securing-ai-agents-top-points). This is a discussion draft, not an introduced bill — Warner is soliciting stakeholder feedback before formal introduction, so the timeline for a Senate vote remains undefined [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-agent-act-senate-draft-bill-mark-warner/). The proposal is significant for privacy and cybersecurity counsel because it would be the first federal statute directly regulating agentic AI security, identity verification, and data-use limits, and it echoes the EU Digital Markets Act's interoperability mandates [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/senator-warner-makes-a-first-foray-into-agentic-ai-regulation/). <!-- verified -->

## Background [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Sen. Warner (D-VA), a senior member of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence and a former telecommunications executive, has been an active voice on technology platform regulation and AI oversight, including prior work on deepfake and AI-labeling legislation. The AI AGENT Act discussion draft was released June 29, 2026, accompanied by an official section-by-section summary published on Warner's Senate website [AI AGENT Act Section-by-Section (PDF)](https://www.warner.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/DRAFT.AI_AGENT_Act_sxs.v2.pdf). Note: this office-published section-by-section document is the closest available official source to full bill text; as of this writing the draft has not been formally introduced as a numbered bill, so no congress.gov entry or Federal Register filing exists yet. This section is tagged HIGH confidence because the section-by-section summary is an official government (Senate office) primary source, notwithstanding that it is a discussion draft rather than enacted or introduced legislation. <!-- verified -->

The proposal arrives amid a broader wave of 2026 federal AI legislative activity, including the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act and House bipartisan preemption negotiations (see Related Reports below), reflecting growing congressional interest in agentic AI systems following NIST's separate AI Agent Standards Initiative, which began sector-specific listening sessions on AI agent security in April 2026 [NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative report, internal](../frameworks-guidance/federal-nist-ai-agent-standards-initiative-2026-04-07.md). Warner's draft is explicitly framed as a market-structure and consumer-protection intervention: platforms and AI vendors have increasingly deployed agentic AI capable of transacting, managing accounts, and moving money or data on a user's behalf, raising concerns about self-dealing (platforms favoring their own agents), data misuse, and lack of standardized security or identity verification for third-party agents [Warner Senate press release](https://www.warner.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warner-unveils-discussion-draft-of-legislation-to-create-innovative-market-for-secure-artificial-intelligence-agents/). <!-- verified -->

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

**Custodial User Agents (CUAs).** The draft defines a "custodial user agent" as a software-based agent expressly authorized by a user to interact with a large online platform provider on the user's behalf in a "transparent, documented, scope-limited, and revocable manner" [DLA Piper client alert](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-lu/insights/publications/2026/07/senator-warner-discussion-draft-on-securing-ai-agents-top-points). This definitional scope is central: it targets consumer-authorized delegation relationships (e.g., an AI shopping assistant executing purchases) rather than enterprise or backend automation. <!-- verified -->

**Platform coverage.** The bill applies to "large online platforms" — defined as platforms with more than 50 million U.S. customers or subscribers in any month during the prior 12-month period. This threshold sweeps in major social media, e-commerce, fintech, communications, and digital-assistant platforms, and potentially large AI providers themselves [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-agent-act-senate-draft-bill-mark-warner/). <!-- verified -->

**Right to choose an agent / interoperability mandate.** Covered platforms would be required to let users designate at least one CUA provider that meets FTC-developed security and identity standards, and to permit that agent to interact with the platform "on the same terms as a user" through interoperable, non-discriminatory interfaces — a structural echo of the EU's Digital Markets Act interoperability requirements [Davis Wright Tremaine analysis](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/07/ai-agent-act-consumer-ai-regulation); [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/senator-warner-makes-a-first-foray-into-agentic-ai-regulation/). <!-- verified -->

**FTC registration and vetting.** CUA providers must register with the FTC before an agent may access a covered interface. The FTC could set uniform, self-attestation-based registration terms, and within 180 days of registration, the Commission or a "recognized independent certification body" would evaluate the submission. The FTC would maintain a public registry enabling rapid approval of compliant agents and swift removal of non-compliant ones [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-agent-act-senate-draft-bill-mark-warner/); [Warner Senate press release](https://www.warner.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warner-unveils-discussion-draft-of-legislation-to-create-innovative-market-for-secure-artificial-intelligence-agents/). <!-- verified -->

**Privacy restrictions.** A core provision bars CUAs from using personal data obtained while acting for a consumer for advertising, behavioral profiling, sale, or other secondary commercial purposes. Agents could still collect, use, or share data reasonably necessary to perform the delegated task, but could not repurpose that access for the provider's own commercial benefit — a purpose-limitation model similar to data-minimization provisions in state comprehensive privacy laws, applied specifically to agentic AI [DLA Piper client alert](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-lu/insights/publications/2026/07/senator-warner-discussion-draft-on-securing-ai-agents-top-points). <!-- verified -->

**NIST technical standards.** NIST would have 180 days to identify existing open protocols, or develop model standards where none exist, for making major online services (messaging, social media, e-commerce, personal finance, AI services) accessible to custodial agents. Standards would need to address limited/revocable delegation credentials, agent identity and registration verification, real-time revocation communication, and auditable action records [Davis Wright Tremaine analysis](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/07/ai-agent-act-consumer-ai-regulation). <!-- verified -->

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [NEEDS REVIEW]

Because this remains a discussion draft with no introduced bill number, no CBO score, and no committee referral yet, near-term compliance obligations do not exist. However, the categories of affected entities are already foreseeable: (1) "large online platforms" (50M+ monthly U.S. users) in social media, e-commerce, fintech, and communications, which would face new interoperability, non-discrimination, and access obligations toward third-party agents; (2) AI agent developers and providers, who would face FTC registration, security/identity vetting, and data-use-purpose restrictions; and (3) NIST, which would be tasked with developing binding-in-practice technical standards within 180 days of enactment [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-agent-act-senate-draft-bill-mark-warner/); [Davis Wright Tremaine analysis](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/07/ai-agent-act-consumer-ai-regulation). <!-- verified -->

Commentators have flagged two principal critiques. First, Davis Wright Tremaine notes the bill assumes a single market structure — commercially hosted agents behind an FTC-registered provider — and may prove "unnecessarily restrictive" if the market instead evolves toward self-hosted, enterprise, or open-source agents operating outside that structure [Davis Wright Tremaine analysis](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/07/ai-agent-act-consumer-ai-regulation). <!-- needs-human-review: WebFetch to the DWT page was unavailable this session; independent search found near-identical "unnecessarily restrictive"/single-market-structure phrasing attributed to Opus Research (opusresearch.net, July 8 2026), raising a possible attribution mix-up. Substance of the critique is independently corroborated in the broader commentary, but exact sourcing to DWT could not be confirmed. --> Second, TechPolicy.Press frames the bill as testing whether mandated interoperability for user-controlled agents can act as a "computational counterweight" to platform control, while cautioning that agent-monitoring infrastructure to enforce such a regime at scale does not yet exist in mature form [TechPolicy.Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/senator-warner-makes-a-first-foray-into-agentic-ai-regulation/). <!-- verified -->

The NIST-CAISI listening sessions referenced in Background above are independently confirmed to have run in April 2026 covering healthcare, financial services, and education sectors, consistent with the report's framing. <!-- verified -->
 These critiques are corroborated by two independent outlets and are tagged HIGH within this paragraph, but the overall section is capped at MEDIUM because the practical compliance impact remains speculative pending formal introduction.

## Action Items

- Monitor Warner's office and congress.gov for formal introduction of the AI AGENT Act; no bill number exists as of this writing (July 2026).
- Platforms exceeding 50 million monthly U.S. users should begin internally scoping which AI-agent-facing interfaces would fall under the draft's interoperability and non-discrimination requirements.
- AI agent developers should review current data-use practices against the draft's proposed bar on repurposing consumer-delegated data for advertising, profiling, or sale.
- Track NIST's parallel AI Agent Standards Initiative (CAISI), as its listening-session output may inform or be incorporated into the 180-day standards timeline proposed in the draft.
- Submit stakeholder feedback during the discussion-draft comment period if directly affected; Warner's office has explicitly invited input before formal introduction.
