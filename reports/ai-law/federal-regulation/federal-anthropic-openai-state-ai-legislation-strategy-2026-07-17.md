# Anthropic and OpenAI Diverge on State AI Legislation Strategy: "Ratchet" vs. "Reverse Federalism"

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-07-17

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [DISPUTED]

Politico reported on July 15-17, 2026 that Anthropic is deliberately encouraging U.S. states to keep legislating on frontier AI safety, pursuing what the outlet characterized as a strategy of "one-upmanship" designed to raise the regulatory bar for AI developers state by state, according to [The Hill's summary of the Politico reporting](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5918733-openai-anthropic-ai-regulation/), [WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/07/17/anthropic-challenges-openais-state-ai-rules-strategy-xcxwbn/), and [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/anthropic-pushes-state-by-state-ratchet-on-ai-safety-rules). Anthropic's head of U.S. state and local government relations, Cesar Fernandez, told Politico the company is "not just looking to support the same bill across the country in every single state," but is instead "looking for legislation that meaningfully raises the bar on safety for the most capable AI systems." <!-- verified: exact quote confirmed via independent search cross-referencing Politico-sourced reporting --> This directly contrasts with OpenAI's publicly stated "reverse federalism" approach — articulated by Chief Global Affairs Officer Chris Lehane and described in [OpenAI's own blog post](https://openai.com/index/advancing-ai-safety-through-state-and-federal-action/) — under which OpenAI encourages states to converge on a common, industry-workable template that could harden into a de facto national standard before Congress acts. <!-- verified: OpenAI blog and independent reporting confirm "reverse federalism" framing and Lehane's authorship --> The split follows both companies' endorsements of California's SB 53, New York's RAISE Act, and Illinois's SB 315, and comes as Anthropic has also escalated political spending, doubling its contribution to the pro-regulation group Public First Action to $40 million as of July 22, 2026, per [multiple outlets](https://www.aol.com/articles/anthropic-donate-20-million-us-120431817.html) reporting the donation. <!-- disputed: independent sources (Axios, PYMNTS, AOL) describe the $20M donation/total-$40M announcement as occurring "Tuesday, July 21, 2026," not July 22; amount and cumulative total ($40M) are confirmed correct, only the specific date is one day off --> The finding's original source, an IAPP U.S. Privacy Digest tracking link, and the underlying Politico article itself could not be directly retrieved for this report (see Research Limitations below); this report relies on secondary reporting that corroborates the underlying facts across multiple independent outlets.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence] [DISPUTED]

The 2026 debate over AI regulation in the United States has bifurcated along two axes: (1) whether Congress should preempt state AI laws, and (2) if state laws remain in force, whether frontier AI developers should try to harmonize them into a lenient common baseline or push individual states to adopt progressively stronger safeguards. This finding concerns the second axis and follows a wave of state frontier-AI legislation in 2025-2026: California's SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, signed September 29, 2025, effective January 1, 2026 — see this outlet's prior report on [SB 53 compliance obligations](reports/ai-law/california/california-sb53-compliance-timeline-2026-2027-2026-07-17.md)); New York's RAISE Act (S6953B/A6453B, signed by Governor Hochul December 19, 2025, per [NYSenate.gov](https://www.nysenate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/andrew-gounardes/landmark-ai-safety-bill-signed-law) and [the Governor's own announcement](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-signs-nation-leading-legislation-require-ai-frameworks-ai-frontier-models)); and Illinois's SB 315 (Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, signed by Governor Pritzker in early July 2026 — see this outlet's prior report on [Illinois SB 315](reports/ai-law/frontier-models/illinois-sb315-ai-safety-measures-act-2026-06-01.md)), which became the first state to mandate independent third-party audits of frontier AI developers' safety practices. <!-- verified: SB 53 sign/effective dates, RAISE Act signing date, and Illinois SB 315 signing date/first-in-nation audit mandate all independently confirmed against official state records and law-firm alerts -->

Anthropic was the only major frontier AI lab to publicly endorse SB 53 while it was pending, stating in its [own endorsement post](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/1965027311717388673) that the bill "provides a strong foundation to govern powerful AI systems built by frontier AI companies like ours" through transparency rather than "technical micromanagement," even while noting Anthropic's stated preference that frontier AI safety ultimately be addressed at the federal level rather than through a state-by-state patchwork, per [TechCrunch's contemporaneous reporting](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/anthropic-endorses-californias-ai-safety-bill-sb-53/). Both Anthropic and OpenAI also endorsed the Illinois bill before its passage in May 2026, according to [reporting on the Illinois AI Safety Measures Act](https://www.governing.com/artificial-intelligence/illinois-sets-a-new-standard-for-ai-oversight). <!-- verified: Anthropic's "only major lab" SB 53 endorsement and the Illinois House's May 27, 2026 passage date independently confirmed -->

Separately, at the federal level, the Trump administration's [National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](reports/ai-law/federal-regulation/federal-national-policy-framework-ai-preemption-2026-04-14.md) (released March 20, 2026, following a December 11, 2025 executive order) has urged Congress to broadly preempt state AI laws, and bipartisan House negotiations on a federal baseline (see [prior report](reports/ai-law/federal-regulation/federal-house-bipartisan-ai-preemption-2026-05-19.md)) remain unresolved. <!-- verified: December 11, 2025 EO date confirmed; House preemption talks (GAAIA discussion draft, June 2026) remain unresolved/not yet formally passed as of review date, consistent with report's characterization --> Anthropic has been notably vocal in opposing broad federal preemption of state AI law absent a strong substitute federal standard, a position that helps explain why the company would prefer states to keep legislating rather than wait for Congress. <!-- disputed: this sentence has no inline source citation; independently confirmed as accurate via search (Anthropic has publicly called on Congress not to override state AI laws absent a "rigorous" federal framework), but the claim should carry a citation per report sourcing standards -->

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence] [DISPUTED]

According to the Politico reporting relayed by [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5918733-openai-anthropic-ai-regulation/) and [WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/07/17/anthropic-challenges-openais-state-ai-rules-strategy-xcxwbn/), Anthropic's strategy has two components: first, endorsing state bills that go further than the prior state's law (e.g., Illinois's third-party audit mandate, which exceeds the disclosure-only requirements of California's SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act); and second, backing political candidates and groups aligned with stronger AI-safety regulation. On the latter point, Fernandez told Politico that Anthropic "back[s] candidates for election and re-election when their point of view of AI safety regulation is aligned with our mission," and that the company is "very much supporting candidates where there's ideological alignment," per the same secondary reporting. <!-- verified: both Fernandez quotes confirmed verbatim via independent search of Politico-sourced coverage -->

This posture is corroborated by Anthropic's campaign-finance activity: the company [donated an additional $20 million](https://www.aol.com/articles/anthropic-donate-20-million-us-120431817.html) to Public First Action on July 22, 2026, bringing its total commitment to that group to $40 million for the 2026 midterm cycle, according to [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/anthropic-doubles-ai-policy-donation-to-40m-for-2026-midterms) and other outlets covering the donation. <!-- disputed: independent sources (Axios, PYMNTS, Anthropic's own newsroom post) report the donation as announced "Tuesday, July 21, 2026," not July 22 -- dollar amounts ($20M/$40M total) are correct --> Public First Action was founded by two former members of Congress specifically to counter Leading the Future, a rival AI-industry-backed political group associated with OpenAI President Greg Brockman and investor Marc Andreessen that has amassed a reported $125 million war chest, per the same reporting. <!-- verified: Leading the Future's ~$125M 2025 fundraising and Brockman/Andreessen involvement independently confirmed (CNBC) --> This spending pattern is consistent with — though distinct from — the state-legislative "ratchet" strategy described in the Politico article; it is included here as corroborating context on Anthropic's broader political posture toward AI regulation, not as direct evidence of the state-bill strategy itself.

By contrast, OpenAI has framed its own approach as "reverse federalism" in an [official OpenAI blog post](https://openai.com/index/advancing-ai-safety-through-state-and-federal-action/), under the theory that if enough states converge on substantially similar — and industry-workable — AI safety rules, those rules can become a template that Congress adopts as a uniform national standard, obviating the need for a patchwork. Chris Lehane, OpenAI's Chief Global Affairs Officer, has been publicly identified as the architect of this approach, per [The Hill](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5918733-openai-anthropic-ai-regulation/) and [additional coverage of OpenAI's political strategy](https://www.tun.com/home/openai-pushes-reverse-federalism-to-build-us-ai-safety-rules/). Notably, OpenAI has separately and simultaneously lobbied the White House for relief from state AI rules and supported federal preemption efforts, an apparent tension between its "reverse federalism" messaging and its preemption advocacy that several outlets have flagged without fully reconciling. <!-- verified: OpenAI's White House lobbying for relief from state AI rules independently confirmed via search -->

Both companies' involvement in Illinois's SB 315 illustrates the limits of the divide: despite their differing long-term strategies, Anthropic and OpenAI both endorsed the Illinois bill before passage, per [Skadden's client alert](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/07/illinois-enacts-ai-safety-law-becoming-first-state) and [Crowell & Moring's analysis](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/illinois-imposes-transparency-and-safety-obligations-on-frontier-ai-systems), suggesting the rivalry is less about opposing any given bill and more about each company's preferred end-state for the overall regulatory architecture. <!-- verified: both companies' pre-passage endorsement of Illinois SB 315 independently confirmed -->

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

For frontier AI developers, the practical effect of Anthropic's strategy is that state AI safety requirements are likely to continue ratcheting upward rather than converging on a lenient common baseline, at least in states where Anthropic is active politically (e.g., California, New York, Illinois, and reportedly Massachusetts, where pending rules may require independent evaluation of whether advanced AI systems could assist catastrophic harms such as bioweapon development, per [WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/07/17/anthropic-challenges-openais-state-ai-rules-strategy-xcxwbn/)). Compliance and government-affairs teams at AI companies operating in multiple states should not assume that having satisfied California's SB 53 or New York's RAISE Act obligations will be sufficient going forward — Illinois's third-party audit mandate already exceeds those requirements, and further states may follow. <!-- verified: Massachusetts bioweapon/catastrophic-harm evaluation detail confirmed via independent search corroborating WinBuzzer's reporting -->

For policy and legislative-affairs teams tracking the federal preemption debate, this development is a signal that the two most prominent frontier labs are not aligned on preemption strategy despite sharing surface-level support for some of the same state bills — complicating any assumption that "industry" speaks with one voice on state AI law. This matters directly for how Congress and the White House calibrate any federal AI framework, since a unified industry ask for preemption is less politically available than it might otherwise be.

For political-spending and campaign-finance observers, the escalating war chests on both sides (Anthropic/Public First Action at $40 million vs. the OpenAI-linked Leading the Future network at a reported $125 million) indicate that the 2026 midterms are the first federal election cycle in which frontier AI companies are treating state and federal AI regulatory outcomes as material to their business, per [TVNewsCheck's coverage of the donation increase](https://tvnewscheck.com/ai/article/anthropic-doubles-midterm-spending-to-40m-to-push-ai-regulation/). <!-- verified: $40M vs. reported $125M comparison independently corroborated -->

## Action Items

- **Multi-state compliance and government-affairs teams:** Do not treat compliance with California SB 53, New York's RAISE Act, or Illinois SB 315 as a ceiling; monitor for additional "ratchet" bills in states where Anthropic is politically active, and track pending Massachusetts frontier-AI safety rules specifically.
- **Legislative-affairs and public-policy teams:** Reassess any planning premised on a unified industry position favoring federal preemption — Anthropic's public opposition to broad preemption without a strong federal substitute is a live consideration in the ongoing House and Senate preemption negotiations.
