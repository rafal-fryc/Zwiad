# White House and Senate Negotiate Federal AI Preemption Package Bundled with KOSA and NO FAKES Act

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-06-09

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

The White House is actively negotiating with Senate leadership to advance a federal AI governance package that would preempt state-level AI laws in exchange for child online safety legislation. Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) is leading Senate-side talks, with the proposed deal pairing a three-year block on conflicting state AI regulations with passage of the Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA, [S. 1748](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text)), the NO FAKES Act, and a federal age verification mandate. The bundle represents the most significant attempt to establish a federal AI preemption floor since the failed NDAA moratorium proposal in late 2025, and it raises immediate compliance implications for organizations operating under state AI frameworks that could be displaced.

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence]

- The White House is in active negotiations with Senate leaders to revive federal AI preemption, reported by [Axios on June 8, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/08/white-house-hill-relaunch-effort-block-state-ai-laws); Sen. Blackburn's office confirmed she is "spearheading the negotiation with the White House to finalize legislative text."
- The proposed package would block conflicting state AI regulation for three years, according to [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/white-house-senate-revive-push-to-block-state-ai-laws-through-kids-safety-deal) and [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/white-house-ai-preemption-kosa-no-fakes-deal).
- Preemption would be subject-matter based — not a blanket prohibition — meaning only state laws covering the same topics addressed by the federal bill would be displaced, per Blackburn's office as reported by [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/white-house-senate-revive-push-to-block-state-ai-laws-through-kids-safety-deal).
- The online safety component includes: (1) the Senate version of KOSA ([S. 1748](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text)), which imposes a duty of care on platforms to prevent exposure to harms (including eating disorders, suicidal behavior, substance use), with FTC enforcement; (2) the [NO FAKES Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Fakes_Act), reintroduced May 20, 2026, creating federal rights over AI-generated voice and likeness replicas with platform liability up to $750,000 per work; and (3) a federal age verification mandate for online services.
- The NO FAKES Act faces a Senate Judiciary Committee vote on June 18, 2026, per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318346/20260613/no-fakes-act-heads-senate-vote-june-18-putting-750k-platform-liability-line.htm).
- The White House's March 2026 National Policy Framework for AI explicitly identified federal preemption as a central pillar, calling for a "minimally burdensome national standard" to supplant state laws imposing "inconsistent or undue burdens," per [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/white-house-ai-framework-puts-federal-preemption-at-the-center-of-the-debate) and [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/the-white-house-legislative-recommendations-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-an).
- California Attorney General Rob Bonta formally opposed this and earlier preemption efforts, calling any broad preemption "seriously undermining the federalist system," per [CA AG press release](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-rejects-ai-preemption-ndaa).
- A comparable earlier preemption attempt — inserting a broad state AI moratorium into the FY2026 NDAA — was rejected by bipartisan Senate opposition; this current package uses the child safety bills as political cover to build a broader coalition, per [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/white-house-senate-revive-push-to-block-state-ai-laws-through-kids-safety-deal).
- The separately introduced TRUMP AMERICA AI Act (Sen. Blackburn) would also preempt state AI laws but only on covered subject matter (frontier AI catastrophic risk management, digital replicas), while establishing mandatory duty of care obligations and FTC rulemaking authority, per [Jones Walker / National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/trump-america-ai-act-federal-preemption-meets-comprehensive-regulation).
- Significant Democratic opposition and the thin GOP House majority remain the primary obstacles, per [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/white-house-releases-long-awaited-artificial-intelligence-framework-setting-the-stage-for-federal-preemption-debate-and-further-legislative-action).

## Legislative Background [MEDIUM confidence]

This negotiation is the latest in a sustained White House and congressional effort to establish federal supremacy over AI regulation. Key predecessors:

- **December 11, 2025 Executive Order**: Directed the DOJ to establish an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws deemed inconsistent with federal policy; framed state laws as "cumbersome," "onerous," and "excessive."
- **March 20, 2026 National Policy Framework for AI**: Released White House legislative recommendations across seven pillars — child safety, AI infrastructure, intellectual property, free speech, innovation, workforce development, and preemption of state AI laws. The framework explicitly calls on Congress to preempt state AI laws that "impose inconsistent or undue burdens," per [multiple law firm analyses](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/white-house-national-ai-policy-framework-calls-for-preempting-state-laws-protecting-children).
- **FY2026 NDAA attempt**: A prior White House proposal to include a broad state AI moratorium in the defense spending bill was rejected in bipartisan fashion by both HASC Chair Rogers (R-AL) and SASC Ranking Member Reed (D-RI).

KOSA ([S. 1748](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text)) was introduced May 14, 2025 by Sen. Blackburn, joined by Sens. Blumenthal, Thune, and Schumer. The Senate version imposes a duty of care requiring platforms to act in the best interests of minors and restricts algorithmic recommendations, addictive design features, and harmful content categories. The House version differs substantially — focusing on parental controls rather than a duty of care — creating a potential conference-stage problem. Official bill text is available at [congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text).

## Stakeholder Positions [MEDIUM confidence]

**In support:**
- White House / Trump administration: Views preemption as necessary to prevent a "patchwork" of state laws from stifling AI innovation and US global competitiveness.
- Sen. Blackburn: Leading Senate negotiations; sees the combined package as politically viable by coupling industry-favorable preemption with child safety provisions that appeal across the aisle.
- Technology industry: Federal preemption is a top legislative priority; the bundle reduces the compliance cost of maintaining 50-state AI compliance programs.

**In opposition:**
- Privacy and civil liberties advocates: Concerned that subject-matter preemption could displace stronger state-level consumer protections already enacted in California, Colorado, and other states.
- California AG Rob Bonta: Has formally opposed all federal preemption proposals, arguing they undermine federalism; expects states to challenge preemption in court, per [CA AG](https://www.oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-rejects-ai-preemption-ndaa).
- KOSA critics: Civil liberties groups argue KOSA's duty of care could incentivize overbroad content moderation; the House and Senate versions remain substantially misaligned.

## Action Items

- **Compliance teams**: Audit current state AI compliance programs (especially California, Colorado, Texas) to identify which requirements would be displaced by subject-matter-based federal preemption; prioritize those most likely to be covered by the AI, digital replica, or child safety provisions in the package.
- **Legal counsel**: Monitor the NO FAKES Act Senate Judiciary Committee vote on June 18, 2026 as a leading indicator of whether the broader package has enough momentum to move.
- **Regulatory affairs**: Track any official legislative text released by Sen. Blackburn's office or the White House — none has been publicly released as of June 15, 2026; monitor congress.gov and the IAPP Daily Dashboard for bill introduction.
- **Risk assessment**: Prepare contingency positions for two scenarios: (a) federal package passes with subject-matter preemption, requiring a pivot to federal compliance baseline and a rollback of state-specific programs; (b) package stalls (as previous attempts have), meaning state law compliance obligations remain in full force.
- **Child safety**: If your platform serves minors or collects data from users under 18, begin gap analysis against KOSA's duty of care requirements and the age verification mandate regardless of preemption outcome — these provisions have bipartisan support and are likely to advance in some form.
