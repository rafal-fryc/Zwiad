# Trump Executive Order Establishes National AI Policy Framework Preempting State Laws

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On December 11, 2025, President Trump signed an executive order titled ["Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/), carrying the operative heading "Eliminating State Law Obstruction of National Artificial Intelligence Policy." The order declares it U.S. policy to pursue "global AI dominance through a minimally burdensome national policy framework" and directs a sequenced set of federal agency actions to challenge, preempt, or financially disincentivize state AI laws inconsistent with that policy, per the [Paul Hastings client alert](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/president-trump-signs-executive-order-challenging-state-ai-laws) and [Mayer Brown analysis](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/president-trump-issues-executive-order-on-ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence).

## Key Directives [HIGH confidence]

### DOJ AI Litigation Task Force
The Attorney General is required to establish an AI Litigation Task Force whose sole mission is to challenge state AI laws inconsistent with the policy on grounds such as unconstitutional regulation of interstate commerce, federal preemption, or other unlawfulness. Attorney General Pam Bondi formally established the [DOJ AI Litigation Task Force on January 9, 2026](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/unpacking-the-december-11-2025-executive-order).

### BEAD Broadband Funding Conditions
Within 90 days of the order, the Secretary of Commerce must issue a Policy Notice specifying conditions under which states are eligible for remaining Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment (BEAD) Program funding, and making states with "onerous" AI laws ineligible for non-deployment funds "to the maximum extent allowed by Federal law," per [Sidley Austin's analysis](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/12/23/unpacking-the-december-11-2025-executive-order-ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence/).

### FTC Policy Statement on Bias Mitigation
The FTC is directed to issue a policy statement by March 11, 2026 explaining when state laws requiring alterations to the "truthful outputs of AI models" are preempted by the FTC Act. The operative theory is that state requirements compelling developers to adjust AI outputs for bias mitigation could force "deceptive" outputs under federal law, as detailed in the [Baker Botts analysis](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102mirs/march-2026-federal-deadlines-that-will-reshape-the-ai-regulatory-landscape).

### FCC AI Reporting and Disclosure Standard
The order also launches an FCC proceeding to consider a federal reporting and disclosure standard for AI models, per [Wiley's client alert](https://www.wiley.law/alert-White-House-Issues-Executive-Order-to-Promote-National-AI-Policy-Framework-and-Challenge-Certain-State-AI-Laws).

## Context [HIGH confidence]

The EO arrives against the backdrop of more than 1,000 AI-related bills introduced across U.S. states and territories in 2025, as documented in the [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/president-trump-issues-executive-order-on-ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence) and [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/12/23/unpacking-the-december-11-2025-executive-order-ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence/) analyses. High-profile state laws potentially in the crosshairs include Colorado's AI Act, California's suite of 2024-2025 AI statutes, and New York City's algorithmic bias audit rule.

## Legal Limits and Outlook [MEDIUM confidence]

Commentators at [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/examining-the-landscape-and-limitations-of-the-federal-push-to-override-state-ai-regulation) note substantial legal limits on the EO's reach: executive orders cannot unilaterally preempt state law, and any DOJ suits or FTC policy statements must still survive judicial review. Preemption theories under the FTC Act — particularly that state bias-mitigation laws are per se deceptive — are novel and untested. The [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/president-trumps-ai-national-policy-executive-order-is-an-unambiguous-threat-to-states-beyond-just-ai/) has characterized the order as an "unambiguous threat to states beyond just AI," highlighting the political controversy.

## Action Items

- Track the FTC's March 11, 2026 policy statement on state bias-mitigation preemption and assess impact on any ongoing bias-audit or algorithmic-fairness compliance work.
- Monitor DOJ AI Litigation Task Force filings for which states and laws are targeted first (Colorado's AI Act is the most-cited candidate).
- For clients receiving or expecting BEAD funding, review state AI statutes against the forthcoming Commerce Department Policy Notice.
- Reassess multi-state AI compliance programs for areas where federal preemption may create safe harbors — but maintain state-law compliance until preemption is judicially confirmed.
