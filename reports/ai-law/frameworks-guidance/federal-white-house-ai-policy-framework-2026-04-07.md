# White House Releases National Policy Framework for AI, Urges Congress to Preempt State AI Laws

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On March 20, 2026, the White House released a [National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf), providing legislative recommendations to Congress pursuant to President Trump's [December 11, 2025 Executive Order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/). <!-- verified --> The Framework calls for broad federal preemption of state AI laws imposing "undue burdens" on developers and general-purpose AI systems, while preserving state authority over child safety, fraud prevention, and consumer protection. <!-- verified --> The Framework is non-binding and does not itself alter existing legal obligations, but it represents the clearest statement yet of the administration's preferred architecture for federal AI legislation. <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- The Framework was issued pursuant to President Trump's December 11, 2025 Executive Order titled "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence," which directed the development of legislative recommendations and established a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws deemed inconsistent with federal policy ([White House Fact Sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/12/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-ensures-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence/)). <!-- verified -->

- The Framework recommends Congress preempt state laws that regulate AI model development or impose liability on AI developers for unlawful conduct by third parties using their systems, targeting laws that create "undue burdens" on innovation ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/03/the-white-house-legislative-recommendations-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-an)). <!-- verified -->

- States would retain authority to enforce generally applicable laws protecting children (including prohibition of AI-generated CSAM), preventing fraud, and safeguarding consumers -- the Framework explicitly does not preempt these traditional police powers ([Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/white-house-national-ai-policy-framework-calls-for-preempting-state-laws-protecting-children)). <!-- verified -->

- The Framework proposes targeted federal standards for child safety, unauthorized digital replicas, and workforce development, but does not recommend creating a new federal AI regulatory agency ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-25-white-house-releases-ai-regulatory-blueprint-what-the-national-policy-framework-means-for-companies)). <!-- verified: no new agency confirmed independently; specific three-part list (child safety, digital replicas, workforce) unverifiable: source unavailable -->

- On copyright and AI training data, the Framework defers to courts rather than urging Congress to legislate, declining to take a definitive position on fair use ([National Law Review / Proskauer](https://natlawreview.com/article/white-house-ai-framework-stakes-out-national-policy-position-developers-and)). <!-- verified -->

- Multiple state AI laws took effect on January 1, 2026 (including Texas's Responsible AI Governance Act), and Colorado's AI Act (SB 24-205) is set for June 30, 2026. <!-- verified --> Congress has so far declined to enact comprehensive federal preemption, including rejecting such provisions in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act ([King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/new-state-ai-laws-are-effective-on-january-1-2026-but-a-new-executive-order-signals-disruption); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/ai-enforcement-accelerates-as-federal-policy-stalls-and-states-step-in)). <!-- disputed: The One Big Beautiful Bill Act contained a moratorium on state AI enforcement (not a preemption provision per se); the Senate voted 99-1 to strip it. Characterizing it as rejecting 'preemption provisions' is slightly imprecise. -->

## Action Items

- **Monitor congressional action:** Track whether Congress introduces legislation based on the Framework's recommendations. Until legislation is enacted, existing state AI laws remain in full effect and enforceable.
- **Maintain state compliance programs:** Organizations subject to Colorado SB 24-205 (effective June 30, 2026), Texas RAIGA, and other state AI laws should continue compliance preparations. Federal preemption is not assured and may not materialize.
- **Assess preemption exposure:** Identify which internal AI compliance obligations derive from state laws that the Framework targets for preemption (e.g., laws regulating AI model development or imposing developer liability for third-party misuse) to prepare for potential federal legislative action.
- **Track DOJ AI Litigation Task Force activity:** The December 2025 EO established a DOJ task force to challenge state AI laws. Monitor for enforcement actions or legal challenges that could affect state law validity before any legislation passes.
