# Federal AI Procurement Framework: M-25-21, M-25-22, M-26-04, and Unverified May 2026 Signals

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-05-18

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Three confirmed OMB memoranda establish the current federal AI procurement and governance framework. [M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf) and [M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf), both issued April 3, 2025, established the "high-impact AI" governance regime requiring pre-deployment testing, risk mitigation plans, and ongoing monitoring; M-25-21's 365-day compliance deadline for high-impact AI documentation expired approximately April 3, 2026, while M-25-22 procurement terms applied to new solicitations issued on or after October 1, 2025. [M-26-04](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf) (December 11, 2025) implemented [Executive Order 14319](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-14319-preventing-woke-ai-the-federal-government) "Preventing Woke AI in the Federal Government," requiring LLM vendors to certify that their models meet "truth-seeking" and "ideological neutrality" standards, with contract termination authority for non-compliance.

**UNVERIFIED SIGNAL — for situational awareness only:** IAPP reported in May 2026 that the White House was preparing or had issued additional OSTP/OMB guidance requiring AI safety evaluations prior to procurement of high-risk AI systems. This specific guidance memo could not be independently verified through official government sources, law firm alerts, or contemporaneous news reporting at time of writing. The balance of this report covers the confirmed framework. The IAPP signal is discussed separately under its own heading.

## Confirmed Framework: Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- [M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf), issued April 3, 2025, requires agencies deploying "high-impact AI" — AI whose output serves as a principal basis for decisions with legal, material, or significant effect on civil rights, safety, or access to services — to complete pre-deployment testing, prepare risk mitigation plans, conduct impact assessments, and ensure ongoing monitoring, per [PilieroMazza's analysis](https://www.pilieromazza.com/omb-issues-memoranda-on-use-and-acquisition-of-ai-by-federal-agencies-part-1-what-it-means-for-government-contractors/). The 365-day compliance deadline for high-impact AI documentation under M-25-21 ran from April 3, 2025 and expired approximately April 3, 2026. Agency compliance plans filed with OMB (e.g., [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/2025-hhs-ai-compliance-plan.pdf), [GSA](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/government-it-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/ai-guidance-and-resources/ai-compliance-plan)) confirm this timeline.
- [M-25-22](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf), also issued April 3, 2025, directs agencies to test AI solutions prior to contract award and maintain ongoing testing and monitoring rights post-award, per [Wiley's analysis](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Trump-Administration-Revamps-Guidance-on-Federal-Use-and-Procurement-of-AI). The 180-day figure associated with M-25-22 is a contract applicability trigger: new solicitations issued on or after October 1, 2025 (180 days from issuance) must include M-25-22's AI contract terms. It is not a general agency compliance deadline.
- [M-26-04](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf) (December 11, 2025) implemented EO 14319 "Preventing Woke AI in the Federal Government." Its primary purpose — per [Crowell & Moring's analysis](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/in-bid-to-ban-woke-ai-white-house-imposes-transparency-requirements-on-contractors) — is to prohibit federal procurement of LLMs that embed ideological bias. Vendors must certify that their LLMs satisfy two "Unbiased AI Principles": (1) **truth-seeking** (prioritizing historical accuracy, scientific objectivity, and acknowledging uncertainty) and (2) **ideological neutrality** (not encoding partisan or ideological judgments absent explicit user prompting). Agencies may terminate contracts for default where vendors refuse corrective action. All agencies were required to revise LLM procurement policies consistent with M-26-04 by March 11, 2026. Secondarily, the memo requires vendors to supply documentation including model cards, training data summaries, and acceptable use policies to enable agency oversight — but this is a mechanism for verifying the ideological neutrality certification, not a standalone safety or transparency initiative. M-26-04 applies specifically to LLM procurement; it does not govern AI broadly, per [GovCIO Media's coverage of M-26-04](https://govciomedia.com/white-house-releases-new-ai-guidance-expanding-federal-ai-oversight/).
- The White House's July 2025 AI Action Plan directed NIST to partner with industry on AI-inclusive incident response standards and required Chief Information Security Officers to consult their agency's Chief AI Officer during cybersecurity incidents involving AI, per [CSET's summary](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/trumps-plan-for-ai-recapping-the-white-houses-ai-action-plan/).
- As of May 2026, the White House was actively "studying" a potential AI security executive order that would require AI systems that "could create vulnerabilities" to undergo a proven-safe process before federal release — described by officials as analogous to FDA drug evaluation, per [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/05/wh-studying-ai-security-executive-order/). This EO has not been signed as of time of writing.

## Unverified Signal: Reported May 2026 OSTP/OMB Guidance [LOW confidence]

IAPP reported in May 2026 that the White House (through OSTP and/or OMB) was issuing or had issued additional guidance requiring federal agencies to conduct AI safety evaluations before procuring or deploying high-risk AI systems. The reported elements include a new compliance deadline and incident reporting obligations.

**Verification status:** The specific May 2026 guidance memo could not be independently retrieved. Web searches found no corroborating law firm client alerts, Federal Register entries, or contemporaneous government announcements of a new OSTP/OMB AI procurement memo in May 2026. The OMB Memoranda listing page at https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/guidance/memoranda/ should be checked manually for any new M-26 series memo. A previously included claim that this guidance would create an "inter-agency AI risk register" is removed from this report entirely — no authoritative source confirms that mechanism exists.

Any compliance deadline attributed to this unverified May 2026 guidance is speculative. Practitioners should treat IAPP's report as a signal warranting monitoring, not as an established compliance obligation.

## Action Items

- **Federal contractors supplying LLMs**: Assess compliance with M-26-04's Unbiased AI Principles certification requirements immediately. The March 11, 2026 deadline for agencies to revise LLM procurement policies has passed; new solicitations and contract modifications may already include M-26-04 terms. Review model training and output processes against the truth-seeking and ideological neutrality standards and prepare corrective action documentation if gaps exist.
- **Federal contractors supplying high-impact AI broadly**: Confirm compliance with M-25-21's 365-day documentation requirements, which expired approximately April 3, 2026. Agencies that did not meet this deadline were expected to suspend use of non-compliant AI tools. Verify that all high-impact AI use cases are inventoried, tested, and documented.
- **New solicitation respondents**: Any solicitation issued on or after October 1, 2025 should incorporate M-25-22 AI procurement terms. Review RFP language for pre-award testing requirements and post-award monitoring rights and price those obligations into proposals.
- **Monitor whitehouse.gov/omb** for confirmation of the May 2026 OSTP guidance memo. Expected URL: https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/guidance/memoranda/
- **Watch** for the anticipated AI security executive order described by White House officials in May 2026, which would impose pre-deployment vetting requirements comparable to FDA drug evaluation for high-risk frontier models used in federal contexts. No action required until signed.
