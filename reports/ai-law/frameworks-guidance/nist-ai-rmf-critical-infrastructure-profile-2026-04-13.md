# NIST Releases Concept Note for AI RMF Profile on Trustworthy AI in Critical Infrastructure

## Overview [HIGH confidence]

On April 7, 2026, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) released a [concept note for an AI Risk Management Framework (RMF) Profile on Trustworthy AI in Critical Infrastructure](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/concept-note-ai-rmf-profile-trustworthy-ai-critical-infrastructure). The concept note, published by the NIST Information Technology Laboratory (ITL), launches development of a sector-tailored profile extending the [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/nist.ai.100-1.pdf) (originally issued January 2023) to address the specific risk management needs of operators and suppliers deploying AI-enabled capabilities across the nation's critical infrastructure.

The effort is intended to help critical infrastructure (CI) entities manage AI risk across Information Technology (IT), Operational Technology (OT), and Industrial Control Systems (ICS).

## Key Provisions / Scope [HIGH confidence]

The concept note frames the forthcoming profile as a guidance resource — not a binding regulation — aimed at steering critical infrastructure operators and their suppliers toward risk management practices appropriate to AI-enabled capabilities in high-consequence environments:

- **Audience**: Critical infrastructure operators, suppliers, and supply-chain partners across all 16 federally designated CI sectors.
- **Technology coverage**: AI deployed in IT, OT, and ICS contexts — a broader scope than pure enterprise-IT guidance, reflecting that AI failures in CI can have safety and physical-world consequences.
- **Stakeholder process**: NIST is creating a "Trustworthy AI in Critical Infrastructure Profile Community of Interest" to gather feedback from across the CI ecosystem.
- **Output**: A profile document that maps AI RMF functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage) to CI-specific use cases, threat scenarios, and trustworthiness characteristics (safety, security, resilience, accountability, etc.).

## Connection to AI RMF 1.0 [HIGH confidence]

The [AI RMF 1.0](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), released in January 2023, established a voluntary, lifecycle-based framework organized around four core functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — and seven trustworthiness characteristics. NIST has since issued profiles tailoring the framework to particular contexts, most notably the Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024).

The Critical Infrastructure Profile follows that same pattern: it does not replace AI RMF 1.0 but overlays it with CI-specific guidance. It also complements NIST's parallel [preliminary draft Cybersecurity Framework Profile for AI (NIST IR 8596 iprd)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8596/iprd), released in December 2025, which addresses cybersecurity risks of AI systems. Together, the two profiles position CI operators to address AI-specific cybersecurity concerns alongside broader AI trustworthiness concerns.

## Compliance Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

The profile is voluntary guidance, consistent with NIST's statutory role. However, several practical compliance implications typically follow from NIST profiles:

- **Contractual flow-down**: Federal agencies and CI owners frequently reference NIST frameworks in procurement clauses. AI suppliers to CI sectors should expect buyers to begin citing the profile once finalized.
- **Sector regulators**: Sector Risk Management Agencies (e.g., TSA for pipelines, EPA for water, FERC/NERC for bulk electric, CISA cross-sector) have historically incorporated NIST frameworks into binding directives and rules. Operators should anticipate similar incorporation for AI risk.
- **Insurance and litigation**: NIST frameworks are widely treated as a reasonableness benchmark in cyber insurance underwriting and in tort litigation. Adoption of the profile is likely to carry similar evidentiary weight for AI-related incidents in CI.
- **Supply-chain due diligence**: The explicit inclusion of "suppliers" in the audience signals NIST's expectation that CI operators will push AI risk management requirements into their vendor management programs.

Because the document released is a concept note rather than a draft profile, there are no concrete control statements yet to implement. Organizations should focus on joining the Community of Interest and preparing baseline AI inventories for CI deployments.

## Timeline [MEDIUM confidence]

- **January 2023**: AI RMF 1.0 published.
- **July 2024**: Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1) published.
- **December 2025**: Preliminary draft Cyber AI Profile (NIST IR 8596 iprd) published for public comment.
- **April 7, 2026**: Concept note for the AI RMF Trustworthy AI in Critical Infrastructure Profile released; Community of Interest opened.
- **Next steps**: A draft profile is expected to follow the concept note after stakeholder workshops. NIST has not published a specific draft release date or public comment deadline as of April 2026.
