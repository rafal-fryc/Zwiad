# Connecticut SB 5 (Public Act No. 26-15): Automated Employment Decision Tool Requirements

**Jurisdiction:** Connecticut | **Category:** AI Law — Employment AI | **Date:** May 20, 2026

> **Scope note:** Connecticut SB 5 is a broad omnibus AI law. This report focuses exclusively on its automated employment-related decision technology (AEDT) provisions — including the developer-deployer framework, notice obligations, anti-discrimination amendments, and WARN Act AI disclosure mandate. For coverage of the full law (frontier model safety, synthetic content provenance, youth social media protections), see the related report linked below.

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Governor Ned Lamont signed [Connecticut Senate Bill 5](https://www.cga.ct.gov/asp/CGABillStatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&bill_num=SB5) — enacted as [Public Act No. 26-15](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf) — creating Connecticut's first binding framework governing artificial intelligence in employment decisions. The law imposes a two-phase compliance structure: a developer-deployer information-sharing framework and anti-discrimination amendments take effect **October 1, 2026**; full point-of-interaction disclosure and pre-decision written notice obligations for applicants and employees take effect **October 1, 2027**. A separate WARN Act AI disclosure obligation also activates October 1, 2026, requiring employers conducting AI-related layoffs to flag this in filings with the Connecticut Department of Labor. Connecticut joins Illinois and New York City as the leading U.S. jurisdictions with enacted AEDT-specific employer obligations.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Enactment:** Governor Lamont signed SB 5 into law on **May 29, 2026** as [Public Act No. 26-15](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00015-R00SB-00005-PA.pdf); the House passed it 131-17 on May 1, 2026, the Senate 32-4 on April 21, 2026, per [CT Mirror](https://ctmirror.org/2026/05/01/artificial-intelligence-house-regulation-passage-ct/) and [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/connecticuts-lamont-signs-ai-law-with-employer-notice-mandate).
- **AEDT definition:** "Automated employment-related decision technology" means any computational process — including those using machine learning, statistical modeling, or AI — that generates an output (score, rank, classification, recommendation, or constraint) that is a **substantial factor** in making or materially influencing a decision about hiring, promotion, discipline, discharge, employment renewal, or selection for training or apprenticeship, per [Littler Mendelson](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/connecticut-passes-law-significantly-regulating-use-ai-employment) and [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/ai-law-blog/connecticut-takes-aim-ai-employment-decisions-sb-5). The definition is broad and technology-neutral — it captures resume screeners, applicant ranking platforms, video-interview analytics, productivity monitoring tools, and scheduling algorithms when those outputs materially influence personnel decisions.
- **No size threshold:** SB 5 does not establish a minimum employee count for covered employers. Any employer that deploys a qualifying AEDT is subject to the law regardless of workforce size, per [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/Connecticut-Passes-Legislation-Regulating-the-Use-of-AI-in-Employment-Decisions-5-15-2026) and [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/connecticut-employers-need-to-prepare-for-new-workplace-ai-law).
- **Developer-deployer split (October 1, 2026):** The law allocates compliance duties between "developers" (entities that build, sell, license, or configure covered tools) and "deployers" (employers that put those tools to use). Developers whose products are marketed or intended to materially influence employment decisions must furnish deployers with sufficient information to satisfy the law's disclosure and notice requirements. Parties may contractually reallocate certain obligations, but any such arrangement must be express, per [Ogletree Deakins](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/new-connecticut-law-restricts-employer-ai-use-mandates-notice-for-ai-caused-rifs/) and [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/05/18/connecticut-new-ai-legislation).
- **Point-of-interaction disclosure (October 1, 2027):** Employers deploying AEDT that is designed to interact directly with applicants or employees must disclose — in plain language — that the individual is interacting with an automated system, unless it would be obvious to a reasonable person, per [Shipman & Goodwin](https://www.shipmangoodwin.com/insights/connecticuts-ai-responsibility-and-transparency-act-key-impacts-on-the-workplace.html).
- **Pre-decision written notice (October 1, 2027):** When AEDT output will serve as a substantial factor in an employment decision, the employer must provide the affected applicant or employee with a written notice **before** the decision is made. The notice must identify: (1) the fact that an automated system is being used; (2) the purpose of the system and the type of decision involved; (3) the commercial name of the product; (4) the categories of personal data processed and the sources from which that data is obtained; (5) the methodology by which data is evaluated; and (6) the employer's contact information. Trade secrets are shielded — if an employer withholds information on that basis, it must notify the recipient and identify the applicable protection, per [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/the-growing-patchwork-of-state-ai-laws-what-it-means-for-employers) and [Ogletree Deakins](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/new-connecticut-law-restricts-employer-ai-use-mandates-notice-for-ai-caused-rifs/).
- **Anti-discrimination amendment (October 1, 2026):** The law amends Connecticut's anti-discrimination statutes to state expressly that use of an AEDT is **not a defense** to a discrimination complaint. An employer cannot escape liability by asserting the tool — rather than a human — made the adverse decision. However, courts and the Commission on Human Rights and Opportunities may consider evidence of proactive anti-bias testing and the employer's response to those results as mitigating factors — these efforts do not create a formal safe harbor, per [Troutman Privacy](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/analyzing-connecticuts-new-employment-ai-law/) and [Shipman & Goodwin](https://www.shipmangoodwin.com/insights/connecticuts-ai-responsibility-and-transparency-act-key-impacts-on-the-workplace.html).
- **WARN Act AI disclosure (October 1, 2026):** Any employer filing a federal WARN Act notice (60-day advance notice of mass layoffs or plant closings) with the Connecticut Department of Labor must additionally disclose whether the layoffs are **related to the employer's use of AI or other technological change**. Connecticut is among the first U.S. states to attach an AI-disclosure obligation to WARN Act filings, per [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/connecticuts-lamont-signs-ai-law-with-employer-notice-mandate) and [Ogletree Deakins](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/new-connecticut-law-restricts-employer-ai-use-mandates-notice-for-ai-caused-rifs/).
- **Enforcement:** The Connecticut Attorney General holds exclusive enforcement authority for AEDT violations under the Connecticut Unfair Trade Practices Act. There is **no private right of action** for the AEDT sections. Through December 31, 2027, the AG must issue a 60-day cure notice before initiating an enforcement action for a curable violation, per [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/sb-5-in-five-what-to-know-about-connecticuts-new-ai-law/) and [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/connecticut-passes-legislation-regulating-use-ai-employment-decisions).
- **No mandatory bias audits:** Unlike NYC Local Law 144, Connecticut SB 5 does not require independent bias audits as a precondition to deploying AEDT. The anti-bias testing mitigation factor incentivizes voluntary auditing without making it mandatory, per [Troutman Privacy](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/analyzing-connecticuts-new-employment-ai-law/).

## Action Items

- **By October 1, 2026 — all employers:** Audit all AI tools used in hiring, promotion, discipline, discharge, employment renewal, and training selection to identify which qualify as AEDT under the law's broad definition. Cast a wide net — resume screeners, video interview platforms, productivity analytics, and scheduling systems may all qualify if their outputs materially influence personnel decisions.
- **By October 1, 2026 — all employers:** Review and update federal WARN Act notice procedures and forms to include the new Connecticut disclosure indicating whether a planned layoff is related to AI or other technological change. Brief HR and legal teams handling workforce reductions.
- **By October 1, 2026 — all employers using third-party AEDT:** Contact vendors and technology providers to confirm they can supply the developer-side information required by the law (data categories, sources, methodology, product name) in time to populate the October 2027 notice obligations. Negotiate contractual allocation of compliance duties expressly in writing.
- **By October 1, 2026 — anti-discrimination posture:** Implement or expand proactive anti-bias testing for any AEDT in use. While testing does not create a safe harbor, documented mitigation efforts are the only lever courts and the CHRO may weigh favorably when a discrimination complaint is filed.
- **By October 1, 2027 — all deployers:** Stand up written pre-decision notice workflows for every AEDT-driven employment decision — covering all six required disclosure elements. Build systems to deliver notices before decisions are finalized and to retain records of delivery.
- **By October 1, 2027 — interactive AEDT deployers:** Implement point-of-interaction disclosures for any AEDT that communicates directly with applicants or employees (e.g., AI chatbot interviewers, automated assessment platforms).
- **Ongoing:** Monitor Connecticut Attorney General guidance and any rulemaking interpreting the AEDT provisions. Track whether the cure period (effective through December 31, 2027) is extended.
