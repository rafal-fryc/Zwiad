# California Executive Order N-6-26: State Agencies Directed to Study and Mitigate AI Workforce Disruption

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** AI Law — Employment AI | **Date:** May 21, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 21, 2026, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed [Executive Order N-6-26](https://www.gov.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/5.21.26-AI-Workforce-EO-FINAL-SIGNED.pdf), directing multiple state agencies to assess artificial intelligence's impact on the California labor market and recommend reforms to existing worker protection frameworks. The order creates no immediate legal obligations for private employers, but establishes binding agency deadlines extending through late 2026 that will produce policy recommendations likely to become the basis for legislation and formal rulemaking. The order's explicit focus on Cal-WARN Act revisions, automated decision-making transparency, and worker voice in AI adoption signals California's intent to build a comprehensive employment AI regulatory regime.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Governor Newsom signed Executive Order N-6-26 on May 21, 2026, characterizing it as the first executive order in the nation specifically aimed at preparing workers and businesses for AI-driven economic disruption — per the [official Governor's press release](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/05/21/governor-newsom-signs-first-of-its-kind-executive-order-to-prepare-workers-and-businesses-for-potential-ai-disruption/).
- The order does **not** impose any new legal obligations on private employers at this time, nor does it create enforceable employee rights, per analysis by [Littler Mendelson](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/california-executive-order-focuses-worker-protections-disruption-caused-ai).
- The [California Labor and Workforce Development Agency (LWDA)](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/05/21/governor-newsom-signs-first-of-its-kind-executive-order-to-prepare-workers-and-businesses-for-potential-ai-disruption/) must deliver, within 90 days (by approximately August 19, 2026), a review of academic research on AI's labor market impact, including disproportionate demographic effects and early economic warning signals — per [Akin Gump's analysis](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/california-governor-issues-executive-order-directing-review-of-ai-workforce-impacts).
- The [Employment Development Department (EDD)](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/05/21/governor-newsom-signs-first-of-its-kind-executive-order-to-prepare-workers-and-businesses-for-potential-ai-disruption/) must launch a public dashboard by approximately August 19, 2026 tracking AI's employment impacts across sectors, drawing on unemployment insurance data — per [Reed Smith's analysis](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/employment-law-watch/102mx9t/whats-new-in-californias-response-to-ai-workforce-disruption/).
- Within 180 days (approximately November 17, 2026), the LWDA must recommend revisions to the [California WARN Act (Cal-WARN)](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/cal-governors-executive-order-aims-at-shielding-workers-from-ai-displacement/) to modernize it as an early warning system for AI-driven workforce disruptions, potentially including new disclosure requirements when AI plays a role in workforce reductions — per [Ogletree Deakins](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/cal-governors-executive-order-aims-at-shielding-workers-from-ai-displacement/).
- By October 15, 2026, the LWDA must review how collective bargaining is incorporating AI and "how worker voice is incorporated in adoption of emerging technologies" — per [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/California-Lays-the-Groundwork-for-More-Sweeping-AI-Workforce-RegulationEmployers-Should-Start-Preparing-Now-6-8-2026).
- The order also directs review of policies providing workers with a safety net, plans to expand enrollment in employment insurance, and recommendations for boosting worker training programs — per [CDF Labor Law](https://www.cdflaborlaw.com/blog/governor-newsom-signs-executive-order-to-confront-economic-impacts-of-ai).
- The order was signed two days after the California Senate passed SB 947, the ["No Robo Bosses Act"](https://sd05.senate.ca.gov/news/ca-senate-approves-no-robo-bosses-act-2026-ensure-human-oversight-ai-workplace), which would prohibit employers from relying solely on automated decision systems (ADS) to terminate or discipline workers. As of the date of this report, SB 947 remains pending in the California Assembly.
- [Littler Mendelson shareholder Joy C. Rosenquist](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/california-executive-order-focuses-worker-protections-disruption-caused-ai) flagged that the order's direction toward transparency and mitigation obligations could raise complex legal issues, including potential de facto severance mandates (inconsistent with at-will employment), tensions with federal labor law, and compliance with securities and wage-and-hour laws for equity-based compensation.
- [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/california-governor-aims-to-soften-ais-impact-on-workers-through-new-executive-order) noted the order is paired with at least two additional pending bills: SB 951 (California Worker Technological Displacement Act, requiring 90 days' advance notice before AI-driven layoffs) and AB 1898 (imposing notice, acknowledgment, and inventory obligations on employers using AI for employment decisions).

## Legislative and Regulatory Context [HIGH confidence]

Executive Order N-6-26 sits within a rapidly developing California AI employment regulatory landscape:

**SB 947 — No Robo Bosses Act of 2026 (pending Assembly vote):** [SB 947](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/california-sb-947-no-robo-bosses-act-new-proposed-guardrails-on-use-of-automated-decision-systems-in-employer-discipline-and-termination-decisions) would prohibit employers from relying solely on ADS to fire, discipline, or deactivate workers and require human oversight for any ADS-assisted adverse employment action. Workers would gain the right to request 12 months of their own data used in ADS decisions, and employers would be required to provide written post-use notice after ADS-based disciplinary actions. SB 947 is the successor to SB 7 (2025), which passed both chambers but was vetoed by Governor Newsom as overbroad.

**SB 951 — California Worker Technological Displacement Act (pending):** This bill would require employers to give at least 90 days' advance notice before layoffs caused by "technological displacement," including AI — a significant extension of existing Cal-WARN requirements per [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/california-set-to-restrict-ai-use-in-the-workplace-with-no-robo-bosses-act).

**AB 1898 — AI Workplace Notice and Disclosure (pending):** [AB 1898](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/california-workplace-ai-notice-and-disclosure-bill-would-impose-hefty-penalties/) would impose significant obligations on employers using AI tools for employment-related decisions, including advance written notice to employees, signed acknowledgments, and maintenance of annual inventories of AI tools, with substantial penalties for noncompliance.

**Cal-WARN Act (existing law):** California's [Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1400.&lawCode=LAB) currently requires covered employers (75+ employees) to provide 60 days' advance written notice before mass layoffs, plant closings, or relocations. EO N-6-26 directs the LWDA to revise this framework to address AI-driven disruptions specifically, potentially by lowering thresholds or adding AI-specific disclosure triggers.

## Employer Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

The order creates no immediate legal exposure for private employers. However, [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/California-Lays-the-Groundwork-for-More-Sweeping-AI-Workforce-RegulationEmployers-Should-Start-Preparing-Now-6-8-2026) and [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/california_state_agencies_ordered_study_impact_ai_employment_0526.html) both characterize the order as laying deliberate groundwork for sweeping employment AI regulation in 2027 and beyond. Several specific implications warrant monitoring:

- **Cal-WARN exposure:** Employers planning AI-enabled workforce reductions should assume the Cal-WARN framework will be materially revised within 18 months. The LWDA's November 2026 recommendations may introduce AI-specific triggers, enhanced notice periods, or ADS disclosure requirements that affect layoff planning timelines.
- **Collective bargaining obligations:** The October 2026 review of how AI is incorporated in collective bargaining may yield recommendations requiring unions to be consulted before AI deployment — a change that could affect any employer in California with a collective bargaining agreement.
- **Data inventory groundwork:** The pending AB 1898 framework — likely to be shaped in part by the EO's agency studies — would require employers to maintain an inventory of all AI tools used in employment decisions. Employers should begin that inventory now.
- **Automated decision-making exposure:** If SB 947 passes the Assembly without further veto, employers using ADS for termination or discipline decisions will face new procedural and documentation requirements. The combination of the EO and SB 947 signals that automated employment decisions will require a documented human review layer in California.

[Littler Mendelson](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/california-executive-order-focuses-worker-protections-disruption-caused-ai) specifically warns that de facto severance mandates embedded in forthcoming recommendations could challenge at-will employment principles and create tension with the National Labor Relations Act.

## Action Items

- **Monitor LWDA August 2026 report:** The 90-day agency report (due approximately August 19, 2026) will be the first official government signal on which specific Cal-WARN and safety-net reforms are under serious consideration. Assign tracking responsibility now.
- **Track SB 947 Assembly progress:** SB 947 passed the Senate May 19, 2026. If signed, employers relying on ADS for disciplinary or termination decisions will need documented human review protocols and new written notice procedures. Prepare contingency compliance drafts.
- **Audit AI tool inventory:** AB 1898's pending annual inventory requirement covers AI tools used in any employment decision. Begin documenting currently deployed tools, vendor relationships, and decision inputs now to avoid remediation under compressed legislative timelines.
- **Review Cal-WARN procedures:** In anticipation of LWDA recommendations in November 2026, review current Cal-WARN compliance procedures and assess whether AI is or will be a factor in workforce reduction decisions that could trigger expanded notice obligations.
- **Assess collective bargaining agreements:** Employers with CBAs should evaluate whether existing management rights provisions cover AI deployment and whether forthcoming state guidance would require union consultation before deploying AI tools in the workplace.
- **Engage employment counsel in Q3 2026:** Given the volume of interrelated developments (EO N-6-26, SB 947, SB 951, AB 1898), a comprehensive California AI employment compliance review with counsel is advisable before the end of Q3 2026.
