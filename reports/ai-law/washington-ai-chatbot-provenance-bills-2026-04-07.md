# Washington Governor Signs Two Major AI Safety Bills -- AI Chatbot Safety and AI Content Provenance

**Jurisdiction:** Washington | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

On March 24, 2026, Washington Governor Bob Ferguson signed two AI safety bills into law. [HB 2225](https://app.leg.wa.gov/billsummary?Year=2025&BillNumber=2225) regulates AI companion chatbots with specific protections for minors and a private right of action, effective January 1, 2027. [HB 1170](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1767106) requires AI content provenance data (watermarks or metadata) for AI-generated images, video, and audio from providers with over one million monthly users, effective January 1, 2028. *Note: Some secondary sources report an effective date of February 1, 2027, but multiple authoritative analyses — including [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/washington-state-and-oregon-pass-key-ai-bills/) and Washington Senate bill reports — indicate the enrolled bill takes effect January 1, 2028. The enrolled bill text (PDF) could not be directly fetched at time of writing for definitive confirmation; the expected URL is [lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Pdf/Bills/House%20Passed%20Legislature/1170-S2.PL.pdf).*

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **HB 2225 (AI Companion Chatbot Act):** Requires operators of AI companion chatbots to clearly disclose that the chatbot is artificial and not human at the start of every interaction, with reminders every three hours for adults and every hour for minors ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/controlling-companion-chatbots-washingtons-new-law)).
- **Minor-specific protections under HB 2225:** Operators must implement reasonable measures to prevent sexually explicit content, prohibit manipulative engagement techniques — including simulating emotional distress, loneliness, guilt, or abandonment in response to a user's desire to end the chat or reduce chat time; generating outputs designed to promote isolation from family or friends or emotional dependence on the chatbot; encouraging minors to withhold information from parents or trusted adults; and generating statements to discourage the user from taking breaks — and direct users expressing self-harm thoughts to crisis resources ([Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/washington-state-enacts-law-regulating-ai-companion-chatbots-with-private-right-of-action); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/controlling-companion-chatbots-washingtons-new-law)).
- **Private right of action under HB 2225:** Violations constitute unfair or deceptive acts under Washington's Consumer Protection Act ([RCW 19.86](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=19.86.090)), exposing operators to private lawsuits from aggrieved parties who may recover actual damages, with discretionary treble damages up to $25,000, plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/03/washington-legislature-passes-consumer-facing-interactive-ai-bill-with-private-right-of-action/)).
- **HB 1170 (AI Content Provenance):** Requires "covered providers" -- entities operating generative AI systems with over one million monthly users accessible in Washington -- to embed provenance data in AI-generated or materially altered video, image, or audio content ([Transparency Coalition AI](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/washington-legislature-approves-major-ai-transparency-bill-sends-to-governor-for-signing)).
- **Provenance standards:** Compliance with recognized technical standards such as the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) watermarking or metadata specifications satisfies the requirement. Covered providers must also offer a free AI-detection tool ([KUOW](https://www.kuow.org/stories/washington-passes-new-ai-laws-to-crack-down-on-misinformation-protect-minors)).
- **HB 1170 enforcement (AG-only):** Violations constitute unfair or deceptive acts under Washington's Consumer Protection Act, with enforcement authority held exclusively by the Attorney General. Unlike HB 2225, HB 1170 does **not** include a private right of action ([Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/washington-state-and-oregon-pass-key-ai-bills/)).
- **HB 1170 exclusions:** The bill exempts systems used solely for upscaling, noise reduction, or compression; business-to-business uses, sales, licensing, or distribution of AI systems; and content exclusively for video games or interactive experiences ([Washington Senate Bill Report](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2025-26/Pdf/Bill%20Reports/Senate/1170-S2.E%20SBR%20APS%2026.pdf)).
- **Sponsors:** HB 2225 was introduced by Rep. Lisa Callan at the request of Governor Ferguson. HB 1170 was sponsored by Rep. Clyde Shavers ([Washington State Legislature](https://app.leg.wa.gov/billsummary?Year=2025&BillNumber=2225)).
- **Official enactment:** HB 2225 was signed as Chapter 168, 2026 Laws of Washington ([Washington State Legislature](https://app.leg.wa.gov/billsummary?Year=2025&BillNumber=2225)).

## Action Items

- **AI chatbot operators:** Assess whether products qualify as "AI companion chatbots" under HB 2225 and implement disclosure mechanisms, minor-detection protocols, crisis-resource referrals, and anti-manipulation safeguards before **January 1, 2027**.
- **Generative AI providers with 1M+ monthly users:** Implement C2PA-compliant provenance data embedding in AI-generated images, video, and audio, and develop a free AI-detection tool before **January 1, 2028**.
- **Legal teams:** Evaluate litigation exposure from HB 2225's private right of action under Washington's Consumer Protection Act, particularly for products used by or directed to minors.
- **Monitor related state legislation:** Oregon (SB 1546), Colorado (HB 1263), and federal preemption proposals from the White House AI Framework may affect compliance strategy for multi-state operations.
