# California SB 53: Frontier AI Obligations in Effect Now and Into 2027

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** ai-law | **Date:** 2026-07-10

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

California's [SB 53](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53), the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act (TFAIA), signed by Governor Newsom on September 29, 2025 and effective January 1, 2026, is now in active enforcement for its first-year obligations — transparency reports, critical safety incident reporting, and whistleblower protections — while a second wave of state-level reporting and consortium-dissolution deadlines arrives on January 1, 2027. <!-- verified --> A July 10, 2026 client alert from [Bradley Arant Boult Cummings](https://www.bradley.com/) (syndicated via Lexology) prompted this update; the National Law Review published the same underlying alert, "[California's SB 53: Understanding the Obligations in Effect Now and Into 2027](https://natlawreview.com/article/californias-sb-53-understanding-obligations-effect-now-and-2027)," on which this report primarily relies. Frontier model developers — particularly "large frontier developers" with more than $500 million in annual gross revenue — should treat 2026 obligations as live and already enforceable, and should calendar the January 1, 2027 milestones described below. <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- **Statute and enactment**: SB 53 (Chapter 138, Statutes of 2025), the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, was signed by Governor Newsom on September 29, 2025, per the [official chaptered bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53) and the [Governor's signing announcement](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/09/29/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53-advancing-californias-world-leading-artificial-intelligence-industry/). <!-- verified: confirmed via gov.ca.gov press release and chaptered bill text (Chapter 138) corroborated by independent law-firm sources -->
- **Core definitions**: A "frontier model" is trained using more than 10^26 floating-point operations (FLOPs), including cumulative compute from fine-tuning; a "frontier developer" is any entity that trained or initiated training of such a model; a "large frontier developer" is a frontier developer whose annual gross revenue (with affiliates) exceeded $500 million in the preceding calendar year, per the [statutory text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53) and [Future of Privacy Forum's analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/californias-sb-53-the-first-frontier-ai-law-explained/). <!-- verified: independently confirmed via FPF, Brookings, Wharton AI & Analytics Initiative, and Carnegie Endowment analyses -->
- **Obligations currently in effect (since January 1, 2026)**: Large frontier developers must publish and maintain a frontier AI framework describing how they incorporate national/international standards to manage catastrophic risk; issue a transparency report before deploying a new or substantially modified frontier model, describing capabilities, intended uses, and risk-assessment results; and report critical safety incidents to the California Office of Emergency Services (OES) within 15 days of discovery, or within 24 hours if the incident poses imminent risk of death or serious injury. Per the [National Law Review alert](https://natlawreview.com/article/californias-sb-53-understanding-obligations-effect-now-and-2027) and corroborating analysis from [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251001-california-enacts-ai-safety-transparency-regulation-tfaia-sb-53) and [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20251001-transparency-in-frontier-artificial-intelligence-act-sb-53-california-requires-new-standardized-ai-safety-disclosures). <!-- verified: 15-day/24-hour incident reporting window independently confirmed via National Law Review and Carnegie Endowment coverage -->
- **Whistleblower protections (in effect now)**: Large frontier developers must maintain an anonymous internal reporting channel for employees to raise good-faith concerns about catastrophic risk, provide notice of whistleblower rights, and are prohibited from retaliating against covered employees who report to the developer, OES, or the Attorney General. Per the [statutory text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53) and [Mayer Brown's analysis](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/california-enacts-sb-53-creating-new-requirements-for-developers-of-frontier-artificial-intelligence-models-and-related-whistleblower-provisions). <!-- verified: corroborated by National Law Review excerpt (anti-retaliation, anonymous channel, attorneys' fees) -->
- **2027 deadlines**: Beginning January 1, 2027 and annually thereafter, OES must publish an anonymized, aggregated report on critical safety incidents it has reviewed; the Attorney General must separately publish an anonymized, aggregated annual report on whistleblower disclosures received under the Act's Labor Code provisions (Section 1107 et seq.); and the California Department of Technology must issue annual recommendations on whether to update the statutory definitions of "frontier model," "frontier developer," and "large frontier developer." Per the [statutory text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53). <!-- verified: OES 2027 reporting deadline independently confirmed via National Law Review excerpt; AG whistleblower report and Dept. of Technology recommendation provisions rely on the official statutory text itself as primary source and were not independently re-derived from a second source in this session -->
- **CalCompute sunset deadline**: The Government Operations Agency must submit the CalCompute consortium's public-cloud-computing framework report to the Legislature on or before January 1, 2027, at which point the consortium is dissolved. Per the [statutory text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53) and the [ETO AGORA policy tracker](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2041). <!-- verified: confirmed via independent search results describing the CalCompute report/dissolution provision and the official CalCompute consortium site -->
- **Penalties**: A large frontier developer that fails to publish required documents, makes a prohibited false statement, fails to timely report a critical safety incident, or fails to comply with its own published frontier AI framework is subject to a civil penalty of up to $1,000,000 per violation, recoverable only by the Attorney General in a civil action. Per the [statutory text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53). <!-- verified: $1M per-violation cap and AG-only enforcement confirmed via multiple independent sources; minor note -- one independent source (Latham & Watkins) characterizes penalties as scaled by severity up to the $1M cap, a nuance not stated in this report but not contradictory to it -->

## Action Items

- **Confirm large-frontier-developer status now.** Organizations with affiliated annual gross revenue above $500 million that train models exceeding 10^26 FLOPs should confirm they have a published frontier AI framework and a current transparency report on file, since these obligations have been enforceable since January 1, 2026.
- **Operationalize the 15-day/24-hour incident reporting pathway** to OES if not already in place; confirm the internal escalation chain can meet the 24-hour imminent-danger trigger.
- **Stand up (or audit) the anonymous whistleblower reporting channel** and confirm required employee notices are distributed, given this obligation is already live and enforceable.
- **Calendar January 1, 2027** for three distinct, separate government-side deliverables that affect compliance posture: the OES aggregated incident report, the Attorney General's aggregated whistleblower report, and the Department of Technology's annual recommendation on updating the FLOP/revenue thresholds — the latter could change who qualifies as a "large frontier developer" in future years.
- **Track CalCompute framework submission** (due January 1, 2027) if participating in or monitoring the consortium process, since the consortium dissolves upon submission.
- **Coordinate with parallel multi-state compliance efforts.** Organizations building SB 53 compliance programs should align them with comparable frameworks under New York's RAISE Act and Illinois SB 315, which share similar thresholds and framework requirements (see Related Reports).
