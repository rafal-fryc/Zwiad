# US and UK Governments Expand Pre-Deployment AI Model Evaluation Programs with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI

**Jurisdiction:** Federal, UK | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-05-06

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 5, 2026, NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) announced new pre-deployment evaluation agreements with [Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI](https://thehill.com/homenews/5863937-google-microsoft-xai-ai-testing/), expanding its frontier model testing program to all five major US AI laboratories. Simultaneously, the UK AI Security Institute (AISI) announced a separate but coordinated [partnership with Microsoft](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/partnering-with-microsoft-to-strengthen-frontier-ai-safety) to conduct pre-launch safety evaluations and societal resilience research. The announcements come amid escalating concern over Anthropic's Claude Mythos model and growing EU pressure over exclusion from pre-launch access to frontier AI capabilities.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- CAISI announced agreements with [Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI on May 5, 2026](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/nist-ai-model-testing-caisi-google-microsoft/819452/), following earlier agreements with OpenAI and Anthropic signed in August 2024 — meaning all five major US AI labs are now participating in the program.
- Under the agreements, developers provide CAISI with model versions that have reduced or removed safeguards so evaluators can probe for national security risks, including [cybersecurity, biosecurity, and chemical weapons threats](https://www.ciodive.com/news/Google-Microsoft-xAI-to-face-security-testing/819375/).
- CAISI has completed [more than 40 evaluations](https://knowledgehubmedia.com/caisi-signs-frontier-ai-testing-agreements-with-google-deepmind-microsoft-and-xai-what-you-need-to-know/) to date, including assessments of models that were never publicly released.
- An interagency task force called [TRAINS](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/caisi-google-deepmind-microsoft-xai-frontier-model-evaluation/) — drawing evaluators from the Departments of Defense, Energy, and Homeland Security — conducts the reviews, which can occur in classified settings.
- The UK AISI announced its own partnership with Microsoft on the same date, covering three workstreams: [frontier safety and security research, safeguard effectiveness evaluation, and societal resilience research](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/05/05/advancing-ai-evaluation-with-the-center-for-ai-standards-us-and-innovation-and-the-ai-security-institute-uk/) examining how conversational AI interacts with users in sensitive contexts.
- Microsoft's simultaneous signing with both CAISI and AISI represents a coordinated transatlantic posture — one pre-release safety review profile across both leading government-backed AI safety institutes.
- Separately, [Politico reported](https://letsdatascience.com/news/politico-reports-eu-concern-over-anthropic-mythos-model-1f331774) that EU institutions are increasingly critical of Anthropic's lack of transparency around Claude Mythos, with [EU Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis confirming](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-05-04/european-commission-is-in-contact-with-anthropic-on-mythos-dombrovskis-says) the Commission is in direct contact with Anthropic about access for European organizations.

## Key Facts — Program Structure [MEDIUM confidence]

- CAISI's evaluations are government-conducted assessments, not independent third-party certifications. They confer no regulatory approval and do not substitute for compliance with the EU AI Act or other binding frameworks.
- The AISI partnership with Microsoft is described as "sustained two-way collaboration between government and companies," per [AISI's blog](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/partnering-with-microsoft-to-strengthen-frontier-ai-safety). Societal resilience research under the agreement covers emotional dependency, mental health interactions, and erosion of trust in human professionals.
- The CAISI agreements are voluntary and non-binding. No statutory authority requires AI developers to submit models for pre-deployment government review.

## EU Context and the Mythos Exclusion [MEDIUM confidence]

The CAISI and AISI announcements occurred against the backdrop of ongoing controversy over Anthropic's restricted distribution of Claude Mythos. Under [Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing), Mythos Preview access was limited to a US-anchored consortium of approximately 50 organizations, with European banks and critical infrastructure providers largely excluded. Euro-area finance ministers met in Brussels on May 4 — the day before the CAISI/AISI announcements — to [press for access to Mythos](https://thenextweb.com/news/europe-mythos-anthropic-cybersecurity-access-crisis). The EU AI Act does not compel a US company to provide European institutions with access to its frontier models, and the Commission currently lacks a statutory mechanism to compel such access.

The CAISI/AISI program expansions announced May 5 do not address the EU exclusion from Mythos: they are bilateral US and UK government agreements with developers, not multilateral access frameworks.

## Action Items

- **AI developers not yet participating in CAISI:** Assess whether participation in CAISI's voluntary pre-deployment evaluation program is consistent with company policy, customer commitments, and potential government procurement positioning. All five major US labs now participate.
- **Organizations deploying frontier models in critical sectors (finance, healthcare, defense):** Understand that CAISI evaluations do not constitute regulatory clearance. Maintain separate compliance programs under applicable sector-specific law (e.g., HIPAA, FISMA, EU AI Act).
- **EU-based organizations and regulators:** Track Commission-level negotiations with Anthropic over Mythos access and monitor whether analogous EU-side evaluation frameworks emerge in response to the US/UK bilateral model.
- **Compliance and legal teams:** Note that the voluntary nature of CAISI agreements means results are not public and do not create enforceable standards. However, NIST frameworks historically shape industry best practice and can become de facto compliance benchmarks in federal procurement contexts.
- **UK-nexus organizations:** Monitor AISI's evolving evaluation approach — the Microsoft partnership's societal resilience workstream (mental health, emotional dependency) signals expanding scope beyond cybersecurity and biosecurity risk assessments.
