# White House and Anthropic in Active Negotiations Over Frontier AI Cybersecurity Compliance Framework

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-06-22

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

The White House and Anthropic are in active negotiations to establish a cybersecurity compliance framework specific to frontier AI model developers — distinct from the export control action that suspended global access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 on June 12, 2026. The negotiations follow the June 2, 2026 executive order "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," which directed the Secretary of the Treasury to establish an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse within 30 days, and directed agencies to design a voluntary pre-release model-review framework within 60 days (by approximately August 1, 2026). The outcome of these talks would create an industry-first category of cybersecurity obligations tied directly to frontier model capabilities, with implications for every advanced AI developer operating in the United States.

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence]

- On June 2, 2026, President Trump signed [Executive Order "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/), directing agencies within 60 days to design a voluntary framework under which developers of "covered frontier models" must provide the federal government up to 30 days of pre-release access and collaborate with the government on selecting trusted release partners.

- The EO directed the Secretary of the Treasury — in consultation with the National Cyber Director, NSA Director, and CISA Director — to form an [AI cybersecurity clearinghouse](https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-AI-Executive-Order-Addresses-Frontier-Models-and-Cybersecurity-Vulnerabilities) within 30 days to coordinate vulnerability scanning, validation, and patch distribution in voluntary collaboration with industry.

- NSA is tasked with designing a [classified benchmarking process](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/president-trump-signs-executive-order-establishing-ai-cybersecurity-and-frontier-model-framework) to set the capability threshold at which a model is designated a "covered frontier model," with final designation authority resting with the NSA Director.

- On June 9, 2026, Anthropic launched [Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-fable-5-mythos-5): Fable 5 is a generally accessible model with automated routing of cybersecurity, biology, and chemistry queries to the less-capable Claude Opus 4.8; Mythos 5 is an unrestricted version limited to vetted Project Glasswing partners and critical infrastructure operators.

- On June 12, 2026, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security invoked the Export Control Reform Act via an ["Is Informed" letter](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access) ordering Anthropic to suspend all access to both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for any foreign national — including foreign national employees at Anthropic's US offices — citing a possible jailbreak that could bypass Fable 5's cybersecurity safeguards.

- On June 26-27, 2026, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick notified Anthropic that [Mythos 5 may be redeployed](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/26/us-government-anthropic-claude-mythos5-ai.html) to approximately 100 US organizations in critical infrastructure sectors; Fable 5 general availability remains suspended as of June 28, 2026.

- The cybersecurity vulnerability disclosure framework under negotiation per [IAPP reporting](https://iapp.org/news/a/white-house-anthropic-negotiate-frontier-ai-cybersecurity-regulatory-framework) is described as separate from the export control action — it would establish standing requirements for how frontier model developers detect, disclose, and remediate cybersecurity vulnerabilities embedded in or exploitable by advanced AI systems.

- Anthropic's [Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/project/glasswing), announced April 9, 2026, established the voluntary controlled-access model for the Mythos model family; since launch, Glasswing partners have disclosed more than [10,000 high or critical-severity vulnerabilities](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/ai-anthropic-claude-mythos-project-glasswing-expand/821714/) across major operating systems and web browsers.

- A separate and ongoing dispute involves Anthropic's litigation against the Pentagon's [supply chain risk designation](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/anthropic-challenges-the-pentagon-s-supply-chain-risk-determination) of the company, which bars Anthropic from DoD contracts while appeals proceed. The Pentagon's tech chief stated in May 2026 — prior to the Mythos 5 launch — that the Glasswing/Mythos access dispute [is separate from the DoD supply chain risk blacklisting](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/01/pentagon-anthropic-blacklist-mythos-michael.html); no post-June 12 official statement has addressed the Mythos 5 export control situation specifically.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The June 2, 2026 executive order represents the first US federal action to systematically address the national security implications of frontier AI models with advanced cybersecurity-relevant capabilities. It emerged after months of internal administration debate — documented in earlier pipeline reports — that pitted national security officials' concerns about AI-accelerated cyberattacks against industry advocates opposing regulatory overhead.

The catalyst for the EO's cybersecurity provisions was largely Anthropic's own April 2026 disclosure that Claude Mythos, its most capable model, could identify and exploit software vulnerabilities at a pace and scale exceeding all but the most elite human security researchers. That disclosure drove the administration to fast-track the voluntary model-sharing framework that the EO eventually codified.

The EO expressly prohibits interpreting the voluntary framework as authorization for mandatory licensing or preclearance of AI model releases — a concession to industry that narrows the scope of compliance obligations, at least under current law. However, the ongoing negotiations reported by IAPP suggest the administration views the voluntary framework as a starting point rather than a ceiling.

## Compliance Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

The framework under negotiation would, if finalized, create a new category of cybersecurity obligations for frontier model developers beyond those already applicable under CISA guidance and sector-specific cybersecurity regulations. Key compliance dimensions include:

**Vulnerability Disclosure:** Frontier model developers may face obligations to report cybersecurity vulnerabilities that their models can discover or exploit, analogous to existing software vulnerability disclosure programs (VDPs) under NIST SP 800-216. The [June 22, 2026 executive order on cryptographic security](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/securing-the-nation-against-advanced-cryptographic-attacks/) separately directed FAR amendments requiring contractors to implement VDPs — creating a potential template for AI-specific disclosure requirements.

**Pre-Release Access Windows:** Under the EO's voluntary framework, developers who engage in the program must provide up to 30 days of pre-release access to covered frontier models before broader distribution. Organizations that do not participate in the voluntary program face no legal obligation under the current EO text, but the Fable 5/Mythos 5 export control action demonstrates that unilateral government action can functionally impose pre-release review outside the voluntary framework.

**Access Control for Cybersecurity Capabilities:** The Mythos 5 restoration — limited to US organizations cleared by Commerce — establishes a practical precedent for government-managed tiered access to AI models based on their cybersecurity capabilities. Future frameworks may require developers to maintain equivalent access tiers and approval processes internally.

**Cybersecurity Clearinghouse Participation:** The EO's AI cybersecurity clearinghouse, to be operational within 30 days of June 2, represents a new government-industry coordination body. Developers who participate gain potential liability and reputational protections; those who decline may face greater scrutiny during any future mandatory review.

## What Is Not Yet Known [LOW confidence]

- The specific vulnerability disclosure thresholds or formats that the White House-Anthropic negotiations are targeting have not been publicly disclosed. IAPP's June 22 report does not specify whether the framework would apply to all frontier model developers or only those whose models are designated "covered frontier models" under the EO's classified benchmarking process.

- Whether the framework will be codified as an agency rule (requiring notice-and-comment under the APA), an executive guidance document, or a voluntary agreement with no regulatory force is unknown. The administration's preference for avoiding mandatory licensing suggests a non-binding framework is more likely, but the Fable 5 export control action demonstrates that existing legal authorities can be used coercively regardless.

- The timeline for completing the framework is not publicly stated. The 60-day deadline for the voluntary model-review program (approximately August 1, 2026) provides an implicit deadline for at least the pre-release access component.

## Action Items

- **Frontier model developers:** Assess whether your models would qualify as "covered frontier models" under NSA's forthcoming classified benchmarking criteria (expected by approximately August 1, 2026). Engage counsel with security clearances if needed to participate in the classified evaluation process.

- **All AI developers with cybersecurity-relevant capabilities:** Review current vulnerability disclosure policies against NIST SP 800-216 guidance and anticipate that the framework may require AI-specific VDP enhancements. Begin mapping which model capabilities could trigger disclosure obligations.

- **Critical infrastructure operators using AI:** Monitor Commerce Department announcements on Mythos 5 access expansion. If your organization was not included in the initial ~100 approved organizations, engage with the AI cybersecurity clearinghouse process to be evaluated for access.

- **Government contractors:** Note the parallel June 22 EO on cryptographic security requires FAR contract amendments for contractor VDPs within 270 days. AI contractor VDP requirements are likely to be harmonized with these amendments.

- **All affected organizations:** Do not assume the "voluntary" nature of the EO framework insulates you from government intervention. The Fable 5/Mythos 5 action shows BIS export control authorities can be applied to achieve mandatory outcomes. Engage proactively with the AI cybersecurity clearinghouse.

- **Monitor:** Watch for further IAPP and official government reporting on the specific terms of the White House-Anthropic framework negotiations, and for Commerce Department announcements on Fable 5 restoration, which may signal the terms agreed upon.
