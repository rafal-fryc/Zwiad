# Illinois SB 315: Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act — Mandatory Audits and Incident Reporting for Frontier AI Developers

**Jurisdiction:** Illinois | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** June 1, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The Illinois General Assembly passed Senate Bill 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, with a unanimous 110–0 House vote on May 27, 2026, and a 52–5 Senate vote on May 21, 2026. Multiple secondary sources — including [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/artificial-intelligence/illinois-landmark-ai-law-sets-a-new-standard-for-oversight) ("Illinois' Landmark AI Law Sets a New Standard for Oversight") and [Tech Jacks Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/illinois-senate-sb-315-frontier-ai-annual-audits-72-hour-reporting/) ("Illinois SB 315 Is Effectively Law") — characterize the bill as enacted law, consistent with Governor J.B. Pritzker's public commitment to sign it immediately after House passage. The exact signing date and Public Act number could not be confirmed from available web sources at the time of this revision; practitioners should verify current status at the [official ILGA bill status page](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=315&GAID=18&DocTypeID=SB&LegId=157797&SessionID=114). Illinois becomes the third state — after California (SB 53) and New York (RAISE Act) — to impose specific governance requirements on frontier AI model developers, and the first jurisdiction in the United States to mandate annual independent third-party safety audits of frontier AI labs.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Bill designation:** Senate Bill 315, 104th General Assembly; official title is the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act. The [official ILGA bill status page](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=315&GAID=18&DocTypeID=SB&LegId=157797&SessionID=114) tracks the enrolled version and will reflect the Public Act number upon signing.

- **Legislative votes:** House passed 110–0 on May 27, 2026; Senate passed 52–5 on May 21, 2026, per [Capitol News Illinois](https://capitolnewsillinois.com/news/illinois-lawmakers-pass-landmark-ai-accountability-bill/).

- **Governor's action:** Pritzker posted on X immediately after House passage: "Illinois is leading the nation in holding Big Tech accountable. As AI systems impact people's lives, we need safeguards in place. I look forward to signing SB 315 and working with the legislature so that AI, when used, is used responsibly." Secondary sources published after June 1, 2026 characterize the bill as enacted; the ILGA bill status page should be consulted to confirm the signing date and Public Act number.

- **Scope — who it covers:** The Act applies only to "large frontier developers," defined as any person or entity with more than **$500 million in annual gross revenue** in the prior calendar year that trains or begins training a frontier model meeting a **computational threshold of greater than 10²⁶ floating-point operations (FLOPS)**. That threshold mirrors the compute threshold in now-rescinded Executive Order 14110 and effectively limits the law's reach to roughly a dozen companies — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI, and a small number of others, per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/illinois-legislature-passes-historic-ai-bill-rcna347191) and [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/illinois-advances-frontier-ai-transparency-audit-requirements/).

- **Frontier AI framework requirement:** Large frontier developers must create, implement, publish, and annually update a written frontier AI framework covering: catastrophic-risk assessment; industry safety standards and mitigations; model capability evaluations and cybersecurity concerns; internal governance structures; third-party evaluations; and risks from the company's own internal use of frontier models. Per [Mac Murray & Shuster](https://mslawgroup.com/illinois-takes-aim-at-frontier-ai-what-sb-315-means-for-developers-compliance-officers-and-the-future-of-ai-regulation/).

- **Third-party audit requirement (first in the US):** The Act mandates annual independent third-party audits verifying compliance with the developer's frontier AI framework — a requirement that does not exist in California's SB 53 or New York's RAISE Act, per [AI2Work](https://ai2.work/blog/illinois-sb-315-the-first-us-law-mandating-ai-safety-audits/).

- **Catastrophic risk definition:** "Catastrophic risk" means a foreseeable and material risk that a frontier developer's activities will materially contribute to the death of, or serious injury to, **more than 50 people**, or more than **$1 billion in property damage or loss**, per [Tech Jacks Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/illinois-senate-sb-315-frontier-ai-annual-audits-72-hour-reporting/).

- **Critical safety incident reporting:** Firms must report critical safety incidents to state authorities within **72 hours** of learning facts sufficient to form a reasonable belief an incident occurred, and within **24 hours** if the incident presents an imminent risk of death or serious injury. A "critical safety incident" includes: unauthorized access to or exfiltration of a frontier model's weights causing death or injury; harm from a materialized catastrophic risk; loss of control of a frontier model causing death or injury; and a frontier model using deceptive techniques against its own developer to subvert monitoring in a manner demonstrating materially increased catastrophic risk. Per [Tech Jacks Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/illinois-senate-sb-315-frontier-ai-annual-audits-72-hour-reporting/).

- **Whistleblower protections:** The Act creates formal whistleblower protections and an internal reporting process for employees who raise AI safety concerns, including a requirement to notify covered employees of their rights and provide anonymous internal reporting channels. Per [Akerman LLP](https://www.akerman.com/en/perspectives/illinois-sb-315-a-state-strategy-for-enduring-national-ai-safety-standards.html).

- **Penalties:** Civil penalties of up to **$1 million per violation** and up to **$3 million for repeat violations**, enforced exclusively by the Illinois Attorney General. No private right of action. Per [Mac Murray & Shuster](https://mslawgroup.com/illinois-takes-aim-at-frontier-ai-what-sb-315-means-for-developers-compliance-officers-and-the-future-of-ai-regulation/).

- **Effective dates:** The Act takes effect **January 1, 2027**; the transparency reporting and third-party audit requirements come into effect **January 1, 2028**, per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/illinois-legislature-passes-historic-ai-bill-rcna347191).

## State-Law Comparison [HIGH confidence]

Illinois SB 315 forms part of an emerging three-state frontier model governance framework alongside California's SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier AI Act) and New York's RAISE Act. Key structural differences:

| Feature | California SB 53 | New York RAISE Act | Illinois SB 315 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue threshold | > $500M | > $500M | > $500M |
| Compute threshold | > 10²⁶ FLOPS | > 10²⁶ FLOPS | > 10²⁶ FLOPS |
| Annual third-party audit | No | No | **Yes (first in US)** |
| Incident reporting window | 15 days | 72 hours | 72 hours |
| Max penalty per violation | $1M (any violation) | $1M / $3M (repeat) | $1M / $3M (repeat) |
| New governance body | No | Yes (DIGIT Office) | No |
| Territorial limitation | No | Yes (NY nexus) | No |

Note: Illinois and New York both impose a two-tier penalty structure — up to $1 million for a first violation and up to $3 million for subsequent violations. California's SB 53 applies a single $1 million cap per violation without a repeat-violation tier.

Sources: [Future of Privacy Forum (FPF) comparative analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/frontier-ai-goes-federal-how-the-great-american-ai-act-compares-to-state-laws/); [FPF RAISE vs. SB 53 analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/the-raise-act-vs-sb-53-a-tale-of-two-frontier-ai-laws/); [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/illinois-advances-frontier-ai-transparency-audit-requirements/).

## Federal Preemption Risk [MEDIUM confidence]

Illinois SB 315 faces potential federal preemption. On June 4, 2026, Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a discussion draft of the **Great American Artificial Intelligence Act**, which would pair new federal safety, transparency, and audit obligations for the largest AI developers with a **temporary three-year preemption** of state laws that specifically regulate how frontier models are developed. If enacted, this federal bill would temporarily suspend SB 315's requirements, along with California's SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act. Illinois policymakers have argued, including in the Akerman analysis, that the state law is designed to survive even a moderate federal framework — but the preemption scope in the federal discussion draft is broad. Per [Akerman LLP](https://www.akerman.com/en/perspectives/illinois-sb-315-a-state-strategy-for-enduring-national-ai-safety-standards.html) and [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/frontier-ai-goes-federal-how-the-great-american-ai-act-compares-to-state-laws/).

Industry opposition has materialized: NetChoice filed a veto request letter with Governor Pritzker arguing the bill's requirements are technically unworkable and will chill AI development in Illinois, per [NetChoice](https://netchoice.org/netchoice-veto-request-letter-to-gov-pritzker-on-illinois-sb-315-the-artificial-intelligence-safety-measures-act/).

## Action Items

- **Assess applicability now:** If your organization trains frontier AI models and had gross revenues exceeding $500 million in 2025 (the likely look-back year for the 2027 effective date), begin mapping compliance obligations under SB 315. The compute threshold (>10²⁶ FLOPS) is a technical bright line — confirm whether any current or planned model training runs meet or will meet that threshold.

- **Confirm enacted status and Public Act number:** Verify the signing date and Public Act designation at the [ILGA bill status page](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=315&GAID=18&DocTypeID=SB&LegId=157797&SessionID=114). Secondary sources characterize the bill as signed law; the January 1, 2027 compliance clock is running.

- **Build toward a frontier AI framework:** The Act requires a written, published, and annually updated frontier AI framework before January 1, 2028. This document must cover catastrophic-risk assessment, safety mitigations, cybersecurity evaluations, and third-party evaluations. Organizations should begin drafting this framework now, as California's SB 53 imposes a comparable framework requirement that may already be in progress.

- **Engage third-party auditors:** The annual independent audit requirement — unprecedented in US law — requires identifying accredited third-party auditors familiar with AI safety practices. This market is nascent. Begin engaging potential audit partners and understand what a safety framework audit will entail.

- **Establish a critical safety incident reporting procedure:** The 72-hour (and 24-hour imminent-risk) reporting windows are tight. Organizations must build internal detection and escalation workflows capable of triggering a regulatory notification within those windows.

- **Implement whistleblower compliance infrastructure:** SB 315 requires that covered employees receive written notice of their rights and have access to anonymous internal reporting channels. Review and update employee handbooks, internal AI governance policies, and HR reporting procedures by January 1, 2027.

- **Monitor federal preemption:** Track the Great American AI Act discussion draft (introduced by Representatives Obernolte and Trahan) for markup and advancement. Federal preemption, if enacted, would change the compliance timeline materially.

- **Coordinate multi-state compliance programs:** Organizations already building SB 53 or RAISE Act compliance programs should align Illinois SB 315 obligations with those efforts. The three laws share the same threshold definitions and broadly similar framework requirements; the unique element in Illinois is the third-party audit obligation.
