# President Trump Signs Executive Order on Advanced AI Innovation and Security

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-06-02

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On June 2, 2026, President Trump signed an executive order titled ["Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) — the administration's resolution to a May 21, 2026 standoff in which Trump pulled a nearly identical order hours before signing following last-minute lobbying from David Sacks, Elon Musk, and Mark Zuckerberg. The signed order establishes a voluntary pre-release engagement framework for frontier AI models, directs CISA to issue binding operational directives (BODs) to harden federal civilian systems against AI-enabled threats within 30 days, and requires the Attorney General to prioritize criminal enforcement of existing computer fraud statutes against AI-assisted cybercrimes. Crucially, the order is expressly voluntary for AI developers and explicitly disclaims creating any licensing, pre-clearance, or permitting requirement for AI model development or distribution — a key concession to the innovation camp that blocked the May draft.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The order was signed on June 2, 2026, twelve days after Trump abruptly canceled a nearly identical draft on May 21, 2026. The signed version reduced the voluntary pre-release window from 90 days (in the May draft) to 30 days and softened the scope of the benchmark classification process. ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/president-trump-signs-executive-order-establishing-ai-cybersecurity-and-frontier-model-framework))
- The order explicitly states it "does not create any licensing, preclearance, or permitting requirement for AI model development or distribution," directly addressing the objection raised by Sacks and Musk that voluntary review could become a de facto regulatory choke point. ([Skadden, Arps](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/new-ai-executive-order))
- Within 30 days (by July 2, 2026), the Secretary of Homeland Security, through CISA, must issue Binding Operational Directives (BODs) and guidance to expedite and prioritize the cyber defense of civilian federal government information systems, expand federal programs offering AI-enabled defensive tools, and facilitate access to cybersecurity services — including covered frontier models — for state and local governments and critical infrastructure operators such as rural hospitals, community banks, and local utilities. ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/trump-administration-issues-executive-order-on-ai-and-cybersecurity))
- Within 30 days, Section 2 of the order directs the Secretary of the Treasury — in coordination with the National Cyber Director, NSA, and CISA — to form a voluntary "AI cybersecurity clearinghouse" with the AI industry and critical infrastructure operators to coordinate vulnerability scanning, validate discovered vulnerabilities, and prioritize distribution of patches. ([Sidley Austin / Data Matters Blog](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/04/cyber-strategy-at-the-ai-frontier-president-trump-releases-executive-order-to-promote-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/))
- Within 60 days (by August 1, 2026), the NSA (through the Department of War), CISA, and the Treasury Department must develop and maintain a *classified* benchmarking process to assess the advanced cyber capabilities of AI models and designate which models qualify as "covered frontier models." The NSA Director makes the designation; the National Cyber Director, CISA, and Defense advisors participate but cannot veto. ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260602-new-executive-order-addressing-early-government-access-to-frontier-ai-models))
- Frontier AI developers may *voluntarily* provide the federal government with early access to models for up to 30 days before releasing those models to other trusted partners. Participation is optional; the order does not require developers to submit models before release. ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260605-trump-issues-executive-order-seeking-to-promote-collaboration))
- The Attorney General is directed to prioritize enforcement of 18 U.S.C. §§ 1028 (identity fraud), 1030 (Computer Fraud and Abuse Act), and 1343 (wire fraud), as well as all other applicable federal criminal statutes, against persons who use AI to commit unauthorized computer access or to advance other crimes. ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/president-trump-signs-executive-order-on-advanced-ai-innovation-and-security))
- On June 5, 2026, President Trump issued a companion [National Security Presidential Memorandum (NSPM) on AI in the National Security Enterprise](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/06/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-promotes-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/), establishing a parallel framework for DoD and intelligence community AI procurement and use, organized around four pillars: Adoption, Adaptation, Assurance, and Accountability.
- The [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/06/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-promotes-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) frames the order as building on the July 2025 AI Action Plan ("Winning the Race"), which outlined more than 90 policy recommendations prioritizing US AI dominance over China.

## Background and Policy Context [HIGH confidence]

The June 2, 2026 order is the latest in a sequence of Trump administration AI policy actions:

- **January 20, 2025:** Trump revoked Biden's October 2023 Executive Order 14110 on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy AI, characterizing it as burdensome regulation impeding private-sector innovation.
- **December 11, 2025:** Trump signed ["Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence"](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/) (operative heading: "Eliminating State Law Obstruction of National Artificial Intelligence Policy"), directing federal preemption efforts against state AI laws.
- **July 2025:** The administration released the AI Action Plan ("Winning the Race"), outlining 90+ recommendations to maintain US AI leadership.
- **May 21, 2026:** A draft cybersecurity-focused AI executive order was pulled hours before signing after lobbying by Sacks, Musk, and Zuckerberg against a proposed 90-day pre-release review window. ([A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/trump-administration-issues-executive-order-on-ai-and-cybersecurity))

The June 2 order resolves the intra-administration standoff documented in Politico's reporting on three competing factions (Sacks/innovation camp; Hegseth/frontier-safety camp; and national-security pragmatists at NSA/CISA). The signed version reflects a compromise: mandatory cybersecurity hardening obligations fall on federal agencies, not on AI developers. The developer-facing framework is entirely voluntary.

Analysts at [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-executive-order-shifts-us-ai-policy-toward-national-security/) and [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/trumps-new-ai-executive-order-drastically-shifts-the-administrations-stance-on-the-tech/) note that the order represents a shift from the administration's earlier posture of pure deregulation toward active federal engagement with AI security risks — without imposing obligations on the private sector. The [Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/assessing-trumps-executive-order-on-ai-oversight) characterized the order as incomplete, flagging that the classified nature of the "covered frontier model" designation process makes compliance expectations opaque for developers and limits public accountability.

## Voluntary vs. Mandatory Scope

The order's compliance framework distinguishes sharply between obligations on federal agencies (mandatory) and obligations on AI developers (voluntary):

- **Mandatory:** Federal civilian agencies must comply with CISA BODs and guidance. Critical infrastructure operators may receive expanded access to AI-enabled cybersecurity services, but are not compelled to participate.
- **Voluntary:** AI developers are invited — not required — to submit frontier models for early government review. The order explicitly disclaims creating a regulatory submission requirement. Participation in the AI cybersecurity clearinghouse for vulnerability coordination is also voluntary.

This structure reflects the administration's stated preference for collaborative frameworks over regulatory mandates. [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/trump-executive-order-on-ai-voluntary-framework-cybersecurity-focus-and-key-ta-102n18b/) notes that the voluntary posture means the government's ability to assess frontier model risks before public release depends entirely on developer cooperation — a structural limitation if developers with the most capable models decline to participate.

## Action Items

- **AI developers:** No mandatory action required. Monitor whether CISA or NSA establishes formal channels for voluntary pre-release model submission and assess whether participation serves organizational interests (e.g., access to government markets, relationship with national security community). Note that the 30-day pre-release window is optional.
- **Federal contractors and vendors:** Monitor CISA Binding Operational Directives expected by July 2, 2026. BODs issued under this authority will likely impose updated AI-specific cybersecurity controls on federal civilian systems — vendors supporting those systems should expect corresponding flow-down requirements.
- **Critical infrastructure operators (hospitals, banks, utilities):** CISA is directed to facilitate expanded access to AI-enabled cybersecurity tools and services. Organizations should engage with CISA outreach programs and assess whether new government-provided resources address identified security gaps.
- **Legal and compliance teams:** The AG's direction to prioritize enforcement of 18 U.S.C. §§ 1028, 1030, and 1343 against AI-assisted cybercrimes signals increased DOJ scrutiny of AI-enabled fraud and unauthorized access. Incident response programs should account for AI-assisted attack vectors. Companies that are potential victims should document AI involvement in any breach for potential referral.
- **Policy teams:** Track the classified NSA benchmarking process. The "covered frontier model" designation will ultimately determine which models trigger the voluntary pre-release channel. Although the classification level limits transparency, CISA BODs and subsequent implementing guidance may provide indirect insight into what the government considers threshold capabilities.
- **Track NSPM implementation:** The June 5 NSPM on AI in the National Security Enterprise will drive separate DoD and IC procurement and use requirements. Contractors in the defense/intelligence space should monitor implementing guidance from those agencies.

**Summary:** On July 14, 2026, National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross publicly confirmed that the voluntary AI cybersecurity clearinghouse required by Executive Order 14409 (Section 2, 30-day deadline of July 2, 2026) has been stood up under the codename "Gold Eagle," jointly by the Treasury Department, the Department of Homeland Security, and the Department of War (Pentagon), in consultation with AI companies.
