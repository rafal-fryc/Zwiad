# Commerce Department Lifts Anthropic Export Controls as California Signs Statewide Claude Procurement Deal

**Jurisdiction:** Federal, California | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-06-29

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence] [DISPUTED]

On June 29-30, 2026, two significant Anthropic-related developments landed within days of each other. First, the U.S. Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) formally withdrew the export control directive it had imposed on Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 models on June 12, 2026, after Anthropic agreed to a package of security commitments including a new safety classifier and ongoing coordination with the government on future model releases ([Anthropic, "Redeploying Claude Fable 5"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-says-trump-admin-has-lifted-export-controls-on-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5.html)). Separately, California Governor Gavin Newsom announced a "first-of-its-kind" agreement making Anthropic's Claude available to all state agencies and local governments at a 50% discount, procured centrally through the California Department of Technology's new Statewide IT Shared Services (SITeS) portal ([Governor's Office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/06/29/governor-newsom-announces-a-first-of-its-kind-partnership-providing-anthropic-tools-to-state-agencies-and-improving-services-for-californians/); [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/california-agencies-get-access-to-anthropics-ai-tools-at-half-price/)). <!-- disputed: "SITeS" is expanded here as "Statewide IT Shared Services" but later in the report (Detailed Analysis) as "Statewide Information Technology Shared Services" -- confirmed correct full name is "Statewide Information Technology Shared Services" per StateScoop/CDT; fix the Executive Summary expansion --> Together, the two events mark a rapid swing from federal restriction of Anthropic's most advanced models on national-security grounds to a normalization of access — including preferential adoption by a major state government — within roughly two and a half weeks. The export-control episode is also notable as the first instance of the U.S. government applying export controls directly to AI model *access* rather than to underlying hardware, and it triggered a legal challenge that was mooted when the controls were withdrawn. <!-- disputed: "first instance...applying export controls directly to AI model access" is an uncited factual/precedent claim; CSIS analysis (cited elsewhere in the report) supports characterizing the Section 4817(b)(1) use as unprecedented, but this specific broader framing has no inline citation here -->

## Background [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

**The export control directive.** On June 12, 2026, BIS sent Anthropic an "Is Informed" letter — an administrative mechanism used to impose ad hoc licensing requirements — asserting authority under Section 4817(b)(1) of the Export Control Reform Act of 2018 (ECRA), 50 U.S.C. § 4817(b)(1), and requiring a license before any export, reexport, or in-country transfer of Fable 5 or Mythos 5 to any foreign person, anywhere, including Anthropic's own foreign-national employees ([ExportComplianceDaily](https://exportcompliancedaily.com/article/2026/06/25/lawsuit-us-cant-block-anthropic-models-with-export-controls-that-dont-exist-2606240029); prior Zwiad report on the June 12 directive, [reports/ai-law/frontier-models/federal-anthropic-fable5-mythos5-export-control-2026-06-15.md](../frontier-models/federal-anthropic-fable5-mythos5-export-control-2026-06-15.md)). The trigger was a reported jailbreak vulnerability identified by Amazon researchers that could allow Fable 5's cybersecurity guardrails to be bypassed to surface exploit-relevant information ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/13/anthropic-disables-fable-mythos-export-controls-national-security-threat/)). Because the directive took effect immediately and Anthropic had no reliable real-time mechanism to verify user nationality, the company disabled both models for all customers globally rather than attempt selective compliance ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)).

On June 26, 2026, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick partially relaxed the directive, exempting roughly 100 "trusted partner" organizations participating in Anthropic's Project Glasswing cybersecurity program, along with Anthropic's own foreign-national staff, from the Mythos 5 license requirement — while Fable 5 remained suspended for general use ([Zwiad prior report](../frontier-models/federal-anthropic-fable5-mythos5-export-control-2026-06-15.md)).

**Legal uncertainty and challenge.** Commentators, including the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), characterized the action as an unprecedented use of ECRA authority: BIS had never before relied on Section 4817(b)(1) as a freestanding basis for a control absent an implementing rule in the Export Administration Regulations, and it remained legally contested whether a foreign national's remote query of a model hosted on Anthropic's own U.S. servers constitutes an "export" at all ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/department-commerce-restricted-access-anthropics-latest-models-what-comes-next); [Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/142745/law-anthropic-export-controls/)). On June 23, 2026, legal-technology company Legion filed suit against President Trump, Secretary Lutnick, and BIS Undersecretary Jeffrey Kessler, arguing the directive exceeded ECRA's export-control authority ("export controls that don't exist") ([ExportComplianceDaily](https://exportcompliancedaily.com/article/2026/06/25/lawsuit-us-cant-block-anthropic-models-with-export-controls-that-dont-exist-2606240029)). The plaintiff voluntarily dismissed the case after the controls were lifted, so the underlying legal questions were not resolved by a court ([JD Journal](https://www.jdjournal.com/2026/07/07/anthropic-ai-access-restored-legal-tech-lawsuit/)).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

**Withdrawal of the export controls.** On June 30, 2026, Anthropic announced that BIS had formally withdrawn its export-control directive after what the company described as "productive" discussions with the government ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)). Under the resulting arrangement, Anthropic agreed to: (1) deploy an improved safety classifier specifically designed to detect and block the cybersecurity-exploit behavior that had triggered the original Amazon-reported jailbreak; (2) proactively detect and address future security risks in these and subsequent frontier models; (3) work with the U.S. government on protocols and standards governing model release; and (4) inform the government of malicious activity involving the models ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-says-trump-admin-has-lifted-export-controls-on-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5.html); [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/30/tech/anthropic-export-control-ban-lifted-white-house)). Fable 5 became available again to Claude Platform, Claude.ai, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork users globally starting July 1, 2026, with usage-limit accommodations (up to 50% of weekly usage limits at no additional cost through July 7) for Pro, Max, Team, and select Enterprise plans ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)). Mythos 5 access was similarly restored, building on the partial exemption Lutnick had already granted on June 26 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/redeploying-fable-5)). No formal ECRA rulemaking implementing AI-model export controls has been published in the Federal Register as of this writing; the episode was resolved through negotiated withdrawal of an informal directive rather than through notice-and-comment rulemaking, leaving the underlying legal authority question (whether remote model access constitutes an "export" under ECRA/EAR) unaddressed for future cases ([CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/department-commerce-restricted-access-anthropics-latest-models-what-comes-next)).

**California-Anthropic government procurement agreement.** On the same day the export controls were resolving, June 29, 2026, Governor Newsom's office announced a statewide agreement with Anthropic ([Governor's Office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/06/29/governor-newsom-announces-a-first-of-its-kind-partnership-providing-anthropic-tools-to-state-agencies-and-improving-services-for-californians/)). Under the deal:

- State departments and agencies may purchase Claude seats at a 50% discount, with free workforce training and Anthropic-provided technical/workflow assistance ([California Department of Technology](https://www.cdt.ca.gov/newsroom/2026/07/california-expands-ai-access-across-state-government-through-anthropic-partnership/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/29/anthropic-and-gov-newsom-forge-deal-allowing-california-government-to-use-claude-at-half-price/)).
- Procurement is centralized through the California Department of Technology's newly created Statewide Information Technology Shared Services (SITeS) portal, which allows agencies to access Claude without running individual competitive-bid processes ([StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/california-agencies-get-access-to-anthropics-ai-tools-at-half-price/)).
- The same discounted pricing and support terms extend to California cities and counties, not just state agencies ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/newsoms-office-touts-anthropic-partnership-50-discount-claude-ai-california-agencies-localities)).
- Existing pilot uses cited by the state include the DMV (customer service) and the Department of Health Care Services (internal workflows) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/29/anthropic-and-gov-newsom-forge-deal-allowing-california-government-to-use-claude-at-half-price/)).
- Newsom's office emphasized that generative AI is meant to "augment, not replace" state employees, and that individual agencies retain responsibility for accuracy, transparency, and privacy compliance when deploying the tools ([Governor's Office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/06/29/governor-newsom-announces-a-first-of-its-kind-partnership-providing-anthropic-tools-to-state-agencies-and-improving-services-for-californians/)).

The agreement does not itself create new binding legal obligations — it is a procurement/pricing arrangement, not legislation or rulemaking — but it operates against the backdrop of California's existing AI governance requirements (e.g., state AI use policies, algorithmic transparency and privacy obligations under the CCPA/CPRA and related executive orders) that continue to apply to any agency deployment of Claude.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

**For Anthropic and frontier AI developers generally:** The episode demonstrates that the federal government is willing to use export-control authority as a lever over frontier model deployment even without an implementing regulation, and that such directives can be issued and reversed on short timescales through negotiation rather than litigation or rulemaking. Companies operating comparably advanced models should anticipate similar informal "is-informed letter" actions and should have compliance playbooks (age/nationality verification is largely infeasible at scale, so the realistic mitigation path is negotiated security commitments, as Anthropic pursued here).

**For state and local government AI procurement:** The California-Anthropic deal is likely to be referenced as a model by other states considering centralized AI procurement vehicles. Because it channels adoption through a single shared-services portal at negotiated pricing, other jurisdictions may look to SITeS as a template for reducing per-agency contracting friction. Vendors of competing AI tools may see this as precedent for seeking similar statewide framework agreements.

**Legal/regulatory outlook:** Because BIS withdrew its directive rather than defending it in court (the Legion suit was voluntarily dismissed), no judicial precedent was created on whether remote access to a U.S.-hosted model by a foreign national constitutes a regulable "export" under ECRA. This question remains open and could resurface with a future frontier-model release or a different administration posture ([Just Security](https://www.justsecurity.org/142745/law-anthropic-export-controls/); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/department-commerce-restricted-access-anthropics-latest-models-what-comes-next)).

## Action Items

- Frontier-model developers should monitor BIS Federal Register notices for any follow-on formal rulemaking that would codify export-control thresholds for AI model access (none has been published as of this writing).
- Enterprises and government agencies using Fable 5/Mythos 5 internationally should confirm current access status directly with Anthropic, since restoration was phased (Fable 5 from July 1; Mythos 5 tied to the June 26 exemption list plus the June 30 broader withdrawal).
- State and local government IT/procurement offices in other jurisdictions should track SITeS as a potential template for centralized AI vendor agreements, including how it addresses data privacy, transparency, and accuracy oversight obligations at the agency level.
- California agencies deploying Claude under the new agreement should confirm compliance with existing state AI use policies and applicable privacy law (CCPA/CPRA) independent of the procurement discount, since the agreement does not waive those obligations.
- Watch for any renewed export-control action tied to future Anthropic frontier model releases, given BIS's demonstrated willingness to use informal "is-informed letter" directives.
