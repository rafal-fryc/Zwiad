# Trump Pulls AI Cybersecurity Executive Order Hours Before Signing Amid Industry Lobbying

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-05-22

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

President Trump abruptly canceled the signing of a landmark AI cybersecurity executive order on May 21, 2026 — hours before the ceremony was scheduled and after technology executives had already begun traveling to the White House to attend. The order would have established a voluntary pre-release review framework for frontier AI models through the Office of the National Cyber Director (ONCD), addressing security risks from advanced AI systems. Trump personally cited concerns that the order could impede US AI competitiveness against China, following last-minute calls from former AI czar David Sacks, Elon Musk, and Mark Zuckerberg. The White House has since stated it is "studying" revised approaches to AI security, but no timeline has been provided.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Trump abruptly postponed the signing on May 21, 2026, telling reporters: "I didn't like certain aspects of it. We're leading China, we're leading everybody, and I don't want to do anything that's going to get in the way of that lead." ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/21/trump-ai-executive-order-postponed.html))
- The signing was canceled after last-minute calls to Trump from former White House AI and crypto czar David Sacks, Elon Musk, and Mark Zuckerberg — all of whom opposed the order. A source familiar with the situation told [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/21/trump-ai-executive-order-postponed-why) that Trump "just hates regulation" and that Sacks "hated it" as well.
- The draft order, published by Politico after the cancellation and analyzed by [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/22/ai-executive-order-cancelled-white-house), would have created a voluntary framework requiring AI developers to submit frontier models to the federal government at least 90 days before public release for classified cybersecurity benchmarking.
- The review process would have been led by the NSA, CISA, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, with the Treasury Department also given a prominent role — a structural choice that drew criticism from cybersecurity experts who noted Treasury's limited AI security expertise. ([Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/may/22/heres-donald-trumps-postponed-ai-executive-order-would-done/))
- The order was split into two components: (1) a frontier model cybersecurity review framework, and (2) broader cybersecurity hardening measures for DoD, federal agencies, hospitals, and banks, including AI-threat sharing programs. ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/heres-the-executive-order-on-ai-that-gave-trump-cold-feet-2000762751))
- The cancellation came directly in the wake of the White House's May 2026 review process, which itself was triggered by Anthropic's restricted release of Claude Mythos — a frontier model with autonomous zero-day vulnerability discovery capabilities. ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/22/tech-billionaires-convince-trump-to-back-off-ai-executive-order-but-much-of-maga-favors-ai-regulation/))
- OpenAI was reported to have supported the order, putting it at odds with xAI (Musk) and Meta (Zuckerberg), who lobbied against it. ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/05/22/last-minute-lobbying-by-tech-industry-officials-led-trump-cancel-ai-order/))
- The White House's Office of the National Cyber Director indicated it continues to work on AI security initiatives separate from the now-shelved executive order. ([Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/05/wh-studying-ai-security-executive-order/))

## California Counterpoint [HIGH confidence]

On the same day as the federal cancellation, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed [Executive Order N-6-26](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/05/21/governor-newsom-signs-first-of-its-kind-executive-order-to-prepare-workers-and-businesses-for-potential-ai-disruption/), described as the first-of-its-kind order by a US governor to prepare workers, businesses, and communities for AI-driven economic disruption. The order directs the Labor and Workforce Development Agency (LWDA) to:

- Study potential labor market shifts tied to AI, including layoffs, hiring changes, and skills gaps
- Report within 180 days on potential updates to California's WARN Act to strengthen early warning systems for AI-displaced workers
- By October 15, 2026, review collective bargaining practices involving AI and develop an "AI Playbook" for dislocated workers in AI-exposed occupations
- Review policies on severance and displaced-worker safety nets, with a comparative analysis of other countries' approaches

The Newsom order carries no binding private-employer obligations and does not establish new statutory rights of action, but it sets up a 180-day policy development cycle that could inform future California legislation. ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/california-governor-aims-to-soften-ais-impact-on-workers-through-new-executive-order); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/California-Governor-issued-Executive-Order-on-AI-workforce-disruption))

## Why It Matters [HIGH confidence]

The Trump order's collapse is significant for several reasons:

**Federal AI governance vacuum.** The cancellation leaves no statutory or executive framework governing pre-release review of frontier AI models at the federal level. The ONCD's informal review meetings (held in late April 2026 with major AI developers) remain the closest analog to structured oversight, but carry no legal force. This creates regulatory uncertainty — AI companies cannot calibrate compliance postures against requirements that do not exist.

**Revealed administration fractures.** The episode exposed a deep internal split: national security officials and portions of the administration argued that the Anthropic Mythos model's capabilities required government oversight before deployment, while Sacks, industry allies, and Trump himself prioritized minimal regulation to accelerate commercial AI development. The public last-minute reversal is unusual even by recent executive branch standards.

**Industry influence over federal AI policy.** The successful lobbying by Sacks, Musk, and Zuckerberg establishes a clear precedent that a coalition of prominent AI developers can block executive action on AI safety through direct access to the President. This has implications for the likelihood of any future federal AI governance measure advancing without industry consent.

**State-federal divergence widens.** Newsom's simultaneous signing of N-6-26 underscores the growing contrast between California's proactive (if non-binding) workforce AI governance posture and the federal government's indefinite deferral. Employers operating in California should track the LWDA's 180-day reporting cycle, as it will likely seed legislative proposals in the 2026-27 California legislative session.

## Action Items

- **AI developers and deployers:** Monitor the White House ONCD for any revised executive order or informal AI security guidance; the administration has indicated it is studying alternatives, but no timeline exists. Voluntary engagement with ONCD's ongoing review process may be prudent.
- **California employers:** Track the LWDA's October 15, 2026 AI workforce disruption report and recommendations for potential WARN Act amendments; plan for possible enhanced disclosure requirements for AI-driven layoffs in 2027.
- **Compliance and legal teams:** Document the absence of a signed federal frontier model review framework in risk assessments; assess how the regulatory vacuum affects obligations under existing OMB memoranda (M-25-21, M-25-22) and sector-specific AI guidance.
- **National security contractors:** The cybersecurity hardening provisions in the shelved order — DoD, hospital, and banking infrastructure security — may resurface in standalone rulemaking or appropriations riders; track CISA and ONCD rulemaking dockets.
