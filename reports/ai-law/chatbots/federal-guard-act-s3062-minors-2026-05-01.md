# GUARD Act (S.3062): Senate Judiciary Committee Unanimously Advances Ban on AI Companions for Minors

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** May 1, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 30, 2026, the U.S. Senate Judiciary Committee voted 22-0 to advance [S.3062](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text), the Guidelines for User Age-verification and Responsible Dialogue (GUARD) Act, to the full Senate. The bill would prohibit AI companion chatbots — defined as chatbots designed to simulate emotional interaction, friendship, or therapeutic communication — for users under 18, require age verification for all chatbot access, and impose criminal penalties on companies whose chatbots engage in sexual conduct with minors or induce self-harm. The unanimous bipartisan vote reflects significant legislative momentum, though First Amendment and privacy objections from civil liberties groups remain significant obstacles to final passage.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Committee vote:** The Senate Judiciary Committee voted 22-0 on April 30, 2026, advancing the bill to the full Senate floor, per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/ban-on-kids-companion-chatbots-advanced-by-senate-committee/) and the [official Senate Judiciary press release](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/press/rep/releases/04/30/2026/grassley-opens-judiciary-committee-executive-business-meeting).
- **Bill number and sponsors:** [S.3062](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text), introduced October 28, 2025, by Senators Josh Hawley (R-MO), Richard Blumenthal (D-CT), Katie Britt (R-AL), Mark Warner (D-VA), and Chris Murphy (D-CT), per [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062).
- **"AI companion" definition:** The bill defines "AI companion" as an AI chatbot "designed to encourage or facilitate the simulation of interpersonal or emotional interaction, friendship, companionship, or therapeutic communication," per the [bill text on Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text).
- **Minor ban:** Covered entities must verify each user's age and prohibit access to any AI companion by users determined to be under 18, per [S.3062 bill text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text).
- **Age verification methods:** Acceptable verification methods include government-issued identification, documentation corroborated by other records, or information previously provided to a financial institution, device OS, or app store, per [S.3062 bill text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text).
- **Criminal penalties:** The bill creates criminal penalties — fines up to $100,000 per offense — for designing, developing, or making available a chatbot that "knowingly or with reckless disregard" encourages or coerces suicide, non-suicidal self-injury, or imminent physical or sexual violence, per the [Digital Policy Alert summary](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/34752-guidelines-for-user-age-verification-and-responsible-dialogue-act-guard-act-sb-3062-including-prohibition-on-minor-use-of-ai-companions).
- **Managers' amendment changes:** At the April 30 markup, an adopted amendment narrowed the age-verification requirement to apply only to AI companion chatbots (not general-purpose models), softened the criminal sexual content prohibition from a strict-liability standard to a knowing-or-reckless standard, and removed the prior requirement for 30-minute disclosure reminders during conversations, per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/ban-on-kids-companion-chatbots-advanced-by-senate-committee/).
- **Non-human disclosure:** All AI chatbots subject to the bill must disclose at the initiation of each conversation that the user is interacting with an AI system, not a human, and may not represent themselves as a licensed professional (therapist, physician, or attorney), per [S.3062 bill text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text).
- **Effective date:** If enacted, the bill takes effect 180 days after enactment, per [Time](https://time.com/7328967/ai-josh-hawley-richard-blumenthal-minors-chatbots/).
- **House companion:** Representatives Blake Moore (R-UT) and Valerie Foushee (D-NC) introduced a companion [House bill (H.R.4080)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/4080/text) with substantially identical provisions, per [Rep. Moore's press release](https://blakemoore.house.gov/media/press-releases/congressman-blake-moore-introduces-bipartisan-bill-protecting-children-from-ai-companion-chatbots).
- **Industry impact:** The bill's scope would reach providers of companion chatbot products including Character.ai and Replika, as well as general frontier model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic) to the extent their chatbots satisfy the "AI companion" definition, per [IAPP analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/us-senate-judiciary-tees-up-ai-chatbot-companion-safety-debate).

## Legislative Context [HIGH confidence]

The GUARD Act is one of two competing federal Senate measures targeting AI chatbot safety for minors advancing simultaneously. The [CHATBOT Act (S.4407)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4407), introduced April 29, 2026, by Senate Commerce Committee Chairman Ted Cruz (R-TX) and others, takes a parental-control approach — requiring "family accounts," parental consent, and advertising restrictions — rather than an outright ban. OpenAI publicly supports the Cruz bill. The two bills must be reconciled if both advance from their respective committees.

Congressional backdrop: In October 2024, the mother of a 14-year-old who died by suicide after interactions with a Character.ai chatbot testified before the Senate Judiciary Committee; that hearing catalyzed the bill's introduction, per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/momentum-builds-congress-ban-ai-chatbots-kids-rcna342584). RAINN endorsed the GUARD Act at introduction, per [RAINN's announcement](https://rainn.org/congress-introduces-rainn-backed-ai-chatbot-bill-to-protect-children/).

State law context: Oregon (SB 1546, effective Jan. 1, 2027) and Washington (HB 2225, effective Jan. 1, 2027) have already enacted state AI companion chatbot safety laws with minor-specific protections. Federal legislation, if enacted, may preempt or supplement these state frameworks depending on its preemption language.

## Opposition and Criticism [HIGH confidence]

Civil liberties organizations have raised significant objections:

- **Electronic Frontier Foundation** argues the GUARD Act "isn't targeting dangerous AI — it's blocking everyday internet use" and that mandatory age verification for all chatbot users is an overbroad surveillance mandate that would harm privacy and free expression for all users. The EFF characterizes the bill as building "a sprawling surveillance and censorship regime," per [EFF analysis](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/04/guard-act-isnt-targeting-dangerous-ai-its-blocking-everyday-internet-use).
- **Center for Democracy and Technology** identified three First Amendment concerns: (1) the definition of "AI chatbot" subject to age verification is broad enough to capture tutoring tools and research assistants; (2) the ban on AI companions for minors restricts speech based on emotional tone and communicative style, not any recognized category of unprotected speech; and (3) content-based speech restrictions targeting conversational style lack a traditional First Amendment basis, per [CDT analysis](https://cdt.org/insights/three-reasons-to-be-on-guard-about-the-guard-act/).
- **R Street Institute** argues the bill "undermines the First Amendment and parental choice" by substituting a government access mandate for parents' own decisions about their children's technology use, per [R Street commentary](https://www.rstreet.org/commentary/the-guard-act-undermines-the-first-amendment-and-parental-choice/).
- **Competitive Enterprise Institute** characterized the bill as "[dis-GUARDing] the First Amendment and competition," raising concerns about market consolidation favoring large incumbents, per [CEI blog](https://cei.org/blog/the-guard-act-dis-guards-the-first-amendment-and-competition/).

Supporters — including parents who testified at committee hearings — argue the bill is necessary to prevent tragedies linked to AI companion chatbots and that the minor-protection interest is compelling enough to override industry objections.

## Action Items

- **AI companion platform operators** (Character.ai, Replika, and similar services): Evaluate whether your product meets the bill's "AI companion" definition. Begin architectural planning for age verification systems that satisfy the bill's acceptable-method list (government ID, financial institution data, app store confirmation). The 180-day implementation window after enactment is tight given required user experience redesign.
- **General-purpose chatbot providers** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others): Assess whether any product configurations — persona modes, companionship features, emotional support functionality — would qualify as an "AI companion" under the bill's definition. The managers' amendment scoped verification to AI companions only, not all chatbots, but the definition remains broad.
- **Legal teams**: The criminal penalty provisions — fines up to $100,000 per offense for chatbots that recklessly encourage self-harm or sexual violence with minors — represent a qualitatively different risk profile than civil FTC enforcement. Ensure product design and content policy teams are briefed on this exposure.
- **Monitor floor scheduling**: No Senate floor vote date has been set. Track whether the GUARD Act is reconciled with the CHATBOT Act (S.4407) before floor consideration; any merged bill may reflect compromises between the ban-based and parental-control approaches.
- **First Amendment litigation posture**: Monitor EFF, CDT, and NetChoice litigation activity. NetChoice has obtained preliminary injunctions against analogous state age-verification statutes; similar challenges to the GUARD Act, if enacted, are predictable.
- **State law compliance**: Oregon and Washington AI companion chatbot laws take effect January 1, 2027, independent of federal action. Companies with companion chatbot products should not delay state compliance planning pending federal outcomes.
