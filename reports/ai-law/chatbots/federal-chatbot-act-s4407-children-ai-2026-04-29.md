# CHATBOT Act (S.4407): Bipartisan Senate Bill Would Require Family Accounts for Children's AI Chatbot Use

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** AI Law | **Date:** 2026-04-29

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 29, 2026, U.S. Senate Commerce Committee Chairman Ted Cruz (R-TX) and Senators Brian Schatz (D-HI), John Curtis (R-UT), and Adam Schiff (D-CA) introduced [S.4407](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4407), the Children's Health, Advancement, Trust, Boundaries, and Oversight in Technology (CHATBOT) Act. The bill would require AI chatbot providers to establish mandatory "family accounts" for users under 13 and make them available — with required parental consent — for teen users, while banning targeted advertising to minors and restricting manipulative design features. The bill joins a competing measure (S.3062, the GUARD Act) advancing through the Senate Judiciary Committee, creating legislative pressure for floor action before Congress adjourns.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Sponsors:** Senators Ted Cruz (R-TX), Brian Schatz (D-HI), John Curtis (R-UT), and Adam Schiff (D-CA) introduced the bill on April 29, 2026, according to the [Senate Commerce Committee press release](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/rep/release/cruz-schatz-curtis-schiff-introduce-new-bill-giving-parents-control-over-kids-ai-chatbot-use/).
- **Bill number:** [S.4407](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4407), 119th Congress, introduced in the Senate Commerce Committee.
- **Family accounts for under-13s:** Chatbot providers must create family accounts for children under 13, giving parents control over privacy settings, session time limits, and access to full conversation logs, per the [Senate Commerce Committee one-pager](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/CHATBOT-Act-One-Pager-with-Logo.pdf).
- **Teen opt-in with parental consent:** For users ages 13-17, family accounts are optional but require verifiable parental consent before the teen may create a chatbot account; if parents decline to establish a family account, the chatbot's settings default to their most protective level.
- **Advertising prohibition:** The bill bans chatbot providers from using minors' personal data for targeted advertising, according to [Roll Call's coverage](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/29/family-accounts-would-be-mandated-for-kids-in-chatbots-bill/).
- **Manipulative design limits:** The bill prohibits rewards, push notifications, and other engagement-boosting features directed at minors.
- **Enforcement:** The FTC and state attorneys general receive enforcement authority; no private right of action is created.
- **Research mandate:** The National Science Foundation must study chatbots' effects on children's mental health, relationships, and social development; the Government Accountability Office must report on compliance and family account effectiveness.
- **Industry support:** OpenAI publicly supports the Cruz bill, per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/29/family-accounts-would-be-mandated-for-kids-in-chatbots-bill/). The bill is positioned as a parental-control model rather than an outright ban.

## Legislative Context [HIGH confidence]

The CHATBOT Act is one of at least two competing Senate proposals targeting AI chatbot safety for minors introduced in the same week:

**S.3062 — GUARD Act (Hawley):** The Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously passed Senator Josh Hawley's (R-MO) [GUARD Act (S.3062)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text) on April 30, 2026, with a 22-0 vote, according to [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/ban-on-kids-companion-chatbots-advanced-by-senate-committee/). The GUARD Act takes a harder approach: it would ban AI "companion" chatbots for minors entirely, impose criminal penalties on companies whose chatbots engage in sexually explicit conduct with minors or solicit self-harm, and mandate non-human disclosure at the start of each conversation. Parents' advocacy groups have rallied behind the Hawley bill; the AI industry, including OpenAI, favors the Cruz approach.

The two bills reflect a broader congressional push on child online safety. Related federal and state measures include the [FPF 2026 Chatbot Legislation Tracker](https://fpf.org/2026-chatbot-legislation-tracker/), which catalogs pending chatbot bills across jurisdictions.

## Industry Opposition [MEDIUM confidence]

[NetChoice](https://netchoice.org/chatbots-shouldnt-be-legislative-vehicles-for-government-censorship/), whose members include OpenAI, Google, and Meta, argues the CHATBOT Act "runs into protected rights" under the First Amendment. NetChoice VP Amy Bos stated the bill "takes the same unlawful identity mandate framework NetChoice has successfully challenged in court across the country — including ID gates, requirements to hand over documentation proving parent-child relationships, and state-supervised access to lawful speech — and ports those onto AI tools." NetChoice has successfully obtained preliminary injunctions against similar state-level age-verification statutes, making First Amendment litigation a credible risk if the CHATBOT Act passes. A [Reason analysis](https://reason.com/2026/05/04/how-a-bill-banning-ai-companions-for-kids-could-usher-in-widespread-id-checks-online/) also raises concerns that the bill's identity-verification requirements could normalize widespread identity checks across online platforms.

## Legislative Prospects

The CHATBOT Act was introduced directly by the Senate Commerce Committee chairman, giving it a procedural advantage for markup. However, the bill must be reconciled with the GUARD Act if both advance from their respective committees. The competing approaches — parental controls versus outright ban — may require negotiation before any floor vote. The [Axios bipartisan AI roundup](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/28/congress-ramps-up-bipartisan-ai-efforts) notes that April 2026 saw a wave of bipartisan AI legislative activity, but floor scheduling in the current Congress remains uncertain.

## Action Items

- **AI chatbot providers** (particularly those with users under 18): Assess current account management architecture and identify what engineering changes would be required to implement family accounts, parental consent flows, and conversation logging — even if the bill is not yet law. The likelihood of some form of child-focused AI regulation passing in 2026 is elevated given bipartisan sponsorship and committee activity.
- **Advertising-supported AI platforms**: Review minor-targeting advertising practices now. Both the CHATBOT Act and GUARD Act restrict data use for minors; a ban on targeted advertising to minors in AI contexts appears likely to be included in any final legislation.
- **Legal/compliance teams**: Monitor the GUARD Act's floor prospects separately from S.4407. If Hawley's bill advances without Cruz's, companion chatbot products for minors could face criminal liability exposure — a qualitatively different risk profile than civil FTC enforcement under the CHATBOT Act.
- **First Amendment considerations**: Companies considering challenging any enacted legislation should monitor NetChoice litigation strategy, which has produced preliminary injunctions in multiple states against analogous age-verification requirements.
- **Track companion state legislation**: Washington (HB 2225, effective Jan. 1, 2027) and Oregon (SB 1546, effective Jan. 1, 2027) have already enacted AI chatbot safety laws with minor-specific protections. Federal legislation, if enacted, may preempt or supplement these state frameworks.
