# CalPrivacy Opens Preliminary Comment Period on Data Broker DROP Audits

**Bottom line:** The California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy / CPPA) is soliciting preliminary stakeholder input to shape forthcoming audit regulations for data brokers participating in the Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP). Comments are due **May 7, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. PT**. Data brokers, prospective third-party auditors, and advisors should file now — this is the pre-rulemaking window that sets the baseline for audits starting in 2028.

## What Happened [HIGH confidence]

On April 7, 2026, CalPrivacy issued an [Invitation for Preliminary Comments on DROP Audits](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/drop.html), inviting stakeholder input to support future rulemaking under the California Delete Act (SB 362) and related CCPA provisions. The [announcement from the National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/hot-press-calprivacy-inviting-preliminary-comments-delete-request-and-opt-out) confirms the comment window runs from April 7, 2026 to May 7, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. PT, and that the comments are intended to "assist CalPrivacy with its preliminary rulemaking activities" without reflecting any decisions yet made by the Agency.

According to the IAPP U.S. Privacy Digest item that originated this finding, the Agency is specifically soliciting feedback on:

- **Credential requirements** for third-party auditors;
- **Audit standards** and methodology; and
- **AI and security safeguards** potentially required of data brokers and their auditors.

## Background: DROP and the Delete Act [HIGH confidence]

The DROP system is the statewide accessible deletion mechanism mandated by the California Delete Act. Per CalPrivacy's [Data Brokers page](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/) and the [DROP regulations page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/drop.html), DROP lets a California consumer submit a single deletion request that is propagated to every registered data broker. DROP became available to consumers on **January 1, 2026**.

The Delete Act further requires that, **beginning January 1, 2028, and every three years thereafter**, every registered data broker undergo an audit by an independent third party assessing compliance with the statute; the data broker must submit the audit report to the Agency upon written request. These forthcoming DROP-audit regulations will define who may perform those audits and what they must cover.

CalPrivacy's operational readiness has accelerated in parallel: the Agency formally established an **Audits Division** in early 2026 under Chief Privacy Auditor Sabrina Boyce Ross, as reported by the [Cybersecurity Law Report](https://www.cslawreport.com/21398736/calprivacy-director-discusses-new-audits-division-and-other-2026actions-to-come.thtml) and [Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2026/03/calprivacy-update-shifting-to-structural-compliance-and-auditing/). That institutional build-out signals the Agency intends to enforce the audit regime rigorously once it takes effect.

## Why This Matters [HIGH confidence]

1. **Scope of affected entities.** Every entity on California's [Data Broker Registry](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_broker_registry/) will be subject to triennial audits once regulations are finalized. The Delete Act's definition sweeps in a broad class of businesses that knowingly collect and sell personal information of consumers with whom they do not have a direct relationship.

2. **Auditor market formation.** Credentialing decisions (e.g., CPA-style licensing, privacy-specific certifications, independence requirements) will determine who can supply audit services. Early stakeholder input is the cheapest way to influence those gates before a draft rule is published.

3. **AI safeguards.** By flagging AI as a dedicated topic, CalPrivacy is signaling that algorithmic processing by data brokers — including inference, enrichment, and automated decisioning — will likely be within audit scope. This aligns with CalPrivacy's broader 2026 rulemaking on automated decisionmaking technology (ADMT), risk assessments, and cybersecurity audits tracked on the [CCPA Updates regulations page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html).

4. **Enforcement posture.** CalPrivacy has already signaled an aggressive data-broker enforcement stance, including the Data Broker Strike Force described by [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/california-privacy-agency-launches-data-broker-strike-force-amid-delete-act-crackdown). The audit regime is the structural backbone of that enforcement program.

## Action Items

- **Data brokers on the registry:** Inventory current compliance evidence (deletion workflows, DROP integration logs, vendor contracts, security controls) against likely audit topics, and submit comments on cost, feasibility, and scope.
- **In-house privacy / audit teams:** Decide whether to argue for credential standards the organization's existing auditors already meet (e.g., SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / IAPP certifications) versus bespoke CalPrivacy-specific credentials.
- **Prospective third-party auditors (law firms, accounting firms, boutique privacy firms):** File comments on independence, conflict-of-interest, and qualification criteria.
- **Technology and AI vendors serving data brokers:** File comments on how AI safeguards should be scoped (inputs, models, outputs, human review) to avoid over-broad obligations cascading to upstream vendors.
- **Calendar:** Comments must be submitted per instructions on [cppa.ca.gov/regulations/drop.html](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/drop.html) by **5:00 p.m. PT, May 7, 2026**.

## Open Questions [MEDIUM confidence]

- Will CalPrivacy require auditor licensure analogous to CPA independence rules, or a novel privacy-audit credential?
- How will DROP audits interact with the separate CCPA **cybersecurity audit** rule that took effect earlier in 2026 (see [Ropes & Gray analysis](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/californias-ccpa-cybersecurity-audit-rule-takes-effect-what-businesses-need-to-know))? Overlapping scopes may create duplicative obligations.
- Will "AI safeguards" in DROP audits mirror the ADMT rulemaking's risk-assessment requirements, or develop as a distinct standard?
