# State Privacy Law Tracker: Maine LD 1822 Stalls, Ohio HB 807 Targets Data Broker Sales, Multiple States Face Session Deadlines

**Jurisdiction:** Multi-State (ME, OH, AL, KS, KY, NE, TN) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

Maine's comprehensive consumer data privacy bill (LD 1822) suffered a significant setback when five Democratic legislators reversed their positions on a procedural House vote, potentially killing one of the most protective state privacy proposals in the country. Meanwhile, Ohio introduced HB 807 on March 31, 2026, a Democrat-sponsored bill that would prohibit government and private entities from selling sensitive personal data to data brokers for profit. Several other states with active privacy bills -- including Alabama, Kansas, Kentucky, Nebraska, and Tennessee -- face April legislative deadlines that will determine whether additional states join the 20 that already have comprehensive privacy laws in effect.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

The state privacy law landscape has expanded rapidly in recent years. As of early 2026, [20 states have comprehensive privacy laws in effect](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/2/4/all-of-the-comprehensive-privacy-laws-that-take-effect-in-2026), with Indiana, Kentucky, and Rhode Island joining the ranks on January 1, 2026. The current roster includes California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, and Virginia ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/new-year-new-rules-us-state-privacy-requirements-coming-online-as-2026-begins)).

Maine's LD 1822, titled "An Act to Enact the Maine Online Data Privacy Act," had been considered one of the strongest privacy bills in the country. Sponsored by Rep. Amy Kuhn (D-Falmouth), the bill would have granted consumers a right no other state provides: the right to know the actual names of every third party to which a controller sells their personal data ([Verrill](https://www.verrill-law.com/news/maines-house-of-representatives-adopts-comprehensive-privacy-act/)). The bill previously [passed the House in amended form](https://www.maine.gov/housedems/news/house-passes-amended-version-kuhn-data-privacy-bill) and then passed the Maine Senate, but because the Senate adopted changes, it required House concurrence on the amended version.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### Maine LD 1822: Democratic Defections Derail Privacy Bill

According to [Troutman Pepper Locke's April 6, 2026 tracker](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/proposed-state-privacy-law-update-april-5464708/), five key Democrats flipped their votes on a procedural motion, dealing a potentially fatal blow to LD 1822. The bill had faced strong opposition from Maine businesses, and the vote reversal came during a return to the House for concurrence on Senate amendments.

Key provisions of LD 1822 included robust data minimization requirements limiting collection to what is necessary for service delivery, a prohibition on collecting biometrics unless strictly necessary, strong civil rights protections against data-driven discrimination, and an effective date of September 1, 2027 (delayed from an earlier July 2026 date via House amendment) ([ACLU of Maine](https://www.aclumaine.org/press-releases/maine-house-passes-landmark-privacy-bill/); [EPIC](https://epic.org/maine-senate-passes-strong-privacy-bill/)). The bill's official text and status are tracked on the [Maine Legislature website](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?LD=1822&snum=132).

### Ohio HB 807: Targeting Data Broker Profit from Sensitive Data

Ohio [HB 807](https://www.legislature.ohio.gov/legislation/136/hb807), introduced on March 31, 2026, takes a narrower approach than comprehensive privacy bills. The bill would amend Ohio Revised Code sections 1347.01, 1347.10, and 1347.99 and enact new section 1347.072 to prohibit government and private entities from selling sensitive personal data to data brokers or private entities for profit, except for enumerated permitted purposes ([LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/OH/bill/HB807/2025?guid=7OmmWoER0iQrvZB0fx1pSU)). The bill is a partisan Democrat measure sponsored by Representatives Sean P. Brennan, Juanita O. Brent, Darnell T. Brewer, Dontavius L. Jarrells, Joseph A. Miller III, C. Allison Russo, and Veronica R. Sims. The bill has been introduced but has not yet received a committee hearing.

### States Facing April Session Deadlines

The [Troutman Pepper Locke tracker](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/proposed-state-privacy-law-update-april-5464708/) identifies several states with privacy bills facing imminent legislative session deadlines:

- **Alabama** (session closes April 16): A consumer data privacy bill passed the House unanimously (104-0) on February 25, 2026, and was voted out of a Senate committee, but has not advanced further.
- **Kansas** (session closes April 10): Active privacy legislation faces an imminent deadline.
- **Kentucky** (session closes April 15): Privacy bills are under consideration (note: Kentucky already has a comprehensive privacy law effective January 1, 2026).
- **Nebraska** (session closes April 17): Privacy legislation is pending (note: Nebraska also already has a comprehensive privacy law in effect).
- **Tennessee** (session closes April 24): Privacy legislation pending before session close (note: Tennessee's comprehensive privacy law is already in effect).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

The failure of Maine's LD 1822 is significant because the bill was widely viewed as a model for strong consumer privacy protection. Its data minimization requirements and unique third-party disclosure rights would have set a new standard. Privacy advocates, including the [ACLU of Maine](https://www.aclumaine.org/press-releases/maine-house-passes-landmark-privacy-bill/) and [EPIC](https://epic.org/maine-senate-passes-strong-privacy-bill/), had championed the legislation.

Ohio's HB 807, while narrower in scope, reflects growing legislative interest in regulating data broker practices specifically. As a Democrat-sponsored bill in a Republican-controlled legislature, its prospects remain uncertain.

For organizations operating across multiple states, the April deadline cluster could result in several new privacy regimes being enacted in rapid succession. Alabama's bill, having already passed the House unanimously, appears closest to passage among the states with approaching deadlines.
