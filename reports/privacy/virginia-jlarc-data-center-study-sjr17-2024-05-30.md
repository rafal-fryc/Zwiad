# Virginia JLARC Data Center Study (SJR 17): Energy, Land Use, and Tax Incentive Challenges

**Jurisdiction:** Virginia | **Category:** Privacy / Regulatory | **Date:** 2024-05-30

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

On May 21, 2024, the Virginia Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission (JLARC) convened an initial public discussion to launch its legislative study of the state's data center industry, as mandated by Senate Joint Resolution 17 (SJR 17). The study — completed and formally presented to the Commission in December 2024 — examined economic benefits, energy demand, land use, noise, water use, and the effectiveness of Virginia's sales and use tax exemption for data centers, which by FY2024 cost the state approximately $1 billion annually and accounted for over half of Virginia's total economic incentive spending. (Note: more recent FY2025 figures place the exemption cost at $1.9 billion, but that figure postdates the December 2024 JLARC report.) JLARC found that while data centers contribute approximately $9.1 billion annually to Virginia's GDP and support 74,000 jobs, unconstrained data center energy demand could cause Virginia's electricity demand to double within a decade according to E3's commissioned model, with a separate UVA Weldon Cooper Center analysis projecting demand could triple under unconstrained growth — driving up electricity rates for all Virginia customers. The report offered the General Assembly three paths for the tax exemption — extend, allow to expire, or modify with conditions — and made multiple recommendations on energy management, generator emissions, land use authority, and residential proximity standards. The study has directly shaped a wave of data center legislation introduced in the 2025 and 2026 General Assembly sessions.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

Virginia hosts the world's largest concentration of data centers by power and floor space. Northern Virginia's "Data Center Alley" — a corridor stretching from Reston to Manassas, centered on Loudoun County — is frequently cited in industry and government materials as handling 70% of global internet traffic, though that figure is disputed by telecom analysts. [TeleGeography's published analysis](https://resources.telegeography.com/does-70-of-the-worlds-internet-traffic-flow-through-virginia) concludes the claim "simply can't be true" because only approximately 23% of global international internet capacity connects to the United States, and a [2025 Cardinal News investigative report](https://cardinalnews.org/2025/07/29/the-famous-claim-that-70-of-the-worlds-internet-traffic-goes-through-northern-virginia-is-wrong/) found no credible source for the specific percentage, suggesting it may originate from the 1990s AOL era. What is not in dispute is that Northern Virginia is the world's leading data center market and contains over 200 data centers in Loudoun County alone. As of late 2024, Dominion Energy (Virginia's primary utility) reported an 88 percent increase in contracted data center power capacity within five months in 2024, from 21 GW in July 2024 to approximately 40 GW by December 2024.

Virginia has incentivized data center development since 2010 through a sales and use tax exemption allowing qualifying large-scale data centers to purchase equipment — servers, cooling systems, generators — without paying retail sales taxes. The exemption grew substantially: by fiscal year 2024, it cost the state approximately $1 billion annually (up from $685 million in FY2023), and between 2021 and 2024 the cumulative benefit to the industry totaled $2.73 billion, representing 53% of Virginia's total economic incentive spending.

During the 2024 Virginia General Assembly session, numerous bills were introduced that would have imposed new constraints on the data center industry — addressing energy costs, noise, siting near residential areas, and environmental compliance. Most of those bills were referred to JLARC for further study rather than enacted, effectively pausing new regulation pending a comprehensive review. [Senate Joint Resolution 17](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/resolutions/2024_Data%20centers_JLARC.pdf) formally directed JLARC to conduct that study, examining the full range of opportunities and challenges associated with the data center industry in Virginia.

JLARC had previously examined data center incentives in an earlier study ([Data Center and Manufacturing Incentives, 2022](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/presentations/RptPres518.pdf)), but SJR 17 authorized a far broader and more comprehensive review covering economic impacts, energy infrastructure, environmental considerations, local land use authority, and the tax incentive structure.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### Study Scope and Process

JLARC staff conducted over 300 interviews across a wide range of stakeholders, including data center companies, Dominion Energy and other utilities, local governments, state agencies, Virginia residents living near data centers, environmental groups, and independent subject-matter experts. JLARC also commissioned two independent technical studies: (1) an energy grid modeling study from consulting firm E3 to project future load growth and grid infrastructure needs, and (2) an economic impact analysis. The study addressed five major topic areas:

1. **Economic development and tax incentives** — the value and conditions of Virginia's sales and use tax exemption
2. **Energy demand and infrastructure** — projected load growth and grid investment requirements
3. **Land use and local authority** — adequacy of zoning authority and existing siting practices
4. **Environmental and community impacts** — noise, air quality, water use, and proximity to residences
5. **State and local revenue** — fiscal impacts of data center growth on state and county budgets

### Energy Demand: A Defining Finding

JLARC's commissioned energy modeling produced two projections. E3's commissioned model found that unconstrained data center demand growth could cause Virginia's total electricity demand to double within ten years, with data centers as the primary driver. A separate UVA Weldon Cooper Center analysis, also cited by JLARC, projected that demand could triple under unconstrained growth. Even under a constrained scenario — where only half of projected unconstrained demand is met — substantial new power generation and transmission infrastructure would be required, infrastructure that would not otherwise need to be built.

This infrastructure carries fixed costs that utilities must recover through rates, meaning all Virginia electricity customers — including residential and small business customers — face higher bills because of data center growth. JLARC found that "[d]ata centers' increased energy demand will likely increase system costs for all customers," with rate increases expected to begin materializing as early as 2026. As of December 2024, 26 GW of data center electricity demand was in the substation engineering queue at Dominion — a 245% increase from the 8 GW reported just five months earlier.

### Land Use, Proximity, and Community Impacts

JLARC found that nearly one-third of Virginia's existing data centers are located within 200 feet of residential homes. In Fairfax County, 55% of data centers are within 200 feet of homes; statewide, 70% are within 500 feet. Residents living near data centers frequently reported persistent low-frequency noise from cooling systems and backup generators — noise that, while below hearing-damage thresholds, affects quality of life and sleep.

The report identified structural weaknesses in local land use authority. Some localities had zoned industrial-use areas adjacent to residential zones in violation of standard land use planning principles. Local elected officials had in some cases granted rezonings or exceptions permitting data centers adjacent to sensitive residential areas, schools, and historic sites. JLARC concluded that current local zoning authority, while nominally adequate, was in practice being applied inconsistently and insufficiently to protect nearby communities.

### Water Use

JLARC found that current data center water use in Virginia is sustainable but growing, and recommended better monitoring and management frameworks. Many data centers use significant quantities of water for cooling, though the degree varies by cooling technology employed.

### Tax Incentive Structure and Options

Since 2010, Virginia has exempted large-scale data centers from the state sales and use tax on qualifying equipment purchases. Approximately 90% of the industry uses this exemption. The JLARC study presented three policy paths for the General Assembly:

- **Extend the exemption** (currently set to expire in 2035) to maintain data center investment and the associated economic benefits
- **Allow the exemption to expire** to slow data center growth and reduce associated energy and community impacts
- **Modify the exemption with conditions** — tying continued eligibility to energy efficiency standards, clean energy targets, Tier IV generator use, demand response program participation, or other criteria

JLARC's specific recommendations included requiring data centers to meet and certify to an energy management standard as a condition of the tax exemption, and requiring use of Tier IV (or equivalent low-emission) generators.

### December 2024 Final Report

JLARC presented its final report — formally titled *Data Centers in Virginia* (Report 598) — to the Commission on December 9, 2024. The full report, summary of recommendations, and Commission briefing presentation are available on [JLARC's official study page](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/landing-2024-data-centers-in-virginia.asp).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Regulated Entities

The JLARC study and its legislative aftermath primarily affect:

- **Data center operators** in Virginia (especially hyperscalers such as AWS, Microsoft, Google, and Meta, plus colocation providers) seeking to expand or maintain the sales tax exemption
- **Dominion Energy and other utilities** required to plan for and finance massive new generation and transmission capacity
- **Local governments** in Northern Virginia (particularly Loudoun, Fairfax, Prince William, and Fauquier Counties) managing zoning decisions and community complaints
- **Residential communities** adjacent to data centers, facing noise, visual, and potential property value impacts, as well as higher electricity rates

### Legislative Outcomes: 2025 and 2026

The JLARC report directly shaped two years of General Assembly activity. In the **2025 session**, legislators debated extending or reforming the sales and use tax exemption (set to expire 2035). House Bill 2578 and related measures were considered, with a House-passed provision extending the exemption through 2050 conditional on environmental requirements (including a ban on fossil fuel self-generation for data centers built after July 2027), while the Senate sought to eliminate the exemption entirely. The fundamental disagreement between chambers remained unresolved as of the 2025 session's close.

In the **2026 session**, Virginia lawmakers considered 61 data center bills — the most comprehensive legislative effort in state history on the topic. Of these, 15 passed and were sent to the governor. Key measures included:

- **HB 507** (Del. McAuliff, D-Fauquier): Requires the Department of Environmental Quality to deny air permits for new data center generators after July 2026 unless they use Tier IV generators or equivalent low-emission technology — directly implementing one of JLARC's tax-exemption conditions as a standalone environmental requirement
- **HB 284**: Directs electric providers to create voluntary demand flexibility programs for high-energy customers (25 MW+) by 2029, prohibiting those program costs from being passed to other customers
- Additional siting, transparency, and State Corporation Commission oversight bills

The tax exemption itself — valued at $1.6–$1.9 billion annually in more recent FY2025 figures — remained the central unresolved issue as of early 2026, with the Senate seeking phase-out and the House proposing conditional extension.

### Compliance and Regulatory Implications for Operators

Organizations operating or planning Virginia data centers should monitor several regulatory pressure points arising from the JLARC study:

- **Generator standards**: New air permits for data center backup generators face Tier IV requirements beginning July 2026 (HB 507 if signed)
- **Demand response**: Facilities using 25 MW or more face mandatory enrollment in demand flexibility programs under proposed legislation
- **Tax exemption conditions**: Any future extension of the sales and use tax exemption is likely to carry energy efficiency, clean energy, or generator standards as conditions
- **Siting restrictions**: Multiple bills proposed setback requirements from residential areas, schools, and historic resources; some carried over to 2027
- **Transparency**: Bills requiring disclosure of energy consumption, water use, and generator test schedules were advanced in committee

## Action Items

- **Monitor 2026 session outcomes**: Fifteen bills have passed the Virginia General Assembly and are awaiting Governor Spanberger's action. Track which are signed, vetoed, or amended — particularly HB 507 (generator standards) and any tax exemption legislation
- **Evaluate generator compliance**: Data centers planning new backup generator installations in Virginia should immediately assess whether current or planned equipment meets Tier IV emissions standards given the pending July 2026 permitting deadline
- **Assess tax exemption eligibility**: Operators relying on Virginia's sales and use tax exemption should model exposure under scenarios where the exemption expires (2035 current sunset) or is conditioned on environmental compliance
- **Review siting plans**: Any data center development near residential areas in Virginia should account for potential new setback requirements and community impact mitigation obligations that may emerge from 2026–2027 legislation
- **Engage in rulemaking processes**: JLARC recommended several areas for future State Corporation Commission and DEQ rulemaking; operators should monitor agency dockets and participate in comment periods
- **Review the full JLARC report**: The [complete Report 598](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/reports/Rpt598-2.pdf) and [recommendations summary](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/summary/Rpt598Rec.pdf) are essential reading for any entity with Virginia data center interests
