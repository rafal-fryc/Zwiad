# Seventh Circuit Holds BIPA 2024 Damages Amendment Applies Retroactively

**Jurisdiction:** Illinois / Federal (Seventh Circuit) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** April 1, 2026

> **Note:** A companion report filed April 12, 2026 ([illinois-bipa-7th-circuit-retroactivity-2026-04-12.md](illinois-bipa-7th-circuit-retroactivity-2026-04-12.md)) provides extended analysis of the same ruling. This report was generated from a second scan approval of the same event. Cross-reference both for complete coverage.

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 1, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit unanimously held in [*Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, No. 25-2185, 2026 WL 891902](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/25-2185/25-2185-2026-04-01.html) that the August 2024 amendment to Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) applies retroactively to all cases pending at enactment. The ruling eliminates per-scan damages, capping recovery at a single statutory award per person — $5,000 for intentional/reckless violations or $1,000 for negligent violations — regardless of how many scans occurred. Defendants in hundreds of active BIPA class actions now face dramatically reduced exposure, fundamentally reshaping the litigation landscape.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Decision date and citation:** April 1, 2026 — [*Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, Nos. 25-2185 et al.](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/25-2185/25-2185-2026-04-01.html) (7th Cir. 2026).
- **Three cases reversed:** The Seventh Circuit consolidated and reversed district court rulings in *Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, *Gregg v. Central Transport LLC*, and *Willis v. Universal Intermodal Services, Inc.*, per [Duane Morris Class Action Defense blog](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/04/03/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively-reversing-three-illinois-federal-court-decisions/).
- **The 2024 amendment (SB 2979 / [Public Act 103-0769](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm)):** Signed August 2, 2024, it amends [Section 20 of BIPA (740 ILCS 14/20)](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K20.htm) to provide that repeated collection or disclosure of the same person's biometric identifier using the same method constitutes "a single violation" entitling the aggrieved person to "at most, one recovery."
- **Damages capped per person:** $5,000 per person for intentional or reckless violations; $1,000 per person for negligent violations — replacing potential per-scan exposure that could reach millions per plaintiff, per [Jackson Lewis analysis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/bipa-cases-7th-circuit-rules-change-illinois-laws-damages-provision-retroactively-limits-defendant-exposure).
- **Retroactivity reasoning — remedial/procedural:** Applying the Illinois retroactivity framework, the panel held the amendment is remedial (governing damages, not liability) and therefore procedural, requiring retroactive application per [Sidley Data Matters](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/04/08/seventh-circuit-limits-potential-damages-under-bipa-holds-2024-amendment-applies-retroactively/).
- **Key textual anchor:** The amendment touched only Section 20 (damages) and left Section 15 (substantive liability) untouched — the panel's primary indicator that the change is remedial, per [DLA Piper Privacy Matters](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/seventh-circuit-holds-bipas-2024-damages-amendment-applies-retroactively/).
- **Cothron v. White Castle distinguished:** The panel clarified that the Illinois Supreme Court's 2023 per-scan accrual ruling in [*Cothron v. White Castle*](https://law.justia.com/cases/illinois/supreme-court/2023/128004.html) addressed statute-of-limitations timing under Section 15, not damages calculation under Section 20 — they are separate provisions, per [Fisher Phillips analysis](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/major-biometric-win-for-business-in-illinois).
- **Practical scale:** Before the amendment, a single plaintiff scanned 1,500 times could seek $7.5 million; the per-person cap now limits that same plaintiff to $5,000 for intentional violations, per [SGR Law](https://www.sgrlaw.com/client-alerts/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-damages-amendment-applies-retroactively-reshaping-liability-for-hundreds-of-pending-cases/).
- **Geographic binding effect:** The Seventh Circuit's ruling binds federal courts in Illinois, Indiana, and Wisconsin. Illinois state courts are not bound, per [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/seventh-circuit-court-appeals-holds-bipa-amendment-limiting-damages-applies).
- **No vested rights in speculative damages:** The court rejected plaintiffs' constitutional argument, noting that BIPA's statutory damages are discretionary — plaintiffs have no vested right to a particular remedy, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/illinois-damages-limitation-for-biometric-privacy-violations-applies-retroactively).

## Action Items

- **Recalculate BIPA litigation reserves immediately.** Pending BIPA class actions in federal court in the Seventh Circuit (Illinois, Indiana, Wisconsin) must be revalued under the per-person cap — aggregate exposure may drop by orders of magnitude.
- **Reassess CAFA jurisdiction.** Many BIPA class claims previously clearing CAFA's $5 million threshold on a per-scan basis may no longer meet the amount-in-controversy requirement; explore remand motions to state court where strategically beneficial.
- **Renegotiate pending settlements.** Settlements negotiated under per-scan damages assumptions are now overpriced for defendants; seek renegotiation of any deal not yet finalized.
- **Consider removal to federal court for state-filed cases.** *Clay* is binding in federal court within the Seventh Circuit; defendants in Illinois state court BIPA cases may benefit from removal to federal court.
- **Monitor Illinois Supreme Court.** The Illinois Supreme Court has not yet ruled on retroactivity. A contrary state ruling would create a state/federal split and could revive per-scan damages in state court proceedings.
- **Maintain BIPA compliance programs.** The ruling reduces damages exposure but does not eliminate liability under BIPA Section 15. Biometric data collection policies, written retention schedules, and informed-consent requirements remain fully operative.
