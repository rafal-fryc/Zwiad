# Louisiana Becomes 22nd State to Enact Comprehensive Privacy Law (SB 386 — Louisiana Data Privacy Act)

**Jurisdiction:** Louisiana | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-29

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On May 29, 2026, Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry signed [Senate Bill 386](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1475339), the Louisiana Data Privacy Act ("LDPA"), making Louisiana the [22nd U.S. state](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260622-louisiana-enacts-nations-twenty-second-state-comprehensive-privacy-law) to enact a comprehensive consumer data privacy statute and the third state to do so in 2026, following Oklahoma and Alabama. <!-- verified --> The law takes effect [January 1, 2027](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/06/louisiana-enacts-consumer-data-privacy-law/), giving covered businesses roughly seven months to build compliance programs. <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- Governor Jeff Landry signed SB 386 into law on [May 29, 2026](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/louisiana-data-privacy-act-signed-governor); the enrolled bill text is available from the [Louisiana Legislature](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1475339). <!-- verified -->
- The LDPA applies to persons or entities conducting business in Louisiana that meet at least one of three thresholds: (1) annual gross revenue exceeding $25 million; (2) annually buy, receive, sell, or share the personal data of 75,000 or more consumers, households, or devices; or (3) derive 50% or more of annual revenue from selling consumers' personal data ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/06/louisiana-enacts-consumer-data-privacy-law/); [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/louisiana-enacts-comprehensive-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- The Act exempts GLBA-covered financial institutions and data, HIPAA-covered entities and business associates, nonprofits, institutions of higher education, and public utilities, along with data-level exemptions for FCRA, FERPA, and employee data ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/06/louisiana-enacts-consumer-data-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- Consumer rights track the now-standard Virginia-model framework: access/confirmation, correction, deletion, data portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising, sale of personal data, and profiling producing legal or similarly significant effects ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/06/louisiana-enacts-consumer-data-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- Enforcement is vested exclusively in the Louisiana Attorney General as a violation of the state's Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law; the LDPA contains no private right of action ([Wilmer Hale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260622-louisiana-enacts-nations-twenty-second-state-comprehensive-privacy-law); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/06/louisiana-data-privacy-act-sb-386)). <!-- verified -->
- A time-limited mandatory cure period applies: from January 1, 2027 through July 31, 2027, the Attorney General must give 30 days' written notice before initiating an investigation, and a business can avoid enforcement by curing the violation, certifying the cure, notifying affected consumers, and adopting internal policies to prevent recurrence; the mandatory cure right sunsets after July 31, 2027 ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/06/louisiana-data-privacy-act-sb-386)). <!-- verified -->
- Unlike several peer state laws, the LDPA does not itself set a fixed per-violation civil penalty; penalties instead flow from Louisiana's general Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law framework, which can include per-violation civil penalties, attorney fees, costs, and treble damages for willful violations ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/06/louisiana-data-privacy-act-sb-386)). <!-- verified: independent search confirms LUTPA treble-damages mechanism (La. R.S. 51:1409), though statute frames the trigger as "knowingly" continuing conduct after AG notice rather than "willful" -- a minor terminology variance, not a substantive error -->
- Notably, an earlier reengrossed Senate-passed version of SB 386 (passed 36-0 on April 8, 2026) included explicit "artificial intelligence system" language in the processor-duty section — requiring processors to assist controllers in securing personal data "collected, stored, and processed by an artificial intelligence system," which would have made Louisiana only the second state (after Texas) to name AI systems by term in a comprehensive privacy law's processor duty. That AI-specific language was removed before Governor Landry signed the final bill ([Jones Walker LLP](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/louisiana-becomes-the-twenty-second-privacy-state-and-almost-the-second-to-embed.html?id=102n12k)). <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- Businesses with Louisiana revenue or consumer-data operations should assess applicability against the $25 million revenue / 75,000-consumer / 50%-revenue-from-sale thresholds before the January 1, 2027 effective date.
- Build or update data subject request (DSR) intake and fulfillment processes to cover access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out rights consistent with the Virginia-model framework already required in most other states.
- Update data processing agreements with processors to reflect Louisiana-specific obligations; note that the enacted version does NOT contain the AI-system-specific processor language that appeared in the April Senate-passed draft, so compliance programs built around the Texas AI-processor-duty model should not assume parity with Louisiana.
- Monitor for Louisiana Attorney General guidance or rulemaking, and preserve internal capability to respond within the 30-day cure notice window during the January 1 - July 31, 2027 mandatory cure period, since that safe harbor sunsets automatically.
- Track this statute alongside other 2026 enactments (Oklahoma, Alabama) as part of a broader wave of state comprehensive privacy laws for multi-state compliance mapping.
