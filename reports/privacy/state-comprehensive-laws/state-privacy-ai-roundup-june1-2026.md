# State Privacy and AI Law Roundup: Week of June 1, 2026 (Vermont, Louisiana, Illinois, California)

**Jurisdictions:** Vermont | Louisiana | Illinois | California | Multi-state | **Category:** Privacy / AI Law | **Date:** 2026-06-02

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

[Troutman Pepper Locke's weekly state privacy and AI legislation tracker](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/proposed-state-privacy-and-ai-law-update-june-1-2026/) for the week of June 1, 2026 recorded a historically dense period of state legislative activity across privacy and AI law. Four major developments define the week: (1) the Vermont legislature passed S.71, the Data Privacy and Online Surveillance Act, by a near-unanimous 129-3 House vote, sending it to Governor Phil Scott — who subsequently vetoed the bill on June 8, 2026; (2) Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry signed SB 386, the Louisiana Data Privacy Act, into law as Act No. 502, making Louisiana the twenty-second state with a comprehensive consumer privacy statute effective January 1, 2027; (3) the Illinois General Assembly passed SB 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act — the first US law to mandate third-party audits of frontier AI models — and sent it to Governor Pritzker; and (4) eight bills crossed legislative chambers in California covering children's privacy, chatbots, surveillance pricing, workplace monitoring, and healthcare AI. The week reflects an accelerating state-level patchwork in the absence of federal comprehensive privacy and AI legislation.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The State Privacy Law Patchwork in 2026

As of June 2026, the United States has no federal comprehensive consumer data privacy law. In the absence of federal action, states have enacted a growing patchwork of statutes. The 2026 legislative season has been particularly active: Oklahoma enacted the Oklahoma Consumer Data Privacy Act ([SB 546, signed March 20, 2026](https://www.okhouse.gov/posts/news-20260323_2)), becoming the twentieth state to pass comprehensive privacy legislation. Alabama followed on April 16, 2026, when Governor Kay Ivey signed the Alabama Personal Data Protection Act ([HB 351](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260422-alabama-enacts-nations-twenty-first-state-comprehensive-privacy-law)), making Alabama the twenty-first state. Louisiana's enactment in late May 2026 brought the count to twenty-two.

This spring 2026 wave follows years of state-led rulemaking modeled largely on the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (2021) and California Consumer Privacy Act (2018). The dominant legislative templates in 2026 remain the Virginia/Texas model (controller/processor framework, no private right of action, AG enforcement) and the Connecticut/Colorado model (which adds opt-out rights for automated profiling decisions). States diverge on applicability thresholds, sensitive data categories, and enforcement mechanisms.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### Vermont S.71 — Data Privacy and Online Surveillance Act: Legislature Passes, Governor Vetoes

Vermont's Senate Bill 71, titled "An act relating to consumer data privacy and online surveillance," passed the Vermont House by a 129-3 vote on May 26, 2026, with the Senate concurring the same day. The bill's [official status page](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/S.71) reflects passage and delivery to the Governor. [Governor Phil Scott vetoed the bill on June 8, 2026](https://iapp.org/news/a/the-vermont-veto-is-a-step-backward-for-privacy), the second consecutive veto of comprehensive privacy legislation in Vermont — he previously vetoed H.121 in June 2024.

The 2026 bill was designed to address the concerns that sank H.121: the 2026 version removed the private right of action entirely, leaving enforcement solely with the Attorney General, while retaining the provisions supporters consider most essential — robust data minimization, online surveillance restrictions, and a ban on geofencing within 1,850 feet of any health facility. Despite these concessions to industry concerns, Governor Scott again cited business risk as the basis for his veto.

Key provisions of S.71 (as passed, prior to veto):
- **Consumer rights:** access, correction, deletion, and data portability, plus opt-out of targeted advertising, sale of personal data, and profiling decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects.
- **Data minimization:** controllers may collect only data adequate, relevant, and limited to the purposes for which it is processed.
- **Sensitive data consent:** processing of sensitive categories — health, biometric, genetic, children's data, precise geolocation — requires express consumer consent.
- **Health geofencing ban:** unique among US state laws, S.71 would have prohibited data brokers and ad networks from deploying geofences near health facilities to track or target consumers.
- **Applicability thresholds:** entities processing personal data of at least 35,000 consumers, or sensitive data of at least 3,000 consumers, or selling personal data of at least 3,000 consumers per [ComplianceHub.Wiki](https://compliancehub.wiki/vermont-s71-data-privacy-online-surveillance-act-2026/).
- **Enforcement:** Attorney General only; no private right of action.
- **Effective date (had it been signed):** January 1, 2028.

The legislature adjourned in late May 2026 without remaining in session to mount an override attempt. Vermont remains without a comprehensive consumer privacy law. The high vote margin (129-3 in the House) suggests strong legislative will and a likely reintroduction in the 2027 session.

### Louisiana SB 386 — Louisiana Data Privacy Act: Signed Into Law

Governor Jeff Landry signed Louisiana Senate Bill 386 into law as [Act No. 502](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/louisiana-data-privacy-act/) on May 29, 2026, making Louisiana the twenty-second state to enact comprehensive consumer data privacy legislation and the third to do so in 2026. The law takes effect January 1, 2027.

The Louisiana Data Privacy Act (LDPA) is modeled primarily on the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act, with some notable distinctions per [Jones Walker LLP's analysis](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/louisiana-becomes-the-twenty-second-privacy-state-and-almost-the-second-to-embed.html?id=102n12k):

- **Applicability thresholds:** Applies to entities doing business in Louisiana that (a) have annual gross revenues exceeding $25 million, (b) process personal data of 75,000 or more consumers or devices annually, or (c) derive 50% or more of gross revenues from the sale of personal data. The $25 million standalone gross revenue threshold is notable — some other states use the revenue threshold only as a secondary qualifier.
- **Consumer rights:** Access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising, data sale, and profiling producing significant effects.
- **Sensitive data:** Consent required for health, biometric, genetic, children's data, precise geolocation, and financial data.
- **Profiling:** The LDPA defines profiling as "solely automated processing" and provides opt-out rights from profiling that drives "a decision that produces a legal or similarly significant effect."
- **Data protection assessments:** Required when profiling presents reasonably foreseeable risk of unfair or deceptive treatment, unlawful disparate impact, or financial or reputational harm.
- **Enforcement:** Exclusive authority vested in the Louisiana Attorney General; violations are classified as unfair or deceptive trade practices. No private right of action.
- **Effective date:** January 1, 2027.

Louisiana also passed five additional AI-related bills that were sent to Governor Landry around the same time, per [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/louisiana-passes-five-ai-disclosure-and-privacy-bills): S 243 and S 264 require disclosure when AI simulates a human (chatbot disclosure), and HB 475 mandates patient notification when AI transcription is active in healthcare settings.

The official legislative documents are available from the [Louisiana Legislature's document viewer](https://legis.la.gov/legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1473755) (Senate Summary of House Amendments to SB 386).

### Illinois SB 315 — Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act: Nation's First Mandatory Frontier AI Audit Law

The Illinois General Assembly passed SB 315, the [Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act](https://capitolnewsillinois.com/news/illinois-lawmakers-pass-landmark-ai-accountability-bill/), by a unanimous 110-0 House vote on May 27, 2026 (the Senate had passed it on May 21). Governor J.B. Pritzker publicly stated his intention to sign the bill.

SB 315 is the first US state law to mandate independent third-party audits of frontier AI model safety protocols, per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/illinois-legislature-passes-historic-ai-bill-rcna347191). Its key provisions, per [Akerman LLP's analysis](https://www.akerman.com/en/perspectives/illinois-sb-315-a-state-strategy-for-enduring-national-ai-safety-standards.html) and [Capitol News Illinois](https://capitolnewsillinois.com/news/bill-regulating-powerful-ai-models-advances-as-advocates-say-its-only-the-first-step/):

- **Scope:** Applies only to "frontier AI developers" with annual gross revenues exceeding $500 million that train or maintain AI models meeting a frontier-scale compute threshold. Effectively captures OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, and a small number of other large AI labs.
- **Frontier AI frameworks:** Covered developers must establish documented technical and organizational protocols to assess, manage, and mitigate catastrophic risks from frontier models. Frameworks must address capability thresholds, mitigation strategies, use of third-party evaluators, and cybersecurity for unreleased model weights.
- **Third-party audits:** Beginning January 1, 2028, covered developers must retain independent auditors to conduct annual compliance audits.
- **Critical safety incident reporting:** Developers must report a "critical safety incident" to the Illinois Attorney General and the Emergency Management Agency and Office of Homeland Security within 72 hours of learning facts sufficient to establish reasonable belief that a critical safety incident has occurred.
- **Civil penalties:** Up to $3 million per violation, enforced solely by the Illinois Attorney General.
- **Effective dates:** General provisions take effect January 1, 2027; transparency reporting and audit requirements take effect January 1, 2028.

Illinois would become the third state to set frontier AI model standards, following New York's RAISE Act and California's SB 53 (the Transparency in Frontier AI Act), per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/notable-ai-privacy-bills-hit-finish-line-in-illinois-connecticut-and-new-york). The [Transparency Coalition](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/illinois-lawmakers-send-significant-ai-frontier-model-safety-bill-to-gov-pritzker) hailed it as a landmark in AI safety accountability.

### California — Eight Bills Cross Chambers

The Troutman Pepper Locke June 1, 2026 [weekly update](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/proposed-state-privacy-and-ai-law-1440009/) reported that eight bills crossed legislative chambers in California during the week, with the Assembly passing seven bills and the Senate passing one. The bills address:

- **Children's privacy:** Measures tightening obligations for platforms with minor users.
- **Chatbot disclosure:** At least one bill requiring disclosure when AI systems simulate human communication.
- **Surveillance pricing:** Legislation targeting algorithmic pricing practices that may constitute consumer harm.
- **Workplace surveillance:** A bill regulating employer use of monitoring tools.
- **Healthcare AI:** Measures governing AI use in clinical and healthcare settings.

California's legislative session runs through September 2026, and Governor Gavin Newsom's signature will be required before any bills become law. California's track record in 2025 — where Newsom vetoed several high-profile AI bills including SB 1047 — means these bills face additional scrutiny at the signing stage. The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) remains active in parallel rulemaking proceedings.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Compliance Obligations: Louisiana (Effective January 1, 2027)

Louisiana's LDPA creates immediate compliance obligations for businesses that process Louisiana residents' personal data and meet the applicability thresholds. The January 1, 2027 effective date gives affected organizations approximately six months from the law's signing to prepare. Compliance teams should note:

- The $25 million gross revenue threshold may sweep in mid-sized companies that would not qualify under pure-consumer-count thresholds.
- Louisiana joins a growing list of states where existing privacy programs (built for Virginia, Texas, or Colorado models) will need only targeted adjustments given the LDPA's similar framework — but the $25 million standalone revenue trigger and profilig definitions warrant specific review.
- Louisiana's AI disclosure bills (S 243, S 264, HB 475) create separate obligations for chatbot operators and healthcare organizations.

### Vermont: Veto Does Not Eliminate Legislative Risk

The veto of S.71 does not close the Vermont compliance file. The 129-3 House vote signals overwhelming legislative consensus. With the legislature adjourned, an override is not currently on the calendar. However, a reintroduction in the 2027 session — and potential enactment — is highly likely given the political dynamics. Organizations with Vermont consumer exposure should monitor the 2027 Vermont legislative session and maintain contingency compliance plans, particularly for the health geofencing ban and data minimization obligations.

### Illinois Frontier AI: Narrow Scope, Broad Signal

SB 315's $500 million revenue threshold means the vast majority of AI companies are not directly covered. However, the law signals where state AI regulation is heading: third-party audits, incident reporting, and documented risk frameworks. Companies developing or deploying AI systems should begin assessing whether they fall within the frontier developer definition and, even if not, consider voluntarily adopting audit-style safety frameworks as regulatory expectations solidify.

### Multi-State Compliance Complexity

The pace of state privacy and AI legislation in 2026 — twenty-two comprehensive privacy laws, and growing AI-specific requirements — substantially increases the complexity of multi-state compliance programs. The absence of federal preemption means each state law's unique thresholds, definitions, and timelines must be tracked individually. Automated compliance management tools and regular review cycles (at minimum quarterly) are now practically necessary for any mid-to-large enterprise processing US consumer data.

## Action Items

- **Louisiana (immediate):** Assess whether your organization meets any of the LDPA's applicability thresholds ($25M gross revenue, 75,000 consumers processed, or 50% revenue from data sales). If yes, begin gap analysis against the January 1, 2027 effective date.
- **Louisiana chatbot/healthcare AI:** Review Louisiana's AI disclosure bills (S 243, S 264, HB 475) once signed — chatbot operators and healthcare organizations should check whether new disclosure requirements apply.
- **Illinois frontier AI:** Determine whether your organization qualifies as a "frontier AI developer" under SB 315's criteria. If Governor Pritzker signs (as indicated), the law takes effect January 1, 2027 with audit requirements beginning January 1, 2028.
- **Vermont (monitor):** Flag the Vermont 2027 legislative session for tracking. The overwhelming legislative vote makes reintroduction and eventual enactment likely. Begin contingency planning for the health geofencing ban and data minimization requirements.
- **California:** Monitor the eight bills that crossed chambers; track Governor Newsom's signing decisions through September 2026 session end.
- **Multi-state programs:** Review whether your existing privacy compliance program (privacy notices, data inventory, consumer rights workflows) can be extended to Louisiana on the Virginia/Texas template, or whether Louisiana-specific configurations are needed.
