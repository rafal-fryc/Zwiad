# Virginia Bans Sale of Precise Geolocation Data: VCDPA Amendment SB 338 Signed Into Law

**Jurisdiction:** Virginia | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-19

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 13, 2026, Virginia Governor Abigail Spanberger signed [Senate Bill 338](https://lis.virginia.gov/bill-details/20261/SB338) into law, amending the [Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA), Va. Code § 59.1-578](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/pdf/vacode/59.1-575/), to prohibit data controllers from selling or offering for sale consumers' precise geolocation data. Effective July 1, 2026, Virginia becomes the third state — alongside Oregon and Maryland — to enact an outright ban on the sale of precise location data. The law passed both chambers of the General Assembly with unanimous bipartisan votes and received backing from a broad consumer-advocate coalition.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Signed:** Governor Spanberger signed SB 338 on April 13, 2026, per the [Virginia Governor's announcement via Consumer Reports Advocacy](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/virginia-governor-signs-landmark-location-privacy-bill-into-law/).
- **Effective date:** July 1, 2026 — compliance must be in place before that date, per [Virginia LIS — SB338](https://lis.virginia.gov/bill-details/20261/SB338).
- **Core prohibition:** Controllers may not "sell or offer for sale precise geolocation data concerning a consumer," added as subdivision 6 to Va. Code § 59.1-578(A) ([bill text on file](https://lis.blob.core.windows.net/files/1089668.PDF)).
- **Precise geolocation defined:** Data that directly identifies a natural person's specific location within a radius of 1,750 feet, consistent with the existing VCDPA definition and the thresholds adopted by Oregon and Maryland, per [National Law Review — Proskauer analysis](https://natlawreview.com/article/virginia-expands-vcdpa-ban-sale-precise-consumer-geolocation-data).
- **"Sale" defined narrowly:** The VCDPA defines "sale" as "the exchange of personal data for monetary consideration by the controller to a third party." Non-monetary data-sharing arrangements — such as receiving free SDK access or analytics services in exchange for location data — may fall outside the prohibition's literal scope, though regulators may view such arrangements skeptically, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/virginia-bans-sale-of-geolocation-data).
- **Sponsors:** Patrons are Senators Perry and Suetterlein ([bill text](https://lis.blob.core.windows.net/files/1089668.PDF)).
- **Vote:** Passed the Senate 39-0 on February 3, 2026, and the House unanimously on February 26, 2026 (96-0), per [EPIC Virginia signing statement](https://epic.org/virginia-governor-signs-bill-banning-sale-of-precise-location-data/).
- **Scope:** The ban applies to controllers that collect location data for their own services and then sell it onward — including data brokers, ad-tech intermediaries, and location data aggregators — not merely to companies that collect location data for internal operational use.
- **Existing consent framework preserved:** Companies may continue to collect precise geolocation data with user consent for service delivery; the new provision prohibits the monetization of that data through sale to third parties.
- **Enforcement:** The Virginia Attorney General has exclusive enforcement authority. VCDPA violations carry civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation, with a mandatory 30-day cure notice period before enforcement action, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/virginia-bans-sale-of-geolocation-data) and [Bloomberg Law coverage](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/privacy-and-data-security/virginia-joins-oregon-maryland-with-geolocation-data-sale-ban).
- **National context:** Virginia joins [Oregon](https://therecord.media/oregon-passes-geolocation-kids-data-bill) (HB 2008, effective January 1, 2026) and [Maryland](https://www.arrivalist.com/insights_plus/maryland-oregon-privacy-law-changes) (Maryland Online Data Privacy Act, effective October 2025) as the third state to ban the outright sale of precise geolocation data, per [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/privacy-and-data-security/virginia-joins-oregon-maryland-with-geolocation-data-sale-ban).

## Action Items

- **Data brokers and location data companies** must immediately audit whether any revenue streams involve selling precise geolocation data attributed to Virginia consumers. Any such arrangements must be unwound or restructured before July 1, 2026.
- **Ad-tech and programmatic advertising platforms** receiving location data from SDK partners or demand-side platforms should verify upstream data providers have not sourced Virginia consumer geolocation data through a sale transaction — downstream acquirers face the same prohibition as direct sellers.
- **Mobile app developers** who license precise location data to third-party analytics or advertising SDKs should evaluate whether those arrangements constitute a "sale" under the VCDPA's monetary-consideration definition and terminate or renegotiate prohibited arrangements.
- **Legal and compliance teams** should map data flows involving precise geolocation data against the 1,750-foot threshold definition and document which flows are operational (permissible) versus commercial/monetization (prohibited).
- **Multi-state compliance teams** should harmonize Virginia's ban with the Oregon and Maryland prohibitions already in effect — all three use the identical 1,750-foot definition, simplifying a unified compliance approach.
- **Monitor for AG enforcement signals:** Virginia AG Jay Jones has signaled active VCDPA enforcement. Review [EPIC's signing announcement](https://epic.org/virginia-governor-signs-bill-banning-sale-of-precise-location-data/) and track any public cure notices related to SB 338.
