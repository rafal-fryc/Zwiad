# California Privacy Regs Advance But Vote on Drafts Delayed: CPPA July 16, 2024 Board Meeting

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 23, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

At its July 16, 2024 public meeting, the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) Board declined to vote to advance its comprehensive draft rulemaking package to formal rulemaking proceedings, citing an incomplete Standardized Regulatory Impact Assessment (SRIA) and unresolved board disagreements over scope. The phrase "advance but vote delayed" captures the session's dual character: the Board engaged in substantive, public debate over the draft regulations — effectively advancing the intellectual discussion — while deferring the formal procedural vote. The rulemaking package at issue covers automated decisionmaking technology (ADMT), annual cybersecurity audits, privacy risk assessments, insurance sector coverage, and updates to existing CCPA regulations across more than 200 pages. Board members directed staff to complete the required economic analysis and narrow key provisions — particularly the ADMT definition and risk assessment triggers — before returning to the Board, likely at the September 2024 meeting. The regulations were ultimately advanced to formal rulemaking in November 2024 and finalized in July 2025, with most obligations operative as of January 1, 2026.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The CPPA and Its Rulemaking Authority

The [California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)](https://cppa.ca.gov) was created by Proposition 24 (the California Privacy Rights Act, or CPRA) in November 2020. The CPPA is the first dedicated state consumer privacy enforcement agency in the United States, with independent rulemaking and enforcement authority under the [California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100.). CPRA amendments to the CCPA took effect January 1, 2023.

Among the CPPA's statutory mandates is to promulgate regulations on cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and automated decisionmaking technology — categories that the CPRA added to the agency's rulemaking agenda but left for the CPPA to implement in detail.

### Pre-Rulemaking History (2021–2024)

The CPPA began stakeholder outreach on cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and ADMT in 2021. By February 2023, it issued a formal [Invitation for Preliminary Comments on Cybersecurity Audits, Risk Assessments, and Automated Decisionmaking](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pre_rulemaking_activities_pr_02-2023.html), soliciting written input through March 27, 2023.

In April 2024, the CPPA held [three statewide pre-rulemaking stakeholder sessions](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20240424.html) across California to gather additional input. On July 15, 2024 — the day before the Board meeting — the CPPA released a full draft rulemaking package exceeding 200 pages, consolidating proposed rules on ADMT, cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, insurance sector coverage, and updates to existing CCPA regulations. This was the first public release of the consolidated package.

### Procedural Requirements for Formal Rulemaking

Under California's Administrative Procedure Act (APA), before the CPPA can initiate formal rulemaking it must submit a completed Standardized Regulatory Impact Assessment (SRIA) to the California Department of Finance for review and comment. The SRIA must quantify the economic impact of the proposed regulations on California businesses. This procedural prerequisite was not yet complete at the time of the July 16 meeting, making a formal rulemaking vote premature on procedural grounds alone.

## What Happened at the July 16, 2024 Board Meeting [HIGH confidence]

### The Vote Was Declined — Not the Rulemaking

The key distinction in how this meeting was characterized by [Squire Patton Boggs' Privacy World blog](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2024/07/california-privacy-regs-advance-but-vote-on-drafts-delayed/) is important: the regulations "advanced" in the sense that the Board engaged deeply in substantive review of the draft rules, directing specific revisions and publicly airing disagreements — but the Board did not vote to commence formal rulemaking. The vote was deferred, not the project.

As [Perkins Coie reported](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/cppa-regulatory-delays-and-enforcement-updates-takeaways-july-board-meeting), the CPPA Board declined to advance the proposed regulations for two overlapping reasons:

1. **Incomplete SRIA**: Staff had not yet completed the Standardized Regulatory Impact Assessment required before formal rulemaking proceedings can begin under the APA.
2. **Board disagreements on scope**: Board members — particularly founding Board Member Alastair MacTaggart — questioned whether the ADMT definition and risk assessment trigger provisions were overbroad.

### ADMT Scope Debates

The Board's most contentious discussions centered on the ADMT provisions. [FKKS Technology Law noted](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102jdj9/takeaways-from-the-cppas-july-2024-board-meeting) that the draft definition of ADMT — "any technology that processes personal information and uses computation to execute a decision, replace human decisionmaking, or substantially facilitate human decisionmaking" — was criticized for potentially capturing routine computational tools that pose no high risk to consumers. **Note:** This July 2024 draft definition was subsequently narrowed in the final regulations adopted in July 2025; the final text defines ADMT as technology that uses computation "to replace or substantially replace human decisionmaking," removing the earlier "execute a decision" and "facilitate" prongs that the Board found overbroad.

Specific issues raised:
- The draft would require a risk assessment any time a business uses ADMT for a "significant decision concerning a consumer," broadly defined to include decisions in financial services, housing, education, employment, and healthcare. Board members debated whether this trigger was overly inclusive.
- MacTaggart and others questioned whether behavioral advertising should constitute "extensive profiling" that triggers ADMT obligations alongside higher-risk use cases like credit and employment decisions.
- The definition of ADMT itself was seen by some as capturing technologies far beyond what the rulemaking was intended to address.

### Public Comment Instruction

Even without a formal rulemaking vote, the Board directed staff to issue a [call for public comment](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) on specific alternative regulatory approaches. In particular, the Board asked staff to make clear in the comment solicitation whether risk assessments for processing that results in "consequential decisionmaking" should apply to all processing or only to processing using automated decisionmaking technologies. This question about the scope of the consequential-decision trigger — whether ADMT is a required element or merely one path — became a focal point for public comment.

### Enforcement Priorities Update

The July 16 meeting also included an update from CPPA staff on enforcement priorities. Consumer complaints most frequently involved: the right to delete, unauthorized collection or disclosure of personal information, and failure to honor opt-out of sale or sharing. The Board noted additional complaint volume on children's privacy, financial incentives programs, and loyalty program disclosures.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The Five Regulatory Tracks in the Package

The July 2024 draft package comprised five overlapping regulatory tracks:

**1. Automated Decisionmaking Technology (ADMT)**
Draft ADMT regulations would grant consumers (a) the right to access information about how ADMT was used in decisions affecting them; (b) the right to opt out of ADMT for significant decisions, extensive profiling, and AI/ADMT training; and (c) appeal rights regarding ADMT-driven significant decisions. Businesses would be required to provide pre-use notices before deploying ADMT for covered purposes.

**2. Cybersecurity Audits**
The draft required qualifying businesses to conduct annual third-party cybersecurity audits covering at least 17 program components and certify completion annually to the CPPA. The consumer threshold for triggering audit requirements was presented as a range of options (250,000 / 500,000 / 1,000,000 consumers or more), reflecting unresolved policy choices the Board was expected to settle before formal rulemaking.

**3. Privacy Risk Assessments**
Covered businesses would be required to complete and maintain privacy risk assessments before undertaking processing activities presenting "significant risk," including selling or sharing personal information, processing sensitive personal information, using ADMT for significant decisions or extensive profiling, and training ADMT or AI models.

**4. Insurance Sector Coverage**
The package proposed extending or clarifying CCPA/CPPA obligations as applied to insurance companies, a sector that has historically operated under distinct state insurance law frameworks. Board members flagged these provisions as likely to be sequenced with pending California insurance model code legislation.

**5. Updates to Existing CCPA Regulations**
Approximately 72 pages of the package covered proposed amendments to the existing CCPA regulatory framework — consent mechanisms, technical standards for honoring consumer requests, and disclosure requirements.

### The SRIA Bottleneck

The Standardized Regulatory Impact Assessment is a substantive economic analysis, not a formality. For a rulemaking of this scope — with projected first-year compliance costs in the billions — the Department of Finance review process is meaningful. The CPPA's preliminary estimate for year-one compliance costs was approximately $4.2 billion for California businesses alone, per [Wilson Sonsini's analysis](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2024/08/substantial-new-ccpa-regulations-inch-closer-to-reality-a-detailed-overview-of-the-new-requirements-and-their-projected-4-billion-cost-to-california-businesses/). When the regulations were eventually finalized in 2025, CPPA Executive Director Tom Kemp noted that the projected 10-year cost to businesses had been reduced to $4.8 billion (from $10 billion in earlier projections), with projected economic benefits of $282 billion over 10 years largely driven by expected reductions in cybercrime.

### What "Advancing" Means

The Squire Patton Boggs framing — "advance but vote delayed" — reflects a distinction between substantive and procedural progress. Substantively, the July 16 meeting moved the rulemaking forward: the Board publicly debated specific provisions, identified priorities for narrowing, and instructed staff on what to fix. Procedurally, no vote was taken and no formal rulemaking commenced. The next procedural step — a vote to initiate formal rulemaking — was expected at the September 2024 meeting. As [Hunton Andrews Kurth explained](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/cppa-board-discusses-upcoming-formal-rulemaking-on-automated-decisionmaking-technology-risk-assessments-cybersecurity-audits-insurance-and-updates-to-existing-regulations), following a formal vote, the CPPA would open a 45-day public comment period before proceeding to finalization.

## Subsequent Developments and Final Outcome [HIGH confidence]

The July 2024 vote delay was a pause in a process that ultimately reached completion:

- **August 2024**: CPPA released revised draft regulations incorporating board feedback; [Wilson Sonsini published a detailed analysis](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2024/08/substantial-new-ccpa-regulations-inch-closer-to-reality-a-detailed-overview-of-the-new-requirements-and-their-projected-4-billion-cost-to-california-businesses/) of the revised package.
- **November 8, 2024**: The CPPA Board [voted to advance the rulemaking package to formal proceedings](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241108_2.html), including adoption of separate data broker regulations and advancement of the ADMT package.
- **November–December 2024**: CPPA opened the formal 45-day public comment period.
- **July 24, 2025**: The CPPA Board [unanimously adopted the final regulations (5-0)](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250729.html) on ADMT, risk assessments, cybersecurity audits, insurance coverage, and CCPA updates.
- **September 23, 2025**: The California Office of Administrative Law (OAL) [approved the regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html) and filed them with the Secretary of State.
- **January 1, 2026**: Most regulations became operative, with staggered compliance deadlines for cybersecurity audit certifications and ADMT obligations extending to 2027–2030.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Industries Most Affected

Because the ADMT, cybersecurity audit, and risk assessment obligations are triggered by processing activities rather than business type, the regulations reach broadly. Key affected sectors include:

- **Technology and AI companies**: Heavy reliance on algorithmic decision systems triggers ADMT access, opt-out, and appeal obligations across product lines;
- **Financial services**: Loan origination, credit underwriting, and credit line decisions constitute "significant decisions," triggering ADMT and risk assessment requirements;
- **Healthcare**: Medical triage, benefits determinations, and similar applications qualify as significant decisions under the draft framework;
- **Retail and advertising**: Behavioral advertising and targeted marketing profiles implicate the "extensive profiling" ADMT trigger and corresponding risk assessment requirements;
- **Data brokers**: Already subject to the California Delete Act, data brokers face additional cybersecurity audit and risk assessment requirements under this package.

### Compliance Timelines (As Finalized)

Based on the final regulations approved in September 2025 and operative as of [cppa.ca.gov](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html):

| Requirement | Compliance Date |
|---|---|
| Risk assessments (conduct) | January 1, 2026 |
| Risk assessment attestation to CPPA | April 1, 2028 |
| ADMT rights (significant decisions) | January 1, 2027 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification (>$100M revenue) | April 1, 2028 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification ($50M–$100M) | April 1, 2029 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification (<$50M) | April 1, 2030 |

### Economic Scale

The compliance burden is substantial even at revised estimates. The CPPA projected the finalized rules would cost California businesses approximately $4.8 billion over 10 years — reduced from the $10 billion initial estimate due to narrowing of scope following the July 2024 board debates. The projected economic benefit, largely from reduced cybercrime exposure, was estimated at $282 billion over 10 years.

## Action Items

- **ADMT system inventory**: Identify all automated or algorithmic systems used for significant decisions (financial, employment, housing, healthcare) or for extensive behavioral profiling. These are the primary triggers for ADMT pre-use notice, opt-out, and appeal obligations under the finalized regulations.
- **Cybersecurity audit gap assessment**: Determine whether your organization meets the revenue or consumer threshold for mandatory annual cybersecurity audits. Review the CPPA's 17-component audit framework and assess your current program's coverage. The earliest certification deadline (for businesses with over $100M revenue) is April 1, 2028.
- **Risk assessment program**: Map data processing activities against the finalized risk assessment trigger list. Prioritize activities involving ADMT for significant decisions, sensitive personal information, and sale or sharing of PI. Begin documentation now; the conduct obligation runs from January 1, 2026.
- **Monitor CPPA interpretive guidance**: The CPPA is expected to issue clarifications on scope questions left open by the July 2024 debates — particularly on the ADMT definition and risk assessment triggers. Monitor [cppa.ca.gov/announcements](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/) for updates.
- **Update privacy notices**: Revise consumer-facing privacy policies to reflect ADMT pre-use notice requirements and new consumer rights (access, opt-out, appeal) once the ADMT compliance deadline approaches in 2027.
