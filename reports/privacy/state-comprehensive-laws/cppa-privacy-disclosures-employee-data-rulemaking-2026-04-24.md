# CalPrivacy Solicits Preliminary Comments on Privacy Disclosures and Employee Data Under CCPA

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** April 24, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy) issued a [preliminary comment invitation on April 20, 2026](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/notices_disclosures_employee_data.pdf), opening a pre-rulemaking inquiry on two fronts: (1) whether CCPA regulations governing privacy notices and disclosures should be updated to improve their effectiveness for consumers, and (2) whether additional regulation of employee and job applicant data is needed. Comments are due by 5:00 p.m. PT on May 20, 2026. This is a pre-formal-rulemaking step — CalPrivacy is gathering information to decide whether to initiate full rulemaking, making it a critical early window for businesses to shape potential new requirements before proposed rules are drafted.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- On April 20, 2026, [CalPrivacy published an official preliminary comment invitation](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/notices_disclosures_employee_data.pdf) on notices and disclosures, and employee data, under the CCPA and its implementing regulations.
- The comment deadline is **May 20, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. PT**. Comments must be submitted electronically to [regulations@cppa.ca.gov](mailto:regulations@cppa.ca.gov), per the [official CPPA regulations page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/).
- **Topic 1 — Notices and Disclosures**: CalPrivacy is asking what information consumers find most important in privacy policies, what language they find confusing, and what operational challenges businesses face when describing their data practices. The Agency is evaluating whether executive summaries, standardized forms, or other tools could improve disclosure effectiveness, per [Hunton's analysis of the request](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/calprivacy-requests-preliminary-comments-on-notices-disclosures-employee-data).
- **Topic 2 — Employee Data**: CalPrivacy is seeking information on how privacy notices are deployed in employment settings, workers' expectations around data collection and use, challenges businesses face providing CCPA-required notices to employees and job applicants, and how businesses oversee service providers' CCPA compliance for employee data. This is detailed in the [official preliminary comment document](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/notices_disclosures_employee_data.pdf).
- California is unique among U.S. states in fully applying its comprehensive privacy law to employees. The temporary CCPA exemption for HR/employee data expired January 1, 2023, meaning employees have had full CCPA rights for over three years. [Fox Rothschild notes](https://dataprivacy.foxrothschild.com/2026/04/articles/general-privacy-data-security-news-developments/employee-privacy-rights-under-ccpa-calprivacy-calls-for-comments/) this comment period is a rare opportunity for employers to shape CCPA employee privacy rules before they are formally proposed.
- CalPrivacy's next board meeting on **April 30–May 1, 2026** includes agenda items on enforcement activity updates and a review of adequacy requirements with the European Union and the existing EU-US adequacy decision, per the [official meeting materials page](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20260430_0501.html) and [Hunton's board meeting preview](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/calprivacy-announces-the-agenda-for-its-april-30-may-1-board-meeting).
- New CCPA regulations took effect January 1, 2026 — including requirements for cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and automated decision-making technology — setting the backdrop for this next wave of rulemaking. [BakerHostetler's 2026 California privacy overview](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/california-privacy-in-2026-regulations-enforcement-ai-and-more/) details the January 2026 regulatory package in full.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

The preliminary comment process at CalPrivacy is a pre-rulemaking step distinct from the formal public comment period required under the California Administrative Procedure Act. CalPrivacy uses preliminary comment periods to gather information from regulated industries and civil society before deciding whether to initiate formal rulemaking and what topics to address. Comments submitted now will inform whether the Agency proceeds and, if so, what questions a formal rulemaking will address.

For **privacy notices and disclosures**, the impetus is broadly recognized: long, legalistic privacy policies are widely regarded as ineffective at informing consumers. CalPrivacy's inquiry signals the Agency may consider requiring more accessible formats — layered notices, standardized summaries, or icon-based disclosures similar to approaches discussed at the EU level. The [JD Supra overview of California privacy in 2026](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/california-privacy-in-2026-regulations-8237246/) notes that this topic has been on CalPrivacy's longer-term rulemaking agenda.

For **employee data**, the context is that California is the only U.S. state applying full consumer privacy rights to workers. Since January 1, 2023, employers covered by the CCPA must provide employees with a Notice at Collection, honor data access and deletion requests from employees, and oversee service providers' compliance for HR data systems. The [IAPP's summary of the CCPA HR exemption expiration](https://iapp.org/news/a/ccpa-cpra-grace-period-for-hr-and-b2b-ends-jan-1) explains the history of the temporary exemptions that were not extended. Despite three-plus years of employer obligations, CalPrivacy has not yet issued employee-specific regulations, and enforcement has not heavily targeted employer practices — a gap this preliminary inquiry could begin to close.

The April 30–May 1 board meeting's EU adequacy agenda item reflects CalPrivacy's separate but related international dimension: the Agency has been exploring whether California could seek EU GDPR adequacy status, which would allow personal data to flow from the EU to California without supplemental transfer mechanisms. The [CPPA board meeting materials](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20260430_0501.html) include the EU adequacy review as a distinct agenda item.

## Action Items

- **Submit comments by May 20, 2026** to regulations@cppa.ca.gov. Both employers and businesses with consumer-facing operations should participate; this is a preliminary, non-binding step, but submissions will directly inform whether and how CalPrivacy proceeds with formal rulemaking.
- **Employers specifically** should address the employee data questions: practical challenges in deploying CCPA notices to employees and job applicants, how employee data subject access requests are handled operationally, and difficulties overseeing service providers (payroll processors, benefits administrators, ATS vendors) for CCPA compliance.
- **Businesses with consumer-facing privacy policies** should address what disclosure formats would be operationally feasible, what information is genuinely useful to consumers versus burdensome to produce, and whether standardized forms or layered notices would reduce complexity.
- **Monitor the April 30–May 1 board meeting** for enforcement activity updates, which may preview where CalPrivacy is focusing investigative resources in 2026. Meeting materials are posted at [cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20260430_0501.html](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20260430_0501.html).
- **Track the EU adequacy discussion**: if CalPrivacy moves toward pursuing EU adequacy, the privacy notice and employee data frameworks being considered now would need to meet GDPR-equivalent standards — which could make upcoming regulations significantly more stringent than current CCPA requirements.
- **Begin internal audits** of employee privacy notice practices and HR data vendor contracts now, ahead of any formal rulemaking. Identifying gaps before proposed rules are issued positions organizations to comment with specificity and to comply earlier.
