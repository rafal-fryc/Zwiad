# Kentucky HB 692: First State Law Targeting Smart TV Automatic Content Recognition

## Overview [HIGH confidence]

Kentucky's House Bill 692, sponsored by Representative Josh Branscum (R-Russell Springs), amends the state's consumer data privacy law to classify "automatic content recognition" (ACR) data as sensitive data, requiring opt-in consumer consent before collection by smart television manufacturers. The bill passed the Kentucky House 92-0 on March 13, 2026 and the Senate 38-0 on March 31, 2026. The bill has been delivered to the Governor for signature. If signed, it takes effect July 1, 2027, and would be the first state legislation in the United States to specifically target smart TV surveillance technology.

## Key Provisions [HIGH confidence]

HB 692 amends Kentucky's consumer data privacy statute in three principal ways:

- **New definitions**: Introduces statutory definitions for "smart monitor" and "automatic content recognition."
- **Sensitive data classification**: Adds ACR data to the enumerated list of sensitive data categories under Kentucky's consumer data privacy law, triggering heightened protections.
- **Consent requirement**: Smart television manufacturers (and other "smart monitor" devices) must obtain explicit consumer consent before collecting ACR data.

ACR technology captures frames of on-screen content at high frequency — reportedly every 500 milliseconds — and matches audio/video fingerprints against reference databases to identify programs, ads, or streaming content viewed. The resulting viewing-behavior data is commonly monetized through advertising and data-broker channels.

## Compliance Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

Smart TV manufacturers (Samsung, LG, Vizio, TCL, Hisense, Roku-integrated OEMs) and ACR platform operators (e.g., Inscape, Samba TV) would need to:

- Implement Kentucky-specific opt-in consent flows at device setup and for the existing installed base prior to the July 1, 2027 effective date.
- Treat ACR data with the same safeguards Kentucky already requires for other sensitive data categories (data protection assessments, processor contracts, consumer rights requests).
- Re-examine downstream ad-tech and data-broker arrangements that monetize ACR signals from Kentucky residents.

Kentucky's existing consumer data privacy law (effective January 1, 2026) already requires consent for sensitive data processing, so adding ACR leverages an established enforcement framework administered by the Kentucky Attorney General. No private right of action appears to be created. Because Kentucky is the first state to call out ACR explicitly, the definitional language may serve as a template for other states.
