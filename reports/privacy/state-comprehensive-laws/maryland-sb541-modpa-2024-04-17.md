# Maryland Enacts the Online Data Privacy Act (SB 541/MODPA) -- The Nation's Most Stringent State Privacy Law

**Jurisdiction:** Maryland | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-04-17

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 6, 2024, the Maryland General Assembly passed Senate Bill 541, the Maryland Online Data Privacy Act of 2024 (MODPA). Governor Wes Moore [signed the bill on May 9, 2024](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2024/5/10/maryland-joins-the-privacy-party-enacts-comprehensive-data-privacy-law-and-child-privacy-law). MODPA is widely regarded as the most stringent comprehensive state consumer privacy law in the United States, distinguished by its aggressive data minimization requirements, an outright prohibition on the sale of sensitive data regardless of consumer consent, heightened protections for minors under 18, and a requirement to honor universal opt-out mechanisms. The law took effect on October 1, 2025, with enforcement by the Maryland Attorney General beginning [April 1, 2026](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/privacy-and-data-security/now-in-effect-maryland-law-raises-bar-on-sensitive-data-data-minimization-and-children-s-privacy).

## Background [HIGH confidence]

MODPA emerged from a growing wave of state-level comprehensive privacy legislation in the United States. As of its passage, approximately 18 states had enacted or were considering comprehensive consumer data privacy laws. Maryland's effort is notable because it goes significantly further than the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA) model that most states adopted, introducing provisions that are closer to -- and in some respects exceed -- the strictness of the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

[Senate Bill 541](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/sb0541?ys=2024RS) was introduced during the 2024 Regular Session of the Maryland General Assembly. The bill passed both chambers and was transmitted to Governor Wes Moore, who signed it into law on May 9, 2024. The [enrolled bill text](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2024RS/bills/sb/sb0541e.pdf) is available through the Maryland General Assembly website. The Future of Privacy Forum described the bill as the "Old Line State" doing ["something new on privacy,"](https://fpf.org/blog/the-old-line-state-does-something-new-on-privacy/) noting its departure from the increasingly standardized template used by other states.

Maryland simultaneously enacted a separate children's online privacy law (HB 603, the Maryland Age-Appropriate Design Code Act), complementing MODPA's minor-specific provisions with additional requirements for online services likely to be accessed by children.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Applicability Thresholds

MODPA applies to persons that conduct business in Maryland or target products or services to Maryland residents and that during the preceding calendar year either: (1) controlled or processed the personal data of at least 35,000 consumers (excluding data processed solely for payment transactions), or (2) controlled or processed the personal data of at least 10,000 consumers and derived more than 20% of gross revenue from the sale of personal data. The 35,000-consumer threshold represents approximately 0.56% of Maryland's population, which is [proportionally lower than thresholds in other states](https://www.blankrome.com/publications/maryland-passes-unique-and-operationally-challenging-privacy-law) such as Colorado (1.72%) and Oregon (2.35%), meaning MODPA will capture a broader range of businesses.

### Data Minimization

MODPA's data minimization requirements are the most restrictive of any U.S. state privacy law. Controllers must limit their collection of personal data to what is "reasonably necessary and proportionate" to provide or maintain a specific product or service requested by the consumer. For sensitive data, the standard is elevated to ["strictly necessary"](https://mslawgroup.com/marylands-modpa-a-new-standard-for-data-privacy-and-data-minimization/) to provide or maintain the requested product or service. Unlike most other state privacy laws -- which permit data collection for broadly stated "business purposes" -- [MODPA ties permissible collection directly to a consumer-initiated request](https://www.osano.com/articles/maryland-online-data-privacy-act-modpa), severely limiting pretextual data harvesting.

### Sensitive Data Protections

MODPA defines sensitive data to include racial or ethnic origin, national origin, religious beliefs, health conditions or diagnoses, sex life information, sexual orientation, status as transgender or nonbinary, citizenship or immigration status, genetic or biometric data, data of known children under 13, and precise geolocation data. The law imposes an [outright prohibition on the sale of sensitive data](https://www.datagrail.io/blog/data-privacy/why-marylands-new-privacy-law-could-be-the-strictest-in-the-us/) under any circumstances -- even with affirmative consumer consent. This is the first such blanket ban under any U.S. state privacy law. Controllers must obtain opt-in consent before processing sensitive data, and such processing is limited to what is "strictly necessary" for the requested product or service.

### Children's and Minors' Data

MODPA prohibits the processing of personal data of consumers known or reasonably believed to be under 18 for purposes of targeted advertising or data sales. The ["known or should have reasonably known" standard](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/what-you-need-know-maryland-data-privacy-law/) effectively imposes constructive knowledge requirements, going beyond the actual-knowledge standard used in most other state privacy laws (and COPPA). Personal data of a known child under 13 is automatically classified as sensitive data, triggering the strictest data minimization and processing restrictions.

### Consumer Rights

MODPA grants Maryland consumers the following rights:

- **Right to Access:** Confirm whether a controller is processing their personal data and obtain a copy of that data.
- **Right to Correction:** Request correction of inaccurate personal data.
- **Right to Deletion:** Request deletion of personal data, subject to certain exceptions.
- **Right to Data Portability:** Obtain their data in a portable, readily usable format.
- **Right to Opt Out:** Opt out of the processing of personal data for targeted advertising, data sales, or profiling in furtherance of decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects.

Controllers must respond to consumer requests within 45 days, with a possible 45-day extension where reasonably necessary.

### Universal Opt-Out Mechanism

MODPA [requires controllers to honor universal opt-out mechanisms](https://www.koleyjessen.com/insights/publications/maryland-online-data-privacy-act) (UOOMs), such as browser-based Global Privacy Control signals. This aligns Maryland with approximately 11 other states that mandate recognition of UOOMs, reducing friction for consumers exercising their opt-out rights.

### Transparency Requirements

Controllers must provide a reasonably accessible privacy notice that includes: the categories of personal data collected, the purposes of processing, the categories of third parties with whom data is shared, and instructions for exercising consumer rights. [MODPA goes further than most states](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2024/08/maryland-online-data-privacy-act--an-overview.html) by requiring disclosure of the categories of third parties that receive personal data -- not just the categories of data shared.

### Exemptions

MODPA exempts state and local government entities, certain financial institutions subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), and data regulated by HIPAA, FERPA, and other federal sector-specific laws. Unlike many other state privacy laws, [MODPA does not broadly exempt nonprofits](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/maryland-legislature-passes-state-privacy-bill-with-robust-requirements-and-broad-threshold-for-application); only nonprofits that process or share personal data solely to assist (1) law enforcement agencies investigating criminal or fraudulent acts relating to insurance, or (2) first responders responding to catastrophic events are exempt. Employee data, de-identified data, and B2B contact data are excluded from coverage.

### Enforcement and Penalties

MODPA is enforced exclusively by the Maryland Attorney General. A violation constitutes an "unfair, abusive, or deceptive trade practice" under the Maryland Consumer Protection Act (MCPA). Through April 1, 2027, the AG may -- at its discretion -- issue a notice of violation and provide a [60-day opportunity to cure](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/maryland-legislature-passes-comprehensive-data-privacy-law) before pursuing civil penalties. After April 1, 2027, this discretionary cure period expires and the full enforcement provisions of the Maryland Consumer Protection Act apply without any cure opportunity. Courts may impose penalties of up to $10,000 per initial violation and up to $25,000 for each subsequent violation. These penalties are among the highest for state privacy laws -- Virginia's VCDPA, for example, caps penalties at $7,500 per violation. MODPA does not provide for a private right of action.

### Effective Dates

MODPA took effect on [October 1, 2025](https://www.ketch.com/regulatory-compliance/maryland-online-data-privacy-act-modpa), but the law does not apply to companies' data processing activities until April 1, 2026, providing a compliance grace period. AG enforcement began on April 1, 2026.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

MODPA's relatively low applicability threshold means a broad range of businesses are affected, including mid-size companies that may fall below thresholds in other states. Any entity conducting business in Maryland or targeting Maryland residents that meets either of the two processing thresholds must comply. The lack of a broad nonprofit exemption is [particularly notable](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/maryland-legislature-passes-state-privacy-bill-with-robust-requirements-and-broad-threshold-for-application) and may catch organizations that assumed exemption based on their experience with other state laws.

### Compliance Challenges

The strict data minimization standard -- tying permissible collection to what is "reasonably necessary and proportionate" for a consumer-requested product or service -- may require [significant operational changes](https://www.blankrome.com/publications/maryland-passes-unique-and-operationally-challenging-privacy-law) for businesses accustomed to broader collection practices. Companies that rely on sensitive data monetization face the most disruptive impact, given the absolute prohibition on sensitive data sales. Businesses must also implement mechanisms to honor universal opt-out signals and adopt the "constructive knowledge" standard for identifying minors.

### National Implications

MODPA may influence future state privacy legislation. As [Blank Rome LLP observed](https://www.blankrome.com/publications/maryland-passes-unique-and-operationally-challenging-privacy-law), MODPA represents a shift away from the VCDPA-model approach and toward more consumer-protective frameworks. Compliance programs designed solely around the VCDPA/CPA model may be inadequate for Maryland, requiring a separate compliance workstream. MODPA's provisions could accelerate federal privacy legislation efforts by demonstrating that states will not wait for Congress to act and will continue to raise the bar independently.

## Action Items

- **Conduct a MODPA gap analysis** against existing privacy compliance programs, paying particular attention to data minimization practices, sensitive data handling, and minor identification procedures.
- **Review and revise data collection practices** to ensure personal data collection is limited to what is "reasonably necessary and proportionate" for consumer-requested products or services; eliminate any reliance on broad "business purpose" justifications.
- **Cease all sales of sensitive data** to Maryland residents, regardless of consent obtained. Audit data broker and third-party sharing arrangements for sensitive data flows. Note that the sensitive data definition includes national origin and transgender/nonbinary status in addition to the categories found in most other state laws.
- **Implement universal opt-out mechanism recognition** (e.g., Global Privacy Control) if not already in place.
- **Update privacy notices** to disclose categories of third parties receiving personal data, in addition to data categories collected.
- **Adopt constructive-knowledge procedures** for identifying consumers under 18 and ensure no targeted advertising or data sales occur for those consumers.
- **Assess nonprofit applicability** -- organizations that assumed nonprofit exemptions under other state laws should confirm whether they qualify for MODPA's narrow exemption, which covers only nonprofits assisting law enforcement investigations of insurance-related criminal or fraudulent acts, or first responders at catastrophic events.
- **Plan for the cure period sunset on April 1, 2027.** Until that date, the AG has discretion to offer a 60-day cure opportunity before filing enforcement actions. After April 1, 2027, no cure period is available and the AG may proceed directly to civil penalties.
- **Monitor AG enforcement actions** beginning April 1, 2026, for guidance on interpretation of key provisions, particularly data minimization and the "strictly necessary" standard for sensitive data.
