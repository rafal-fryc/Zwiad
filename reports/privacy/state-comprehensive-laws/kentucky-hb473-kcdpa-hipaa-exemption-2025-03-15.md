# Kentucky HB 473: KCDPA Amended to Exempt HIPAA-Covered Health Data and Narrow Disparate Impact Assessment Trigger

**Jurisdiction:** Kentucky | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2025-03-24

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On March 15, 2025, Kentucky Governor Andy Beshear signed [House Bill 473](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/25rs/hb473.html) (25 RS HB 473) into law, amending the Kentucky Consumer Data Protection Act (KCDPA) (KRS 367.3611–367.3629) ahead of the statute's January 1, 2026 effective date. The bill makes two substantive changes. First, it explicitly exempts two categories of health data from the KCDPA's coverage: (1) information collected by HIPAA-covered health care providers maintaining protected health information (PHI) in accordance with HIPAA; and (2) information maintained in limited data sets as described in 45 C.F.R. § 164.514(e) by HIPAA-covered entities. Second, the bill modifies the data protection impact assessment (DPIA) requirement for profiling activities by inserting the qualifier "unlawful" before "disparate impact," narrowing the scope of profiling activities that trigger a mandatory assessment. The [official enrolled act text](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/acts/25RS/documents/0013.pdf) is available from the Kentucky Legislative Research Commission. The amendments take effect simultaneously with the KCDPA on January 1, 2026. Healthcare providers and other covered organizations should evaluate which of their data processing activities now fall outside the KCDPA's scope and update their compliance programs accordingly.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

Kentucky enacted the KCDPA on April 4, 2024, when Governor Beshear signed [House Bill 15](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/recorddocuments/bill/24RS/hb15/bill.pdf) into law, making Kentucky the fifteenth state to adopt a comprehensive consumer data privacy statute. The KCDPA is modeled on Virginia's Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA) and is codified at KRS 367.3611 through 367.3629. The law covers controllers that process personal data of at least 100,000 Kentucky consumers annually, or of at least 25,000 consumers while deriving more than 50% of gross revenue from the sale of personal data. Enforcement rests exclusively with the Kentucky Attorney General, with civil penalties up to $7,500 per violation; there is no private right of action. The KCDPA was designed to take effect on January 1, 2026, with data protection assessment obligations deferred to June 1, 2026.

Even before the law's effective date, stakeholders — particularly in the healthcare sector — raised concerns about potential overlap between the KCDPA and existing federal health data obligations under HIPAA. The KCDPA as originally enacted contained a general exemption for HIPAA-covered entities but did not specifically address protected health information and limited data sets maintained by those entities. Healthcare providers operating as HIPAA-covered entities were uncertain whether some of their data processing activities might fall outside the general entity-level exemption and remain subject to KCDPA obligations. Representative Josh Branscomb introduced HB 473 as a targeted "cleanup" measure to resolve this ambiguity. The bill unanimously passed the Kentucky Senate on March 12, 2025, and was signed into law three days later.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

**HIPAA Health Data Exemptions**

HB 473 amends the KCDPA's exemption provisions to carve out two discrete categories of health data:

1. **Protected Health Information maintained by HIPAA-covered providers.** The amendment explicitly exempts information collected by health care providers acting as covered entities under HIPAA that maintain PHI in accordance with HIPAA and applicable regulations. This provision tracks the language of HIPAA's Privacy Rule and clarifies that data already flowing within the HIPAA regulatory structure is not subject to KCDPA controller obligations, consumer rights requests, or data minimization requirements.

2. **Limited Data Sets held by HIPAA-covered entities.** The amendment also exempts information maintained in limited data sets as described in [45 C.F.R. § 164.514(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.514). Under HIPAA's Privacy Rule, a limited data set is health information from which most direct identifiers (names, addresses, etc.) have been removed, but which may still contain geographic data down to city level, dates, and ages. Covered entities commonly share limited data sets for research, public health, and health care operations under a data use agreement rather than a full business associate agreement. HB 473 confirms that limited data sets compliant with 45 C.F.R. § 164.514(e) are equally outside the KCDPA's scope.

Together, the two exemptions resolve the pre-existing statutory ambiguity identified by [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/kentucky-amends-consumer-privacy-law-to-exempt-certain-hipaa-covered-data) and [Orrick, Herrington & Sutcliffe](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2025-03-21/kentucky-enacts-technical-amendments-to-the-kentucky-consumer-data-protection-act/) as the principal motivation for the amendment. The exemptions are data-specific, not entity-specific: a HIPAA-covered hospital that also runs a consumer wellness application collecting non-PHI data would still be subject to the KCDPA for that separate data stream. Practitioners at [McBrayer PLLC](https://www.mcbrayerfirm.com/blogs-intellectual-property-blog,kentuckys-comprehensive-data-privacy-law-what-healthcare-providers-need-to-know) have noted that healthcare providers operating in hybrid scenarios — for example, those collecting data from consumer wearables that do not automatically become PHI — must still account for potential KCDPA obligations for non-HIPAA data.

**Disparate Impact Assessment Modification**

The second principal change in HB 473 addresses the KCDPA's data protection impact assessment (DPIA) requirements for profiling activities. Under the original KCDPA, a DPIA was required when processing personal data for profiling purposes where that profiling presented a reasonably foreseeable risk of, among other things, "disparate impact on consumers." HB 473 inserts the word "unlawful" before "disparate impact," so the trigger now reads "a reasonably foreseeable risk of unlawful, disparate impact on consumers."

As [WilmerHale's March 21, 2025 state privacy law update](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20250321-state-comprehensive-privacy-law-update-march-21-2025) and the [Consumer Financial Services blog](https://consumerfsblog.com/2025/03/kentucky-consumer-data-protection-act-amendments-expand-exemptions-narrow-disparate-impact-assessment/) both observe, the addition of "unlawful" narrows the field of profiling activities that require a formal DPIA. Under the amended text, a DPIA is triggered only when profiling creates a foreseeable risk of impermissible discrimination under applicable law — such as the Fair Credit Reporting Act, the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, or other anti-discrimination statutes — rather than any statistical disparity in outcome across demographic groups. This aligns the Kentucky standard more closely with existing federal anti-discrimination frameworks and reduces the compliance burden for organizations whose profiling activities produce disparate outcomes without crossing into legally prohibited conduct.

**Legislative Vote and Effective Date**

HB 473 passed the Kentucky Senate unanimously on March 12, 2025, and was signed by Governor Beshear on March 15, 2025. The official enrolled chapter is [Chapter 13 of the 2025 Regular Session Acts](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/acts/25RS/documents/0013.pdf). The amendments are effective January 1, 2026, simultaneously with the KCDPA's broader effective date.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

**Healthcare Sector**

The HIPAA exemption clarifications offer the greatest relief to hospitals, health systems, physician practices, health plans, and clearinghouses operating as HIPAA-covered entities. Organizations that maintain PHI or limited data sets under HIPAA governance can exclude that data from their KCDPA compliance programs, eliminating the need to respond to KCDPA consumer rights requests (access, deletion, correction, portability) for that data or to include it in data minimization assessments. [HIPAA Times](https://hipaatimes.com/kentucky-amends-consumer-data-protection-act-with-new-exemptions) notes that the amendment resolves a compliance gap that had created uncertainty for the industry.

However, relief is not universal. Healthcare providers that collect personal data outside the HIPAA framework — through consumer-facing wellness applications, employer wellness programs not subject to HIPAA, or consumer wearable data that the provider chooses not to incorporate into the medical record — will still be subject to KCDPA obligations for those data streams. The exemptions are keyed to whether the data is "maintained" as PHI or a limited data set, not merely to whether the entity is a covered entity.

**Data-Intensive Organizations Using Profiling**

The narrowing of the "disparate impact" DPIA trigger benefits organizations in financial services, insurance, employment, housing, and marketing that use algorithmic profiling tools. Under the amended standard, only profiling that creates a foreseeable risk of unlawful discrimination must be assessed — a higher bar than the original text's unqualified "disparate impact" formulation. Organizations that had designed compliance programs around the broader trigger may be able to streamline their assessment workflows. However, organizations should be careful not to conflate this narrowing with the elimination of DPIA obligations: profiling activities still require assessments for other foreseeable risks (unfair or deceptive treatment, financial, physical, or reputational injury, etc.).

**Effective Date and Transition**

All amended provisions take effect January 1, 2026. Organizations have until that date to finalize their compliance programs in light of the clarifications. The KCDPA's DPIA obligations for data protection assessments related to sensitive data processing and targeted advertising remain on the June 1, 2026 schedule under the original KCDPA text.

**Enforcement Outlook**

The Kentucky Attorney General holds exclusive enforcement authority. The 30-day cure period applicable to KCDPA violations has no sunset and remains in place. Civil penalties remain capped at $7,500 per violation. No enforcement guidance specific to HB 473 has been issued at the time of this writing. The Attorney General's office has not publicly commented on its enforcement priorities for the amended statute.

## Action Items

- **Healthcare providers:** Conduct a data inventory to identify all data streams, distinguishing PHI and limited data sets subject to the HIPAA exemption from consumer data that remains subject to KCDPA obligations.
- **Hybrid data processors:** Update KCDPA compliance programs to reflect that the HIPAA exemption is data-specific, not organization-specific; ensure non-HIPAA consumer data (e.g., wearable/wellness data) is still addressed in KCDPA-required privacy notices and consumer rights workflows.
- **Profiling and algorithmic decisioning teams:** Review existing DPIA triggers and update internal assessment policies to reflect the new "unlawful disparate impact" standard; confirm that assessments are still required for other foreseeable profiling risks under the KCDPA.
- **Business associate agreements:** Audit existing BAAs to determine whether any KCDPA responsibilities need to be allocated contractually for data streams that remain outside the HIPAA exemption.
- **All covered organizations:** Update privacy notices and consumer rights procedures to reflect the final amended KCDPA ahead of the January 1, 2026 effective date.
- **Monitor:** Watch for enforcement guidance from the Kentucky Attorney General's office as the January 2026 effective date approaches.
