# CPPA Releases Draft CCPA Regulations on ADMT, Cybersecurity Audits, and Risk Assessments Ahead of July 2024 Board Meeting

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 23, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On July 15, 2024, the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) published a substantial draft rulemaking package — exceeding 200 pages — ahead of its July 16, 2024 Board meeting. The package proposed sweeping new obligations under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) covering automated decisionmaking technology (ADMT), cybersecurity audits, privacy risk assessments, insurance sector coverage, and updates to existing CCPA regulations. The Board declined to advance the regulations to formal rulemaking at that meeting, citing an incomplete economic impact assessment and disagreements among board members over scope. Staff were directed to revise and narrow the package, with a revised draft expected for the September 2024 meeting. The regulations were ultimately advanced to formal rulemaking in November 2024 and finalized by the CPPA Board in July 2025 — representing the culmination of a rulemaking process that began in 2021. The initial compliance cost estimate of $4.2 billion in year one for California businesses alone underscores the regulation's significance.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### California's Privacy Regulatory Framework

California's consumer privacy regime is the most comprehensive of any US state. The [California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100.), enacted in 2018, gave California consumers core rights: to know what personal information is collected, to delete it, and to opt out of its sale. In November 2020, California voters passed [Proposition 24](https://cppa.ca.gov/about_us/), known as the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), which strengthened the CCPA and created the CPPA — the first dedicated state privacy enforcement agency in the United States. The CPPA assumed regulatory and enforcement authority from the California Attorney General, with CPRA amendments operative as of January 1, 2023.

### Rulemaking Origins (2021–2024)

The CPPA's rulemaking authority extends to multiple domains beyond the initial CCPA/CPRA framework. From 2021 onward, the agency conducted pre-rulemaking stakeholder sessions on automated decisionmaking, cybersecurity, and risk assessments. By late 2023, the CPPA had circulated draft cybersecurity audit and ADMT regulations for preliminary review. In May 2024, the CPPA [held three statewide pre-rulemaking stakeholder sessions](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20240424.html) (announced April 24, 2024) — held May 13 in Los Angeles, May 15 in Fresno, and May 22 in Sacramento — to gather public input before finalizing the draft package.

### July 2024 Context

Prior to the July 16, 2024 meeting, the [CPPA published board materials](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20240716.html), including a [draft text of the proposed regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20240716_item8_draft_text.pdf) — a comprehensive overlay of proposed new language on the existing CCPA regulatory framework. This was the first time the full multi-subject package had been released publicly in consolidated form for scrutiny. A [Katten Muchin Rosenman client alert](https://quickreads.ext.katten.com/post/102jdxg/the-california-regulator-publishes-new-ccpa-regulations-for-public-comment) noted the release as a significant development for businesses across all industries operating in California.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Automated Decisionmaking Technology (ADMT)

The draft ADMT regulations represented the most novel aspect of the proposed package. Under the draft, [ADMT](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) was defined as "any technology that processes personal information and uses computation to execute a decision, replace human decisionmaking, or substantially facilitate human decisionmaking." This broad definition was one point of contention: some board members, notably Board Member Alastair MacTaggart, criticized its expansive reach.

The draft imposed obligations on businesses that use ADMT for three covered use cases:
1. **Significant decisions** affecting a consumer (e.g., in financial services, employment, housing, education, or healthcare contexts);
2. **Extensive profiling** of a consumer, including behavioral advertising; and
3. **Training ADMT or artificial intelligence models** for such purposes.

For covered uses, businesses would be required to:
- Provide **pre-use notices** informing consumers of the business's use of ADMT;
- Offer consumers the **right to opt out** of ADMT use;
- Provide consumers **access rights** to information about how ADMT was used in decisions affecting them; and
- Honor consumer **appeal rights** regarding ADMT-driven decisions.

### Cybersecurity Audits

The draft cybersecurity audit regulations would require qualifying businesses to conduct **annual cybersecurity audits** performed by independent, objective auditors and to certify completion annually to the CPPA. The audit must cover at least 17 program "components" specified in the regulations. The threshold for who qualifies was presented with numerical options rather than a single fixed figure — prior drafts proposed options of 250,000, 500,000, or 1,000,000 or more consumers as alternative applicability thresholds, with parallel options for sensitive personal information and data involving minors.

Per [Wilson Sonsini's detailed analysis](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/substantial-new-ccpa-regulations-inch-closer-to-reality-a-high-level-overview-of-the-new-requirements-and-their-projected-dollar4-billion-cost-to-california-businesses.html), the regulations would require businesses to implement qualifying cybersecurity programs prior to the audit requirement taking effect — a phase-in approach designed to give businesses time to build compliant programs before they must be audited against them.

### Privacy Risk Assessments

The draft risk assessment regulations would require covered businesses to complete and maintain **privacy risk assessments** before processing personal information in ways that present a "significant risk" to consumer privacy. Triggering activities under the draft included:
- Selling or sharing personal information;
- Processing sensitive personal information;
- Using ADMT for a significant decision or for extensive profiling (including behavioral advertising); and
- Training ADMT or artificial intelligence.

Risk assessments would need to involve relevant stakeholders and produce a written report maintained by the business. As [Hunton Andrews Kurth explained](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/cppa-board-discusses-upcoming-formal-rulemaking-on-automated-decisionmaking-technology-risk-assessments-cybersecurity-audits-insurance-and-updates-to-existing-regulations), a Standardized Regulatory Impact Assessment (SRIA) must be completed by the CPPA and submitted to the California Department of Finance before formal rulemaking can commence — this was one of the unfinished prerequisites cited when the Board declined to vote at the July meeting.

### Insurance Regulations

The draft package included proposed regulations extending CCPA coverage to the insurance sector, which has historically operated under a separate regulatory framework. Board members discussed whether the pending California insurance model code legislation should be allowed to progress before these regulations were finalized. As noted by the [FKKS Technology Law blog](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102jdj9/takeaways-from-the-cppas-july-2024-board-meeting), the insurance provisions were flagged as likely to be delayed pending developments in the California legislature.

### Updates to Existing Regulations

The package also included approximately 72 pages of proposed updates and amendments to the existing CCPA regulatory framework — covering topics such as consent mechanisms, disclosure requirements, and technical standards for honoring consumer requests.

## Outcome of the July 16, 2024 Board Meeting [HIGH confidence]

The CPPA Board **declined to advance** the draft regulations to formal rulemaking at its July 16, 2024 meeting. As [Alston & Bird reported](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/cppa-board-declines-to-advance-ccpa-regulations-to-formal-rulemaking-cppa-highlights-enforcement-priorities/), the board cited two primary reasons:

1. **Incomplete SRIA**: Staff had not yet completed the Standardized Regulatory Impact Assessment required before formal rulemaking can begin.
2. **Scope disagreements**: Board members disagreed on how broadly the ADMT and risk assessment obligations should apply, with MacTaggart and others arguing the draft's scope was too expansive.

The Board directed staff to **revise the draft regulations to narrow scope** and return with a revised package for review at the September 2024 meeting.

Separately, the CPPA used the meeting to update board members on its enforcement priorities. Consumer complaints most commonly involved: the right to delete, the collection or disclosure of personal information, and opt-out of sale or sharing — with additional complaints relating to children's privacy, financial incentives, and loyalty programs.

### Subsequent Developments

The July 2024 vote was not the end of the rulemaking process but rather a significant pause that shaped the final regulations:

- **November 8, 2024**: The CPPA Board [advanced the revised regulations to formal rulemaking](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/11/cppa-board-advances-ccpa-regulations-to-formal-rul).
- **November 22, 2024**: The CPPA [opened the formal 45-day public comment period](https://natlawreview.com/article/cppa-opens-public-comment-period-proposed-ccpa-regulations) on the revised draft.
- **July 24, 2025**: The CPPA Board [unanimously approved the finalized regulations (5-0)](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html) on ADMT, risk assessments, and cybersecurity audits — concluding a process that began in 2021.
- **September 22, 2025**: The California Office of Administrative Law (OAL) approved the final regulations.
- **January 1, 2026**: The regulations became operative (with staggered phase-in dates for certain requirements).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected

The proposed regulations as drafted would affect tens of thousands of California businesses across virtually all industry sectors. The CPPA's own preliminary estimates pegged year-one compliance costs at approximately **$4.2 billion** — a figure that the [Wilson Sonsini analysis noted](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/substantial-new-ccpa-regulations-inch-closer-to-reality-a-high-level-overview-of-the-new-requirements-and-their-projected-dollar4-billion-cost-to-california-businesses.html) does not include companies headquartered outside California that nonetheless do business there and are subject to the CCPA.

Key affected sectors include:
- **Technology companies**: Heavy users of AI and algorithmic decisionmaking face the broadest ADMT obligations;
- **Financial services**: Significant decisions affecting loans, credit, or financial products would trigger ADMT and risk assessment requirements;
- **Healthcare and insurance**: Both the ADMT provisions and the dedicated insurance regulations directly target these sectors;
- **Retailers and advertisers**: Behavioral advertising would constitute "extensive profiling," triggering both ADMT notice/opt-out and risk assessment obligations;
- **Data brokers**: Already regulated under the CCPA and California Delete Act, data brokers face additional scrutiny under both the cybersecurity and risk assessment provisions.

### Compliance Timeline (as of July 2024 Draft)

At the time of the July 2024 release, the CPPA had not yet initiated formal rulemaking. Under California's Administrative Procedure Act, formal rulemaking requires at minimum a 45-day public comment period; substantial changes trigger additional 15-day periods. As [FKKS analyzed](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102jdj9/takeaways-from-the-cppas-july-2024-board-meeting), if the Board had advanced to formal rulemaking in September 2024, the earliest operativity would have been Q2 2025 given typical OAL timelines.

The actual timeline extended further: with formal rulemaking initiated in November 2024, final OAL approval came in September 2025 and most obligations are now operative as of January 1, 2026, with some requirements subject to staggered compliance deadlines.

### Economic and Operational Impact

The scale of the proposed compliance obligations — annual third-party cybersecurity audits, mandatory risk assessments before each qualifying processing activity, pre-use ADMT notices and opt-out mechanisms, and consumer access and appeal infrastructure — represents a significant new operational burden. Businesses will need to:
- Audit and document existing ADMT systems;
- Implement opt-out and appeal workflows;
- Engage independent cybersecurity auditors;
- Maintain written risk assessment reports; and
- Update consumer-facing notices across all digital touchpoints.

## Action Items

- **Inventory ADMT use**: Identify all automated or algorithmic systems that make or substantially influence significant decisions, conduct extensive profiling, or are used for training — these are the primary triggers for ADMT obligations under the finalized regulations.
- **Assess cybersecurity audit readiness**: Determine whether your business meets the threshold for mandatory annual cybersecurity audits, and begin building compliant cybersecurity programs aligned to the CPPA's 17-component framework.
- **Conduct a risk assessment gap analysis**: Map existing data processing activities against the finalized risk assessment trigger list (sale/sharing, sensitive PI, ADMT use, AI training) and prioritize documentation for highest-risk activities.
- **Review consumer-facing notices**: Evaluate existing privacy notices, opt-out mechanisms, and consumer request workflows for compliance with the finalized ADMT pre-use notice and opt-out requirements.
- **Monitor CPPA guidance**: The CPPA is expected to issue interpretive guidance on the finalized regulations. Sign up for agency announcements at [cppa.ca.gov](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/) and monitor enforcement advisories.
- **Engage in any remaining comment opportunities**: Watch for any further CPPA guidance or supplemental rulemaking actions that may refine scope or create safe harbors.
