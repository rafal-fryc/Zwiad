# Colorado Finalizes CPA Rule Amendments: Biometric Data, Minors' Online Privacy, and AG Opinion Letters

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** January 14, 2025

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On December 5, 2024, the Colorado Attorney General's Department of Law adopted final amendments to the [Colorado Privacy Act Rules (4 CCR 904-3)](https://www.coloradosos.gov/CCR/DisplayRule.do?action=ruleinfo&ruleId=3396&deptID=0&agencyID=11&deptName=900+Department+of+Law&agencyName=904+Attorney+General-Consumer+Protection+Section&seriesNum=4+CCR+904-3), filed with the Secretary of State on December 17, 2024 and effective January 30, 2025. The amendments implement two 2024 legislative changes to the [Colorado Privacy Act (CPA), C.R.S. § 6-1-1301 et seq.](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-6/fair-trade-and-restraint-of-trade/article-1/part-13/): [House Bill 24-1130](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1130) on biometric data (effective July 1, 2025) and [Senate Bill 24-041](http://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-041) on minors' online privacy (effective October 1, 2025). A third component establishes a new formal process for businesses to request Opinion Letters and Interpretive Guidance from the AG — effective January 30, 2025 — that provides a good-faith reliance defense against enforcement. Separately, the 60-day cure period that had been available to businesses facing alleged CPA violations expired on January 1, 2025, materially raising enforcement risk.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The Colorado Privacy Act

Colorado enacted the CPA via [Senate Bill 21-190](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/), signed July 7, 2021 and effective July 1, 2023. The CPA is a Virginia-model omnibus consumer privacy statute applying to controllers of personal data of 100,000 or more Colorado consumers annually, or 25,000 or more consumers when the controller derives revenue or receives a discount on the price of goods or services from the sale of personal data. It grants consumers rights of access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising, data sale, and profiling.

The original CPA Rules (4 CCR 904-3) were first adopted March 15, 2023, filed March 25, 2023, and took effect July 1, 2023. They addressed general controller and processor obligations, data protection assessments, consumer rights request processes, and the universal opt-out mechanism (UOOM) requirement — under which the Global Privacy Control (GPC) browser signal is the only currently recognized UOOM in Colorado.

### 2024 Legislative Amendments Requiring New Rules

The Colorado General Assembly passed two significant CPA amendments in 2024 that required implementing rulemaking:

- **HB 24-1130** (signed May 31, 2024): Added biometric identifier and biometric data requirements to the CPA, including new consent obligations, written policy mandates, and retention/deletion requirements. Effective July 1, 2025.
- **SB 24-041** (signed May 31, 2024): Extended CPA protections specifically for minors under 18, imposing heightened consent requirements and prohibiting certain high-risk data processing without consent for that population. Effective October 1, 2025.

The AG's office published proposed draft amendments to implement these bills on September 13, 2024, held a public comment period (September 25 to November 7, 2024), and conducted a rulemaking hearing on November 7, 2024. Attorney General Phil Weiser filed a formal opinion on December 16, 2024 concluding the adopted rules are constitutional with no legal deficiencies.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### 1. Opinion Letters and Interpretive Guidance (Effective January 30, 2025)

The most immediately effective portion of the rule amendments establishes a two-tier guidance system under which entities subject to the CPA may seek formal written positions from the AG:

- **Opinion Letters**: Formal written opinions applying the CPA to a specific, prospective factual situation described by the requester. Requests must concern activities the requestor will undertake contingent on the AG's advice. Although the AG will not independently investigate the underlying facts, a business that receives and relies on an Opinion Letter in good faith has a defense against enforcement actions arising from the described conduct.
- **Interpretive Guidance**: Broader statements that "call attention" to well-established CPA interpretations or principles without applying them to a specific situation. These serve as general compliance resources rather than fact-specific opinions.

This guidance mechanism significantly reduces legal uncertainty for businesses operating at the margins of CPA applicability. The good-faith reliance defense is particularly valuable after the cure period sunset (see below).

### 2. Biometric Data Rules (Effective July 1, 2025)

The rules implement HB 24-1130's requirements by defining standards for the mandatory "biometric identifier notice" that covered entities must provide. Key requirements include:

- The notice must be "reasonably accessible" to individuals and written in plain, straightforward language
- The notice must describe: the specific biometric identifiers being collected; the purpose of collection; the length of the retention period; and any third-party disclosures
- **Employer-specific requirements**: Employers must obtain affirmative consent from employees and prospective employees before collecting and processing biometric identifiers. An exception exists permitting employers to condition employment on biometric collection — without prior consent — when identifiers are used solely for access control to physical locations, attendance monitoring, or workplace safety and security purposes
- Controllers must adopt a written biometric data policy covering: a retention schedule; a data security incident response plan; and deletion guidelines requiring deletion at the earliest of (a) when the initial collection purpose is satisfied, (b) 24 months after the individual's last interaction, or (c) 45 days after the controller determines storage is no longer necessary

Unlike Illinois's [Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA)](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004), HB 24-1130 and these rules do not create a private right of action. Enforcement is reserved for the AG and district attorneys.

### 3. Minors' Online Privacy Rules (Effective October 1, 2025)

The rules implementing SB 24-041 establish definitional clarity and compliance guidance for the new minor-specific CPA obligations:

- The rules confirm that "child" means an individual under 13; "minor" means any consumer under 18
- Controllers offering online services, products, or features to known or willfully disregarded minors must exercise reasonable care to avoid "heightened risk of harm" to minors and conduct data protection assessments for any such service posing such risk
- **Consent thresholds**: For children under 13, parental or guardian consent is required. Minors aged 13-17 may provide their own consent. Without consent, controllers are prohibited from:
  - Processing minor personal data for targeted advertising, data sales, or profiling with legally significant effects
  - Processing for purposes beyond those disclosed at collection
  - Retaining data longer than reasonably necessary
  - Using design features that significantly increase or extend a minor's use of the service ("addictive design" prohibition)
  - Collecting precise geolocation data of minors (except when strictly necessary to provide the service, with in-app notification)

### 4. End of the 60-Day Cure Period (Effective January 1, 2025)

Separate from but contemporaneous with the rule amendments, the CPA's statutory 60-day cure period — which had allowed businesses to remediate alleged violations after receiving notice from the AG — expired by its own sunset provision on January 1, 2025. The AG now has full discretion to pursue enforcement actions immediately upon identifying violations without any notice-and-cure obligation. This materially increases enforcement risk for businesses that have not yet come into full CPA compliance.

The [Colorado AG has already demonstrated active enforcement intent](https://captaincompliance.com/education/colorados-privacy-enforcement-heats-up-what-the-gpc-sweep-and-end-of-cure-period-mean-for-your-business/), having previously conducted GPC compliance sweeps and, following the cure period's expiration, reached a $250,000 settlement with an adtech company and a $300,000 consent decree with a national health app for sensitive data violations.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected

- **All CPA-covered businesses** face the immediate impact of losing the cure period protection and must ensure existing compliance programs meet CPA standards without any grace period for remediation
- **Employers that use biometric identifiers** (fingerprint scanners, facial recognition, retinal scans) for access control, timekeeping, or authentication must update consent processes and adopt compliant written biometric policies before July 1, 2025
- **Businesses offering digital products or services directed at or used by consumers under 18** face significant new consent infrastructure requirements by October 1, 2025, including data protection assessments and redesign of any features that could be deemed addictive design
- **Any business operating at the margins of CPA applicability** now has a formal channel — Opinion Letters — to obtain binding guidance and a reliance defense

### Compliance Timeline Summary

| Deadline | Requirement |
|----------|------------|
| January 1, 2025 | 60-day cure period expires; AG may enforce immediately |
| January 30, 2025 | Opinion Letters/Interpretive Guidance rules take effect |
| July 1, 2025 | Biometric data rules under HB 24-1130 take effect |
| October 1, 2025 | Minors' online privacy rules under SB 24-041 take effect |

### Enforcement Outlook

The combination of expired cure rights and active AG enforcement signals a high-risk environment for CPA non-compliance in 2025. AG Weiser's office has explicitly prioritized GPC/UOOM compliance in prior sweeps and has demonstrated willingness to pursue formal consent decrees. Businesses should not assume advance notice before enforcement.

## Action Items

- **Immediately**: Audit current CPA compliance status across all consumer-facing data processing activities, given the cure period has expired. Any gaps now expose the business to immediate enforcement.
- **By January 30, 2025**: Familiarize compliance and legal teams with the Opinion Letter request process. Consider submitting Opinion Letters for genuinely uncertain data processing activities to obtain a good-faith reliance defense.
- **By June 1, 2025**: Inventory all biometric data collection touchpoints (employee access systems, facial recognition, voiceprint tools). Draft or update written biometric data policies to cover retention schedules, incident response, and deletion timelines. Update consent processes for employees and prospective employees.
- **By August 1, 2025**: Conduct data protection assessments for all online services, products, or features accessible to consumers under 18. Map minor-specific data flows and consent mechanisms. Audit design features for addictive-design patterns that would require consent or modification.
- **Ongoing**: Monitor the AG's Interpretive Guidance publications and any new Opinion Letters for guidance on evolving CPA interpretations. Track GPC compliance — the AG has made it a priority enforcement area.
