# New Jersey Enacts Fair Price Protection Act, Banning Surveillance Pricing on Groceries

**Jurisdiction:** New Jersey | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-24

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On July 23, 2026, Governor Mikie Sherrill signed the [Fair Price Protection Act](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/2026/approved/20260723a.shtml) (S3612/A4085) into law at a Newark grocery store, making New Jersey the third state -- after Maryland and Connecticut -- to actually enact legislation banning "surveillance pricing," and the first to attach a private right of action to such a law, per [EPIC](https://epic.org/nj-governor-signs-grocery-surveillance-pricing-ban-with-private-right-of-action-into-law/) and the [New Jersey Monitor](https://newjerseymonitor.com/2026/07/23/nj-ban-surveillance-pricing-grocery/). The law prohibits retail food stores and third-party grocery delivery platforms from using an algorithm or automated system to set, adjust, or recommend prices for groceries and other foodstuffs based, in whole or in part, on a consumer's personal data -- including biometric data, genetic information, location, browsing activity, and purchase history -- where doing so results in different prices for different consumers for the same or a substantially similar product, per the bill text cached by [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/NJ/text/S3612/id/3397517) and reporting from [The Shelby Report](https://theshelbyreport.com/2026/07/02/new-jersey-passes-fair-price-protection-act-to-ban-personalized-grocery-pricing/). The law also imposes a one-year moratorium on retailers' new deployment of electronic shelf labels (digital price tags), though it does not require removal of labels already in use, according to [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/new-jersey-bans-surveillance-pricing-and-freezes-digital-shelf-labels/) and [The Packer](https://www.thepacker.com/news/retail/new-jersey-becomes-first-nation-ban-grocery-surveillance-pricing-freeze-digital-tags).

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Governor Sherrill signed the Fair Price Protection Act on July 23, 2026, combining Senate Bill S3612 with companion Assembly Bill A4085/A4523, per the [official gubernatorial press release](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/2026/approved/20260723a.shtml) and [ROI-NJ](https://www.roi-nj.com/2026/07/24/politics/sherrill-signs-fair-price-protection-act-to-shield-grocery-shoppers-from-surveillance-pricing/).
- The Governor stated: "New Jersey families are already feeling the pressure of higher costs. The last thing they need is companies secretly using their personal data to charge them more than someone else for the exact same product... This law puts New Jersey shoppers first by protecting their privacy and ensuring fairness in pricing," per the [official press release](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/2026/approved/20260723a.shtml).
- The law makes surveillance pricing and "personalized algorithmic pricing" an unlawful practice under New Jersey's Consumer Fraud Act; a first violation carries a $10,000 penalty and subsequent violations carry $20,000 penalties, matching the $20,000 figure cited in the Governor's public remarks, per [The Shelby Report](https://theshelbyreport.com/2026/07/24/nj-gov-signs-surveillance-pricing-ban-despite-industry-objections/) and [EPIC](https://epic.org/nj-governor-signs-grocery-surveillance-pricing-ban-with-private-right-of-action-into-law/).
- Separately, the Attorney General may bring a civil enforcement action seeking actual monetary damages or $50,000 per violation, whichever is greater, plus restitution and other equitable relief, per [The Shelby Report](https://theshelbyreport.com/2026/07/24/nj-gov-signs-surveillance-pricing-ban-despite-industry-objections/).
- Starting January 1, 2027, both the Attorney General and individual consumers may sue under a private right of action -- the first surveillance-pricing statute in the country to give consumers a direct cause of action -- with liable businesses potentially facing refunds, permit loss, and the statutory penalties above, per [EPIC](https://epic.org/nj-governor-signs-grocery-surveillance-pricing-ban-with-private-right-of-action-into-law/).
- The law's covered "groceries and other foodstuffs" definition is broad, extending beyond food to household cleaning items, health and beauty products, and pet supplies sold by retail food stores or grocery delivery platforms, per bill text via [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/NJ/text/S3612/id/3397517).
- The bill does not ban traditional loyalty programs, coupons, or across-the-board discounts -- only pricing that varies by individual consumer or consumer segment based on personal data, per [retail-insight-network.com](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/new-jersey-fair-price-protection-act/).
- Industry groups opposed the bill before signing: NetChoice argued the law's algorithmic-pricing ban would sweep in beneficial "dynamic discounting" tools used to reduce food waste and pass savings to consumers, per its [letter of opposition](https://netchoice.org/netchoice-letter-of-opposition-on-new-jerseys-a-4085-s-3612-the-fair-price-protection-act/), and the New Jersey Business & Industry Association warned of compliance fallout for grocers, per [NJBIA](https://njbia.org/governor-signs-bill-banning-surveillance-pricing-as-njbia-warns-of-fallout/).

## Action Items

- Grocery retailers and third-party grocery delivery platforms operating in New Jersey should audit any pricing algorithms, loyalty-app personalization, or dynamic-pricing tools that factor in individual consumer data before January 1, 2027, when the private right of action takes effect.
- Confirm that any electronic shelf label rollout plans in New Jersey stores are paused; new deployments are barred for one year from the law's effective date.
- Distinguish permissible practices (loyalty discounts, general dynamic/time-based discounting not tied to individual consumer data) from prohibited "surveillance pricing" and "personalized algorithmic pricing" under the statute's definitions, and document the distinction for compliance purposes.
- Monitor New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs guidance for interpretive rules on the Consumer Fraud Act designation, given the DCA's history of delayed/clarifying guidance on other recent NJ privacy statutes (e.g., the data broker law).
- Track parallel surveillance-pricing legislation in New York (One Fair Price Act, awaiting Governor Hochul's signature) since a fourth-state pattern would further raise the compliance stakes for multi-state grocery and retail operators.
