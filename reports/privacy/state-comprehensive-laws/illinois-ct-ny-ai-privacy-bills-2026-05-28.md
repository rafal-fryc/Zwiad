# Wave of AI and Privacy Laws Enacted in Illinois, Connecticut, and New York (2026)

**Jurisdictions:** Illinois | Connecticut | New York | **Category:** Privacy / AI Law | **Date:** 2026-05-28

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The 2026 state legislative season closed with a significant burst of AI and privacy lawmaking in three major states. Illinois passed the [Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act (SB 315)](https://www.nprillinois.org/illinois/2026-05-28/illinois-lawmakers-pass-landmark-ai-accountability-bill), the nation's first law mandating annual independent third-party safety audits of frontier AI model developers. Connecticut enacted two companion statutes — [SB 5 (the AI Responsibility and Transparency Act)](https://ctmirror.org/2026/05/01/artificial-intelligence-house-regulation-passage-ct/) and [SB 4](https://ctmirror.org/2026/05/04/consumer-data-privacy-regulation-ct-house/) (a sweeping data broker and privacy update) — creating one of the most comprehensive state AI regulatory frameworks in the country. New York enacted the [Safe by Design Act](https://iapp.org/news/a/notable-ai-privacy-bills-hit-finish-line-in-illinois-connecticut-and-new-york) for children's online safety as part of its FY2027 budget. Organizations operating across these states face overlapping, non-harmonized compliance obligations with staggered effective dates beginning as early as October 1, 2026.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

### Illinois — Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act (SB 315)

- The Illinois General Assembly gave unanimous 110-0 House approval on [May 27, 2026](https://www.nprillinois.org/illinois/2026-05-28/illinois-lawmakers-pass-landmark-ai-accountability-bill), with the Senate previously approving on May 21. Governor JB Pritzker has publicly stated he will sign the bill, posting that he "look[s] forward to signing SB 315."
- **Coverage:** Targets "large frontier developers" — companies with annual revenues exceeding $500 million that develop AI models trained using greater than 10²⁶ computational operations (FLOPs) with compute costs exceeding $100 million. This effectively covers a narrow set of major AI labs including OpenAI and Anthropic (both of whom publicly supported the bill), per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/illinois-legislature-passes-historic-ai-bill-rcna347191).
- **Annual third-party audits:** Mandates independent yearly safety audits of frontier AI developers — the [first such requirement in any U.S. state law](https://ai2.work/blog/illinois-sb-315-the-first-us-law-mandating-ai-safety-audits).
- **Frontier AI framework:** Requires large frontier developers to publish a formal framework addressing risks, governance, and cybersecurity; mandates transparency reports before model deployment; and provides whistleblower protections and internal reporting channels for covered employees, per [Transparency Coalition](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/illinois-lawmakers-send-significant-ai-frontier-model-safety-bill-to-gov-pritzker).
- **Safety incident reporting:** Companies must report critical safety incidents within 72 hours of discovery; incidents posing imminent risk of death or physical harm must be reported within 24 hours, per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/notable-ai-privacy-bills-hit-finish-line-in-illinois-connecticut-and-new-york).
- **Catastrophic risk threshold:** Includes harms capable of mass casualties or economic damages exceeding $1 billion through cyberattack or model malfunction beyond human control.
- **Enforcement:** Civil penalties up to $3 million per violation, enforced solely by the Illinois Attorney General.
- **Effective date:** January 1, 2028 (once signed by Governor Pritzker; signature expected).

### Connecticut — AI Responsibility and Transparency Act (SB 5)

- Governor Ned Lamont signed SB 5 on [May 27, 2026](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/05/ct-ai-transparency-safety-consumer-protection-law), following the legislature's passage on May 1.
- **Coverage:** Imposes obligations on developers (builders of AI tools) and deployers (entities — typically employers — that use AI to make consequential decisions), with a developer-to-deployer information-sharing duty where AI is marketed or intended to materially influence employment decisions, per [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/05/ct-ai-transparency-safety-consumer-protection-law).
- **Scope of subject matter:** A wide-ranging "omnibus" AI law covering automated employment decision tools (AEDTs), AI companion chatbots, social media, provenance data, frontier AI whistleblower protections, AI-related layoff notices (WARN Act amendments), and a state AI regulatory sandbox, per [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/05/unpacking-connecticuts-new-ai-law).
- **Anti-discrimination:** "AI is not a defense" amendments to anti-discrimination statutes take effect October 1, 2026 — employers cannot cite an AI system's output to excuse discriminatory decisions.
- **Frontier developer compute threshold:** The whistleblower protection provisions apply to developers training foundation models using more than 10²⁶ computing operations, per [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/05/ct-ai-transparency-safety-consumer-protection-law).
- **Staggered effective dates:**
  - October 1, 2026: AEDT framework, developer–deployer allocation provisions, anti-discrimination amendments, frontier whistleblower provisions, WARN AI/technology disclosure.
  - January 1, 2027: Large frontier developers must establish anonymous internal reporting processes.
  - October 1, 2027: Interactive disclosure and pre-decision notice obligations for AEDT deployments.
- **Renamed:** Officially titled the Connecticut Artificial Intelligence Responsibility and Transparency Act.

### Connecticut — Data Broker and Privacy Update (SB 4)

- Governor Lamont signed SB 4 alongside SB 5. The House passed it 141-6 on [May 4, 2026](https://ctmirror.org/2026/05/04/consumer-data-privacy-regulation-ct-house/); the Senate passed it 31-4.
- **Data broker registration:** Every business that sells or licenses brokered personal data to third parties must register with the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection by January 1, 2027, including mandatory fees and disclosure of all trade names and websites, per [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/connecticut-senate-bill-4-delivers-major-data-broker-crackdown-and-ctdpa-amendments-as-privacy-enforcement-accelerates-nationwide/).
- **Centralized deletion mechanism:** Creates a state-run portal launching in 2027 enabling consumers to submit a single deletion request to remove personal data from all registered data brokers simultaneously.
- **Surveillance pricing ban:** Prohibits retail sellers and third-party delivery services from engaging in surveillance pricing (using personal data to increase prices) unless they disclose when an automated pricing system is being used.
- **Additional protections:** Prohibits sale of consumers' precise geolocation data; expands deletion rights; adds facial recognition transparency requirements; narrows the definition of "publicly available" information.
- **Effective date:** October 1, 2026, per [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/third-times-the-charm-connecticut-enacts-annual-privacy-update/).

### New York — Safe by Design Act (Children's Online Safety)

- Governor Kathy Hochul signed the Safe by Design Act as part of the FY2027 state budget, per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/notable-ai-privacy-bills-hit-finish-line-in-illinois-connecticut-and-new-york) and [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/proposed-state-privacy-and-ai-law-update-june-1-2026/).
- **Coverage:** New children's online safety requirements for social media, gaming, and digital messaging platforms.
- **Key protections:** Requires covered platforms to implement safeguards restricting adults from interacting with underage users; prevents children's geolocation information from being accessed by users they are not connected to; requires parental consent for users under age 13 to access platforms; applies default-on protective design measures for users under age 17 without requiring age verification.
- **AI companion limits:** Requires entities to disable AI companion features by default for minors on covered platforms.
- **Context:** New York had previously enacted the RAISE Act (Responsible AI Safety and Education Act) in December 2025, creating a comprehensive frontier AI safety and governance regime for developers with revenues exceeding $500 million. The RAISE Act was overhauled by chapter amendments signed March 27, 2026 and takes effect January 1, 2027, per [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/artificial-intelligence-law-advisor/2026/04/ny-overhauls-frontier-ai-transparency-law) and [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-New-York-Finalizes-RAISE-Act-for-Frontier-AI-Models-Law-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027). Penalties under the RAISE Act reach up to $1 million for first violations and $3 million for subsequent violations.

## Why It Matters [HIGH confidence]

The simultaneous enactment of AI and privacy laws in three large commercial states creates an immediately complex compliance environment for multi-state operators:

1. **Frontier AI developers face overlapping state mandates.** Illinois SB 315 (effective 2028), New York RAISE Act (effective January 1, 2027), and Connecticut SB 5 frontier provisions all target the same class of large-compute AI developers. While the compute thresholds align (10²⁶ FLOPs), the specific obligations — audit requirements, incident reporting timelines, framework publication duties — are not identical across states. Developers cannot write a single compliance program that satisfies all three states simultaneously without careful mapping, per [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/the-growing-patchwork-of-state-ai-laws-what-it-means-for-employers).
2. **Connecticut creates the most immediate deadline pressure.** SB 5's AEDT provisions take effect October 1, 2026 — roughly four months away. Employers in Connecticut (or deploying AI tools to Connecticut employees) that use automated decision tools in hiring, performance management, or other employment contexts must have developer-to-deployer information flows in place and must not rely on AI outputs to justify discriminatory employment decisions as of that date.
3. **Data broker compliance obligations are multiplying.** Connecticut SB 4 follows California's Delete Act in creating a registration-and-centralized-deletion framework for data brokers. Organizations that broker personal data now face overlapping registration, deletion, and disclosure mandates across California, Connecticut, and (if enacted) potentially additional states.
4. **Children's online safety regulation is accelerating at the state level.** New York's Safe by Design Act, Connecticut SB 5's companion chatbot rules, and Illinois's Children's Social Media Safety Act (HB 5511, awaiting Senate action as of May 2026) collectively signal that state legislatures are not waiting for federal COPPA updates to impose new platform obligations for minors.
5. **Industry support for frontier AI regulation.** The unanimous passage of Illinois SB 315 with backing from OpenAI and Anthropic signals a shift in industry posture — major AI labs appear to prefer clear, auditable state-level frameworks over regulatory uncertainty, even as federal preemption debates continue.

## Action Items

- **Frontier AI developers (>$500M revenue, >10²⁶ FLOPs):** Map existing safety documentation against Illinois SB 315 (effective January 1, 2028), New York RAISE Act (effective January 1, 2027), and Connecticut SB 5 whistleblower provisions (effective October 1, 2026). The 72-hour critical incident reporting window is common across both Illinois and New York but should be verified against each state's specific definitions of "critical safety incident."
- **Connecticut employers using automated employment decision tools:** Begin assessing AEDT deployments now. Obtain required documentation from AI developers, review anti-discrimination policy implications, and establish pre-decision notice procedures ahead of the October 1, 2026 compliance date.
- **Data brokers operating in Connecticut:** Register with the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection before January 1, 2027. Audit data inventories to identify personal data categories subject to the new deletion portal when it launches in 2027.
- **Digital platform operators:** Review children's online safety obligations under New York's Safe by Design Act. Audit AI companion feature defaults for minor users and access controls between adults and minors. Note that Illinois's HB 5511 (Children's Social Media Safety Act) has passed the House and is pending Senate action — budget for Illinois compliance as well.
- **All multi-state operators:** Do not treat these state laws as interchangeable. Build a compliance matrix mapping each law's definitions, thresholds, and obligations separately. Retain outside counsel for jurisdiction-specific review of Connecticut SB 5's employer provisions given the October 2026 effective date.
- **Monitor:** Illinois SB 315 pending Pritzker signature (veto deadline will be determinative). Illinois HB 5511 (children's social media) pending Illinois Senate action. New York data broker bills (S9088/A09642) pending Governor Hochul's action — not yet signed as of June 1, 2026.
