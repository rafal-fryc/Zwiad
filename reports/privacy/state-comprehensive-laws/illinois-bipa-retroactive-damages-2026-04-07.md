# Seventh Circuit Holds BIPA Damages Amendment Applies Retroactively, Reshaping Liability for Hundreds of Pending Cases

**Jurisdiction:** Illinois, Federal (Seventh Circuit) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 1, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit held in [*Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, No. 25-2185](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/25-2185/25-2185-2026-04-01.html) that the August 2024 amendment to Section 20 of the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) applies retroactively to all pending cases. The ruling caps damages at one recovery per person per statutory subsection -- $5,000 for intentional violations or $1,000 for negligent violations -- rather than per biometric scan, reversing three federal district court decisions and dramatically reducing liability exposure for defendants in hundreds of active BIPA lawsuits.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The Seventh Circuit issued a unanimous panel decision on April 1, 2026, holding that [Illinois Public Act 103-0769](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm), which amended BIPA Section 20 (740 ILCS 14/20) effective August 2, 2024, applies retroactively to cases pending at the time of enactment. [Duane Morris Class Action Defense Blog](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/04/03/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively-reversing-three-illinois-federal-court-decisions/)

- The amendment was a legislative response to the Illinois Supreme Court's 2023 decision in [*Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc.*](https://law.justia.com/cases/illinois/supreme-court/2023/128004.html), which held that each biometric scan constituted a separate BIPA violation, exposing White Castle to an estimated $17 billion in damages. [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2023-may/illinois-supreme-court-finds-white-castle-could-face-up-to-17b-in-damages/)

- The court classified the amendment as "remedial" because it modified Section 20's damages provision rather than Section 15's substantive liability standards, applying the Illinois presumption that remedial legislation operates retroactively. [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/bipa-cases-7th-circuit-rules-change-illinois-laws-damages-provision-retroactively-limits-defendant-exposure)

- The court distinguished the *Cothron* per-scan accrual rule as addressing statute-of-limitations timing under Section 15, not the separate question of damages calculation under Section 20. [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/major-biometric-win-for-business-in-illinois)

- The practical impact is substantial: a worker previously scanned 1,500 times could have sought $7.5 million in damages; under the amended framework, maximum recovery is $5,000 per person for intentional violations. [SGR Law](https://www.sgrlaw.com/client-alerts/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-damages-amendment-applies-retroactively-reshaping-liability-for-hundreds-of-pending-cases/)

- The ruling reversed three federal district court decisions that had held the amendment did not apply retroactively and consolidated the appeals for a single opinion addressing the retroactivity question. [Duane Morris Class Action Defense Blog](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/04/03/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively-reversing-three-illinois-federal-court-decisions/)

- This is a Seventh Circuit decision and is binding on federal courts in Illinois, Indiana, and Wisconsin, but Illinois state courts are not bound by it. The Illinois Supreme Court has not yet addressed the retroactivity question. [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/seventh-circuit-court-appeals-holds-bipa-amendment-limiting-damages-applies)

## Action Items

- **Reassess BIPA litigation reserves immediately.** Companies defending pending BIPA class actions in federal court within the Seventh Circuit should recalculate potential exposure using the per-person (not per-scan) damages model.

- **Consider removal to federal court.** Defendants in BIPA cases pending in Illinois state courts may benefit from removing eligible cases to federal court, where this binding Seventh Circuit precedent applies.

- **Monitor Illinois state court rulings.** The Illinois Appellate Court and Illinois Supreme Court have not yet ruled on retroactivity. State court holdings may differ, and a circuit split between state and federal courts could develop.

- **Evaluate settlement posture.** Pending settlement negotiations should be reassessed in light of the significantly reduced damages exposure. Existing settlement agreements tied to per-scan calculations may warrant renegotiation if not yet finalized.

- **Maintain BIPA compliance programs.** The amendment reduces damages but does not eliminate liability. Companies collecting biometric data in Illinois must still comply with BIPA Section 15 requirements for informed consent and data handling.
