# CPPA Formal Rulemaking on ADMT, Cybersecurity Audits, and Risk Assessments: White & Case Analysis

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** January 22, 2025

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On November 22, 2024, the [California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) opened the formal 45-day public comment period on its proposed rulemaking package covering automated decision-making technology (ADMT), cybersecurity audits, privacy risk assessments, insurance company applicability, and updates to existing CCPA regulations. The package — advanced to formal rulemaking by a unanimous CPPA Board vote on November 8, 2024 — represents the most expansive set of new CCPA obligations since the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) took effect in January 2023. The original comment period ran from November 22, 2024, through January 14, 2025, when the CPPA held its first public hearing; the period was subsequently extended to February 19, 2025, due to the Southern California wildfires. [White & Case LLP's January 2025 Data Privacy Update](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/data-privacy-update-2025) summarized these developments at the close of the initial comment period, identifying ADMT opt-out rights, cybersecurity audit mandates, and risk assessment obligations as the three most consequential components for regulated businesses. The regulations were ultimately adopted by the CPPA Board on July 24, 2025, and approved by the California Office of Administrative Law on September 23, 2025, entering into force January 1, 2026, with phased compliance deadlines through 2030.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Statutory Authority

The [California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100.), as substantially amended by Proposition 24 (the California Privacy Rights Act) in November 2020, created the CPPA as the nation's first dedicated state privacy enforcement agency. Civil Code Section 1798.185 mandates that the CPPA adopt regulations governing consumers' rights with respect to automated decision-making technology, requirements for risk assessments, and requirements for cybersecurity audits. These statutory mandates drove the multi-track rulemaking package initiated in 2023 and completed in 2025.

### Rulemaking History (2021–2024)

The CPPA's path to this formal rulemaking spanned more than three years. In November 2023, the Agency published a [draft regulatory framework](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2023/20231127.html) for ADMT consumer rights. The CPPA Board reviewed but declined to advance a 200-plus page draft at its July 16, 2024, Board meeting, directing staff to refine the package and complete an economic impact analysis. Between May and July 2024, the Agency held three statewide [pre-rulemaking stakeholder sessions](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20240424.html) to gather input on key definitions and contested issues. On [November 8, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241108_2.html), the CPPA Board voted unanimously to advance the revised package to formal rulemaking under the California Administrative Procedure Act (APA) — triggering the statutory public notice and comment requirements.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Rulemaking Package Scope

The formal rulemaking package [noticed on November 22, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241122.html) addresses five regulatory tracks within Title 11 of the California Code of Regulations:

1. **ADMT Rights** — Consumer rights to access information about and opt out of a business's use of automated decision-making technology for significant decisions or profiling.
2. **Privacy Risk Assessments** — Mandatory assessments for businesses whose processing activities present a heightened risk to consumers, documenting purposes, benefits, and negative consumer impacts.
3. **Cybersecurity Audits** — Annual independent audits for larger businesses whose processing poses significant risk to consumer privacy or security, with certifications submitted to the CPPA on a phased schedule.
4. **Insurance Sector** — Clarifications on when insurance companies must comply with CCPA.
5. **CCPA Updates** — Technical amendments and clarifications to existing regulations.

The [full modified text of proposed regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_mod_txt_pro_reg.pdf) and the [formal Notice of Proposed Rulemaking](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_ins_notice.pdf) were published on the CPPA website at notice.

### ADMT Provisions

As [analyzed by McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mwe.com/insights/cppa-opens-public-comment-period-for-proposed-ccpa-regulations/), "automated decision-making technology" is defined as technology that processes personal information and uses computation to replace or substantially replace human decision-making. A decision is not "made by a human" if the reviewer does not know how to interpret the system's output, does not affirmatively review it, or lacks authority to override it.

"Significant decisions" — those triggering the most stringent requirements — include decisions affecting financial or lending services, housing, insurance, education enrollment or opportunities, employment or independent contracting opportunities or compensation, healthcare services, or essential goods or services. Advertising was excluded from this definition, a notable narrowing from earlier drafts.

Per the [White & Case Data Privacy Update](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/data-privacy-update-2025), California's proposed ADMT regulations would give consumers the right to know when automated decisions were made using their data — and to opt out — for such key areas as employment, loans, education, and healthcare. If a business uses ADMT to make a significant decision or for profiling or training, it must:

- Provide a **pre-use notice** disclosing the use of ADMT, its purpose, and how consumers can exercise opt-out rights.
- Honor **consumer opt-out rights**, with the opt-out mechanism required to be as easy to exercise as the corresponding opt-in.
- Complete a **risk assessment** evaluating potential negative impacts, including discrimination, economic harm, reputational harm, and interference with consumer autonomy.

### Cybersecurity Audit Requirements

As described by [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/new-cybersecurity-privacy-and-automated-decisionmaking-rules-coming-soon-to-california/), every business whose processing of consumers' personal information presents a "significant risk" to consumers' security must engage an independent auditor to perform an annual cybersecurity audit. Applicable businesses must meet at least one of the standard CCPA thresholds: (1) annual gross revenue exceeding $25 million; (2) buy, sell, or share personal information of 100,000 or more consumers annually; or (3) derive 50% or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information.

### Risk Assessment Requirements

Covered businesses must conduct and document risk assessments evaluating the "negative impacts" of certain processing activities on consumers — including risks of discrimination, economic or physical harm, reputational harm, or interference with informed consumer choice. Businesses must also document the purpose of processing, the benefits to the business and consumers, and mitigation measures. When businesses use ADMT for significant decisions or profiling, the risk assessment must capture ADMT-specific considerations, such as whether the ADMT works as intended and whether it discriminates based on protected classes.

### Stakeholder Comment Process

The [National Law Review reported](https://natlawreview.com/article/cppa-opens-public-comment-period-proposed-ccpa-regulations) that the CPPA received more than 600 public comments during the comment period, including submissions from major technology companies, business associations, and civil society organizations. The [Electronic Privacy Information Center (EPIC)](https://epic.org/documents/comments-to-the-cppa-on-proposed-regulations-regarding-cybersecurity-risk-assessments-and-admts/) commended the CPPA for the proposed protections. Industry stakeholders — and ultimately Governor Gavin Newsom in an April 2025 letter — urged the CPPA to narrow requirements to avoid chilling AI innovation. [Alston & Bird's analysis](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/cppa-opens-formal-public-comment-period-for-ccpa-proposed-regulations/) documented industry concerns regarding the breadth of the "significant decision" definition, the operability of real-time opt-out mechanisms, and overlap with federal sector-specific requirements (HIPAA, FCRA).

### Comment Period Extension

The original comment deadline of January 14, 2025, was extended to February 19, 2025. On January 10, 2025, the CPPA published a [Notice of Extension](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/addlhearing.pdf) citing the Southern California wildfires' impact on public participation. A second public hearing was scheduled for February 19, 2025, in addition to the January 14 hearing. As [Squire Patton Boggs reported](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/01/cppa-extends-public-comment-period-from-january-14-2025-to-february-19-2025-public-hearings-for-interested-parties-to-be-held-january-14-2025-and-february-19-2025/), both hearings were conducted in hybrid format (in-person in Sacramento plus virtual attendance).

### Final Rule Trajectory and Outcome

Following the close of the extended comment period, [Goodwin Law analyzed](https://www.goodwinprivacyblog.com/2025/02/05/california-forges-a-new-path-on-automated-decision-making-technology-risk-assessments-and-cybersecurity-audits/) key changes in the proposed regulations, noting that the CPPA had already narrowed certain provisions. The [CPPA Board voted 5-0 on July 24, 2025](https://iapp.org/news/a/cppa-board-finalizes-long-awaited-admt-risk-assessment-rules), to adopt final regulations after reviewing hundreds of public comments. The [Office of Administrative Law approved the regulations on September 23, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html), filing them with the California Secretary of State. Final changes from the proposed rules included:

- Removal of references to AI and behavioral advertising in the ADMT context
- Expansion of the scope of when businesses can use ADMT
- Scaling back of consumer opt-out rights
- Removal of a prohibition on processing where privacy risks outweigh benefits (replaced with a "goal" to restrict such activities)

As reported by [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/cppa-board-finalizes-long-awaited-admt-risk-assessment-rules), the CPPA's projected economic impact on businesses decreased from $10 billion over 10 years (proposed) to $4.8 billion over 10 years (final), while economic benefits are estimated at $282 billion over 10 years.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Businesses

The regulations impose new obligations on any business subject to the CCPA that:
- Uses ADMT for significant decisions (e.g., hiring platforms, credit underwriting systems, tenant screening tools, healthcare triage algorithms).
- Processes personal information in a manner that poses a significant risk to consumer privacy or security (cybersecurity audit obligation).
- Conducts processing activities with potential adverse consumer impact (risk assessment obligation).

As [White & Case finalized rule analysis](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/cppa-finalizes-rules-admt-risk-assessments-and-cybersecurity-audits-requirements) indicates, businesses must also amend their service provider agreements to require service providers to assist in completing cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and complying with ADMT requirements — a vendor management dimension that adds compliance scope beyond direct obligations.

### Compliance Timeline

| Milestone | Deadline |
|-----------|----------|
| Regulations enter into force | January 1, 2026 |
| Risk assessment compliance begins | January 1, 2026 |
| ADMT (significant decisions) compliance begins | January 1, 2027 |
| Risk assessment attestation due (all covered businesses) | April 1, 2028 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification due (revenue > $100M) | April 1, 2028 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification due (revenue $50M–$100M) | April 1, 2029 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification due (revenue < $50M) | April 1, 2030 |

### Enforcement Outlook

The CPPA is the nation's first dedicated state privacy enforcement agency and has demonstrated an active enforcement posture. The regulations provide substantial new enforcement surface area. Organizations that delay compliance planning — particularly for ADMT risk assessments with a January 1, 2026 compliance date — face exposure before the phased audit certification deadlines arrive.

## Action Items

- **ADMT inventory:** Conduct a cross-functional audit (privacy, IT, HR, product, procurement, legal) to identify all systems that substantially supplement or replace human decision-making, including hiring platforms, credit scoring tools, healthcare triage systems, and customer segmentation tools.
- **Risk assessment program:** Develop a repeatable risk assessment methodology to evaluate negative consumer impacts from data processing activities; ensure assessments are documented and retained per CPPA requirements before the January 1, 2026, compliance date.
- **ADMT pre-use notice:** Draft consumer-facing disclosures for each significant-decision ADMT use case; build operational workflows to route opt-out requests to relevant systems with parity in ease of use before January 1, 2027.
- **Cybersecurity audit readiness:** Determine whether annual cybersecurity audit obligations apply based on revenue thresholds; if so, engage an auditor now given staggered submission deadlines beginning April 1, 2028.
- **Vendor contract amendments:** Review and update service provider agreements to incorporate ADMT, cybersecurity audit, and risk assessment cooperation obligations as required by the final regulations.
- **Monitor CPPA guidance:** Subscribe to CPPA announcements at cppa.ca.gov for updated implementation guidance and enforcement advisories.
