# New York Legislature Passes One Fair Price Act Banning Personalized Pricing (S.8623B/A.9349B)

**Jurisdiction:** New York | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-15

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The New York State Legislature passed the [One Fair Price Act](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B) (S.8623B/A.9349B) on June 4, 2026, prohibiting businesses from using consumers' personal data — including browsing history, real-time location, inferred income, and household size — to set individualized prices through algorithmic systems. The bill now awaits Governor Kathy Hochul's signature or veto, with a deadline of December 31, 2026; if signed, it takes effect 180 days later. New York would become the third US state to enact a broad surveillance pricing restriction, joining Maryland and Connecticut, while at least 24 states introduced comparable legislation in 2026.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The New York State Legislature passed [S.8623B/A.9349B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B) on June 4, 2026, sponsored by Senator Rachel May and Assemblymember Emérita Torres ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B)).
- The Act prohibits any entity — including out-of-state and online businesses selling to New York consumers — from using consumers' personal data to algorithmically set individualized prices ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-legislature-passes-ban-on-personalized-pricing.html)).
- **Dynamic pricing is permitted with disclosure.** Businesses may continue using dynamic pricing algorithms that do not rely on consumer personal data, subject to a disclosure requirement ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/new-york-surveillance-pricing-stealth-crawler-laws/)).
- **Permitted practices explicitly include:** loyalty programs, coupons, subscribe-and-save discounts, and categorical promotions for veterans, seniors, and similar groups ([Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/the-price-isnt-right-emerging-patchwork-of-state-surveillance-pricing-bans-creates-compliance-complications-for-businesses)).
- **Private right of action was removed** during the amendment process. Consumer advocacy organizations, including Consumer Reports, have called on Governor Hochul to reinstate it ([Consumer Reports](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/new-york-legislature-passes-key-bill-to-curb-personalized-pricing/)).
- **AG enforcement:** The New York Attorney General may bring civil actions for penalties and restitution; penalties reach $5,000 per first violation and $20,000 per subsequent violation ([Attorney General James press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-applauds-passage-legislation-protect-new-yorkers)).
- **Consumer civil remedies** remain: an individual may recover the greater of actual damages or $50; courts may award up to three times actual damages (capped at $1,000) for willful or knowing violations ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-legislature-passes-ban-on-personalized-pricing.html)).
- **Effective date:** 180 days after signing — earliest estimate approximately May 2027, assuming a post-election signature ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/new-york-surveillance-pricing-stealth-crawler-laws/)).
- The Act amends New York's General Business Law and supersedes the prior Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act, striking its individual labeling requirement in favor of the outright prohibition ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/new-york-surveillance-pricing-stealth-crawler-laws/)).
- A companion bill — the Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Discriminatory Pricing Act — would ban electronic shelf labels (ESLs) in large food and drug retail establishments, addressing in-store real-time price changes ([news10.com](https://www.news10.com/capitol/data-center-pricing-bills-2026-legislature/)).
- Governor Hochul is not expected to act before the November 2026 gubernatorial election; the signing deadline is December 31, 2026 ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/new-york-surveillance-pricing-stealth-crawler-laws/)).

## Regulatory Context [HIGH confidence]

### Federal Backdrop

In July 2024, the [FTC ordered eight pricing intermediary firms](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-issues-orders-eight-companies-seeking-information-surveillance-pricing) — including Mastercard, Accenture, PROS, Bloomreach, Revionics, and McKinsey — to produce information about their algorithmic pricing products under Section 6(b) authority. In January 2025, the FTC published [preliminary research findings](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer) confirming that companies routinely use granular personal data — location, demographics, mouse movements, shopping cart abandonment behavior — to generate differentiated pricing. The study was released on a 3-2 party-line vote; the current FTC leadership under Chairman Andrew Ferguson has not continued the investigation at the same pace.

No federal statute currently prohibits surveillance pricing. A federal [One Fair Price Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3387) (S.3387, 119th Congress) was introduced by Senators Gillibrand and Gallego but has not advanced.

### State Legislative Wave

At least [24 states](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/surveillance-pricing-24-states-ban-algorithmic-pricing-tracker-2026/) introduced surveillance pricing legislation in 2026. Three states have enacted restrictions:

| State | Law | Scope | Effective Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maryland | HB 895 — Protection From Predatory Pricing Act | Large food retailers (15,000+ sq ft) and third-party delivery providers; covers tax-exempt food items | October 1, 2026 |
| Connecticut | SB 4 (Public Act 26-64) | All retailers and delivery services; prohibits personal-data-driven pricing | Signed May 27, 2026; effective October 1, 2026 |
| New York | S.8623B/A.9349B — One Fair Price Act | All entities doing business in New York; covers all consumer goods | Awaiting signature; 180 days after signing |

Sources: [Venable LLP](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/05/maryland-leads-wave-of-state-surveillance-pricing), [multistate.ai](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-101-state-surveillance-pricing-laws), [EPIC](https://epic.org/connecticut-is-second-state-to-enact-surveillance-pricing-ban/), [EPIC — NY](https://epic.org/new-york-becomes-third-state-to-pass-surveillance-pricing-ban/).

**California AB 2564** passed the California Assembly on May 27, 2026 and was referred to the California Senate; it would ban surveillance pricing by retailers with civil penalties up to $12,500 per consumer violation, tripled for intentional violations ([Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/surveillance-pricing-update-californias-sweeping-ab-2564-passes-assembly-and-heads-to-senate)).

**Pennsylvania SB 1205** has been referred to the Consumer Protection & Professional Licensure Committee and has not advanced.

### Compliance Complexity

New York's Act is broader in scope than Maryland's and Connecticut's food-sector laws, applying across all consumer goods and all industries. The [Sheppard Mullin analysis](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/the-price-isnt-right-emerging-patchwork-of-state-surveillance-pricing-bans-creates-compliance-complications-for-businesses) notes that businesses operating across multiple states face significant compliance complications because each enacted law differs on covered products, covered entities, exemption structures, and enforcement mechanisms.

Official text could not be retrieved directly at time of writing due to WebFetch being unavailable; bill text is accessible at the [NY Senate legislation page](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B) for manual verification of specific statutory language. ## Action Items

- **Audit all algorithmic pricing tools immediately.** Identify every system that ingests consumer personal data — sign-in identity, browsing history, device fingerprint, location signals, demographic inference — to set or adjust prices for individual consumers in New York.
- **Redesign or disable non-compliant systems before the effective date.** If signed, the law takes effect approximately 180 days after execution (earliest: mid-2027). The compliance window begins now given the engineering and vendor-contract changes required.
- **Prepare disclosure infrastructure for permitted dynamic pricing.** Dynamic pricing algorithms that do not use personal data remain lawful but require consumer-facing disclosure. Design and stage those disclosures now.
- **Audit loyalty and promotional program structures.** Categorical discounts (loyalty programs, coupons, veteran/senior promotions) are explicitly permitted, but must be structured as category-level offers, not individual data-derived price targeting. Retain documentation.
- **Assess multi-state compliance architecture.** Businesses subject to Maryland, Connecticut, and potentially California and New York laws should evaluate whether a unified pricing policy — most restrictive common denominator — is more efficient than jurisdiction-specific tooling.
- **Monitor Governor Hochul's decision.** Track whether Hochul signs, vetoes, or requests amendments (particularly on the private right of action question, which Consumer Reports and UFCW have highlighted). The signing window closes December 31, 2026.
- **Watch California AB 2564 in the Senate.** If California enacts its version with $12,500-per-consumer penalties, it will effectively set the national compliance floor — and its standards diverge materially from New York's.
- **Brief pricing and revenue management teams.** Legal and compliance personnel should communicate now with the business units operating pricing algorithms so technology changes can be scoped and resourced ahead of any effective date.
