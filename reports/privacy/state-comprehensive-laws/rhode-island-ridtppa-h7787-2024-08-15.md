# Rhode Island Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (RIDTPPA): Comprehensive Analysis

**Jurisdiction:** Rhode Island | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** June 25–28, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On June 25–28, 2024, Rhode Island enacted the [Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (RIDTPPA)](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-48.1/INDEX.htm) (H 7787 / S 2500), becoming the nineteenth state to adopt a comprehensive consumer data privacy law and the seventh state to do so in calendar year 2024. Governor Daniel McKee transmitted the bill without signature, which is legally equivalent to enactment under Rhode Island law. The statute takes effect January 1, 2026. It establishes a Virginia-model framework of consumer rights (access, correction, deletion, portability, opt-out), controller and processor obligations, sensitive data opt-in consent, and exclusive Attorney General enforcement. Three provisions set it apart from peer laws: privacy notice obligations apply to all commercial websites operating in Rhode Island regardless of size thresholds; there is no cure period before civil penalties attach; and sale-of-data disclosures must identify specific downstream third parties rather than mere categories. Businesses with Rhode Island consumer data exposure should confirm compliance as the January 1, 2026 effective date has now passed.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

Rhode Island's RIDTPPA entered a rapidly expanding landscape of state omnibus privacy statutes. Starting with Virginia's Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA) in 2021, states have adopted substantially similar "Virginia-model" frameworks at an accelerating pace. By mid-2024, Rhode Island became one of seven states to enact a comprehensive privacy law in that calendar year alone, joining New Jersey, Kentucky, New Hampshire, Maryland, Minnesota, and Vermont in the same legislative cycle.

The Rhode Island legislature introduced companion bills — [House Bill 7787](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText/BillText24/HouseText24/H7787.pdf) and Senate Bill S 2500 — and both chambers passed the measure on June 13, 2024, as documented by [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/20606-passed-rhode-island-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act-h-7787). On June 25–28, 2024, Governor McKee transmitted the bill without his signature, making it law.

The enactment was not without controversy. Consumer advocacy organizations, including [Consumer Reports and a coalition of privacy groups](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/research/privacy-coalition-urges-rhode-island-governor-to-veto-hb-7787-sb-2500-consumer-privacy-legislation/), urged the Governor to veto the bill on grounds that it was weaker than California's CCPA in key areas, including the omission of a data minimization requirement and lack of universal opt-out mechanism support. The Future of Privacy Forum's [detailed blog post](https://fpf.org/blog/comprehensive-privacy-anchors-in-the-ocean-state/) similarly highlighted structural gaps compared to more protective peer laws.

The official codified text is published as [Rhode Island General Laws Title 6, Chapter 6-48.1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-48.1/INDEX.htm). The final amended House version (Substitute A as Amended) is available as a [PDF from the Rhode Island Legislature](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText/BillText24/HouseText24/H7787Aaa.pdf).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Applicability Thresholds

The RIDTPPA's substantive obligations apply to any person or entity that (a) conducts business in Rhode Island or produces products or services targeted to Rhode Island residents, and during the preceding calendar year either (b) controlled or processed personal data of at least **35,000 customers** (excluding data processed solely to complete a payment transaction), or (c) controlled or processed personal data of at least **10,000 customers** and derived more than **20% of gross revenue** from the sale of personal data, as analyzed by [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240717-rhode-island-enacts-nations-nineteenth-comprehensive-privacy-law).

These thresholds are calibrated to Rhode Island's smaller population (approximately 1.1 million residents) and are lower than the 100,000-consumer threshold used by states like Virginia and Colorado. As noted by [Moore & Van Allen](https://www.mvalaw.com/data-points/rhode-island-state-19-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-legislation), the lower threshold means businesses with a smaller national footprint may still be in-scope for Rhode Island obligations even if they have escaped coverage under other state privacy laws. The law has no standalone revenue threshold.

### Broad Privacy Notice Requirements: A Distinctive Feature

The RIDTPPA's most unusual provision is that its **privacy notice obligations extend to all commercial websites and internet service providers** that do business in or target Rhode Island consumers — regardless of whether the entity meets the 35,000/10,000 customer thresholds, per [WilmerHale's analysis](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240717-rhode-island-enacts-nations-nineteenth-comprehensive-privacy-law). A business may be exempt from the full consumer-rights framework yet still be obligated to post a compliant privacy notice.

The notice must disclose:
- All categories of personal data collected through the website or online service
- The specific **third parties to whom personal data has been or may be sold** (the law contemplates naming specific third parties or specific categories, which is more granular than peer laws)
- An active email address or other online mechanism for consumer inquiries
- Clear and conspicuous disclosure if personal data is sold or processed for targeted advertising

As [Clifford Chance observed](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2024/08/rhode-island-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act--an-ov.html), the requirement to identify potential downstream data recipients by name or category is atypical among US state privacy laws and reflects Rhode Island's particular emphasis on supply-chain transparency over data flows.

### Consumer Rights

Customers subject to the RIDTPPA may exercise the following rights, consistent with the Virginia-model framework described by [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/rhode-island-enacts-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act-joining-us-data):

- **Right to confirm** whether their personal data is being processed and to access that data
- **Right to correct** inaccurate personal data
- **Right to delete** personal data provided by or obtained about them
- **Right to data portability** in a portable, readily usable format
- **Right to opt out** of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of solely automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects

Controllers must respond to consumer requests within **45 days**, with one 45-day extension available when reasonably necessary with notice to the consumer. Controllers must also establish an **appeals process** for denied requests; appeals must receive a decision within 60 days of receipt.

The RIDTPPA does **not** require controllers to honor universal opt-out mechanisms (such as browser-based Global Privacy Control signals), distinguishing it from California, Colorado, and Connecticut. As the [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/comprehensive-privacy-anchors-in-the-ocean-state/) noted, this omission breaks a trend set by more recent state laws.

### Sensitive Data

Processing sensitive data requires affirmative **opt-in consent** from the consumer. The RIDTPPA defines sensitive data to include, per [Mintz's analysis](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2826/2024-07-16-rhode-island-enacts-comprehensive-privacy-law):

- Personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health conditions or diagnoses, sex life, or sexual orientation
- Citizenship or immigration status
- Genetic or biometric data processed for the purpose of uniquely identifying an individual
- Precise geolocation data (defined as within a 1,750-foot radius)
- Personal data collected from a known child under 13 years old

This list closely tracks the Virginia VCDPA model. Notably, unlike several 2023–2024 state laws, the RIDTPPA provides **no heightened protections for teenagers** (ages 13–17), as highlighted by the [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/comprehensive-privacy-anchors-in-the-ocean-state/).

### Controller Obligations

Controllers subject to the RIDTPPA must, per [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240717-rhode-island-enacts-nations-nineteenth-comprehensive-privacy-law) and [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/rhode-island-enacts-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act-joining-us-data):

- Provide a compliant privacy notice (all controllers, not just Tier 1)
- Limit the collection and use of personal data to stated purposes (purpose limitation)
- Implement reasonable data security practices
- Obtain affirmative consent before processing sensitive data
- Not process personal data in a manner inconsistent with the consumer's opt-out elections

The RIDTPPA does **not** impose a general **data minimization** obligation — controllers need not limit collection to what is "adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary" for the disclosed purpose. As the [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/comprehensive-privacy-anchors-in-the-ocean-state/) noted, only Iowa and Utah previously omitted data minimization among state omnibus laws; Rhode Island joins that minority position.

### Processor Obligations and Data Processing Agreements

Processors handling personal data on behalf of a controller must be governed by a **data processing agreement** that specifies the scope, nature, and purpose of processing; the type of data involved; the duration of processing; and the processor's obligations regarding security and consumer rights assistance, per [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240717-rhode-island-enacts-nations-nineteenth-comprehensive-privacy-law). Processors must:

- Support controllers in fulfilling consumer rights requests
- Assist with data protection assessments
- Maintain secure processing practices
- Engage sub-processors only pursuant to a written agreement binding sub-processors to equivalent obligations

### Data Protection Assessments

Controllers must conduct **data protection impact assessments** (DPIAs) for processing activities that present a heightened risk of harm, including:

- Targeted advertising
- Sale of personal data
- Certain profiling activities
- Processing of sensitive data

Unlike some peer state laws, the RIDTPPA does **not** specify the factors controllers must consider when conducting these assessments, leaving methodological discretion to the controller, as [Clifford Chance observed](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2024/08/rhode-island-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act--an-ov.html). The FPF noted this is a gap relative to more prescriptive state frameworks.

### Exemptions

The RIDTPPA contains standard entity-level and data-level exemptions, per [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/no-longer-adrift-in-the-ocean-state-rhode-island-enacts-consumer-data-privacy-law/):

**Entity-level exemptions:** Governmental entities, nonprofits, institutions of higher education, financial institutions subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), and covered entities and business associates subject to HIPAA.

**Data-level exemptions:** Data governed by HIPAA, the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), the GLBA, the Driver's Privacy Protection Act (DPPA), the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), and the Farm Credit Act, among others. These carved-out categories track the standard Virginia-model exemption list.

### No Cure Period

A key enforcement risk: the RIDTPPA contains **no right-to-cure provision**. Most state omnibus privacy laws (Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, etc.) give businesses a 30- to 60-day window to remediate violations before the Attorney General may bring an action. Rhode Island omits this entirely. As [Darrow Everett](https://darroweverett.com/rhode-island-data-privacy-act-impact-legal-analysis/) and [DataGrail](https://www.datagrail.io/blog/regulations/what-you-need-to-know-about-rhode-islands-new-privacy-law/) highlight, any violation that comes to the Attorney General's attention is immediately actionable. Businesses cannot rely on a warning before facing penalties.

### Enforcement and Penalties

The Rhode Island Attorney General has **exclusive enforcement authority**. There is no private right of action. Violations constitute violations of Rhode Island's commercial law and further constitute **deceptive trade practices** under Title 6 of Rhode Island General Laws, per the [official statute](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-48.1/INDEX.htm).

Penalty structure:
- Civil penalty of up to **$10,000 per violation** for violations of the substantive Act provisions
- Separate fine of **$100–$500 per violation** for intentional disclosures of personal data in violation of the Act

As [Bass Berry & Sims](https://www.bassberry.com/news/rhode-island-passes-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act/) notes, the $10,000 per-violation cap combined with the absence of a cure period creates meaningful per-incident exposure for businesses with systemic compliance gaps.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Entities

The RIDTPPA creates a two-tier compliance structure:

**Tier 1 — Full substantive obligations:** Entities meeting the 35,000-consumer or 10,000-consumer/20%-revenue thresholds must implement consumer rights workflows, conduct DPIAs, obtain opt-in consent for sensitive data processing, maintain processor agreements, and ensure opt-out functionality for data sales and targeted advertising.

**Tier 2 — Privacy notice only:** Every commercial website or ISP doing business in or targeting Rhode Island consumers must post a compliant privacy notice regardless of size. This is the broadest disclosure obligation attached to any US state privacy law and affects many small and mid-size businesses that would otherwise be under threshold.

### Industries Most Affected

- **Retail and e-commerce:** The universal notice requirement applies to essentially all online retailers with Rhode Island sales; the lower 35,000-consumer threshold brings mid-market businesses into full substantive scope.
- **AdTech and data brokers:** Sale-of-data disclosure requirements, opt-out rights, and third-party identification obligations directly target data monetization models.
- **Healthcare-adjacent businesses:** Sensitive data rules require an opt-in consent layer for health-condition processing not already covered by HIPAA.
- **Employers and HR technology:** Processing employee data from Rhode Island residents may be covered if thresholds are met.

### Comparison to Other State Privacy Laws

| Feature | Rhode Island RIDTPPA | Virginia VCDPA | California CCPA/CPRA | Colorado CPA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer threshold | 35,000 or 10,000/20% | 100,000 or 25,000/50% | $25M revenue / 100,000 consumers | 100,000 or 25,000/25% |
| Data minimization | **No** | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Cure period | **None** | 30 days | N/A | 60 days (sunset 2025) |
| Universal opt-out | **No** | No | Yes (GPC) | Yes |
| Private right of action | No | No | Limited (security breaches) | No |
| Teen protections | **No** | No | Yes (16+) | No |
| Penalty per violation | $10,000 | $7,500 | $7,500 | $20,000 |

As [Moore & Van Allen](https://www.mvalaw.com/data-points/rhode-island-state-19-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-legislation) summarize, the RIDTPPA's lower applicability threshold and universal notice requirement make it meaningfully broader in reach for smaller businesses, while the absence of data minimization and universal opt-out support make it weaker in substantive protection than peer laws enacted in the same year.

### Compliance Timeline

The law became effective **January 1, 2026**. There is no statutory grace period. Key compliance elements that should already be in place:

- Data inventory and mapping to identify RI consumer personal data and third-party recipients
- Privacy notice updated to meet RIDTPPA disclosure requirements, including third-party identification
- Consumer rights request infrastructure supporting access, correction, deletion, portability, opt-out, and sensitive-data opt-in within the 45-day window
- Data protection assessments for targeted advertising, data sales, profiling, and sensitive data processing
- Data processing agreements with all processors containing RIDTPPA-required provisions
- Appeals process for denied consumer requests

## Action Items

- Confirm whether your business meets either applicability threshold (35,000 RI customers, or 10,000 RI customers with 20%+ revenue from data sales) and document the analysis — this determines whether full substantive obligations apply
- Regardless of threshold status, audit your public-facing privacy notice to ensure it includes all categories of personal data collected, specific third-party recipients, and an active contact mechanism
- Verify that consumer rights workflows (access, correction, deletion, portability, opt-out, opt-in for sensitive data) are functioning and meeting the 45-day response window
- Confirm data protection assessments have been completed for targeted advertising, data sales, profiling, and sensitive data processing activities
- Review and update processor agreements to include RIDTPPA-required contractual terms
- Because the RIDTPPA has no cure period, prioritize gap remediation immediately — do not rely on receiving a warning from the AG before enforcement action
- Monitor the Rhode Island Attorney General's office for enforcement guidance, complaint patterns, and interpretive statements as enforcement experience accumulates in 2026 and beyond
