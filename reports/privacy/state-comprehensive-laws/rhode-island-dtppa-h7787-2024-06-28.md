# Rhode Island Enacts the Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (H 7787 / RIDTPPA)

**Jurisdiction:** Rhode Island | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** June 28, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Rhode Island became the nineteenth state to enact comprehensive consumer data privacy legislation when Governor Daniel McKee transmitted H 7787 — the Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (RIDTPPA) — without signature on June 25–28, 2024. The law takes effect January 1, 2026 and follows the "Virginia model" framework common to most post-2021 state privacy statutes, establishing consumer rights, controller obligations, and exclusive Attorney General enforcement. Several provisions distinguish the RIDTPPA from peer laws: its privacy notice requirements extend to all commercial websites operating in Rhode Island regardless of size, it provides no cure period before penalties attach, and it imposes unusually broad third-party disclosure obligations. Businesses operating nationally that have not yet assessed Rhode Island exposure should prioritize a gap analysis before the January 1, 2026 effective date, as violations immediately incur civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation with no opportunity to cure.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

Rhode Island's path to comprehensive privacy legislation followed the multi-year wave of state omnibus privacy statutes that began with the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA) in 2021. By mid-2024, more than twenty states had enacted or were advancing comprehensive frameworks. Rhode Island's legislature introduced companion bills — House Bill 7787 and Senate Bill 2500 — and both chambers passed the measure on [June 13, 2024](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/20606-passed-rhode-island-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act-h-7787), making Rhode Island the seventh state to enact a comprehensive privacy law in calendar year 2024 (joining New Jersey, Kentucky, New Hampshire, Maryland, Minnesota, and Vermont).

Governor McKee declined to sign or veto the bill; under Rhode Island law, transmittal without signature is equivalent to enactment. Consumer advocacy organizations, including Consumer Reports, had [urged the Governor to veto the bill](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/research/privacy-coalition-urges-rhode-island-governor-to-veto-hb-7787-sb-2500-consumer-privacy-legislation/) on grounds that it was weaker than the CCPA and lacked adequate enforcement mechanisms. The bill nevertheless became law effective June 28, 2024.

The official enrolled text is published at [Rhode Island General Laws Title 6, Chapter 6-48.1](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-48.1/INDEX.htm). The final amended House version (Substitute A as Amended) is available as a [PDF from the Rhode Island Legislature](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText/BillText24/HouseText24/H7787Aaa.pdf).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Applicability Thresholds

The RIDTPPA applies to any person or entity that (a) conducts business in Rhode Island or produces products or services targeted to Rhode Island residents, and during the preceding calendar year (b) controlled or processed personal data of at least **35,000 customers**, excluding personal data processed solely to complete a payment transaction, or (c) controlled or processed personal data of at least **10,000 customers** and derived more than **20% of gross revenue** from the sale of personal data.

These thresholds are calibrated to Rhode Island's smaller population (approximately 1.1 million residents) and are lower than California's or Colorado's coverage triggers. Notably, the law has no standalone revenue threshold — a business with large revenue but fewer than 35,000 Rhode Island customers may fall outside the primary obligations.

### Broad Privacy Notice Requirements: A Key Departure

The RIDTPPA's most distinctive feature is that its **privacy notice obligations apply to all commercial websites and internet service providers** that do business in or target Rhode Island consumers — regardless of whether the entity meets the 35,000/10,000 customer thresholds above. This means a business exempt from the substantive rights framework may still be required to post a compliant privacy notice.

Required notice disclosures include:
- Categories of personal data collected
- All categories of third parties to whom the controller **has sold or may sell** personal data (including names or categories of specific third parties — an unusually granular requirement)
- An active email address or equivalent mechanism for consumer inquiries
- Clear disclosure of whether personal data is sold or used for targeted advertising

The requirement to identify potential downstream data recipients by name or category is atypical among state privacy laws and reflects Rhode Island's emphasis on transparency over data flows, as [noted by WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240717-rhode-island-enacts-nations-nineteenth-comprehensive-privacy-law) and [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2024/08/rhode-island-data-transparency-and-privacy-protection-act--an-ov.html).

### Consumer Rights

Customers subject to the RIDTPPA may exercise the following rights:
- **Right to confirm** whether their personal data is being processed
- **Right to access** a copy of their personal data
- **Right to correct** inaccuracies in their personal data
- **Right to delete** personal data provided by or obtained about them
- **Right to data portability** in a portable, readily usable format
- **Right to opt out** of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and certain profiling decisions
- **Right to opt in** (affirmative consent required) before processing of sensitive data

Controllers must respond to consumer requests within 45 days (extendable by an additional 45 days when reasonably necessary). Controllers must also establish an appeals process for denied requests.

### Sensitive Data

Sensitive data requiring affirmative opt-in consent includes:
- Personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health conditions or diagnoses, sex life, or sexual orientation
- Citizenship or immigration status
- Genetic or biometric data processed for identification purposes
- Precise geolocation data (defined as within 1,750 feet)
- Personal data collected from a known child under 13

This list closely tracks the Virginia VCDPA model, as analyzed by [Moore & Van Allen](https://www.mvalaw.com/data-points/rhode-island-state-19-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-legislation).

### Data Protection Assessments

Controllers must conduct data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) for processing activities that present a heightened risk of harm, including:
- Targeted advertising
- Sale of personal data
- Certain profiling activities
- Processing of sensitive data

Unlike some other state laws, the RIDTPPA does not specify the factors controllers must weigh when conducting these assessments, leaving methodological discretion to the controller.

### Exemptions

The RIDTPPA contains standard entity-level and data-level exemptions:

**Entity-level exemptions** include governmental entities, nonprofits, institutions of higher education, financial institutions subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), and covered entities subject to HIPAA.

**Data-level exemptions** include data governed by HIPAA, the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), the GLBA, the Driver's Privacy Protection Act (DPPA), the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), and the Farm Credit Act, among others.

### No Cure Period

A notable enforcement risk: the RIDTPPA includes no right-to-cure provision. Most other state privacy laws (Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, etc.) provide a 30- to 60-day cure period before the Attorney General may bring an enforcement action. Rhode Island's law contains no such safe harbor. Any violation that comes to the Attorney General's attention is immediately actionable, [as highlighted by Darrow Everett](https://darroweverett.com/rhode-island-data-privacy-act-impact-legal-analysis/) and [DataGrail](https://www.datagrail.io/blog/regulations/what-you-need-to-know-about-rhode-islands-new-privacy-law/).

### Enforcement and Penalties

The Rhode Island Attorney General has exclusive authority to enforce the RIDTPPA. There is no private right of action. Consumers who believe their rights have been violated may submit complaints to the Attorney General's office for investigation.

Violations are treated as violations of Rhode Island's deceptive trade practices law ([Title 6 of Rhode Island General Laws](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE6/6-48.1/INDEX.htm)), and each violation may incur a civil penalty of up to **$10,000**. A separate fine of $100–$500 per violation applies to intentional disclosures of personal data in violation of the Act. As of early 2026, the Attorney General's office had reportedly been focused on consumer education ahead of the effective date rather than aggressive enforcement, though no formal enforcement moratorium was announced.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Entities

The RIDTPPA's dual-tier structure creates two distinct compliance populations:

1. **Tier 1 — Full substantive obligations:** Any business meeting the 35,000 or 10,000/20%-revenue thresholds must implement consumer rights workflows, conduct DPIAs, obtain opt-in consent for sensitive data, and maintain contractual controls with processors.

2. **Tier 2 — Privacy notice only:** Every commercial website or ISP that does business in or targets Rhode Island consumers must post a compliant privacy notice, even if it does not meet Tier 1 thresholds. This is the broadest disclosure obligation attached to any US state privacy law and will affect many small and mid-size businesses.

### Industries Most Affected

- **Retail and e-commerce:** Broad notice requirements will apply to essentially all online retail operations with any Rhode Island sales.
- **AdTech and data brokers:** The sale-of-data disclosure requirements and opt-out rights directly target data monetization models.
- **Healthcare-adjacent businesses:** Sensitive data rules add a consent layer for health-condition processing outside HIPAA coverage.
- **Employers and HR tech:** Processing employee data may be covered to the extent Rhode Island residents are involved.

### Compliance Timeline

The law took effect **January 1, 2026**. There is no statutory grace period and no cure period. Businesses that were not compliant on that date faced immediate enforcement exposure. Key preparation milestones include:

- **Data mapping:** Identify categories of personal data collected and all third parties receiving that data (required for the privacy notice)
- **Privacy notice update:** Revise privacy notices to meet the RIDTPPA's disclosure requirements, including third-party identification
- **Consumer rights infrastructure:** Build or verify systems for access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out requests within the 45-day response window
- **Sensitive data consent mechanisms:** Implement opt-in flows for sensitive data categories
- **DPIA program:** Complete assessments for high-risk processing activities
- **Processor contracts:** Ensure data processing agreements with vendors include RIDTPPA-required provisions

### Penalties Context

At $10,000 per violation with no cure opportunity, the RIDTPPA's penalty structure is comparable to other states' laws in nominal value, but the absence of a cure period means violations cannot be remediated before they become penalties. For businesses with large Rhode Island customer bases and systemic compliance gaps, aggregate exposure could be substantial.

## Action Items

- Conduct a data inventory to identify all Rhode Island consumer data flows and third-party recipients — required for both Tier 1 and Tier 2 compliance
- Update your public-facing privacy notice to include the RIDTPPA's third-party disclosure requirements, even if your business does not reach the 35,000/10,000 thresholds
- Audit whether your business meets either applicability threshold (35,000 RI customers, or 10,000 RI customers with 20%+ revenue from data sales) and document the analysis
- Implement or verify consumer rights request workflows: access, correction, deletion, portability, opt-out, and opt-in for sensitive data
- Complete DPIAs for targeted advertising, data sales, profiling, and sensitive data processing activities
- Review and update data processing agreements with processors to include RIDTPPA-required contractual terms
- Train customer service and legal teams on the RIDTPPA rights-request process and the 45-day response deadline
- Monitor the Rhode Island Attorney General's office for enforcement guidance, complaint data, and any interpretive statements as enforcement matures in 2026
