# New York Legislature Passes One Fair Price Act to Ban Surveillance Pricing (S.8623B/A.9349B)

**Jurisdiction:** New York | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-08

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The New York State Legislature passed the [One Fair Price Act](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B) (S.8623B/A.9349B) on June 4, 2026, which would prohibit businesses from using consumers' personal data — including browsing history, location, inferred income, and household size — to algorithmically set individualized prices. The bill now awaits Governor Kathy Hochul's signature, with a deadline of December 31, 2026. If signed, New York becomes the third US state to enact surveillance pricing restrictions, joining Maryland and Connecticut, which both enacted comparable laws earlier in 2026.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The [New York State Legislature passed S.8623B/A.9349B](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B) on June 4, 2026; the bill is sponsored by Senator Rachel May and Assemblymember Emérita Torres ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B)).
- The Act prohibits businesses from using personal data — including purchase history, browsing history, real-time location, inferred income, or household size — to generate individualized prices for consumers ([Attorney General James press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-applauds-passage-legislation-protect-new-yorkers)).
- **Dynamic pricing is not banned.** Businesses may continue to use dynamic pricing algorithms that do not rely on consumer personal data, subject to a disclosure requirement ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-legislature-passes-ban-on-personalized-pricing.html)).
- **Explicit exemptions** include loyalty programs, coupons, subscribe-and-save discounts, and categorical promotions for veterans, seniors, and similar groups ([Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/the-price-isnt-right-emerging-patchwork-of-state-surveillance-pricing-bans-creates-compliance-complications-for-businesses)).
- **Scope:** The Act applies to any entity domiciled in New York or doing business in the state, including out-of-state and online retailers that sell to New York consumers ([ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/consumer-products-watch/one-price-fits-all-new-york-and-california-push-back)).
- **Enforcement:** The New York Attorney General may bring civil cases for penalties and restitution. Penalties reach $5,000 for a first violation and $20,000 for each subsequent violation, or the profits from the violation — whichever is greater ([ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/consumer-products-watch/one-price-fits-all-new-york-and-california-push-back)).
- **No private right of action.** A private right of action was removed during the amendment process; Consumer Reports has called on Governor Hochul to reinstate it ([Consumer Reports](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/new-york-legislature-passes-key-bill-to-curb-personalized-pricing/)).
- **Effective date:** 180 days after becoming law ([NY Senate Bill text](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B)).
- The law amends New York's General Business Law ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8623/amendment/B)).

## Regulatory Context [HIGH confidence]

Surveillance pricing — the practice of using personal data and behavioral signals to set individualized consumer prices — has attracted escalating regulatory scrutiny at both state and federal levels.

**Federal backdrop:** In July 2024, the [FTC issued orders to eight pricing intermediary firms](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-issues-orders-eight-companies-seeking-information-surveillance-pricing) under Section 6(b) authority to study the practice. In January 2025, the FTC published a [preliminary research summary](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer) finding that companies routinely use granular personal data — including location, demographics, and purchase history — to set differentiated prices. FTC leadership confirmed in April 2026 congressional testimony that staff work on the issue is ongoing.

**State legislative wave:** [At least 24 states](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/surveillance-pricing-24-states-ban-algorithmic-pricing-tracker-2026/) introduced surveillance pricing legislation in 2026. Three have enacted restrictions:

| State | Law | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maryland | HB 895 | Large food retailers (15,000+ sq ft) and third-party delivery providers; covers tax-exempt food items only | Enacted; signed April 2026 |
| Connecticut | SB 4 | All retailers and delivery services; prohibits personal-data-driven pricing; exempts loyalty programs and cost-based differences | Signed May 27, 2026; effective October 1, 2026 |
| New York | S.8623B/A.9349B | All businesses domiciled in or doing business in New York; covers all consumer goods | Awaiting Governor's signature; deadline December 31, 2026 |

Sources: [Venable LLP](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/05/maryland-leads-wave-of-state-surveillance-pricing), [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/maryland-enacts-first-of-its-kind-ban-on-surveillance-pricing-for-grocery-sales), [multistate.ai](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-101-state-surveillance-pricing-laws).

**California AB 2564** is also advancing: on May 27, 2026, the California Assembly passed [AB 2564](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/surveillance-pricing-update-californias-sweeping-ab-2564-passes-assembly-and-heads-to-senate), which would ban surveillance pricing by retailers with penalties up to $12,500 per consumer violation (tripled for intentional violations). It now moves to the California Senate ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/californias-bill-ban-surveillance-pricing)).

**Compliance complexity:** New York's Act is notably broader in scope than Maryland's and Connecticut's food-sector-focused laws. The [Sheppard Mullin analysis](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/the-price-isnt-right-emerging-patchwork-of-state-surveillance-pricing-bans-creates-compliance-complications-for-businesses) notes that multi-state businesses face compliance complications because each state law differs on covered products, covered entities, exemptions, and enforcement mechanisms.

## Action Items

- **Audit pricing systems now.** Retailers and e-commerce businesses operating in New York should identify all algorithmic pricing tools and determine whether they ingest consumer personal data (browsing history, location, inferred demographics) to set individualized prices.
- **Map data flows into pricing engines.** If sign-in, device fingerprint, or behavioral data feeds into price determination for New York customers, that system is within scope and must be redesigned or discontinued before the law takes effect (180 days after signing, potentially as early as mid-2027).
- **Review disclosure obligations for dynamic pricing.** Dynamic pricing algorithms that do not use personal data remain permitted but require disclosure under the Act. Prepare customer-facing disclosures now.
- **Document exemption eligibility.** Loyalty programs, coupons, and categorical promotional discounts remain lawful — but must be structured as category-level discounts, not data-derived individual price targeting. Retain documentation supporting exemption claims.
- **Track Governor Hochul's decision.** Hochul has until December 31, 2026 to sign or veto. Monitor for any conditional signing conditions or request for amendments (notably, whether a private right of action is restored).
- **Evaluate multi-state compliance posture.** Businesses operating in Maryland, Connecticut, and New York should consider whether a unified compliance architecture is feasible, noting the material scope differences between the three laws. Track California AB 2564 in the Senate.
- **Assess federal-state interaction.** If the FTC moves toward a federal surveillance pricing rule, it may preempt or supplement state law. Monitor FTC staff developments through 2026.
