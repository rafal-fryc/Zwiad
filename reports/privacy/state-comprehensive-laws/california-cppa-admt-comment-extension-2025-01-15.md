# CPPA Extends ADMT Rulemaking Public Comment Period to February 19, 2025, Citing Southern California Wildfires

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** January 15, 2025

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On January 10, 2025, the [California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) published a [Notice of Extension of Public Comment Period and Additional Hearing Date](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/addlhearing.pdf), extending the formal public comment period on its proposed CCPA rulemaking package from January 14, 2025 to February 19, 2025. The CPPA cited the need to ensure all Californians — including those affected by the devastating Southern California wildfires — have adequate opportunity to participate. A second public hearing was scheduled for February 19, 2025, in addition to the initial hearing already planned for January 14, 2025, with both hearings conducted in hybrid format (in-person in Sacramento plus virtual attendance). The underlying rulemaking package covers automated decision-making technology (ADMT), cybersecurity audits, privacy risk assessments, insurance company applicability, and updates to existing CCPA regulations. The regulations were ultimately adopted by the CPPA Board on July 24, 2025, and approved by the Office of Administrative Law on September 23, 2025.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The CCPA Rulemaking Framework

The [California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.185), as amended by Proposition 24 (the California Privacy Rights Act, CPRA) in November 2020, created the CPPA as the nation's first dedicated state privacy enforcement agency. Section 1798.185 of the Civil Code mandates the CPPA to adopt regulations governing consumer rights with respect to automated decision-making technology, risk assessments, and cybersecurity audits.

Under the California Administrative Procedure Act (APA), agencies conducting formal rulemaking must provide a minimum 45-day public comment period. The CPPA chose to provide a longer period for this rulemaking in recognition of the package's complexity and breadth. A public hearing is not required under the APA unless requested, but the CPPA committed to holding hearings as part of its public engagement process.

### Original Rulemaking Timeline

The CPPA Board [voted on November 8, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241108_2.html) to advance the rulemaking package to formal rulemaking status. The [formal public comment period opened November 22, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241122.html), with an original deadline of January 14, 2025 — exceeding the mandatory 45-day minimum to account for the holiday period. The CPPA scheduled a first public hearing for January 14, 2025, from 2 p.m. to 6 p.m. in a hybrid format.

### Wildfire Context

The January 2025 Southern California wildfires — which broke out in early January 2025 in the Los Angeles area — caused widespread displacement and disruption for millions of Californians. The CPPA's explicit citation of the wildfires as a reason for the extension reflects the agency's stated commitment to ensuring meaningful public participation, particularly for affected communities who may have had limited capacity to engage during the disaster.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope of the Rulemaking Package

The comment period extension applies to the full multi-topic rulemaking package, which [Goodwin Law summarized](https://www.goodwinprivacyblog.com/2025/02/05/california-forges-a-new-path-on-automated-decision-making-technology-risk-assessments-and-cybersecurity-audits/) as covering four principal areas:

**1. Automated Decision-Making Technology (ADMT)**

The proposed ADMT regulations would require businesses to provide consumers with "pre-use notices" before deploying ADMT for significant decisions — defined as decisions affecting finances, housing, education, employment, or healthcare. The pre-use notice must describe how the ADMT works, what personal information affects its outputs, and what alternative decision-making processes exist if the consumer opts out. Consumers would have the right to opt out of ADMT use for significant decisions, and businesses must provide explanations of the logic employed.

Per the [CPPA's regulations page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html), the finalized ADMT regulations require businesses to comply by **January 1, 2027**.

**2. Privacy Risk Assessments**

Covered businesses must conduct and document risk assessments evaluating the "negative impacts" of certain processing activities on consumers — including risks of discrimination, economic or physical harm, reputational harm, or interference with informed consumer choice. A key change in the final regulations from earlier drafts: the CPPA removed a prohibition on processing activities in which privacy risks outweigh benefits, replacing it with a goal of "restricting or prohibiting" such activities. Risk assessment compliance obligations begin **January 1, 2026**; businesses must then submit risk assessment attestations to the CPPA by **April 1, 2028**.

**3. Cybersecurity Audits**

Businesses whose personal information processing imposes a significant risk on consumer privacy must complete an annual cybersecurity audit and certify the audit to the CPPA. Certification deadlines are staggered by revenue:
- Businesses with revenue over $100 million: **April 1, 2028**
- Businesses with revenue of $50–100 million: **April 1, 2029**
- Businesses with revenue under $50 million: **April 1, 2030**

**4. Insurance Company Clarifications and CCPA Updates**

The package also includes clarifications regarding when insurance companies must comply with CCPA obligations, and general updates to existing CCPA regulations.

### The Procedural Significance of the Extension

Under California's APA, the extended comment period — now running November 22, 2024 through February 19, 2025 (approximately 89 days) — nearly doubled the mandatory minimum. The addition of a second formal hearing on February 19, 2025, provided a second structured opportunity for oral and written public comment. Both hearings followed a hybrid format allowing participation in person at CPPA's Sacramento offices or virtually, maximizing accessibility.

Written comments submitted during the extended period are incorporated into the administrative record. The CPPA's rules require the agency to consider and respond to comments in the Final Statement of Reasons. The breadth of this rulemaking — and the unprecedented scope of ADMT obligations — generated substantial industry comment during the extended period.

### Outcome of the Rulemaking

Following the close of the public comment period on February 19, 2025, the CPPA conducted additional review and modified certain provisions. The [CPPA Board adopted the final regulations on July 24, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html). The Office of Administrative Law (OAL) [approved the regulations on September 23, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html), and they were filed with the California Secretary of State, making them operative law.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected

The ADMT regulations have the broadest reach of any component in the package, applying to any business subject to the CCPA that uses automated decision-making to produce outputs — including decisions, recommendations, content delivery, profiling, or automated influence — in the following high-stakes contexts:
- Employment decisions (hiring, promotion, performance evaluation)
- Lending, credit, and financial services
- Housing applications and tenancy
- Healthcare services and eligibility
- Education access and evaluation

Businesses deploying AI-driven scoring, filtering, recommendation, or classification tools in any of these contexts will need to implement pre-use notice and opt-out mechanisms before January 1, 2027.

### Industry Concerns Raised During Comment Period

[Law firm analyses from Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/ag-data-dive/cppa-opens-public-comment-period-for-proposed-ai-regulations) and others documented significant industry concern during the public comment process regarding:
- The breadth of the definition of "significant decision"
- The operability of real-time opt-out mechanisms for complex AI pipelines
- The interaction of ADMT opt-outs with anti-discrimination obligations (where ADMT may be required for equal treatment)
- Overlap between the CPPA's risk assessment requirements and existing federal sector-specific requirements (e.g., HIPAA, FCRA)

### Compliance Horizon

Given the extension's ultimate importance — it significantly shaped the final regulations by allowing deeper industry input — organizations operating in California should benchmark their ADMT inventories against the final adopted regulations now. The January 1, 2027 ADMT compliance deadline is under two years from adoption.

## Action Items

- Review the [final adopted CPPA regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) to determine whether your business's use of automated decision-making tools constitutes ADMT for "significant decisions" under the CCPA.
- Conduct an inventory of AI/ML tools and workflows that produce outputs affecting employment, lending, housing, healthcare, or education decisions in California — ADMT compliance is required by **January 1, 2027**.
- Begin building infrastructure for pre-use consumer notices and opt-out mechanisms for covered ADMT use cases.
- Begin risk assessment compliance immediately — obligations are effective **January 1, 2026** — and calendar CPPA attestation submission by **April 1, 2028** (most covered businesses).
- Monitor CPPA guidance on ADMT definition interpretation, particularly regarding HR technology, credit underwriting, and content recommendation systems.
- If your business qualifies as a data broker under SB 362 (the Delete Act), separately track the [DROP platform rulemaking](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/drop.html) with data broker deletion compliance beginning **August 1, 2026**.
