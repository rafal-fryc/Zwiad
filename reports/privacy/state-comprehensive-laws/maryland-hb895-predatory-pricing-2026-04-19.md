# Maryland Set to Enact Nation's First Surveillance Pricing Ban for Food Retailers and Delivery Platforms

**Jurisdiction:** Maryland | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-19

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Governor Wes Moore announced he will sign [Maryland HB 895, the Protection from Predatory Pricing Act](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0895?ys=2026RS), making Maryland the first U.S. state to prohibit surveillance pricing -- the practice of using personal data to set individualized prices -- in the food retail and third-party delivery sectors. The Maryland General Assembly passed the bill on April 11, 2026, following a House passage on March 21 and a Senate vote on April 10. **As of April 19, 2026, the bill had not yet been signed into law; Governor Moore's signature remains pending.** The United Food and Commercial Workers International Union (UFCW) has publicly called for a veto, arguing the bill contains loopholes that leave room for corporate price exploitation. Once signed, the law takes effect October 1, 2026, giving covered businesses approximately five months to achieve compliance.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Bill and sponsor:** [HB 895](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0895?ys=2026RS) (Protection from Predatory Pricing Act) was introduced by the House Speaker at the request of the Moore Administration in January 2026, passed the House on March 21, 2026, and the Senate passed an amended version on April 10, 2026; final passage was April 11, 2026. ([Maryland General Assembly](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0895?ys=2026RS))

- **Governor's commitment:** Gov. Moore announced he will sign the bill, stating: "Marylanders deserve to know that the price they see on the shelf is the price they will pay at the register." As of April 19, 2026, the bill awaits the Governor's formal signature. ([Governor Moore press release](https://governor.maryland.gov/news/press/pages/governor-moore-announces-legislation-to-protect-marylanders%E2%80%99-pocketbooks,-data-privacy-at-the-grocery-store.aspx))

- **UFCW opposition:** The United Food and Commercial Workers International Union (UFCW) has publicly called for a veto of HB 895, arguing that the bill's exemptions create loopholes that allow large corporations such as Walmart to engage in price-gouging within the law's framework. The UFCW stated that legislators "failed to protect families from surveillance pricing" and urged the legislature to draft stronger legislation. ([UFCW press release](https://www.ufcw.org/press-releases/maryland-legislators-fail-to-protect-families-from-surveillance-pricing-says-ufcw/))

- **What is prohibited -- dynamic pricing:** The Act prohibits food retailers and third-party food delivery service providers from setting a personalized price for a good or service based on a consumer's personal data (defined as "dynamic pricing"). Prices must remain fixed for at least one business day. ([FKKS Technology Law](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mpic/maryland-legislature-passes-personalized-pricing-ban-with-narrow-scope))

- **What is prohibited -- protected class data:** The Act separately prohibits using data about legally protected characteristics (race, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status, or sexual orientation) to offer or sell goods or services in a way that denies accommodations or advantages to individuals. ([EPIC document summary](https://epic.org/documents/maryland-h-b-895-protection-from-predatory-pricing-act/))

- **Scope of coverage:** The law applies to (1) food retailers operating in Maryland and (2) third-party food delivery service providers. It does not apply to non-food retail, apparel, hospitality, or other sectors. ([Supermarket News](https://www.supermarketnews.com/grocery-technology/does-maryland-s-measure-on-surveillance-pricing-do-enough-))

- **Exemptions:** Nine enumerated categories are excluded from the dynamic pricing prohibition, including: loyalty, membership, and rewards programs open to voluntary consumer enrollment; subscription-based contracts; promotional or sale pricing; price differences attributable to objective costs (e.g., shipping, location-based taxes); supply-and-demand price variation across geographic locations; and price differences based on availability or supply of a good or service. ([FKKS Technology Law](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mpic/maryland-legislature-passes-personalized-pricing-ban-with-narrow-scope))

- **Effective date:** October 1, 2026, contingent on Governor's signature. ([Maryland General Assembly](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0895?ys=2026RS))

- **Enforcement:** Violations are treated as unfair, abusive, or deceptive trade practices under the Maryland Consumer Protection Act. Enforcement is exclusively vested in the Division of Consumer Protection within the Maryland Office of the Attorney General -- there is no private right of action. The AG must provide companies with written notice of a violation and a 45-day cure period before pursuing further action. ([Privacy Daily](https://privacy-daily.com/article/2026/04/13/maryland-predatory-pricing-act-expected-to-become-law-2604130035?BC=bc_69dd6f63368d0))

- **Penalties:** Civil penalties of up to $10,000 for a first offense; up to $25,000 for subsequent offenses per violation. ([Good News Network](https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/maryland-to-become-first-us-state-to-ban-surveillance-pricing/))

- **First of its kind:** Maryland is positioned to become the first U.S. state to enact a law specifically targeting surveillance pricing, pending the Governor's signature. ([Good News Network](https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/maryland-to-become-first-us-state-to-ban-surveillance-pricing/))

## What is "Surveillance Pricing"? [HIGH confidence]

"Surveillance pricing" (also called "dynamic pricing" or "personalized pricing") refers to the practice of using data collected through digital surveillance -- shopping behavior, purchase history, browsing patterns, location, demographic characteristics, and even mouse movements on a webpage -- to set individualized prices for the same good or service. Unlike uniform pricing or category-level promotions, surveillance pricing means two consumers purchasing identical items at the same moment can pay different prices based on what an algorithm infers about each consumer's willingness to pay.

The [FTC's January 2025 surveillance pricing study](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-surveillance-pricing-study-indicates-wide-range-personal-data-used-set-individualized-consumer) -- which surveyed eight major surveillance pricing intermediaries -- found that grocery stores were among the clients. The FTC study found intermediaries collected data ranging from precise location and purchase history to unpurchased items left in online shopping carts. This study appears to have directly informed Maryland's legislative response.

Electronic shelf label (ESL) technology has accelerated the practical feasibility of surveillance pricing in physical stores, allowing retailers to change displayed prices in real time across thousands of SKUs without manually replacing paper labels. Maryland's bill specifically targets this capability when deployed for personalized pricing.

## Federal Context: FTC's Prior Inquiry [MEDIUM confidence]

The FTC's [surveillance pricing program](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/features/surveillance-pricing) pre-dates Maryland's state action. In 2024, the FTC issued 6(b) orders to eight intermediary companies offering surveillance pricing technologies and released preliminary findings in January 2025. The [FTC Issue Spotlight on the Rise of Surveillance Pricing](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/sp6b-issue-spotlight.pdf) documented how companies use real-time data to adjust consumer-specific pricing across grocery, apparel, and travel sectors.

The FTC study found that surveillance pricing intermediaries served at least 250 retail clients and that outputs ranged from store-level pricing (e.g., adjusting egg prices across stores with high concentrations of halal meat purchasers) to individually targeted consumer prices. Despite these findings, no federal legislation or FTC rule specifically targeting surveillance pricing has been enacted. Maryland's law fills a gap left by federal inaction.

New York has also advanced legislation on algorithmic pricing, though as of this report it has not reached the same stage as Maryland's Act. ([Epstein Becker Green](https://www.workforcebulletin.com/when-your-data-sets-your-price-new-york-takes-on-algorithmic-pricing))

## Compliance Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

**Who must act before October 1, 2026:**

- **Food retailers operating in Maryland** -- including traditional grocery chains, specialty food stores, warehouse clubs, and convenience stores selling food -- must audit their pricing systems for personal-data-driven personalization.
- **Third-party food delivery platforms** (e.g., DoorDash, Instacart, Uber Eats, and similar) that operate in Maryland must review their pricing algorithms for dynamic personalized pricing based on consumer data.

**Compliance steps:**

1. **Audit pricing systems:** Map all pricing decision processes to identify where personal consumer data influences individual-level price outputs. Any such input constitutes "dynamic pricing" under the Act.

2. **Review loyalty and subscription programs:** Loyalty programs are expressly exempt, but only if open to voluntary consumer enrollment. Assess whether existing loyalty structures meet the exemption criteria.

3. **Evaluate ESL deployments:** If electronic shelf labels are in use or planned, confirm that any personalization features are disabled or that pricing changes are uniform rather than consumer-specific.

4. **Vendor contracts:** Review contracts with third-party pricing algorithm vendors to assess which data inputs drive price outputs and whether those inputs include personal consumer data.

5. **Delivery platform pricing:** Third-party delivery services that display food prices (as opposed to service fees) must ensure displayed food prices are not personalized based on the ordering consumer's data profile.

6. **Document exemptions:** If relying on any of the nine exemptions (loyalty programs, geographic pricing, supply-based pricing, etc.), document the factual basis for the exemption claim.

7. **AG notification readiness:** The Act's 45-day cure period means companies should have a compliance response plan ready if they receive an AG notice of violation.

**What the law does NOT prohibit:**

- Loyalty program discounts available to any consumer who voluntarily enrolls
- Promotional pricing, sale pricing, and coupons applied uniformly
- Price differences reflecting objective cost differences (shipping, taxes, distance)
- Geographic or location-based pricing based on supply and demand conditions
- Subscription-based pricing arrangements

**Critical ambiguity:** The law's scope is limited to "food retailers" and "third-party delivery service providers," but definitions of these terms will require careful legal review. Businesses that sell both food and non-food items (e.g., large general retailers like Target or Walmart) will need to assess whether the food retail component of their operations falls within scope.

- **By July 15, 2026:** Remediate any dynamic pricing systems found to use personal data; confirm loyalty program structures qualify under the voluntary enrollment exemption; update vendor agreements as needed.
- **By September 1, 2026:** Implement compliance controls and staff training; ensure ESL systems do not enable personalized pricing; establish process for responding to AG notices within 45-day cure window.

- **Watch other states:** Maryland's first-in-the-nation status will likely trigger legislative activity in other states; monitor New York and California for similar bills.

## Update 2026-05-04 [HIGH confidence]

Governor Wes Moore signed Maryland HB 895, the Protection from Predatory Pricing Act, into law on April 28, 2026 as [Chapter 154](https://governor.maryland.gov/news/press/Documents/Bills%20to%20be%20Signed%20by%20the%20Governor%20on%20April%2028,%202026%20(Accessible).pdf), making Maryland the first U.S. state to enact a ban on surveillance-based pricing. The signing resolves the pending signature noted in this report's original version; the October 1, 2026 effective date is now confirmed. Consumer Reports acknowledged the law's milestone status while reiterating concerns -- shared with the UFCW -- that broad exemptions for loyalty and membership programs may limit its practical scope for consumers. The [Morgan Lewis analysis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/maryland-enacts-hb-895-becoming-first-state-to-restrict-personalized-pricing-in-the-food-sector) published following enactment confirms the law's scope remains limited to food retailers and third-party delivery providers, with the nine statutory exemptions intact as passed.
