# Alabama Becomes 21st State to Enact Comprehensive Privacy Law (HB 351)

**Client Alert — April 12, 2026**

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 7, 2026, the Alabama Legislature unanimously passed [House Bill 351](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB351-enr.pdf), the Alabama Personal Data Protection Act ("APDPA"), making Alabama the 21st U.S. state to adopt a comprehensive consumer privacy statute. The bill cleared the House 104-0 and the Senate 34-0 and awaits signature by Governor Kay Ivey, per [DLA Piper's Privacy Matters analysis](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/). If signed, the law will take effect on **May 1, 2027**.

While the APDPA broadly tracks the Virginia/Connecticut template, it contains several notable departures — including one of the lowest applicability thresholds in the country, a narrower definition of "sale," and broad entity-level exemptions — that will require tailored compliance mapping.

## Key Provisions [HIGH confidence]

### Applicability Thresholds

The APDPA applies to controllers and processors that conduct business in Alabama or target Alabama residents and meet either of the following:

- Control or process the personal data of **more than 25,000 consumers** (excluding data processed solely to complete a payment transaction); or
- Derive **more than 25% of gross revenue from the sale of personal data**.

The 25,000-consumer floor is the lowest numerical threshold among comprehensive state privacy laws enacted to date, according to [IAPP's analysis of the bill](https://iapp.org/news/a/alabama-set-to-add-variation-to-us-state-privacy-patchwork).

### Consumer Rights

Alabama residents will have the right to:

- Confirm whether a controller is processing their personal data;
- Correct inaccuracies;
- Delete personal data;
- Obtain a portable copy of their data; and
- Opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of personal data, and certain profiling.

### Definition of "Sale"

The APDPA defines "sale" as an exchange of personal data for monetary or other valuable consideration where the controller receives a material benefit **and** the third party is not restricted in subsequent uses of the data. This narrower formulation — requiring the receiving party to be *unrestricted* — is a meaningful departure from the CCPA-style definition, per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/alabama-set-to-add-variation-to-us-state-privacy-patchwork).

The statute also contains sale-exclusion carve-outs not found in other states, including disclosures for the purposes of "providing analytics services" or "providing marketing services solely to the controller."

### Exemptions

**Entity-level exemptions:**
- Small businesses with fewer than **500 employees** (unless they sell personal data) — broader than most state laws, which rely on revenue or consumer thresholds rather than headcount.
- Nonprofits with fewer than 100 employees (with the same selling-data caveat).
- Political action committees, political parties, principal campaign committees, and political organizations defined under [26 U.S.C. § 527](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/527), as well as entities that sell data primarily to such organizations. This exemption was added by Senate amendment to address potential First Amendment concerns.
- Standard carve-outs for financial institutions subject to GLBA and HIPAA-covered entities.

**Data-level exemptions:** HR/employee and B2B data; PHI governed by HIPAA; FCRA consumer-report data; DPPA-regulated data; FERPA educational records; Farm Credit Act data; and data processed under the Airline Deregulation Act.

### Enforcement and Cure

The Alabama Attorney General has **exclusive enforcement authority**. There is **no private right of action**. Before initiating an action, the AG must issue a notice of violation and afford the controller **45 days to cure**. If the controller cures and provides an express written statement that no further violations will occur, no action may be brought. Unlike Virginia, Connecticut, and others where the cure period phases out, **Alabama's cure provision does not sunset**, per [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/).

## What's Notably Absent [MEDIUM confidence]

As [Privacy Daily reported](https://privacy-daily.com/article/2026/02/25/alabama-house-passes-comprehensive-privacy-bill-with-no-mention-of-ai-2602250016), the bill contains no provisions addressing automated decision-making, profiling safeguards beyond an opt-out, or AI governance — a conspicuous absence given the 2025–2026 wave of state AI laws. Alabama also does not require data protection assessments in the form adopted by Virginia and Connecticut, and there is no universal opt-out mechanism ("UOOM") mandate of the kind adopted by Colorado, California, and Texas.

## Action Items for Compliance Teams

- **Re-scope applicability:** the 25,000-consumer threshold will pull in companies that were previously out-of-scope under VCDPA-style 100,000-consumer floors. Mid-sized marketers, e-commerce firms, and ad-tech intermediaries should run fresh Alabama-resident counts.
- **Revisit "sale" categorization:** the narrower Alabama definition, combined with the analytics/marketing carve-outs, may permit activities classified as "sales" under California law to fall outside Alabama's scope — but only where contractual restrictions on downstream use are in place. Audit vendor contracts now.
- **Update privacy notices** before May 1, 2027 to add Alabama-specific rights language and opt-out mechanisms.
- **Extend DSAR workflows** to Alabama residents; the rights catalog is standard but the request-handling windows should be confirmed against the enrolled text.
- **Confirm entity-level exemption eligibility** if the organization has fewer than 500 employees and does not "sell" data as defined.
