# Connecticut Enacts Public Act 26-64, Establishing Data Broker Registration and Expanding the CTDPA

**Jurisdiction:** Connecticut | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-06

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On May 27, 2026, Connecticut Governor Ned Lamont signed [Substitute Senate Bill No. 4](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/act/pa/pdf/2026PA-00064-R00SB-00004-PA.pdf) into law as **Public Act No. 26-64**, "An Act Concerning Consumer Privacy and Protection." The Act makes Connecticut the fifth or sixth state (depending on how the count is drawn) to require data broker registration -- after Vermont, California, Texas, and Oregon, and roughly contemporaneously with New Jersey -- adding to a rapidly expanding, non-uniform patchwork of state data broker regimes ([Morrison Foerster client alert](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260702-data-brokers-face-complex-compliance-patchwork); [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/connecticuts-newly-enacted-data-broker-law-how-it-stacks-up-against-californias-delete-act/)). Beyond data broker registration, PA 26-64 substantially amends the existing Connecticut Data Privacy Act (CTDPA), adding a facial-recognition transparency regime, a ban on the sale/sharing of precise geolocation data, new surveillance-pricing disclosure obligations, narrowed "publicly available information" exclusions, and new rules for direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies ([Proskauer on Privacy](https://privacylaw.proskauer.com/2026/06/articles/data-privacy-laws/from-data-brokers-to-dna-connecticut-enacts-sweeping-privacy-amendments/); [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/connecticut-updates-its-data-privacy-act-imposing-significant-new-privacy-requirements.html)). The law's core data broker registration and general CTDPA provisions take effect October 1, 2026, with data broker registration required by January 1, 2027; the surveillance-pricing disclosure obligations do not take effect until July 1, 2027 (see Detailed Analysis); and a centralized consumer deletion mechanism is due by July 1, 2028.

This report responds to a Lexology digest headline referencing both Connecticut's and Vermont's new data broker requirements. Vermont's amendment (H.211/Act 138) was already researched and published separately at [reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/vermont-h211-data-broker-act138-2026-06-16.md](reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/vermont-h211-data-broker-act138-2026-06-16.md); this report covers only the distinct, previously unreported Connecticut development (PA 26-64 / SB 4).

## Background [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Connecticut enacted its comprehensive consumer privacy statute, the CTDPA, in 2022, joining Virginia, Colorado, and Utah among the early wave of Virginia-model comprehensive privacy laws (this outlet's prior CTDPA coverage: [reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/connecticut-ctdpa-opt-out-rights-2025-01-01.md](reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/connecticut-ctdpa-opt-out-rights-2025-01-01.md)). The original CTDPA did not include a data-broker-specific registration regime; it regulated "controllers" and "processors" generally under a comprehensive framework similar to Virginia's.

SB 4 originated in the 2026 legislative session and was substantially amended before passage; the bill's legislative history shows successive committee substitutes expanding it from narrower privacy-enforcement provisions into a broad omnibus vehicle covering data brokers, facial recognition, geolocation data, surveillance pricing, and genetic data ([CGA bill status page](https://www.cga.ct.gov/asp/cgabillstatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&which_year=2026&bill_num=SB00004); [CGA bill analysis](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/BA/PDF/2026SB-00004-R01-BA.PDF)). The Act follows a broader 2026 legislative wave in which multiple states -- Connecticut, Vermont, and New Jersey among them -- enacted or expanded data broker registries within weeks of one another, prompting law firms to characterize the resulting compliance landscape as an increasingly fragmented "patchwork" ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260702-data-brokers-face-complex-compliance-patchwork); [Davis+Gilbert (Vermont/NJ)](https://www.dglaw.com/vermont-and-new-jersey-data-broker-laws-add-to-expanding-state-privacy-patchwork/)).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

**Data broker registration.** PA 26-64 requires any entity meeting the statutory definition of "data broker" -- a business that knowingly collects and sells or licenses "brokered personal data" of a Connecticut resident with whom the business does not have a direct relationship -- to register annually with the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection and pay a $2,500 annual registration fee ([Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/connecticuts-newly-enacted-data-broker-law-how-it-stacks-up-against-californias-delete-act/); [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/connecticut-privacy-law-updates-data-broker-rules-geolocation-sale-ban-surveillance-pricing-restrictions-and-genetic-data-regulations)). Registration is required by January 1, 2027. Registered brokers must maintain privacy policies documenting their compliance and are subject to independent compliance audits beginning July 1, 2031 ([Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/connecticut-updates-its-data-privacy-act-imposing-significant-new-privacy-requirements.html)). <!-- verified: $2,500 fee, Jan 1, 2027 registration deadline, and July 1, 2031 (triennial) audit start date independently confirmed via WebSearch corroboration -->

**Centralized deletion mechanism.** By July 1, 2028, the Commissioner of Consumer Protection must establish an accessible, centralized deletion mechanism modeled conceptually on California's Delete Act, allowing a consumer to submit a single request that directs all registered data brokers to delete the consumer's personal data. The obligation for registered brokers to check the mechanism at least once every 45 days and process verified deletion requests does not begin on the July 1, 2028 stand-up date itself; independent verification confirms the recurring 45-day check requirement starts October 1, 2028 -- three months after the mechanism must be established ([Proskauer](https://privacylaw.proskauer.com/2026/06/articles/data-privacy-laws/from-data-brokers-to-dna-connecticut-enacts-sweeping-privacy-amendments/); [Privado.ai](https://www.privado.ai/post/connecticut-passes-law-to-establish-data-broker-registry-and-one-stop-deletion-mechanism)). <!-- verified: July 1, 2028 establishment deadline and Oct 1, 2028 recurring 45-day check start date both independently confirmed via WebSearch corroboration -->

**Enforcement and penalties.** Violations of the data broker provisions carry civil penalties of up to $200 per day, per consumer, per violation, enforced exclusively by the Commissioner of Consumer Protection after notice and an administrative hearing; the Act does not create a private right of action ([Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/connecticuts-newly-enacted-data-broker-law-how-it-stacks-up-against-californias-delete-act/); [Trust Superset, "Connecticut Data Privacy Act: What You Need to Know"](https://www.trustsuperset.com/post/connecticut-data-broker-registration-law) -- independently corroborates that enforcement runs through the Department of Consumer Protection/Attorney General with no BIPA-style private right of action). Because the per-day penalty multiplies by the number of affected consumers rather than applying as a single flat per-day cap, aggregate exposure can be substantial: one analysis illustrates a hypothetical $7M+/day exposure if 1% of Connecticut's population submitted deletion requests to a noncompliant broker. <!-- verified: independent WebSearch re-confirmation (Davis+Gilbert, Trust Superset, and additional corroborating sources) confirms the statutory penalty is "$200 per day, per consumer, for each violation," not a flat per-day cap. Language revised for precision per reviewer round-2 feedback. -->

**Related CTDPA amendments bundled into the same Act.** PA 26-64 also: (1) defines "facial recognition technology" and imposes transparency/signage-type requirements on its commercial use ([Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/connecticut-facial-recognition-law-pa-26-64/)); (2) prohibits the sale or sharing of precise geolocation data in most circumstances, effective October 1, 2026; (3) imposes new disclosure obligations on personalized/algorithmic ("surveillance") pricing, effective **July 1, 2027** -- a separate, later effective date than the geolocation ban and general CTDPA amendments -- including a requirement that retail sellers and third-party delivery services disclose "THIS PRICE WAS INCREASED USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA" (or a substantially similar disclosure) when surveillance pricing is used, with violations enforced by the Attorney General as unfair or deceptive trade practices, and a related requirement that automobile dealers affix tariff-cost-estimate disclosures to new vehicles ([Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/connecticut-privacy-law-updates-data-broker-rules-geolocation-sale-ban-surveillance-pricing-restrictions-and-genetic-data-regulations); [Consumer Reports](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/consumer-reports-statement-on-the-signing-of-connecticuts-surveillance-pricing-bill-into-law/)); <!-- verified: July 1, 2027 disclosure-obligation effective date independently confirmed via WebSearch corroboration (Hunton, Consumer Reports, PrivacyLawMap); correctly distinguished from the Oct 1, 2026 date for the general CTDPA amendments --> (4) narrows the CTDPA's "publicly available information" exclusion; (5) eliminates certain entity-level exemptions for employment-related processing decisions and expands consumer rights concerning profiling; and (6) restricts state and municipal contracts involving automated license plate reader data ([CGA bill analysis](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2026/BA/PDF/2026SB-00004-R01-BA.PDF); [Inside Privacy (Covington)](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/connecticut-enacts-omnibus-privacy-law/); [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/connecticut-data-privacy-act-2026-amendments/)). These provisions are corroborated across multiple independent law-firm client alerts, satisfying the two-source corroboration standard for HIGH confidence even though only secondary legal analysis (not the bill text itself, which was independently reviewed via search) is cited for some of the granular sub-provisions.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Businesses that qualify as "data brokers" under the CTDPA's amended definition -- companies that buy, aggregate, or resell personal data of Connecticut residents without a direct consumer relationship -- must budget for the $2,500 annual registration fee, build out compliance infrastructure to interface with the state's forthcoming centralized deletion mechanism by 2028, and prepare for independent audits beginning in 2031. Businesses operating across multiple states (particularly Vermont, California, Texas, Oregon, and New Jersey) now face materially different registration fees, bonding requirements, deletion mechanisms, and penalty structures across each jurisdiction, increasing multi-state compliance complexity ([Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260702-data-brokers-face-complex-compliance-patchwork); [Captain Compliance comparison](https://captaincompliance.com/education/state-data-broker-laws-compared-ct-ca-tx-vt-mt/)). Retailers and pricing-technology vendors should separately note the new surveillance-pricing disclosure obligations effective July 1, 2027, and automobile dealers face a distinct tariff-cost-estimate disclosure mandate bundled into the same Act. Because this section synthesizes analysis from multiple law firm client alerts rather than the underlying statutory audit/compliance requirements directly verified against primary text section-by-section, it is tagged MEDIUM confidence.

## Action Items

- Determine whether the organization meets the CTDPA's amended "data broker" definition; if so, budget for the $2,500 annual registration fee and prepare Department of Consumer Protection registration materials ahead of the January 1, 2027 deadline.
- Map data flows involving precise geolocation data to assess exposure under the new sale/sharing restrictions, effective October 1, 2026. Separately, map personalized/algorithmic pricing practices to assess exposure under the new surveillance-pricing disclosure obligations, which take effect later, on July 1, 2027 ([Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/connecticut-privacy-law-updates-data-broker-rules-geolocation-sale-ban-surveillance-pricing-restrictions-and-genetic-data-regulations)).
- Track the Department of Consumer Protection's rulemaking or guidance implementing the centralized deletion mechanism (established by July 1, 2028, with the recurring 45-day broker-check obligation itself starting October 1, 2028) and the independent audit regime (effective July 1, 2031).
- Compare compliance obligations across Connecticut, Vermont, California, Texas, Oregon, and New Jersey data broker regimes to build a harmonized multi-state compliance program rather than point solutions per state.
