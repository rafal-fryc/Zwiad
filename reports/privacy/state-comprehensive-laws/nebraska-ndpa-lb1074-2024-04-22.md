# Nebraska Enacts the Data Privacy Act (LB 1074/NDPA): Broad Applicability Without Revenue Thresholds

**Jurisdiction:** Nebraska | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-04-22

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 17, 2024, Nebraska Governor Jim Pillen signed [Legislative Bill 1074](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/108/PDF/Slip/LB1074.pdf) into law, enacting the Nebraska Data Privacy Act (NDPA) as part of an omnibus package. Nebraska became the sixteenth state to adopt comprehensive data privacy legislation. The NDPA takes effect on **January 1, 2025**. The law's most distinctive feature is its scope: unlike the majority of state privacy laws, the NDPA does not set revenue or data-volume thresholds for applicability — any entity conducting business in Nebraska or targeting Nebraska residents that processes personal data (and is not a small business under the federal Small Business Act) must comply. This "wide net" approach most closely parallels the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act and stands in contrast to VCDPA-model laws that limit applicability to larger processors. Enforcement rests exclusively with the Nebraska Attorney General, with civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation and a permanent (non-sunsetting) 30-day cure period.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The NDPA emerged in the context of a rapidly accelerating wave of state comprehensive privacy legislation. Prior to Nebraska's enactment, fifteen states had already passed omnibus privacy statutes, beginning with California's CCPA (2018) and accelerating through 2022–2024 when states including Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Iowa, Indiana, Montana, Tennessee, Texas, Oregon, Delaware, New Hampshire, New Jersey, and Kentucky all joined the patchwork.

Nebraska's [LB 1074](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/bills/view_bill.php?DocumentID=54904) was introduced during the 108th Legislature, Second Session. The omnibus bill addressed multiple consumer financial services topics in addition to the data privacy title, but the NDPA provisions — codified at [Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 87-1101 et seq.](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=87-1101) — constitute the most significant new privacy obligations. Governor Pillen signed the bill on April 17, 2024, and the NDPA entered into force on January 1, 2025.

The Nebraska Office of the Attorney General maintains a [Data Privacy Homepage](https://protectthegoodlife.nebraska.gov/data-privacy-homepage) providing consumer-facing guidance. The law's structural model draws most heavily from the [Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/pdf/SB00-2132F.pdf), particularly in its threshold-free applicability for medium and large businesses, opt-out consent requirements, and AG-only enforcement.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope of Applicability

The NDPA applies to any person or entity that:
1. conducts business in Nebraska or produces a product or service consumed by Nebraska residents;
2. processes or engages in the sale of personal data; and
3. is **not** a small business as defined by the federal Small Business Act.

This formulation is deliberately threshold-free on the controller side. Laws like the Virginia VCDPA and Connecticut CTDPA require controllers to process data of at least 100,000 consumers annually (or 25,000 with revenue from data sales), effectively exempting smaller organizations. The NDPA — following the Texas model — has no equivalent consumer-count or revenue threshold. The sole carve-out is the federal Small Business Act definition, which uses a size standard set by the SBA by industry (typically measured by employee count or gross receipts). Any entity larger than that standard that handles Nebraska residents' personal data is presumptively covered.

Law firm analyses from [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2024/08/nebraska-data-privacy-act--an-overview.html) and [McGrath North](https://www.mcgrathnorth.com/nebraska-legislature-passes-consumer-data-privacy-bill-an-overview) confirm that this structure "casts a wide net" over companies that would be exempt under many other state laws.

### Entity and Data Exemptions

Despite the broad threshold, the NDPA includes a range of entity-level and data-level exemptions:

**Entity-level exemptions** include:
- Nebraska state agencies and local governments
- Financial institutions subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA)
- Healthcare entities covered by or governed by HIPAA
- Nonprofit organizations
- Higher education institutions
- Certain utility providers

**Data-level exemptions** include data protected by HIPAA, health records, patient identifying information, FCRA-governed data, DPPA data, FERPA records, Farm Credit Act data, and personal data used solely in employment or commercial contexts.

These exemptions are consistent with the mainstream of state privacy laws and mean that large regulated industries (banking, insurance, healthcare) are largely untouched at the entity level.

### Consumer Rights

The NDPA grants Nebraska residents the following rights against covered controllers:

- **Right of access**: to confirm whether personal data is being processed and to obtain a copy
- **Right to correction**: to correct inaccuracies in personal data
- **Right to deletion**: to request deletion of personal data
- **Right to data portability**: to obtain personal data in a portable format
- **Right to opt out**: of (a) targeted advertising, (b) sale of personal data, and (c) profiling in furtherance of solely automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects

Controllers must respond to consumer rights requests within **45 days**, extendable by an additional 45 days where reasonably necessary. Responses must be provided free of charge up to twice per year.

For **sensitive data** — defined to include data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health diagnosis, sexual orientation, citizenship or immigration status, genetic data, biometric data used to uniquely identify individuals, personal data of a known child, and precise geolocation data — the NDPA requires **opt-in (affirmative) consent** before processing. This is a higher standard than the opt-out default that applies to general personal data.

### Controller and Processor Obligations

Covered controllers must:

1. **Publish a privacy notice** disclosing categories of personal data collected, purposes of processing, how consumers may exercise their rights, and whether personal data is shared with third parties.
2. **Data minimization**: collect only personal data that is adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary for the disclosed purposes.
3. **Implement reasonable safeguards** appropriate to the size and complexity of the controller's operations to protect personal data.
4. **Obtain opt-in consent** before processing sensitive data.
5. **Conduct and document Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs)** for processing activities that present a heightened risk of harm, including: targeted advertising, sale of personal data, profiling with significant effects, processing sensitive data, and any processing presenting heightened consumer risk. A single assessment may cover comparable sets of activities, and assessments conducted under other laws (e.g., GDPR) may satisfy this requirement per [§ 87-1116](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=87-1116).
6. **Enter written contracts with processors** that specify processing instructions, required confidentiality obligations, processor cooperation obligations, subprocessor engagement terms, audit rights, and deletion/return of data upon termination.

Processors are required to comply with controller instructions, ensure personnel subject to confidentiality, provide information necessary for controllers to conduct DPIAs, and cooperate with audits (including through independent third-party auditors).

### Enforcement

The Nebraska Attorney General has **exclusive** enforcement authority. There is no private right of action.

Before bringing an enforcement action, the AG must provide written notice of the alleged violation and allow the controller or processor a **30-day cure period**. Unlike many other state privacy laws (including New Jersey's, which sunsets its cure period after 18 months), Nebraska's cure period is **permanent** — it does not sunset. If a controller cures the violation and provides a written statement with supporting documentation, no penalty is imposed. If the violation is not cured, or if the controller subsequently breaches the written statement, the AG may seek **up to $7,500 per violation**.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Industries and Organizations

The NDPA's lack of consumer-volume or revenue thresholds substantially broadens the universe of covered entities compared to Virginia- and Connecticut-model laws. The following categories of organizations should carefully evaluate coverage:

- **Mid-market technology companies**: Any SaaS, adtech, or data broker operating nationally that handles Nebraska residents' data and exceeds the SBA small business size standard for its industry is potentially covered — regardless of whether Nebraska accounts for a significant share of its business.
- **Retailers and e-commerce operators**: Companies running targeted advertising programs (behavioral advertising, retargeting) must provide opt-out mechanisms accessible to Nebraska residents.
- **Data brokers**: The "sale of personal data" definition — which covers exchange of data for monetary or other valuable consideration — means data brokers are among the most directly targeted by the NDPA.
- **HR and workforce management vendors**: The employment context exemption provides some relief for internal HR processing, but vendors processing consumer data on behalf of employers are not categorically exempt.

Industries that are largely unaffected include financial institutions subject to GLBA, HIPAA-covered healthcare entities, nonprofits, and higher education institutions — all of which benefit from entity-level exemptions.

### Compliance Timeline

| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| LB 1074 signed by Governor | April 17, 2024 |
| NDPA effective date | **January 1, 2025** |
| Ongoing cure period | 30 days (permanent, no sunset) |

Organizations that were not already compliant with comparable state laws (Texas, Virginia, Colorado) had approximately eight months from enactment to achieve compliance before the January 1, 2025 effective date. As of the date of this report, the NDPA is in effect.

### Enforcement Outlook

As a new law, no enforcement actions under the NDPA have been publicly reported as of April 2024. The permanent cure period is relatively business-friendly compared to Maryland's MODPA (which also launched in 2024 with more aggressive provisions) and New Jersey's NJDPA (which sunsets its cure period). The AG's enforcement focus is likely to track patterns seen in Texas and Virginia: initial emphasis on data brokers and high-volume personal data sale activity, then broadening to advertising-tech practices.

## Action Items

- **Assess NDPA applicability now**: Any entity not already covered by a comparable state privacy law should determine whether it exceeds the SBA small business threshold for its industry and whether it processes Nebraska residents' personal data.
- **Audit targeted advertising and data sale practices**: These are the two highest-risk processing categories under the NDPA. Ensure opt-out mechanisms are available and functional for Nebraska residents.
- **Update privacy notices**: Confirm disclosures include all NDPA-required elements (data categories, processing purposes, consumer rights, third-party sharing).
- **Obtain opt-in consent for sensitive data**: If processing sensitive data categories (health, biometric, geolocation, children's data, etc.), confirm affirmative consent workflows are in place.
- **Conduct and document DPIAs**: For targeted advertising, data sales, profiling, and sensitive data processing. Existing GDPR or other-state DPIA documentation may be adapted.
- **Review and update processor agreements**: Ensure data processing agreements with vendors include NDPA-required provisions per § 87-1115.
- **Establish consumer rights request handling**: Implement a process to respond to access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out requests within 45 days.
- **Monitor AG enforcement activity**: Track enforcement communications from the Nebraska AG's office, particularly during the first year of the law's operation.
