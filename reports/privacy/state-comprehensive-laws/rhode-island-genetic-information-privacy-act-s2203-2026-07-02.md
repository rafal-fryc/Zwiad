# Rhode Island Enacts Genetic Information Privacy Act (S 2203)

**Jurisdiction:** Rhode Island | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 2, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Rhode Island Governor Daniel J. McKee signed [S 2203](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText26/SenateText26/S2203.pdf), the Genetic Information Privacy Act, into law on June 19, 2026, following unanimous or near-unanimous passage in both chambers of the General Assembly ([Rhode Island Current](https://rhodeislandcurrent.com/2026/05/05/genetic-privacy-legislation-passes-rhode-island-senate-after-clearing-house/); [Inside Privacy / Covington & Burling](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/); [LegiScan bill history](https://legiscan.com/RI/bill/S2203/2026)). <!-- verified --> The law imposes express-consent, transparency, security, and deletion obligations on direct-to-consumer (DTC) genetic testing companies, making Rhode Island the fifth state to enact genetic-specific privacy legislation in 2026 and the first New England state to do so ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/07/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified --> This is a distinct, narrower statute from Rhode Island's existing comprehensive privacy law, the Rhode Island Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (RIDTPPA). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- **Bill and enactment:** S 2203, "An Act Relating to Commercial Law -- General Regulatory Provisions -- Genetic Information Privacy Act," was sponsored by Sen. Samuel D. Zurier (D-Providence) with a companion bill (H 7639) sponsored by Rep. Edith Ajello (D-Providence) ([Rhode Island Current](https://rhodeislandcurrent.com/2026/05/05/genetic-privacy-legislation-passes-rhode-island-senate-after-clearing-house/); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- **Legislative vote (corrected):** The companion House bill, H 7639, passed the House 68-0 on April 28, 2026. The Senate bill that was ultimately enacted, S 2203, was held for further study by the Senate Commerce Committee on April 14, 2026 (7-0), then passed the full Senate 37-0 (37-0-1-0) on May 5, 2026, and subsequently passed the House **in concurrence** 69-0-6-0 on June 8, 2026 -- a vote not reported by the May 5 Rhode Island Current article because it postdated that story. Gov. McKee signed S 2203 into law on June 19, 2026 ([Rhode Island Current](https://rhodeislandcurrent.com/2026/05/05/genetic-privacy-legislation-passes-rhode-island-senate-after-clearing-house/); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/); [LegiScan bill history for S2203](https://legiscan.com/RI/bill/S2203/2026)). <!-- verified: round-2 independent WebSearch confirms the June 8, 2026 House concurrence vote of 69-0-6-0 and the corrected sequence (H7639 House 68-0 April 28; S2203 Senate 37-0-1 May 5; S2203 House concurrence 69-0-6 June 8; signed June 19) -->
- **Scope:** The Act regulates direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies, requiring them to provide clear notice of, and obtain a consumer's separate express consent for, the collection, use, retention, and disclosure of genetic data -- including distinct consent for sample storage, marketing use, and third-party data sharing ([Providence Business News](https://pbn.com/protection-of-consumers-genetic-information-is-focus-of-new-r-i-law/); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- **Consumer rights:** Consumers may request deletion of their genetic data and destruction of their biological samples, and companies must close consumer accounts without imposing unreasonable barriers ([Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- **Security obligations:** Covered companies and any third parties that receive genetic data must implement reasonable security measures to protect it and must maintain internal policies and procedures to ensure compliance ([Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- **Enforcement and penalties:** The law is enforced exclusively by the Rhode Island Attorney General -- there is no private right of action. Civil penalties reach up to $1,000 per negligent violation and between $1,000 and $10,000 per willful violation, with penalties directed to the affected consumer ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/07/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified -->
- **Model and context (corrected):** The Act is modeled on California's 2021 Genetic Information Privacy Act and follows the March 2025 Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing of 23andMe (filed March 23, 2025 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Eastern District of Missouri), which heightened legislative concern about genetic-data custodianship in the event of a company's insolvency or sale ([Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/07/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/03/24/23andme-files-for-bankruptcy-anne-wojcicki-steps-down-as-ceo.html); [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2025/march/23andmes-bankruptcy-doesnt-mean-genetic-data-will-be-improperly-disclosed/)). Rhode Island joins at least 13 other states with similar DTC genetic-testing privacy statutes ([Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/health-privacy/rhode-island-enacts-genetic-privacy-law/)). <!-- verified: round-2 independent WebSearch (CNBC, GlobeNewswire, CNN, ABC News) confirms 23andMe's Chapter 11 filing on March 23, 2025 in the Eastern District of Missouri -->
- **Distinct from RIDTPPA:** S 2203 is separate from Rhode Island's existing comprehensive consumer privacy statute, the [Rhode Island Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act](reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/rhode-island-ridtppa-h7787-2024-08-15.md), which already treats genetic data as "sensitive data" requiring opt-in consent under general processing rules; S 2203 creates DTC-testing-specific obligations layered on top of that baseline. <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- DTC genetic testing companies serving Rhode Island consumers should map their current consent flows against S 2203's separate express-consent requirements for collection, storage, marketing use, and third-party sharing.
- Effective date: secondary bill-tracking sources (aggregated bill-tracking data corroborating the LegiScan record) indicate S 2203 takes effect upon passage -- i.e., effectively June 19, 2026, the date Gov. McKee signed the bill. This is consistent with typical Rhode Island practice for this bill type but has not been directly confirmed against the enrolled bill text's effective-date section, since automated fetch of the official PDF was not available during this research session. Confirm against the enrolled text at [webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText26/SenateText26/S2203.pdf](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText26/SenateText26/S2203.pdf) or the Rhode Island Secretary of State's public laws database before relying on this date for compliance deadlines. <!-- verified: round-2 independent WebSearch of aggregated bill-tracking sources (BillTrack50, TrackBill, dataguidance) independently corroborates that S 2203 "takes effect upon passage"; direct confirmation against the enrolled PDF text is still recommended and remains appropriately flagged -->
- Update data-subject-request procedures to accommodate genetic-data deletion and biological-sample destruction requests, and review account-closure workflows for unnecessary friction.
- Assess vendor and downstream-recipient contracts to ensure "reasonable security measure" obligations flow through to any third party receiving genetic data.
- Given exclusive AG enforcement and no private right of action, prioritize proactive compliance documentation (policies/procedures) that would support a defense in an AG inquiry.
- Monitor for parallel amendments to the RIDTPPA that could harmonize or conflict with S 2203's DTC-specific consent regime.
