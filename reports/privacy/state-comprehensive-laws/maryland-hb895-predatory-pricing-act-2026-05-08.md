# Maryland Becomes First State to Ban Surveillance Pricing for Food Retailers

**Jurisdiction:** Maryland | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-05-08

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 28, 2026, Maryland Governor Wes Moore signed [House Bill 895, the Protection From Predatory Pricing Act](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/HB0895?ys=2026RS), making Maryland the first state in the nation to prohibit personalized dynamic pricing — often called "surveillance pricing" — in the food retail sector. The law takes effect October 1, 2026, and bars large grocery retailers and third-party delivery platforms from using consumers' personal data to charge individualized higher prices for the same goods. The law carries civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation and is seen by legal observers as a potential template for broader legislative action against AI-driven personalized pricing across industries.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Governor Wes Moore signed HB 895 on April 28, 2026, per the [Maryland Governor's Office press release](https://governor.maryland.gov/news/press/pages/Governor-Moore-Signs-Legislation-to-Protect-Marylanders%E2%80%99-Pocketbooks-in-Grocery-Stores,-Safeguard-Voting-Rights,-and-Streng.aspx). Maryland is the first state to enact a ban of this type.
- The law defines **"dynamic pricing"** as "the discriminatory practice of offering or setting a personalized price for a good or service that is specific to a consumer based on the consumer's personal data," per [Greenberg Traurig analysis](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/5/maryland-enacts-food-sector-personalized-pricing-law).
- A **"food retailer"** is defined narrowly as a merchant operating an establishment with at least 15,000 square feet that sells food exempt from Maryland sales and use tax — targeting large grocery chains while excluding restaurants, specialty food stores, and smaller grocers, per [Morgan Lewis analysis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/maryland-enacts-hb-895-becoming-first-state-to-restrict-personalized-pricing-in-the-food-sector).
- **Third-party food delivery service providers** that deliver tax-exempt food are also covered under the Act's prohibitions.
- The law also prohibits using **protected class data** (race, religion, and other protected characteristics) to deny accommodations or advantages in food sales.
- **Exemptions** are substantial and include: promotional and loyalty program discounts, price differences based on objective shipping/location costs, supply-and-demand pricing by geography, membership/subscription pricing any consumer may voluntarily join, and price corrections for errors or system outages, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/maryland-enacts-first-of-its-kind-ban-on-surveillance-pricing-for-grocery-sales).
- **Enforcement** rests exclusively with the Division of Consumer Protection within the Maryland Attorney General's Office. There is **no private right of action**.
- Civil penalties reach **$10,000 per violation**, escalating to **$25,000 per violation** for repeat offenders. A 45-day cure period applies, per [National Law Review coverage](https://natlawreview.com/article/maryland-enacts-first-its-kind-ban-surveillance-pricing-grocery-sales).
- **Effective date: October 1, 2026.**
- The official enrolled bill text is available at [Maryland General Assembly HB 895 Third Reader](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/bills/hb/hb0895t.pdf).

## Broader Industry Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

Legal observers have flagged HB 895 as a potential warning shot for industries beyond food retail that rely heavily on AI-driven personalized pricing. [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/05/05/maryland-targets-surveillance-pricing-is-it-a-warning-shot-for-ai-driven-pricing-across-industries-including-consumer-financial-services/) notes that consumer financial services — which relies on personalized pricing more than almost any other sector — is a likely future battleground. At the federal level, the FTC investigated surveillance pricing technology in 2024, sending orders to eight companies including Mastercard, Revionics, Bloomreach, JPMorgan Chase, Task Software, PROS, Accenture, and McKinsey.

Several other states have introduced or are advancing analogous bills: California (AB-2564), Colorado (HB26-1210), Illinois (SB-2255), New Jersey (SB-3612), and New York (SB-S8623), per [MultiState tracking](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/30/maryland-becomes-first-state-to-ban-surveillance-pricing-on-some-food-products). Commentators note Maryland's law is narrower than some proposed alternatives — EPIC, which filed testimony supporting HB 895, has described the law as an important first step while calling for broader coverage, per [EPIC's bill page](https://epic.org/documents/maryland-h-b-895-protection-from-predatory-pricing-act/).

## Action Items

- **Food retailers and delivery platforms operating in Maryland:** Audit pricing algorithms and data practices by **October 1, 2026**. Determine whether any system uses consumer personal data to set individualized prices for goods covered by the Act.
- **Technology vendors providing dynamic pricing tools:** Assess whether client contracts and product configurations would cause client violations of HB 895 after October 1, 2026.
- **Compliance and legal teams in adjacent sectors (financial services, insurance, e-commerce):** Begin monitoring legislative activity in California, Colorado, Illinois, New Jersey, and New York for analogous bills that may extend similar prohibitions to non-food sectors.
- **Legal counsel:** Note the absence of a private right of action — all enforcement risk flows through the Maryland AG's Consumer Protection Division. Design compliance programs accordingly.
- **Loyalty program operators:** Review whether loyalty or membership pricing qualifies under the "voluntary enrollment" exemption. Programs must be genuinely open to any consumer to qualify.
