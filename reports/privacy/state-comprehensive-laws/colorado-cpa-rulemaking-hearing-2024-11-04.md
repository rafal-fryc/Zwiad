# Colorado AG Announces Public Hearing on Proposed CPA Regulations Covering Biometric Data, Minors' Privacy, and Opinion Letters

**Jurisdiction:** Colorado | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** November 4, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On October 30, 2024, the Colorado Attorney General announced a public rulemaking hearing scheduled for November 7, 2024, to receive feedback on proposed amendments to the [Colorado Privacy Act (CPA) Rules (4 CCR 904-3)](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/). The proposed amendments address three distinct topics mandated by recent legislation: (1) implementing biometric data protections under [House Bill 24-1130](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1130); (2) establishing heightened online privacy protections for minors under [Senate Bill 24-041](http://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-041); and (3) creating a formal Opinion Letters and Interpretive Guidance process enabling businesses to obtain binding guidance from the AG with a good-faith reliance defense. The hearing was open both in-person and virtually, closing a public comment period that ran from September 25 through November 7, 2024. The rulemaking subsequently concluded with final rule adoption on December 5, 2024, with most provisions effective January 30, 2025.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The Colorado Privacy Act

Colorado enacted the CPA via [Senate Bill 21-190](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/), signed July 7, 2021 and effective July 1, 2023. The CPA is a Virginia-model omnibus consumer privacy statute applying to controllers of personal data of 100,000 or more Colorado consumers annually, or 25,000 or more consumers when the controller derives revenue or receives a discount on the price of goods or services from the sale of personal data. It grants consumers rights of access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising, data sale, and profiling.

The original CPA Rules were first adopted March 15, 2023, taking effect July 1, 2023. They addressed general controller and processor obligations, data protection assessments, consumer rights request processes, and the universal opt-out mechanism (UOOM) requirement — under which the Global Privacy Control (GPC) browser signal became the recognized standard.

### 2024 Legislative Amendments Triggering Rulemaking

Two bills signed by Governor Jared Polis on May 31, 2024 required implementing rulemaking:

- **[HB 24-1130](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1130)** — Privacy of Biometric Identifiers and Data: Amended the CPA to impose new requirements on controllers that process biometric data, including written policies, consent obligations, and retention/deletion schedules. Effective July 1, 2025.
- **[SB 24-041](http://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-041)** — Privacy Protections for Children's Online Data: Extended CPA protections for minors (under 18), prohibiting certain high-risk data processing and targeted advertising directed at minors without consent. Effective October 1, 2025.

The AG's office published proposed draft amendments to implement these bills on September 13, 2024, and the comment period ran from September 25 through November 7, 2024, the same day as the public hearing.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Proposed Amendment 1: Opinion Letters and Interpretive Guidance Process

The proposed rules establish a two-tier formal guidance system that had been mandated by the original CPA legislation but not yet implemented:

- **Opinion Letters**: Formal written AG opinions applying the CPA to a specific, prospective factual situation described by the requesting entity. Requests must concern activities the requestor will undertake contingent on the AG's guidance. The AG will not independently investigate the underlying facts but will issue written positions on how the CPA applies to the described conduct. A business that relies in good faith on an Opinion Letter gains a defense against enforcement actions arising from the described conduct. Proposed amendments also included a modification allowing the AG to specify whether other entities (beyond the requester) may rely on the opinion letter.
- **Interpretive Guidance**: Broader statements that "call attention" to well-established CPA interpretations or principles without applying them to a specific factual situation. These serve as general compliance resources rather than fact-specific opinions.

This mechanism was particularly significant in context: the CPA's 60-day cure period — allowing businesses to remediate violations after receiving AG notice — was set to expire by statute on January 1, 2025. The Opinion Letter process offered businesses a proactive compliance pathway just as the cure period was ending.

### Proposed Amendment 2: Biometric Data Protections (HB 24-1130 Implementation)

The proposed rules define the requirements for the mandatory "biometric identifier notice" and operationalize HB 24-1130's obligations. Key proposed requirements included:

- The notice must be "reasonably accessible" to consumers and written in plain language
- The notice must describe: the specific biometric identifiers being collected; the purpose of collection; the length of the retention period; and any third-party disclosures
- Controllers must adopt a written biometric data policy covering: a retention schedule; a data security incident response plan; and deletion guidelines requiring deletion at the earliest of (a) satisfaction of the initial collection purpose, (b) 24 months after the individual's last interaction, or (c) 45 days after the controller determines storage is no longer necessary
- **Employer-specific provisions**: Employers must obtain affirmative consent from employees and prospective employees before collecting biometric identifiers, with a narrow exception permitting employers to condition employment on biometric collection — without prior consent — when identifiers are used solely for access control to physical locations, attendance monitoring, or workplace safety and security

Notably, unlike [Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA)](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004), HB 24-1130 does not create a private right of action — enforcement rests exclusively with the AG and district attorneys. The proposed rules reinforced this framework.

### Proposed Amendment 3: Minors' Online Privacy (SB 24-041 Implementation)

The proposed rules implementing SB 24-041 established definitional clarity and compliance guidance for the new minor-specific CPA obligations:

- **Definitional framework**: "Child" means an individual under 13; "minor" means any consumer under 18
- **Scope of application**: Controllers offering online services, products, or features to consumers whom they actually know or willfully disregard are minors must exercise reasonable care to avoid "heightened risk of harm"
- **Data protection assessments**: Required for any online service, product, or feature directed at minors that poses a heightened risk of harm
- **Consent thresholds**: Children under 13 require parental or guardian consent. Minors aged 13–17 may provide their own consent. Without applicable consent, controllers are prohibited from:
  - Processing minor personal data for targeted advertising or data sales
  - Profiling minors in furtherance of decisions with legally significant effects
  - Using system design features that significantly increase or extend a minor's use of the service (addictive design prohibition)
  - Collecting precise geolocation data of minors, except when strictly necessary to provide the service

### Public Comment Process

The written comment period ran from September 25 to November 7, 2024. The November 7 public hearing provided the final opportunity for stakeholders to provide oral testimony on the proposed amendments. The Department of Law accepted both in-person testimony at its offices and remote participation via broadcast to maximize stakeholder access.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected

- **All CPA-covered businesses** needed to monitor the rulemaking closely as the hearing represented the final substantive input opportunity before rule adoption
- **Businesses processing biometric identifiers** (facial recognition, fingerprint scanners, voice prints) faced the clearest near-term obligation to prepare for written biometric policies and consent framework changes ahead of the July 1, 2025 effective date
- **Operators of digital products and services with minor users** needed to assess applicability and begin planning consent infrastructure, data protection assessments, and design audits to meet the October 1, 2025 deadline
- **Any CPA-regulated business seeking regulatory clarity** gained a direct channel through the forthcoming Opinion Letter process — a particularly valuable tool given the imminent expiration of the cure period

## Action Items

- Review the [full proposed draft amendments](https://coag.gov/colorado-privacy-act-rulemaking/) posted by the Colorado AG to assess applicability to your organization's data processing activities
- Audit biometric data collection and processing programs to identify gaps before the July 1, 2025 effective date for HB 24-1130 compliance
- Map all online services, products, or features that may be used by Colorado minors and conduct a preliminary heightened-risk assessment ahead of the October 1, 2025 SB 24-041 effective date
- Assess whether your organization's CPA compliance posture would benefit from requesting an Opinion Letter once the formal process becomes available (effective January 30, 2025)
- Ensure full CPA compliance before January 1, 2025 — the expiration of the 60-day cure period removes any remediation grace period after that date
