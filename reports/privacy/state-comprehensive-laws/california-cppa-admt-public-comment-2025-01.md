# CPPA Opens Formal Public Comment Period on ADMT, Cybersecurity Audit, and Risk Assessment Regulations

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** January 14, 2025

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On November 22, 2024, the [California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/ccpa_updates.html) opened the formal 45-day public comment period on its comprehensive proposed rulemaking package covering automated decision-making technology (ADMT), cybersecurity audits, privacy risk assessments, insurance sector applicability, and updates to existing CCPA regulations. The package — advanced to formal rulemaking by Board vote on November 8, 2024 — represents the most significant expansion of CCPA obligations since the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) amendments took effect in January 2023. An initial public hearing was held January 14, 2025; the comment deadline was subsequently extended from January 14 to February 19, 2025, in response to the California wildfires. The regulations were ultimately adopted by the CPPA Board in July 2025 and approved by the Office of Administrative Law in September 2025, with phased compliance deadlines running through 2030. Businesses that deploy AI-driven tools for hiring, lending, housing, healthcare, or other significant decisions face the most demanding new requirements.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### California's Regulatory Framework

The [California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100.), enacted in 2018 and substantially expanded by Proposition 24 (the California Privacy Rights Act) in November 2020, is the most comprehensive state privacy statute in the United States. The CPRA created the CPPA — the nation's first dedicated state privacy enforcement agency — which assumed rulemaking and enforcement authority from the California Attorney General as of January 1, 2023. Section 1798.185 of the Civil Code mandates that the CPPA adopt regulations governing, among other things, consumers' rights with respect to ADMT and requirements for risk assessments and cybersecurity audits.

### Rulemaking History (2021–2024)

The CPPA's path to formal ADMT rulemaking spanned several years. In November 2023, the Agency [published a draft regulatory framework](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2023/20231127.html) establishing consumers' rights to opt out of and access information about ADMT. The CPPA Board reviewed — but declined to advance — a 200-plus page draft package at its July 16, 2024 Board meeting, directing staff to refine the package and complete an economic analysis. Between May and July 2024, the Agency held three statewide [pre-rulemaking stakeholder sessions](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20240424.html) to gather public input on key definitions. On [November 8, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241108_2.html), the CPPA Board voted to advance the revised package to formal rulemaking under the California Administrative Procedure Act — triggering the statutory public notice and comment requirements.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Rulemaking Package Scope

The formal rulemaking package, [noticed on November 22, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241122.html), addresses five distinct regulatory tracks within Title 11 of the California Code of Regulations:

1. **ADMT rights** — Consumers' rights to access information about, and opt out of, a business's use of automated decision-making technology for significant decisions or profiling.
2. **Privacy risk assessments** — Mandatory assessments for businesses whose processing activities present a heightened risk to consumers, documenting the purpose, benefits, and negative impacts of data processing activities.
3. **Cybersecurity audits** — Annual audits required of larger businesses whose processing poses a significant risk to consumer privacy, with certifications submitted to the CPPA on a phased schedule.
4. **Insurance sector** — Clarifications on when insurance companies must comply with the CCPA.
5. **CCPA updates** — Technical amendments and clarifications to existing regulations.

The [full modified text of proposed regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_mod_txt_pro_reg.pdf) was published on the CPPA website at the time of notice.

### ADMT Provisions

Under the proposed ADMT regulations, "automated decision-making technology" is defined as technology that processes personal information and uses computation to "replace or substantially replace" human decision-making. A decision is not considered made by a human if the reviewer does not know how to interpret the system's output, does not affirmatively review it, or lacks the authority to override it.

"Significant decisions" — those triggering the most stringent requirements — include decisions resulting in the provision or denial of: financial or lending services, housing, insurance, education enrollment or opportunities, employment or independent contracting opportunities or compensation, healthcare services, or essential goods or services. Advertising was expressly excluded from this definition, a notable narrowing from earlier drafts.

Businesses using ADMT for significant decisions must:
- Provide a **pre-use notice** to consumers disclosing the use of ADMT, its purpose, and the type of decision being made.
- Honor **consumer opt-out rights**, with the opt-out mechanism required to be as easy to exercise as the corresponding opt-in.
- Complete a **risk assessment** evaluating potential negative impacts, including discrimination, economic or physical harm, reputational harm, and interference with consumer autonomy.

Opt-out rights were narrowed during the rulemaking process and do not apply to ADMT used for workplace or education profiling, public surveillance, or ADMT training activities.

### Cybersecurity Audit Requirements

Businesses must complete an annual cybersecurity audit when their processing of personal information imposes a "significant risk" to consumer privacy or security. Applicable businesses are those that: (1) have annual gross revenue exceeding $25 million; or (2) annually buy, sell, or share the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers; or (3) derive 50% or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information — matching the standard CCPA applicability thresholds — with additional pathways for businesses processing sensitive personal information at scale.

### Risk Assessment Requirements

Risk assessments must evaluate "negative impacts" on consumers — including discrimination, economic or physical harm, reputational harm, or interference with consumers' ability to make informed choices. Businesses must document the purpose of processing, benefits to the business and consumers, and mitigation measures.

### Public Comment Period Extension

The original comment deadline was January 14, 2025. On [January 16, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250116.html), the CPPA extended the deadline to February 19, 2025 — citing the significant challenges posed by the California wildfires — and scheduled a second public hearing for February 19, 2025. The first hearing had been held on January 14 in a hybrid Sacramento/virtual format.

### Subsequent Finalization

Following the extended comment period, the CPPA Board adopted the final regulations on July 24, 2025, and the [Office of Administrative Law approved them on September 22, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html), filing them with the Secretary of State. The regulations entered into force January 1, 2026, with staggered compliance deadlines as set forth below.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Businesses

The regulations impose new obligations on any business subject to the CCPA that:
- Uses ADMT for significant decisions (e.g., hiring platforms, credit underwriting systems, tenant screening tools, healthcare triage algorithms).
- Processes personal information in a manner that poses a significant risk to consumer privacy or security (cybersecurity audit obligation).
- Conducts processing activities with potential adverse consumer impact (risk assessment obligation).

The compliance burden falls most heavily on technology companies, financial institutions, healthcare providers, insurers, and employers that have integrated AI or algorithmic tools into customer-facing or employment decision workflows.

### Compliance Timeline

| Milestone | Deadline |
|-----------|----------|
| Regulations enter into force | January 1, 2026 |
| Risk assessment compliance begins | January 1, 2026 |
| ADMT (significant decisions) compliance begins | January 1, 2027 |
| Risk assessment attestation due (all covered businesses) | April 1, 2028 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification due (revenue > $100M) | April 1, 2028 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification due (revenue $50M–$100M) | April 1, 2029 |
| Cybersecurity audit certification due (revenue < $50M) | April 1, 2030 |

### Enforcement Outlook

The CPPA is the first dedicated state privacy enforcement agency in the US and has demonstrated willingness to pursue enforcement actions — including a [March 2026 $1.1 million action against PlayOn Sports](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/03/youth-sports-media-company-to-pay-1-1-million-fine-change-practices-over-privacy-violations/) for CCPA opt-out and notice violations. Organizations should not treat the phased deadlines as a reason to delay compliance planning, particularly for ADMT risk assessments where the compliance period begins January 1, 2026.

## Action Items

- **Immediate (by end of 2026):** Conduct a cross-functional audit (privacy, IT, HR, product, procurement, legal) to identify all systems that substantially supplement or replace human decision-making — including hiring platforms, credit scoring tools, healthcare triage systems, and customer segmentation tools.
- **Risk assessment program:** Develop a repeatable risk assessment methodology to evaluate negative consumer impacts from data processing activities; ensure assessments are documented and retained per CPPA requirements before January 1, 2026 compliance date.
- **ADMT pre-use notice:** Draft consumer-facing disclosures for each significant-decision ADMT use case; build operational workflows to route opt-out requests to relevant systems with parity in ease of use.
- **Cybersecurity audit readiness:** Determine whether annual cybersecurity audit obligations apply based on revenue thresholds; if so, engage an auditor now given phased submission deadlines beginning April 1, 2028.
- **Monitor CPPA guidance:** The Agency has indicated it will publish implementation guidance; subscribe to CPPA announcements at cppa.ca.gov for updated enforcement advisories.
- **Insurance sector compliance:** Insurance companies should assess whether the clarifications to CCPA applicability affect their current compliance posture.
