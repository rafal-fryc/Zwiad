# Alabama Personal Data Protection Act (HB 351): Compliance Playbook

**Note:** This report is a compliance playbook companion to the Alabama HB 351 legislative analysis (reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/alabama-hb351-pdpa-2026-04-12.md). For legislative background, history, and policy analysis, see that report. This document focuses on practical compliance action items.

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Alabama's House Bill 351, the [Alabama Personal Data Protection Act](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB351-enr.pdf) (APDPA), was **passed by the Alabama Legislature on April 7, 2026 and is awaiting gubernatorial signature** from Governor Kay Ivey. If signed, Alabama will become the [21st state with a comprehensive consumer privacy law](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/). The Act is slated to take effect May 1, 2027 and borrows heavily from the Virginia/Connecticut template but includes several notable departures: a lower 25,000-consumer applicability threshold, a non-sunsetting 45-day cure provision, attorney-general-only enforcement, expanded entity-level exemptions (including political committees and small businesses), and — following a Senate amendment — **no requirement to recognize universal opt-out preference signals** such as Global Privacy Control (GPC).

## Scope and Applicability [HIGH confidence]

The APDPA applies to persons who conduct business in Alabama or produce products or services targeted to Alabama residents and who, during a calendar year, either:

- Control or process the personal data of **more than 25,000 consumers**, excluding data processed solely for completing a payment transaction; or
- Derive **more than 25% of gross revenue** from the sale of personal data ([Maynard Nexsen client alert](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-alabama-legislature-passes-comprehensive-data-privacy-bill); [DLA Piper Privacy Matters](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/)).

The 25,000-consumer threshold is lower than the 100,000-consumer threshold used in most Virginia-derived state laws, expanding the Act's reach to more mid-sized businesses ([IAPP analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/alabama-set-to-add-variation-to-us-state-privacy-patchwork)).

## Consumer Rights [HIGH confidence]

Alabama consumers (defined as Alabama residents acting in an individual/household context, not employment or B2B) receive the now-standard suite of rights:

- Confirm processing and access personal data
- Correct inaccuracies
- Delete personal data
- Obtain a portable copy of data
- Opt out of targeted advertising, sales of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of solely automated decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects

Controllers must respond within 45 days, with a permitted 45-day extension ([Maynard Nexsen](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-alabama-legislature-passes-comprehensive-data-privacy-bill)).

## Controller and Processor Obligations [HIGH confidence]

Controllers must:

- Limit collection to what is adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary
- Establish and maintain reasonable administrative, technical, and physical data security practices
- Provide a clear and accessible privacy notice
- Post a clear, conspicuous link to opt out of sales and targeted advertising
- Obtain consent before processing sensitive data
- Conduct data protection assessments for high-risk processing

Processors must adhere to controller instructions and execute data processing agreements ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/)).

## Entity-Level Exemptions [HIGH confidence]

The APDPA's exemption structure is broader than many peer laws. Exempt entities include:

- State and political subdivisions
- Institutions of higher education
- Financial institutions subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (entity-level, not just data-level)
- HIPAA-covered entities
- Businesses with fewer than 500 employees that do not sell personal data
- Nonprofits with fewer than 100 employees that do not sell personal data
- Political action committees, political parties, and principal campaign committees
- Electric providers subject to NERC reliability standards

The explicit political-organization carve-out and small-business threshold are notable departures from the Virginia template ([Maynard Nexsen](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-alabama-legislature-passes-comprehensive-data-privacy-bill); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/alabama-set-to-add-variation-to-us-state-privacy-patchwork)).

## Enforcement and Penalties [HIGH confidence]

- **Exclusive enforcement:** Alabama Attorney General; no private right of action.
- **Cure period:** 45-day notice-and-cure, which **does not sunset** (unlike Connecticut and several other states whose cure provisions expire). This is one of the most business-friendly enforcement postures among state privacy laws.
- **Civil penalties:** Up to **$15,000 per violation** ([Maynard Nexsen](https://www.maynardnexsen.com/publication-alabama-legislature-passes-comprehensive-data-privacy-bill); [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/)).

## Effective Date and Signing Status [HIGH confidence]

The Act is slated to take effect **May 1, 2027**. As of the date of this report, the bill had cleared both chambers on April 7, 2026 (House 104-0; Senate 34-0) and was **passed by the legislature, awaiting gubernatorial signature**. Gubernatorial signature confirmation should be verified via the Alabama Secretary of State or the [official bill status page](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/HB351-enr.pdf) ([DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/04/u-s-alabama-becomes-21st-state-to-enact-comprehensive-privacy-law/); [GovPing](https://changeflow.com/govping/data-privacy-cybersecurity/alabama-passes-privacy-law-becomes-21st-state-2026-04-10)).

## Unique Features and Comparative Analysis [HIGH confidence]

1. **Lower consumer threshold (25,000):** Casts a wider net than most peer laws.
2. **25% revenue-from-sale trigger:** Matches Virginia/Connecticut but lower than Utah (50%).
3. **Non-sunsetting cure period:** Among the most defendant-friendly in the country.
4. **Political-organization exemption:** Explicitly carves out PACs, parties, and campaign committees — a provision not typically itemized in other state laws.
5. **Small-business carve-out by employee count:** Uncommon structure; most states use revenue/record thresholds alone.
6. **Entity-level GLBA exemption:** Broader than newer laws (e.g., California, Oregon) that use data-level exemptions.
7. **No universal opt-out preference signal (GPC/UOOM) recognition required:** A Senate amendment removed the House-passed requirement that controllers honor universal opt-out mechanisms such as Global Privacy Control. This is a meaningful departure from [Connecticut (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-515b)](https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_743dd.htm), [Colorado (C.R.S. § 6-1-1306)](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2021a_190_signed.pdf), California (CCPA/CPRA), and several other recent state laws that mandate UOOM/GPC recognition. Compliance programs already configured to honor GPC signals across multi-state operations should note that Alabama does not require — nor prohibit — such recognition ([Privacy Daily: Alabama Senators Slash Universal Opt-Out Rule](https://privacy-daily.com/news/2026/03/10/Alabama-Senators-Slash-Universal-OptOut-Rule-from-Comprehensive-Privacy-Bill-2603100063); [ComplianceHub analysis](https://compliancehub.wiki/state-privacy-bills-march-2026-alabama-kentucky-hawaii/)).
8. **No explicit mention of AI or automated decision-making beyond the standard profiling opt-out**, per [earlier reporting on the House-passed version](https://privacy-daily.com/article/2026/02/25/alabama-house-passes-comprehensive-privacy-bill-with-no-mention-of-ai-2602250016?BC=bc_679b511912c1f).

## Compliance Action Items

- **Assess applicability:** Confirm whether Alabama consumer volumes exceed 25,000 or whether 25% of gross revenue is derived from selling personal data.
- **Update privacy notices** to include Alabama-specific disclosures by May 1, 2027.
- **Extend consumer rights infrastructure** to cover Alabama residents (access, correction, deletion, portability, opt-outs).
- **Confirm opt-out mechanisms** for sale, targeted advertising, and profiling are accessible from Alabama-facing properties. Note that GPC/UOOM recognition is not required in Alabama — existing multi-state GPC handling can remain in place but need not be extended specifically for Alabama compliance.
- **Review DPAs** with processors to confirm Alabama requirements are met.
- **Evaluate exemption eligibility** if the entity is GLBA-covered, HIPAA-covered, a nonprofit, a small business, or a political organization.
- **Track gubernatorial signing** via the Alabama Legislature's bill status page to confirm effective-date trigger.
