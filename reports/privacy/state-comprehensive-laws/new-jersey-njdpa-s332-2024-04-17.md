# New Jersey Enacts Comprehensive Data Privacy Act (S332/NJDPA)

**Jurisdiction:** New Jersey | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-04-17

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On January 16, 2024, New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy signed [Senate Bill 332](https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2022/PL23/266_.HTM) into law, establishing the New Jersey Data Protection Act (NJDPA). New Jersey became the thirteenth state to enact comprehensive data privacy legislation and the first to do so in 2024. The law took effect on [January 15, 2025](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/news/562024/20240116k.shtml). The NJDPA is notable for its broad definition of sensitive data (including financial information), its requirement that controllers recognize universal opt-out mechanisms, its lack of exemptions for nonprofits and higher education institutions, and its 30-day cure period available only for the first 18 months of enforcement. The New Jersey Attorney General has exclusive enforcement authority, with civil penalties of up to $10,000 per first violation and $20,000 per subsequent violation.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The NJDPA emerged against the backdrop of a rapidly expanding patchwork of state comprehensive data privacy laws. In 2023 alone, seven states -- Delaware, Indiana, Iowa, Montana, Oregon, Tennessee, and Texas -- enacted their own comprehensive data privacy statutes, bringing the total to twelve states before New Jersey's enactment. California led the way with the CCPA in 2018 (amended by the CPRA in 2020), followed by Virginia (VCDPA, 2021), Colorado (CPA, 2021), Connecticut (CTDPA, 2022), and Utah (UCPA, 2022), among others.

New Jersey's [S332](https://www.njleg.state.nj.us/bill-search/2022/S332/bill-text?f=S0500&n=332_R6) was introduced during the 2022-2023 legislative session. The bill underwent six rounds of revision before final passage by both chambers of the legislature on January 8, 2024. Governor Murphy signed it eight days later, on January 16, 2024, [stating](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/news/562024/20240116k.shtml) that the law would "ensure that New Jersey consumers can better protect their personal data and hold businesses more accountable for the data they collect."

The NJDPA draws structural elements from the Connecticut Data Privacy Act and the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act, but incorporates several provisions that expand upon those models. It is codified at [N.J.S.A. 56:8-166.1 et seq.](https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2022/PL23/266_.HTM)

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Applicability Thresholds

The NJDPA applies to any person or entity that conducts business in New Jersey or produces products or services targeted to New Jersey residents and during a calendar year either: (1) controls or processes the personal data of at least 100,000 New Jersey consumers, excluding data processed solely to complete a payment transaction; or (2) controls or processes the personal data of at least 25,000 consumers while deriving revenue or receiving a discount on the price of goods or services from the [sale of personal data](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/new-jersey-data-protection-act-what-businesses-need-to-know).

Unlike many other state privacy laws, the NJDPA does [not exempt nonprofit organizations or institutions of higher education](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2024/01/new-jersey-passes-comprehensive-privacy-law-2024/). This is a significant departure from laws like the Virginia VCDPA and Connecticut CTDPA, which provide entity-level exemptions for nonprofits.

### Consumer Rights

The NJDPA grants New Jersey consumers the following [core rights](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-jersey-enacts-comprehensive-data-privacy-law):

- **Right to confirm and access**: Consumers may confirm whether a controller processes their personal data and access the specific data held.
- **Right to correction**: Consumers may request correction of inaccurate personal data.
- **Right to deletion**: Consumers may request deletion of personal data provided by or obtained about them.
- **Right to data portability**: Consumers may obtain a copy of their personal data in a portable, readily usable format.
- **Right to opt out**: Consumers may opt out of the processing of personal data for targeted advertising, sale of personal data, or profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

Controllers must respond to consumer requests within [45 days](https://www.ketch.com/regulatory-compliance/new-jersey-data-privacy-act-njdpa), with a possible 45-day extension if reasonably necessary.

### Sensitive Data

The NJDPA adopts a notably [broad definition of sensitive data](https://fpf.org/blog/the-garden-state-joins-the-comprehensive-privacy-grove/) that includes:

- Racial or ethnic origin
- Religious beliefs
- Mental or physical health conditions or diagnoses
- Sex life or sexual orientation
- Citizenship or immigration status
- Genetic or biometric data used for identification
- Personal data of known children under 13
- Precise geolocation data
- **Financial information** -- a category not included in most other state privacy laws

Processing of sensitive data requires [opt-in consent](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240117-new-jersey-enacts-comprehensive-privacy-law). For children ages 13-17, the NJDPA requires affirmative opt-in consent for the sale of personal data and targeted advertising. Processing of personal data of children under 13 must comply with the federal [Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)](https://www.archerlaw.com/en/news-resources/client-advisories/governor-murphy-signs-nj-legislation-protecting-consumer-data).

### Universal Opt-Out Mechanism (UOOM)

The NJDPA requires controllers to recognize [universal opt-out mechanisms](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/01/new-jersey-data-privacy-law-signed), such as the Global Privacy Control (GPC), for opting out of targeted advertising and the sale of personal data. Notably, the law prohibits UOOMs that are configured to opt-in by default unless the controller can determine the consumer affirmatively selected the opt-in setting. Controllers must process opt-out requests submitted via UOOMs within [15 days](https://www.osano.com/articles/new-jersey-data-privacy-act-njdpa) -- a significantly shorter timeframe than the 30-45 days allowed under other state laws.

### Data Protection Assessments

Controllers must conduct [data protection assessments](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/new-jersey-data-protection-act-what-businesses-need-to-know) for processing activities that present a heightened risk of harm to consumers, including:

- Targeted advertising
- Sale of personal data
- Processing of sensitive data
- Profiling that presents a reasonably foreseeable risk of unfair or deceptive treatment, financial injury, physical injury, or intrusion on the solitude or seclusion of a consumer

### Controller Obligations

Controllers subject to the NJDPA must:

- Limit data collection to what is adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary for disclosed purposes ([data minimization](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/new-jersey-adopts-a-comprehensive-data-protection-law-what-you-need-to-know-and-do-now-privacy))
- Provide a clear and accessible [privacy notice](https://www.ketch.com/regulatory-compliance/new-jersey-data-privacy-act-njdpa) disclosing categories of data collected, processing purposes, categories of third parties receiving data, consumer rights and how to exercise them, and contact information
- Establish and maintain data processing contracts with processors
- Implement reasonable data security practices

### Exemptions

The NJDPA provides [data-level exemptions](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2024/01/new-jersey-passes-comprehensive-privacy-law-2024/) for:

- Protected health information under HIPAA (but no entity-level exemption for covered entities)
- Financial data regulated under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA)
- Data covered by the Driver's Privacy Protection Act (DPPA)
- Data collected, processed, sold, or disclosed under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)

The law does not apply to individuals acting in a commercial or employment context, including job applicants and employee beneficiaries, though [employers are not fully excluded](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/new-jersey-joins-data-privacy-party-new-jersey-data-protection-act-becomes-effective-in-january-2025/) from the law's requirements for other types of personal data they process.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Who Is Affected

The NJDPA's applicability thresholds mean it primarily affects mid-to-large businesses that process data of New Jersey residents. However, the inclusion of nonprofits and higher education institutions within the law's scope expands the affected universe beyond what comparable state laws reach. New Jersey's large population (approximately 9.3 million as of 2024) and its role as a commercial hub mean that many national businesses operating online will meet the jurisdictional thresholds.

### Compliance Timeline

- **January 16, 2024**: Law signed by Governor Murphy
- **January 15, 2025**: Law took [full effect](https://www.daypitney.com/new-jersey-data-protection-act-went-into-effect-january-15-is-your-company-in-compliance)
- **First 18 months (through approximately July 2026)**: 30-day [cure period](https://www.ketch.com/regulatory-compliance/new-jersey-data-privacy-act-njdpa) available after AG notice of violation
- **After approximately July 2026**: Cure period expires; AG has full enforcement discretion

### Enforcement

The New Jersey Attorney General has [exclusive enforcement authority](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/news/562024/20240116k.shtml). There is no private right of action. Penalties include:

- Up to **$10,000 per violation** for first offenses
- Up to **$20,000 per violation** for subsequent offenses
- Injunctive relief
- Disgorgement of profits derived from violations

Violations are treated as unlawful practices under the New Jersey [Consumer Fraud Act](https://www.archerlaw.com/en/news-resources/client-advisories/governor-murphy-signs-nj-legislation-protecting-consumer-data), which provides the AG with broad enforcement tools.

### Notable Distinctions from Other State Laws

The NJDPA stands out from the broader state privacy law landscape in several respects:

1. **Financial data as sensitive data**: The inclusion of financial information in the sensitive data category is unique among comprehensive state privacy laws and triggers opt-in consent requirements for financial data processing.
2. **No nonprofit exemption**: Most state privacy laws exempt nonprofits; the NJDPA does not.
3. **15-day UOOM processing window**: The shortened timeline for processing universal opt-out signals is more demanding than comparable requirements in other states.
4. **Prohibition on default opt-in UOOMs**: The law bars controllers from using UOOMs configured to opt-in by default.

## Action Items

- **Assess applicability**: Organizations should evaluate whether they meet the NJDPA's jurisdictional and data processing thresholds, particularly if they serve New Jersey residents or operate in the state.
- **Review sensitive data handling**: Given the broad sensitive data definition (especially the inclusion of financial data), controllers should audit their data processing activities and implement opt-in consent mechanisms where required.
- **Implement UOOM recognition**: Controllers must configure systems to recognize and process universal opt-out signals (e.g., GPC) within 15 days.
- **Conduct data protection assessments**: Businesses should complete required data protection assessments for targeted advertising, sale of data, sensitive data processing, and high-risk profiling activities.
- **Update privacy notices**: Privacy policies must be updated to reflect NJDPA-compliant disclosures.
- **Nonprofits and universities**: These entities should assess their data processing activities and prepare for compliance, as they are not exempt under the NJDPA.
- **Monitor cure period expiration**: The 30-day cure period is expected to expire approximately July 2026. Organizations should ensure full compliance before that date to avoid AG enforcement without opportunity to cure.
