# Delaware General Assembly Passes HB 380, Amending the Delaware Personal Data Privacy Act

**Jurisdiction:** Delaware | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-16

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

The Delaware House passed House Bill 380 on May 21, 2026 (30-9, 2 absent), and the Delaware Senate passed it on June 16, 2026 (15-6), clearing the General Assembly on the latter date. HB 380 is a substantial amendment to the Delaware Personal Data Privacy Act (DPDPA) enacted in 2023. The bill lowers the DPDPA's applicability thresholds, adds a new no-threshold applicability category for third parties that acquire personal data from a controller, significantly expands the definition of "sensitive data," imposes new third-party due diligence and contracting obligations on controllers, and narrows several entity-level exemptions. As of this writing, HB 380 is awaiting Delaware Governor Matt Meyer's signature; industry groups including NetChoice and CCIA have formally requested a veto, while consumer advocacy groups Consumer Reports and EPIC have submitted testimony in support. If signed, the amendments take effect January 1, 2027, according to [Covington & Burling's Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/) and [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/delaware-joins-the-fun-amends-state-consumer-privacy-law/). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

- HB 380 passed the Delaware House on a vote of 30-9 (2 absent) on May 21, 2026, and passed the Delaware Senate on a vote of 15-6 on June 16, 2026, clearing the General Assembly on the latter date, per [Covington & Burling's Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/) and the [House Democrats' press release on the Griffith privacy bills](https://housedems.delaware.gov/2026/05/21/house-passes-griffith-bills-to-improve-data-privacy-for-delawareans/), which is dated May 21, 2026 and confirms that date as the House passage date. The distinct House and Senate passage dates were independently corroborated via WebSearch. <!-- verified -->
- The bill lowers the DPDPA's applicability thresholds to cover controllers/processors handling personal data of at least 10,000 Delaware consumers (down from 35,000), or that derive more than 20% of revenue from selling personal data of at least 5,000 consumers (down from 10,000), per [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/). <!-- verified -->
- HB 380 also adds a new, no-threshold applicability category: **any third party that acquires personal data from a controller becomes directly subject to the DPDPA regardless of size** — a vendor, service provider, data broker, advertising partner, or analytics provider that receives Delaware consumer data from a controller is captured with no numerical floor, per [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/delawares-privacy-law-is-about-to-expand) and corroborating WebSearch summaries of [McDermott Will & Emery's analysis](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/delaware-joins-the-fun-amends-state-consumer-privacy-law/). "Third party" is defined as any person other than the consumer, the controller, or a processor or affiliate of a processor or the controller, with a continued exemption for third parties otherwise excluded from the statute (e.g., government entities). Multiple sources describe this no-threshold trigger as unique among state comprehensive privacy laws. This provision is directly relevant to the vendor/data-broker due-diligence Action Items below. Direct WebFetch access to the underlying law-firm page was unavailable in this session; this bullet is corroborated via WebSearch summaries rather than a direct page fetch, so it is tagged MEDIUM confidence pending direct source re-fetch. <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch (Fisher Phillips, McDermott summaries); direct WebFetch unavailable in reviewer session, consistent with researcher's noted tooling limitation -->
- "Sensitive data" is expanded to include national origin, mental or physical health treatment or status (including reproductive and gender-affirming care), transgender or nonbinary treatment or status, neural data, financial account/payment card information, and government-issued identification numbers, per [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/delaware-joins-the-fun-amends-state-consumer-privacy-law/) and [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/). <!-- verified -->
- Controllers must conduct "reasonable due diligence" on third parties to whom they disclose personal data and must execute new contractual agreements governing such disclosures (e.g., sales or targeted-advertising transfers); a third party is expressly prohibited from processing personal data disclosed to it by a controller or processor without a contract meeting the DPDPA's requirements and must provide sufficient information to controllers to allow compliance assessment, per [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/). <!-- verified -->
- The bill narrows several entity-level exemptions, including limiting the financial-institution exemption to banks, insurers, and their respective affiliates (narrower than the existing GLBA-affiliate exemption), per [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/). <!-- verified -->
- The bill would give consumers a right to obtain a list of third parties to which the controller has disclosed their personal data, subject to certain exceptions, per [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/state-privacy/delaware-general-assembly-passes-hb-380-an-amendment-to-the-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act/) (the existing DPDPA already grants a right to categories of third parties; HB 380 appears to extend this toward named third parties — this distinction should be verified against enacted statutory text once available). <!-- needs-human-review: named-vs-categorical third-party distinction not independently confirmed against enacted statutory text; researcher has already flagged this appropriately -->
- As of this writing, HB 380 has not yet been signed; NetChoice submitted a [veto request letter](https://netchoice.org/netchoice-veto-request-letter-to-gov-meyer-on-delaware-hb-380-an-act-relating-to-personal-data-privacy/) to Governor Meyer stating that the bill "saddles internet businesses with the single most restrictive and Delaware-specific data regime in the country," and separately that it "abandons a balanced, multi-state framework to create a highly restrictive, state-specific regulatory outlier." The Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA) filed a similar veto request. Consumer Reports and EPIC filed testimony and a support letter urging enactment (see [Consumer Reports/EPIC support statement](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/research/consumer-reports-and-epic-support-delaware-h-b-380-delaware-personal-data-privacy-act-amendments/)). <!-- verified: both NetChoice quotes confirmed verbatim via independent WebSearch -->
- If signed, the amendments are reported to take effect January 1, 2027, per [McDermott Will & Emery](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/delaware-joins-the-fun-amends-state-consumer-privacy-law/). <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- Monitor Governor Meyer's action on HB 380 — track [House Bill 380 — Bill Detail, Delaware General Assembly](https://legis.delaware.gov/BillDetail/143105) for signature, veto, or the bill becoming law without signature.
- If signed, re-run the applicability analysis for any organization currently below the DPDPA's existing 35,000/10,000-consumer thresholds — the proposed 10,000/5,000-consumer thresholds would newly capture many mid-size and small businesses with a Delaware footprint.
- Separately assess whether the organization (or any vendor, data broker, ad-tech, or analytics partner it uses) qualifies as a "third party" that acquires personal data from a controller — this new category has no minimum consumer threshold and could apply to small vendors that would otherwise fall below the DPDPA's size thresholds.
- Inventory data covering the newly proposed sensitive-data categories (national origin, health treatment/status including reproductive and gender-affirming care, transgender/nonbinary treatment or status, neural data, financial account data, government ID numbers) and prepare to extend opt-in consent workflows to these categories if the bill is enacted.
- Review vendor and data-broker relationships now to anticipate the "reasonable due diligence" and new third-party contracting requirements, including the requirement that third parties provide sufficient information to controllers to assess DPDPA compliance; begin building a due-diligence framework for third-party data recipients.
- Reassess reliance on the financial-institution exemption — under HB 380 it would be limited to banks, insurers, and their affiliates, narrower than typical GLBA-affiliate exemptions used by fintechs and other financial-adjacent entities.
- Track the outcome of the NetChoice and CCIA veto requests, since industry pushback could affect the bill's final fate or prompt further amendment before an effective date.
- Once enacted (or vetoed), update this report and cross-reference the existing DPDPA compliance guide at `reports/privacy/state-comprehensive-laws/delaware-dpdpa-hb154-2024-05-15.md`, which will need revision to reflect the amended thresholds, no-threshold third-party category, and sensitive-data list.
