# DNI Leadership Vacuum and SAVE America Act Conditioning Trigger First-Ever FISA Section 702 Lapse

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-17

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

FISA Section 702 — the warrantless foreign intelligence surveillance authority that incidentally sweeps up large volumes of Americans' communications — lapsed for the first time in its 18-year history on June 12–13, 2026, following a sequence of events driven by the Trump administration's turbulent Director of National Intelligence (DNI) nominations. President Trump's decision to install Federal Housing Finance Agency director Bill Pulte as acting DNI — a figure with no national security experience — prompted nearly all Senate Democrats to block a short-term reauthorization extension, and the House subsequently failed a fast-track vote 198–218. After Section 702 expired, Trump announced the nomination of former U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton as permanent DNI, but then personally canceled Clayton's Senate confirmation hearing on June 17, demanding the Senate first confirm a separate U.S. Attorney nominee and pass the unrelated SAVE America Act voter ID bill. As of the date of this report, Section 702 has lapsed as a statutory matter, though surveillance operations continue under FISA Court certifications approved in March 2026 that remain valid through approximately March 2027. The lapse creates legal uncertainty for communications providers, challenges the integrity of intelligence oversight, and has reignited the long-running debate over warrant requirements for FBI searches of Americans' incidentally collected data.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Section 702: Statutory Framework and History

[Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/7888), codified at 50 U.S.C. § 1881a, was enacted as part of the FISA Amendments Act of 2008. It authorizes the Attorney General and the Director of National Intelligence to jointly certify annual programs compelling U.S. electronic communications service providers to collect the communications of non-U.S. persons reasonably believed to be located outside the United States. These certifications are approved by the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court (FISC) and authorze the NSA, FBI, CIA, and National Counterterrorism Center to conduct foreign intelligence collection without individualized court orders.

Because foreign targets communicate with Americans, large volumes of U.S.-person communications are "incidentally" collected. Federal analysts — particularly FBI agents — may query these databases using Americans' names, phone numbers, or email addresses without obtaining a warrant, a practice civil liberties advocates call "backdoor searches." Documented abuses include warrantless searches targeting a sitting U.S. senator, political donors, and over 19,000 individuals affiliated with a congressional campaign.

Congress most recently reauthorized Section 702 via the [Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act (RISAA)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/7888), signed April 20, 2024, which set a new sunset date of April 20, 2026. The RISAA imposed new FBI query training requirements and enhanced oversight of "sensitive queries" but did not require a warrant for U.S.-person queries — the central demand of privacy advocates in both parties. The RISAA also expanded the definition of "electronic communications service provider," potentially subjecting data center operators and commercial landlords to compelled assistance, a provision that drew sustained bipartisan criticism.

### The 2026 Reauthorization Cycle: Prior Stops

The 2026 reauthorization was tumultuous before the June lapse. Congress narrowly avoided a lapse in April through two short-term stopgaps:

- On April 17, 2026, Congress passed a 10-day extension after a bloc of approximately 20 House Republicans — who demanded a warrant requirement — derailed both an 18-month and a five-year reauthorization.
- On April 30, 2026, Congress passed a 45-day extension through June 12, after the Senate rejected a House three-year reauthorization bill that contained an unrelated central bank digital currency (CBDC) ban. That extension included a commitment by Senate Intelligence Committee leaders to declassify a FISC compliance opinion within 15 days.

Both extensions deferred but did not resolve the core legislative fault lines: the warrant requirement for U.S.-person queries, the RISAA expanded-provider-definition, and the data-broker loophole allowing agencies to purchase commercially available location and behavioral data without a court order. For detailed background on those earlier stopgaps, see the related reports below.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The Pulte Appointment and Democratic Revolt

The immediate trigger for the June 2026 lapse was the Trump administration's decision to name Bill Pulte — the director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency with no national security, law enforcement, or military experience — as acting Director of National Intelligence. Democrats argued this appointment violated the statutory requirement in [50 U.S.C. § 3023](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title50-section3023&num=0&edition=prelim) that the DNI possess "extensive national security expertise."

Senate Democrats cited Pulte's track record at FHFA: he had used his position to pull mortgage records to support investigations of Trump's political adversaries, including Federal Reserve Board member Lisa Cook, New York Attorney General Letitia James, and Senator Adam Schiff. Democrats argued that placing Section 702's enormous surveillance apparatus under Pulte's control represented an unacceptable risk that the authority would be turned on political opponents. The [New Democrat Coalition](https://newdemocratcoalition.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/new-dems-oppose-placing-fisa-authority-in-the-hands-of-bill-pulte) issued a formal statement opposing placing FISA authority in Pulte's hands.

Every Senate Democrat except Sen. John Fetterman (D-Pa.) voted against a motion to proceed to the House measure extending Section 702. The House then held a fast-track vote on June 11, 2026, on a short-term extension to July 2. The measure failed 198–218 — below the two-thirds threshold required under the "suspension of the rules" procedure and below a simple majority. Nineteen House Republicans voted against the bill on warrant-requirement grounds; seven House Democrats voted in favor.

On June 11–12, 2026, both chambers failed to pass any extension, and [Section 702 lapsed for the first time since 2008](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/11/fisa-section-702-expiration-pulte-trump-johnson). Following the failed votes, Trump [announced the nomination of Jay Clayton](https://thehill.com/homenews/senate/5929200-clayton-dni-trump-fisa-702/) — former chairman of the SEC and most recently U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York — as permanent DNI.

### Trump Cancels Clayton's Confirmation Hearing

The nomination of Clayton initially appeared to provide a path forward: Democrats indicated that Clayton's confirmation was a prerequisite for reauthorizing Section 702. A confirmation hearing was scheduled before the Senate Intelligence Committee for June 17, 2026.

However, on June 17, President Trump personally intervened to cancel the hearing. [Trump announced via social media](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-delays-jay-clayton-nomination-intel-director-fisa-save-america-rcna350470): "Regarding the approval of our Great Patriot, Jay Clayton, we are cancelling the Senate Hearing RE: DNI today, and will not be going forward until Jamie McDonald is approved to be U.S. Attorney." Trump also stated he would not approve FISA renewal unless his voting bill — the SAVE America Act — was attached.

Senate Intelligence Committee Chair Tom Cotton (R-Ark.) initially rebuffed Trump, saying the hearing would proceed. Cotton subsequently reversed course and announced the hearing was canceled, calling the decision "regrettable." The episode illustrated the extent to which Trump was conditioning intelligence oversight leadership not on national security grounds but on unrelated legislative and personnel priorities.

The SAVE America Act is a voter ID bill that would require documentary proof of U.S. citizenship to register to vote and photo ID to cast a ballot. Senate Majority Leader Thune stated the Senate does not have the votes to pass the Act. Some House Republicans — including Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) — also publicly criticized linking FISA renewal to the SAVE America Act as "dumb."

### The FISC Certification Continuity Mechanism

Despite the statutory lapse, Section 702 surveillance has not gone dark. The FISA Court approved annual certifications in March 2026 that authorized collection under the program. Under the transition provision accompanying Section 702's sunset, certifications and directives issued to communications providers before the statutory expiration remain valid until they reach their own expiration dates.

Because FISC certifications run for up to one year, the March 2026 certifications effectively lock in Section 702 surveillance authority through approximately March 2027 — even though the underlying statute has lapsed. As [Wiley Rein's legal analysis explained](https://www.wiley.law/wiley-connect/congress-again-approaches-deadline-for-extending-fisa-702-authorities-creating-uncertainty-for-communications-providers), communications providers operating under existing directives "may continue to receive and respond to such directives" absent statutory reauthorization. However, the legal immunity shield protecting providers for past compliance becomes murkier the longer the lapse continues, and new FISC certifications cannot be issued until Congress reauthorizes the statute.

### Privacy Advocates' Position

Civil liberties organizations have long argued that Section 702's backdoor search mechanism is a Fourth Amendment violation. The [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/victory-702-has-expired) characterized the lapse as a victory, noting that the statute expired without the clean reauthorization the government sought. The [EPIC campaign](https://epic.org/campaigns/fisa-section-702-reform-or-sunset/) had advocated for either comprehensive reforms — including a warrant requirement — or allowing Section 702 to sunset.

Senator Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) had previously noted that "sensitive" FBI warrantless searches tripled in 2025 compared to prior years, with the FBI refusing to explain the increase. Reform advocates, including a bipartisan bloc of lawmakers, have consistently argued that any reauthorization must include a warrant requirement for U.S.-person queries, with narrow exceptions for emergencies, consent, or cases where the subject is already under a valid court order.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Intelligence Community

In practical terms, the intelligence community's existing collection operations are continuing under the FISC's March 2026 certifications. The principal operational impact is that no new FISC certifications can be issued, meaning that any expansion of collection targets or new collection programs would require either a statutory reauthorization or judicial workarounds that would themselves generate legal controversy. Intelligence community officials had warned before the lapse of "fatal consequences" to operations, though the certification continuity mechanism has blunted the immediate operational impact.

### Communications Providers

[Wiley Rein's analysis](https://www.wiley.law/wiley-connect/congress-again-approaches-deadline-for-extending-fisa-702-authorities-creating-uncertainty-for-communications-providers) identifies the principal compliance concern for providers: they remain obligated to comply with existing directives, but the legal immunity protections that protect them against private lawsuits for doing so grow less certain as the lapse extends. Providers that receive new or modified directives during the lapse face the greatest uncertainty about their legal protections.

### Privacy and Civil Liberties

The lapse has reinvigorated the warrant-requirement debate. Seven Republicans in the House had previously stated they would not vote for any reauthorization without a warrant requirement, while the Trump administration has opposed any warrant requirement. The politicization of the DNI nomination process — specifically, conditioning intelligence oversight leadership on unrelated legislative priorities — has added a new dimension to the oversight debate, with surveillance advocates arguing that an unconfirmed or improperly credentialed acting DNI weakens the compliance and oversight mechanisms built into Section 702's framework.

### Data Transfers and International Implications

For organizations relying on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses for transatlantic data transfers, the Section 702 program's legal status is material: the DPF's adequacy determination by the European Commission was predicated in part on Section 702's oversight mechanisms. A prolonged statutory lapse that disrupts those mechanisms could create transfer-impact-assessment complications, though the certification continuity mechanism means day-to-day surveillance operations remain largely unchanged.

## Action Items

- **Communications providers subject to Section 702 directives:** Review existing directives. Under current FISC certifications running through approximately March 2027, existing compliance obligations remain in force. Monitor for any guidance from DOJ or ODNI on the scope of provider obligations during the lapse period. Consult [Wiley Rein's analysis](https://www.wiley.law/wiley-connect/congress-again-approaches-deadline-for-extending-fisa-702-authorities-creating-uncertainty-for-communications-providers) for the most current legal framework on provider immunity during lapses.
- **Compliance and legal teams:** Track the Clayton confirmation process and any linkage to SAVE America Act passage. The political calendar is the primary driver of reauthorization timing; a reauthorization could move rapidly once a DNI is confirmed and political conditions shift.
- **Organizations relying on EU-US data transfer frameworks:** Update transfer impact assessments to note the Section 702 statutory lapse while acknowledging that FISC certifications remain in effect through approximately March 2027. Brief EU data protection counsel on the continuity mechanism and its legal basis.
- **Privacy and government affairs teams:** Monitor whether any reauthorization bill includes a warrant requirement, a data-broker loophole closure, or a narrowing of the RISAA's expanded provider definition. These remain the primary reform demands from both sides of the aisle and are the most likely concessions in any bipartisan deal.
- **All affected organizations:** Set an alert for developments around DNI confirmation proceedings, the SAVE America Act, and any renewed FISA reauthorization negotiations. The window between now and the March 2027 FISC certification expiration is the critical period for Congress to act.
