# Approaching Audit and Certification Deadlines Under DOJ's Bulk Sensitive Data Transfer Rule

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 15, 2026

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Companies subject to the Department of Justice's Data Security Program (DSP) — commonly referred to as the "Bulk Data Transfer Rule" — face approaching deadlines to complete their first annual compliance-program certifications and independent audits under [28 C.F.R. Part 202, Subpart J](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J). The audit and data-compliance-program requirements took effect on October 6, 2025, meaning U.S. persons that engaged in "restricted transactions" involving bulk sensitive personal data or government-related data with countries of concern during the twelve months following that date must have their first annual audit and program certification completed on that anniversary schedule, i.e., by October 2026, per [Paul Hastings' analysis](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/the-approaching-audit-and-certification-deadlines-under-the-bulk-data-transfer-rule). This is the first cycle in which the DSP's due-diligence, audit, certification, and recordkeeping machinery — layered on top of the transaction-level restrictions that took effect April 8, 2025 — becomes fully operative, and the National Security Division (NSD) has signaled it will pursue enforcement, backed by IEEPA's civil and criminal penalty regime, against entities that miss these obligations. <!-- verified -->

## Background [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

### Statutory and Regulatory Origin

The DSP originates from [Executive Order 14117](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/03/01/2024-04573/preventing-access-to-americans-bulk-sensitive-personal-data-and-united-states-government-related), "Preventing Access to Americans' Bulk Sensitive Personal Data and United States Government-Related Data by Countries of Concern," signed by President Biden on February 28, 2024. <!-- verified --> The DOJ's National Security Division promulgated the implementing [final rule at 28 C.F.R. Part 202](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202), published in the [Federal Register on January 8, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-31486/preventing-access-to-us-sensitive-personal-data-and-government-related-data-by-countries-of-concern). This report is part of a broader cluster of Zwiad coverage on the DSP; a related report — [US Lawmakers Flag Critical Loopholes in DOJ Data Security Program](federal-doj-data-security-program-dsp-loopholes-2026-05-22.md) — covers a separate May 2026 development concerning gaps in the program's Government-Related Location Data List. This report addresses a distinct, later-arising compliance milestone: the maturation of the audit and certification cycle.

### Phased Compliance Timeline

The DSP's substantive prohibitions and restrictions on data transactions with China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, and Venezuela (and covered persons connected to them) took effect on April 8, 2025, following a 90-day implementation period. DOJ NSD then applied a good-faith enforcement grace period through July 8, 2025, after which the agency stated it would "expect NSD to pursue appropriate enforcement with respect to any violations," per [Proskauer's summary of the enforcement timeline](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/doj-begins-enforcement-of-new-data-security-program). A second tranche of obligations — the requirement to implement a written data compliance program, conduct due diligence, and prepare for independent audits — carried a delayed effective date of October 6, 2025, as confirmed by [Venable's analysis of the October 6 deadlines](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/10/october-6-new-compliance-deadlines-dojs-bulk) and [Greenberg Traurig's summary of the incoming October 2025 requirements](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/10/incoming-deadlines-and-requirements-for-dojs-data-security-program-on-oct-6-2025). It is this second tranche that now produces the "approaching" July 2026 deadlines: because the audit must cover the preceding twelve months and be completed once each calendar year in which restricted transactions occurred, the first full-cycle audits and certifications for companies that began restricted transactions in October 2025 come due around the one-year mark, i.e., by October 2026. <!-- verified -->

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

### Data Compliance Program and Annual Certification Requirement

Under [28 C.F.R. § 202.1001](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J/section-202.1001), any U.S. person engaging in a restricted transaction was required, no later than October 6, 2025, to "develop and implement a data compliance program" that includes risk-based procedures for verifying data flows involved in restricted transactions — including auditable logging of the types and volumes of government-related data or bulk U.S. sensitive personal data involved and the identity of transaction parties. The regulation requires that the data compliance program include a written policy describing the program, which must be "annually certified by an officer, executive, or other employee responsible for compliance." The text of Subpart J is available in full at [eCFR Part 202 Subpart J](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J). <!-- verified -->

### Independent Audit Requirement

[28 C.F.R. § 202.1002](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J/section-202.1002) requires that U.S. persons who, on or after October 6, 2025, engage in any restricted transaction under § 202.401 conduct an audit "once for each calendar year" in which restricted transactions occurred, with each audit covering the preceding twelve months. The auditor must be independent — the regulation specifically bars the auditor from being a covered person or a country of concern — and must be "qualified and competent to examine, verify, and attest to" the company's compliance with, and the effectiveness of, the DSP's Security Requirements. Independence criteria vary based on factors such as the company's internal corporate structure and the auditor's reporting line to senior leadership or the board. Once complete, the auditor must prepare and submit a written report to the audited company within 60 days of completing the audit. The audit must comprehensively examine the restricted transactions themselves, the data compliance program and its implementation, required recordkeeping, and compliance with the [CISA Security Requirements for Restricted Transactions](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/EO-14117-security-requirements). <!-- verified -->


### Reporting Obligations Running in Parallel

Separately, U.S. persons engaged in restricted transactions involving cloud-computing services in which 20% or more equity is owned, directly or indirectly, by a country of concern or covered person must file annual reports with NSD; the first such report was due March 1, 2026, and had to describe the data transaction as of December 31 of the preceding year — including the type and volume of sensitive data, transfer methods, involved parties and their locations, and supporting documentation, per the [DOJ NSD Compliance Guide](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1396356/dl) and secondary reporting corroborating this deadline. The March 2026 reporting deadline is a distinct, already-passed obligation and is noted here for completeness; the audit and certification deadlines discussed in this report are the ones now "approaching" as of mid-2026. <!-- needs-human-review: DOJ NSD Compliance Guide and Greenberg Traurig/Taft Law sources not independently re-fetched due to WebFetch tool unavailability in this review session; content is consistent with other independently-confirmed sources but should be spot-checked when tooling is available -->

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [DISPUTED]

### Who Is Covered

The DSP's restricted-transaction and audit requirements reach any U.S. person — a broadly defined term covering individuals and entities organized under U.S. law, including their foreign subsidiaries and branches — that engages in vendor agreements, employment agreements, or investment agreements involving bulk U.S. sensitive personal data or government-related data with a country of concern (China, including Hong Kong and Macau, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, or Venezuela) or a covered person connected to one of those jurisdictions. This sweeps in technology companies, cloud service providers, data brokers, financial institutions, healthcare and life sciences organizations, and universities with international data flows or personnel/vendor relationships touching the listed countries.

### Enforcement Exposure

The DSP is enforced by NSD under the authority of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). Civil penalties for unlawful acts under IEEPA reach the greater of $368,136 or twice the value of each violative transaction, while willful violations carry criminal exposure of up to 20 years' imprisonment and a $1,000,000 fine, per multiple corroborating law firm summaries including [Foley & Lardner's analysis of DOJ enforcement authority](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/07/doj-enforcement-under-national-security-divisions-data-security-program/) and [Taft Law's summary of full enforcement beginning July 8](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/doj-data-security-program-full-enforcement-began-july-8/). <!-- disputed: $368,136 matches the cited Foley & Lardner source but independent verification indicates IEEPA civil penalties are adjusted annually for inflation; the 2025 adjustment set the maximum at $377,700, and a further Jan. 14, 2026 inflation-adjustment cycle likely raised it further, so the true current maximum as of this report's publication may exceed the figure stated --> NSD has stated that enforcement actions may target failures to comply with due diligence, security, audit, or recordkeeping requirements — meaning a missed or deficient audit or certification is itself a distinct compliance failure that can trigger liability, independent of any substantive violation involving an actual prohibited data transfer.

### Compliance Timeline Summary

- **April 8, 2025** — DSP's core prohibited- and restricted-transaction provisions took effect.
- **July 8, 2025** — End of DOJ's good-faith enforcement grace period; full enforcement authority in effect.
- **October 6, 2025** — Data compliance program, due diligence, and audit obligations became effective for restricted transactions.
- **March 1, 2026** — First annual report deadline for covered cloud-computing restricted transactions (20%+ country-of-concern ownership).
- **Around October 2026** — First full-cycle annual audits (covering the preceding 12 months) and data-compliance-program certifications come due for companies engaging in restricted transactions since October 6, 2025. <!-- verified -->

Because the audit report must be delivered to the company within 60 days of audit completion, and the audit itself examines a full 12-month look-back period, companies should treat mid-to-late 2026 as the operative window to complete both the audit engagement and the annual certification — hence the "approaching" framing in current law firm commentary. Official DOJ guidance does not appear to specify a single fixed calendar date (e.g., "October 6" each year) for every covered entity, since the triggering date depends on when each company's restricted transactions began; the eCFR text of § 202.1002 ties the cadence to "once for each calendar year" rather than a uniform fixed date, and readers should confirm entity-specific timing against [28 C.F.R. § 202.1002](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J/section-202.1002) directly.

## Action Items

- **Companies engaging in restricted transactions:** Confirm the date restricted transactions began under the DSP and calendar the corresponding 12-month audit look-back period and the associated certification deadline, generally falling around the October 2026 one-year mark for entities that began restricted transactions at the October 6, 2025 compliance date.
- **Compliance and legal teams:** Engage an independent auditor now — the audit report must be delivered within 60 days of audit completion, so engagement should begin with enough lead time to meet the annual deadline. Confirm the auditor meets the independence criteria in [28 C.F.R. § 202.1002](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J/section-202.1002).
- **Responsible compliance officers:** Prepare the annual written certification of the data compliance program required under [28 C.F.R. § 202.1001](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-J/section-202.1001), ensuring the underlying policy documentation reflects actual practice.
- **Companies with cloud-computing restricted transactions involving 20%+ country-of-concern ownership:** Confirm the March 1, 2026 annual reporting deadline was met and calendar the next annual reporting cycle.
- **Government affairs and compliance teams:** Monitor DOJ NSD for any additional guidance on audit/certification timing, and track related developments in the DSP cluster, including the pending Government-Related Location Data List gaps discussed in [Zwiad's May 2026 report](federal-doj-data-security-program-dsp-loopholes-2026-05-22.md).
