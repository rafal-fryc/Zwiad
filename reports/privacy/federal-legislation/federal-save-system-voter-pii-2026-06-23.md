# Court Blocks SAVE System Used to Aggregate Voter PII, Rules Administration Violated Privacy Rights

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-23

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On June 22, 2026, U.S. District Judge Sparkle L. Sooknanan (D.D.C.) granted summary judgment against the Trump administration's overhaul of the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) system, ruling that the consolidation of citizenship, Social Security, and voter-related personal data violated the Social Security Act, the Privacy Act of 1974, and the Administrative Procedure Act (APA). The court found the federal government "knowingly trampled on the privacy rights of American citizens in a manner that threatens the sacred right to vote" and ordered DHS to dismantle the upgraded database and restore it to its pre-modification status. Two days later, the Sixth Circuit became the first appellate court to weigh in on related litigation, ruling 2-1 that the DOJ cannot compel Michigan to produce non-public voter registration data including Social Security numbers — dealing the administration its first appeals-court loss across more than nine failed district-court attempts.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **SAVE case name and docket:** *League of Women Voters v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security*, No. 1:25-cv-03501 (D.D.C., filed Sept. 30, 2025). Plaintiffs include the League of Women Voters, EPIC, and five individual John Does, per [League of Women Voters](https://www.lwv.org/legal-center/league-women-voters-v-department-homeland-security) and [Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/cases-actions/challenging-consolidation-and-distribution-federal-citizenship-data-voter-purges).

- **Executive order at issue:** President Trump's [March 25, 2025 executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/preserving-and-protecting-the-integrity-of-american-elections/) — "Preserving and Protecting the Integrity of American Elections" (EO 14248) — directed DHS and SSA to upgrade the SAVE system into a centralized citizenship-verification database. Agencies were found to have implemented the order while "scrambling" and "haphazardly" combining data they knew was unreliable, per [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/court-rules-save-database-illegal-orders-dismantling/).

- **Scale of the database:** The modified SAVE system pulled together immigration-status records covering approximately 26.5 million people and then expanded to encompass citizenship and Social Security data for most Americans, per [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/22/nx-s1-5866719/save-voter-data-trump-judge-unlawful).

- **Legal violations found:** Judge Sooknanan's 75-page decision held that the SAVE overhaul violated (1) the Social Security Act's prohibition on disclosing Social Security numbers; (2) substantive and procedural protections under the Privacy Act of 1974, including interagency disclosure restrictions and notice-and-comment requirements; and (3) the APA's prohibition on arbitrary, capricious, and procedurally deficient agency action, per [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/court-rules-save-database-illegal-orders-dismantling/) and [The Hill](https://thehill.com/regulation/court-battles/5934681-trump-database-blocked-judge/).

- **Real-world harm:** Texas used the SAVE database to review more than 18 million voter registrations, flagging 2,724 people as potential noncitizens. In Travis County alone, 97 individuals were flagged, and officials confirmed at least 11 of those were in fact U.S. citizens, per [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/in-blow-to-trump-federal-judge-blocks-dhs-from-using-citizenship-database-to-purge-voters/).

- **Remedy ordered:** The court vacated the 2025 modifications to SAVE and the associated System of Records Notices (SORNs), and ordered DHS to return the system to its pre-modification status, per [Votebeat](https://www.votebeat.org/national/2026/06/22/judge-rules-against-trump-overhaul-save-database-noncitizen-voters/).

- **Sixth Circuit Michigan ruling (June 24, 2026):** In a 2-1 decision authored by Circuit Judge Andre Mathis (a Biden appointee, confirmed September 8, 2022) and joined by Judge R. Guy Cole Jr. (a Clinton appointee), the Sixth Circuit affirmed dismissal of the DOJ's suit against Michigan, holding that Michigan's voter file — which it creates and maintains itself — is not a "record" the DOJ can demand under Title III of the Civil Rights Act of 1960. Judge John Nalbandian (Trump appointee) dissented. The opinion is published at [26a0180p-06.pdf](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/26a0180p-06.pdf).

- **Breadth of DOJ voter-data litigation:** The DOJ has sued 30 states and the District of Columbia demanding non-public voter-registration data including Social Security and driver's license numbers. Nine federal district courts have dismissed those suits on the merits; the Sixth Circuit is the first appellate court to rule, per [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/trump-doj-suffers-first-appeals-court-loss-in-floundering-voter-roll-crusade/).

- **California, Michigan, and Oregon dismissals:** Federal district courts dismissed DOJ voter-data lawsuits against all three states. The California court additionally found that the DOJ's demands violated federal privacy laws, per [Brennan Center for Justice](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/federal-courts-reject-trump-administrations-attempts-obtain-private-voter).

## Action Items

- **Federal agencies processing voter or identity data:** Audit all interagency data-sharing arrangements involving Social Security records for compliance with 42 U.S.C. § 405(c)(2)(C) (SSA disclosure restrictions) and the Privacy Act's System of Records Notice requirements. Any sharing not authorized by a published, notice-and-comment SORN carries legal risk identical to that condemned in this ruling.

- **State election officials:** Do not rely on SAVE-derived citizenship data to initiate voter-roll maintenance procedures. The court's order vacates the 2025 SAVE modifications and associated SORNs; data generated from the upgraded system is now of uncertain legal provenance. Consult state counsel before acting on any SAVE flags.

- **Privacy compliance officers:** This ruling signals heightened judicial scrutiny of government-mandated data aggregation across agency silos. Document your organization's response to any federal demand letters seeking voter-registration or Social Security data. States are not required to produce such data under Title III of the Civil Rights Act of 1960, per the Sixth Circuit's published opinion (the DOJ did not appeal its NVRA claim).

- **Organizations subject to DOJ voter-data demand letters:** The Sixth Circuit opinion is now binding precedent in the Sixth Circuit. Organizations in other circuits should monitor DOJ appeals. Given nine district-court losses and the first appellate-court loss, the administration may seek Supreme Court review; watch for cert petitions in late 2026.

- **Monitor ongoing litigation:** The Wisconsin Law School State Democracy Research Initiative maintains a real-time tracker of DOJ voter-data lawsuits at [statedemocracy.law.wisc.edu](https://statedemocracy.law.wisc.edu/our-work/tracker-doj-lawsuits-seeking-states-sensitive-voter-data) and the Brennan Center maintains a complementary tracker at [brennancenter.org](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/research-reports/tracker-justice-department-requests-voter-information).
