# State and Agency Opposition Mounts Against SECURE Data Act Federal Preemption

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** May 11, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy) filed a formal letter with the House Energy and Commerce Committee on April 27, 2026 opposing the preemption provisions of the Securing and Establishing Consumer Uniform Rights and Enforcement over Data (SECURE) Act (H.R. 8413), introduced April 21, 2026 and emerging from Chairman Brett Guthrie's Privacy Working Group. CalPrivacy argues that the bill's broad "relates to" preemption standard would eliminate decades of stronger state-level consumer protections for more than 100 million Americans, reducing privacy rights rather than advancing them. The IAPP Daily Dashboard reported on May 11, 2026 that a coalition of more than 20 Democratic state attorneys general sent a separate letter to Congress opposing the same preemption provisions; that letter could not be independently located in any public state AG press release, NAAG announcement, or news coverage at time of writing, and is treated as unconfirmed pending a publicly accessible source. Both the CalPrivacy letter and the IAPP-reported AG position urge Congress to adopt a federal "floor" — establishing minimum nationwide standards — rather than a preemptive "ceiling."

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **The SECURE Data Act (H.R. 8413)**: Introduced April 21, 2026 (publicly announced April 22, 2026) by Rep. John Joyce (R-PA) and eight co-sponsors. The bill is the product of Chairman Brett Guthrie's (R-KY) Privacy Working Group, established in February 2025, which spent over a year soliciting input from at least 45 stakeholders. It would create federal consumer privacy rights — data access, correction, deletion, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising — but would also preempt all state privacy laws that "relate[] to the provisions of this Act," per the [official bill text on Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8413/text) and confirmed by multiple law firm analyses.

- **CalPrivacy opposition (verified)**: The California Privacy Protection Agency filed a formal letter on April 27, 2026 opposing H.R. 8413. The letter argues the bill would eliminate CCPA rights, the California Delete Act, and opt-out preference signal requirements affecting more than 100 million Americans. CalPrivacy explicitly urges Congress to "follow the precedent of numerous other federal privacy laws and set a floor of protections that allows states to adopt stronger protections," per the [official CalPrivacy press release](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/04/california-privacy-protection-agency-releases-letter-opposing-the-secure-data-act/) and [letter PDF](https://privacy.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/357/2026/04/CalPrivacy-Letter-HR-8413-SECURE-Data-Act.pdf).

- **AG coalition letter (unconfirmed)**: The IAPP Daily Dashboard reported on May 11, 2026 that a coalition of more than 20 Democratic state attorneys general sent a letter to Congress opposing H.R. 8413's preemption provisions. This report cannot be independently verified: exhaustive searches of state AG press release pages (including California, New York, Illinois, and Massachusetts), the NAAG website, and news databases found no publicly available copy of or reference to this specific letter. The source is a tracked IAPP email digest link not publicly archived. This claim is noted but treated as unconfirmed pending a publicly accessible source.

- **Preemption scope**: Section 15 of H.R. 8413 prohibits any state or political subdivision from prescribing, maintaining, or enforcing any law that "relates to" the Act's provisions. Legal analysts across multiple firms assess this language as broad enough to preempt not only omnibus state privacy laws but also sectoral laws such as Illinois BIPA, the Washington My Health My Data Act, the California Delete Act, and state data broker registry laws, per [Venable LLP analysis](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/04/secure-data-act-congress-introduces-new-federal) and [Akin Gump analysis](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/house-gop-unveils-landmark-comprehensive-privacy-draft-alongside-glba-proposal).

- **Floor vs. ceiling**: The California opposition tracks the long-running federal privacy preemption debate. Privacy advocates and Democratic lawmakers have consistently called for a federal "floor" — minimum standards states can exceed — rather than a "ceiling" that locks in a single federal standard. The Electronic Privacy Information Center's deputy director Caitriona Fitzgerald publicly echoed the same position in [Roll Call reporting](https://rollcall.com/2026/05/14/gop-data-privacy-bill-tries-again-to-set-national-standard/) dated May 14, 2026.

- **States already affected**: At least 21 states have enacted comprehensive consumer privacy laws as of May 2026, covering more than half the U.S. population, per [State of Surveillance reporting](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/federal-privacy-preemption-house-republican-bills-state-laws-2026/). Federal preemption would nullify these statutes.

- **Congressional dynamics**: House Ranking Member Frank Pallone (D-NJ) accused Republicans of drafting a bill that "protects corporations and their bottom line, not people's privacy," per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/05/14/gop-data-privacy-bill-tries-again-to-set-national-standard/). Democrats have signaled that broad preemption is a condition-breaker for bipartisan support.

- **No private right of action**: The bill provides enforcement through the FTC and state AGs (for federal violations only), but grants no private right of action, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/house-republicans-introduce-comprehensive-federal-privacy-bill-secure-data-act). This further limits consumer recourse should state enforcement channels be displaced.

## Legislative Status [MEDIUM confidence]

H.R. 8413 was introduced on April 21, 2026 and referred to the House Energy and Commerce Committee. The Committee released an updated discussion draft in early May 2026, per [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/05/12/u-s-house-committee-releases-secure-data-act-to-establish-new-federal-privacy-framework/). No committee markup date has been publicly announced. The bill faces a difficult path given razor-thin Republican House margins, Democratic opposition to preemption, and resistance from states with stronger laws.

## Note on Unconfirmed AG Coalition Letter

The IAPP Daily Dashboard (May 11, 2026) reported that more than 20 Democratic state attorneys general sent a letter to Congress opposing H.R. 8413's preemption provisions. This is not independently confirmed. The following searches were conducted without locating any public record of this letter:

- California AG Bonta press releases (oag.ca.gov) — no mention of a SECURE Data Act coalition letter; a separate Bonta coalition letter on government mass surveillance (March 2026) is unrelated
- New York AG James, Illinois AG Raoul, Massachusetts AG Campbell — no relevant press releases located
- NAAG (naag.org) — no announcement found
- News database searches for "state attorney general" + "SECURE Data Act" + "coalition" — no corroborating coverage found

AG Bonta has a documented history of leading multistate privacy coalitions (including a 2022 APRA-era coalition and a 2026 mass surveillance letter), which lends circumstantial plausibility to the IAPP report. However, circumstantial plausibility is not corroboration. If this report is used in client materials, users should verify the AG letter independently before citing it.

## Action Items

- **Locate the AG letter if it exists**: Request the full text through individual state AG offices (CA, NY, IL, MA are the most likely lead signatories) or through NAAG. If a publicly accessible version is published, update this report to HIGH confidence and cite it directly.

- **Assess state law exposure**: Organizations subject to CCPA, Illinois BIPA, Washington My Health My Data Act, the California Delete Act, or similar state laws should map which rights and obligations would be extinguished if H.R. 8413 passes with current preemption language. Do not wind down state compliance programs prematurely.

- **Track committee markup**: Watch House Energy and Commerce Committee scheduling. If the bill advances to markup, preemption amendments will be central. Committee floor proceedings will clarify how much, if any, of the ceiling preemption language is negotiable.

- **Evaluate enforcement gap**: If enacted in current form, state AGs retain enforcement authority over the federal standard only — not over stronger state standards. Organizations whose primary enforcement risk is state AG action should model the difference in their liability exposure.

- **Do not treat federal preemption as settled**: Based on the political dynamics, preemption remains the primary obstacle to bipartisan passage. A floor-not-ceiling compromise is not improbable. Maintain dual-track compliance readiness.
