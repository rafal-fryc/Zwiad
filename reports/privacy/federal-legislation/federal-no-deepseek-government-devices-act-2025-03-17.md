# AGs Urge Congress to Ban DeepSeek AI from Government Devices: No DeepSeek on Government Devices Act

**Jurisdiction:** Federal, Montana (lead), Multi-State Coalition | **Category:** Privacy / National Security | **Date:** 2025-03-17

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

A coalition of 21 Republican state attorneys general, led by Montana Attorney General Austin Knudsen, sent a letter to congressional leadership in early March 2025 urging passage of the [No DeepSeek on Government Devices Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1121). The AGs argued that DeepSeek, a Chinese AI application developed by the hedge fund High-Flyer, poses acute national security and privacy risks because its code transmits user data to Chinese government-linked servers and because Chinese law compels domestic companies to share data with the state. Two parallel legislative vehicles exist in Congress: [H.R. 1121](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1121), introduced February 7, 2025, and [S. 765](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/765/all-info), introduced February 27, 2025. Both bills direct the Office of Management and Budget to require removal of DeepSeek from all federal executive agency information technology within 60 days of enactment, with narrow carve-outs for national security and law enforcement purposes. As of April 2026, neither bill has advanced out of committee. The AG letter reinforces a broader, rapidly expanding pattern of governmental action at the federal and state levels, echoing earlier TikTok restrictions and reflecting growing legislative concern about Chinese-origin applications on government infrastructure.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### DeepSeek: Corporate Origin and Technical Profile

[DeepSeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeepSeek) is an AI application and large language model developed by [High-Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-Flyer), a Chinese quantitative hedge fund incorporated in 2016 and co-founded by Liang Wenfeng, who serves as CEO of both companies. DeepSeek was formally established in July 2023 as a spinoff of High-Flyer's internal AI research. It attracted global attention in late January 2025 when its R1 model demonstrated performance competitive with leading U.S. AI systems at a fraction of the reported training cost.

DeepSeek's [privacy policy](https://www.iapp.org/news/a/deepseek-and-the-china-data-question-direct-collection-open-source-and-the-limits-of-extraterritorial-enforcement) discloses collection of a wide range of user data: chat and search query history, keystroke patterns, IP addresses, and device activity. The terms of service specify that they are "governed by the laws of the People's Republic of China." The combination of broad data collection, Chinese-law governance, and the PRC's national security statutes — which require domestic firms to cooperate with intelligence agencies — forms the core basis for security concerns.

Independent cybersecurity analysis, including a review flagged by [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2025/02/experts-flag-security-privacy-risks-in-deepseek-ai-app/), identified that DeepSeek's code contains hard-coded encryption keys and sends unencrypted user and device data to Chinese-government-connected infrastructure. Specifically, researchers found code capable of transmitting data to CMPassport.com, the online registry of China Mobile, a state-owned telecommunications company. [ABC News reported](https://abcnews.go.com/US/deepseek-coding-capability-transfer-users-data-directly-chinese/story?id=118465451) that DeepSeek's code has the capability to transfer users' data directly to the Chinese government.

### Regulatory and Legislative Precursors

The pattern of banning Chinese-origin applications from government devices was established through the TikTok enforcement cycle. Virginia Governor Youngkin's [Executive Order 46](https://www.governor.virginia.gov/newsroom/news-releases/2025/february/name-1040839-en.html) banning DeepSeek explicitly cited his earlier executive order targeting TikTok and WeChat as precedent. Congressional and executive actors drew the same parallel: the legislation's sponsors and the AG coalition both invoked TikTok as the archetypal case of Chinese data-collection risk.

Federal agencies moved first. The U.S. House of Representatives Chief Administrative Officer issued a notice on approximately January 30, 2025, prohibiting House staffers from installing DeepSeek on House-issued devices. The U.S. Navy and NASA also prohibited its use. State governors acted nearly simultaneously: Texas Governor Greg Abbott [issued a ban](https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2025/02/three-states-ban-deepseek-use-on-state-devices-and-networks/) on January 31, 2025, making Texas the first state to do so; New York followed on February 10; Virginia on February 11 via Executive Order 46. International allies — including Australia, South Korea, Italy, Taiwan, and the Netherlands — issued parallel bans or warnings on government use.

The [U.S. House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party](https://chinaselectcommittee.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/selectcommitteeontheccp.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/DeepSeek%20Final.pdf) characterized DeepSeek as a "profound threat" to U.S. national security and issued a formal call for congressional action on export controls and AI model governance.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The AG Coalition Letter

On or around March 7, 2025, Montana Attorney General Austin Knudsen [led a 21-state coalition](https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-leads-21-state-coalition-urging-congress-to-ban-china-based-ai-platform-on-all-government-devices/) of Republican AGs in sending a letter to congressional leaders — specifically addressed to Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, Senate Majority Leader John Thune, House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, and Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer — urging passage of the No DeepSeek on Government Devices Act. Separately, AG Knudsen had also [issued a departmental ban](https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-bans-use-of-china-based-ai-platform-on-all-mtdoj-devices/) on DeepSeek use on all Montana Department of Justice devices.

The signing states were: Alabama, Alaska, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Kentucky, Louisiana, Missouri, Montana (lead), Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, and Virginia.

The letter's core arguments, as reported by [The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2025/mar/7/state-attorneys-general-urge-congress-ban-deepseek-chatbot/) and [NBC Montana](https://nbcmontana.com/news/local/attorney-general-knudsen-leads-21-state-push-to-ban-china-based-ai-on-government-devices/), include:

1. **Data transmission to CCP infrastructure**: DeepSeek code is capable of sending user data directly to Chinese Communist Party-affiliated servers.
2. **Inadvertent account creation in China**: Users may be creating accounts subject to Chinese jurisdiction without realizing it, thereby exposing their identities and online activity to the Chinese government.
3. **Chinese national security law**: Chinese law compels domestic companies — including High-Flyer — to share data with PRC intelligence agencies on demand.
4. **Aggregate intelligence value**: Even non-sensitive data in the aggregate can be used to profile populations, craft more effective phishing attacks, or support manipulation campaigns.
5. **TikTok precedent**: The letter explicitly invokes China's "playbook" with TikTok as a demonstrated pattern of data harvesting.

### The Federal Legislation: H.R. 1121 and S. 765

**H.R. 1121** was introduced on February 7, 2025, by Representatives [Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ-5) and Darin LaHood (R-IL-16)](https://gottheimer.house.gov/posts/release-gottheimer-lahood-introduce-new-bipartisan-legislation-to-protect-americans-from-deepseek). The House bill attracted 16 original cosponsors at introduction, with the total cosponsor count growing to 24 across both parties. It was referred to the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform.

**S. 765** was introduced on February 27, 2025, by Senators [Jacky Rosen (D-NV) and Jon Husted (R-OH)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/765/all-info), with Pete Ricketts (R-NE) as a cosponsor. It was referred to the Senate Committee on Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs. The [full bill text is publicly available](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/s765/BILLS-119s765is.pdf) from Congress.gov and [GovTrack](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s765/text).

Both bills define the term **"covered application"** to mean DeepSeek — including any successor application or service developed or provided by High-Flyer or any entity owned by High-Flyer. This future-proofing language prevents evasion through corporate restructuring or rebranding. Key operative provisions:

- **OMB mandate**: The Director of the Office of Management and Budget is directed to develop standards and guidelines — within 60 days of enactment — requiring removal of DeepSeek from all executive agency information technology systems.
- **Exceptions**: Limited carve-outs exist for: (a) law enforcement activities, (b) national security interests, and (c) security research. Agencies invoking these exceptions must develop and document specific risk mitigation actions.
- **Scope**: The prohibition covers devices and systems used by executive branch agencies, not private individuals or contractors (the contractor gap was addressed in separate legislation).

[GovTrack's analysis](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s765) assessed S. 765 as having approximately a 15% chance of getting past committee and a 6% chance of enactment — consistent with the low base rate for bills directed at specific technology products.

### Related and Parallel Legislation

Congress has pursued the DeepSeek issue through multiple complementary bills:

**No Adversarial AI Act** (bipartisan): Introduced by House Select Committee on the CCP Chairman John Moolenaar (R-MI) and Ranking Member Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL), this broader bill would prohibit federal agencies from procuring or deploying AI technologies developed in China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea. It mandates the Federal Acquisition Security Council to maintain and update a list of adversarial AI products every 180 days. Exceptions apply for research, testing, or mission-critical uses with written justification and Congressional notification.

**Protection Against Foreign Adversarial Artificial Intelligence Act of 2025** ([S. 1638](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1638/text)): Introduced by Senators Bill Cassidy (R-LA) and Jacky Rosen (D-NV), this bill extends restrictions to **federal contractors** — filling a gap in the executive-agency-only scope of H.R. 1121/S. 765. It prohibits any entity with an active federal contract from using DeepSeek to fulfill, assist, or support that contract.

The legislative landscape thus presents a layered approach: H.R. 1121/S. 765 targeting executive agencies, the No Adversarial AI Act addressing the broader class of adversarial-nation AI systems, and S. 1638 extending restrictions to the contractor supply chain.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Federal Agencies

If enacted, H.R. 1121 or S. 765 would require all federal executive agencies to remove DeepSeek from their systems within 60 days of OMB issuing the required guidance. Agencies using DeepSeek for productivity, research, or analytical purposes would need to identify and transition to alternative tools. The carve-outs for law enforcement and national security purposes suggest intelligence and defense agencies would retain limited, controlled access under documented risk management frameworks.

Many federal agencies have already taken internal action. The prior bans by the House, the Navy, and NASA mean that for a significant portion of the federal workforce, the bill's requirements would codify existing practice rather than compel new action.

### State Governments

The AG letter and state executive orders represent a parallel track of subnational governance. Montana's AG ban on DOJ devices, Texas's January 31 ban, New York's February 10 ban, and Virginia's February 11 Executive Order 46 demonstrate that at least some states are not waiting for federal legislation. The 21-state AG coalition — all Republican-led — signals that state-level restrictions will likely proliferate regardless of federal legislative outcomes.

### Federal Contractors

Until S. 1638 or a similar measure is enacted, federal contractors are not covered by H.R. 1121/S. 765. Organizations holding federal contracts should monitor S. 1638's progress and assess whether contractual performance involves any use of DeepSeek tools.

### Broader AI Governance Implications

The DeepSeek legislative response is notable for its bipartisan character — both H.R. 1121's House sponsors represent opposite parties — and for the speed of governmental response. Within weeks of DeepSeek's global launch, multiple legislative vehicles, agency directives, and executive orders had been issued. This trajectory mirrors the TikTok legislative cycle but at significantly higher velocity.

The No Adversarial AI Act's approach — a regularly updated list of adversarial-nation AI systems — represents a structurally more durable solution than product-specific bans, as it does not require separate legislation each time a new Chinese AI application gains prominence.

International coordination is also significant: U.S. allies imposing parallel bans creates pressure toward a de facto technology decoupling in government AI procurement, with implications for global AI market fragmentation.

## Action Items

- **Federal contractors**: Assess whether any current or anticipated contractual work involves DeepSeek; monitor S. 1638 (Protection Against Foreign Adversarial AI Act) for enactment, which would impose direct obligations.
- **State government entities**: Review whether your jurisdiction has issued an executive order banning DeepSeek on state devices; 21 AGs have signaled support for broader restrictions.
- **Compliance teams**: Map any organizational use of DeepSeek — including within shadow IT, research environments, or productivity tools — and assess whether applicable government contracts or sub-contracts could implicate future regulatory obligations.
- **AI procurement**: Monitor the No Adversarial AI Act's Federal Acquisition Security Council list mechanism; if enacted, it will expand beyond DeepSeek to all Chinese, Russian, Iranian, and North Korean AI products on a rolling basis.
- **Legislative tracking**: Monitor H.R. 1121 and S. 765 in committee (House Oversight; Senate Homeland Security) for markup activity; the 15%/6% enactment probability reflects the general bill mortality rate but not the political salience of this issue.
