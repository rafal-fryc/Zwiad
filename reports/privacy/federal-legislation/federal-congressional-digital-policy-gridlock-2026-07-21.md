# Congressional Gridlock Threatens Federal AI Preemption and Kids' Online Safety Legislation Before 2026 Midterms

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy — Federal Legislation | **Date:** July 21-24, 2026

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

With only a handful of shared working weeks remaining before the 2026 midterm elections, the 119th Congress' two marquee digital-policy priorities — a federal AI preemption package and children's online safety legislation (KOSA/COPPA 2.0/the KIDS Act) — remain unresolved and increasingly likely to stall again, according to [IAPP's July 21, 2026 analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/congressional-stalemates-take-wind-out-of-us-digital-policy-sails). <!-- verified --> The White House's strategy of trading a narrow state AI preemption for passage of the Senate's stronger, duty-of-care version of the Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA) hit a setback on July 20, 2026, when President Trump met with Sens. Ted Cruz (R-TX) and Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) in the Oval Office but did not endorse Blackburn's combined package after Cruz raised concerns; the White House subsequently pulled a draft Truth Social post that would have announced presidential support, per [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/trump-discusses-ai-framework-with-blackburn-cruz-at-white-house) and [The Washington Post's AI & Tech Brief](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-intelligence/ai-tech-brief/2026/07/21/ai-tech-brief-blackburns-ai-meeting-oval-office/). <!-- verified --> Separately, the House-passed KIDS Act (H.R. 7757) — which strips out KOSA's enforceable "duty of care" — remains stalled in the Senate, where Chairman Cruz's Commerce Committee is now targeting an "early August" markup of kids' safety and AI bills, a date that has already slipped from an initially floated July 29, 2026 target, per [The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jul/23/senate-panel-take-kids-online-safety-ai-bills-early-august/) and [Record of Record](https://recordofrecord.com/articles/2026-07-15-cruz-sets-july-29-target-for-senate-ai-markup-as-industry-states-brace-for-preem/). <!-- verified --> This report synthesizes the current state of play across both legislative tracks and updates the existing Zwiad knowledge base on the AI preemption/KOSA bundle and the KIDS Act.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Zwiad has separately tracked two related but distinct legislative threads throughout 2026:

**Track 1 — Federal AI preemption bundled with kids' online safety.** Since at least June 2026, the White House and Senate Republicans (led by Sen. Blackburn) have negotiated a package that would preempt conflicting state AI laws for a period of years in exchange for enacting the Senate's stronger version of KOSA ([S. 1748](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text)), the NO FAKES Act, and a federal age-verification mandate — covered in Zwiad's [June 9, 2026 report](../../ai-law/federal-regulation/federal-white-house-ai-preemption-kosa-2026-06-09.md). <!-- verified --> That package followed a failed December 2025 attempt to insert a broad state AI moratorium into the FY2026 NDAA and built on the White House's March 2026 National Policy Framework for AI, which identified preemption as a central legislative pillar, per [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/03/white-house-ai-framework-puts-federal-preemption-at-the-center-of-the-debate). <!-- verified -->

**Track 2 — The KIDS Act omnibus.** The House passed H.R. 7757 (the KIDS Internet and Digital Safety Act) 267-117 on June 29, 2026, folding in a revised KOSA (without the Senate's enforceable duty-of-care standard), COPPA 2.0, age-verification and platform-design mandates, AI chatbot safeguards (the SAFE Bots Act), and a data broker registry — covered in Zwiad's [June 28](../childrens-privacy/federal-kids-act-hr7757-2026-06-28.md) and [June 29-30](../childrens-privacy/federal-kids-act-house-passage-2026-06-29.md) reports. <!-- verified --> Senate KOSA co-authors Blumenthal and Blackburn publicly rejected the House's no-duty-of-care approach as insufficient, per [Roll Call's June 29 coverage](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/29/kids-online-safety-push-clouded-by-house-senate-divide/), and Zwiad's July 17, 2026 update to that report noted Senate Commerce Chairman Cruz was expected to hold his own markup in July 2026. <!-- verified -->

These two tracks are now converging: the same White House-Blackburn effort to pair AI preemption with kids' safety legislation described in Track 1 is, as of July 2026, explicitly using the Senate's bipartisan KOSA (the stronger, duty-of-care version) — not the House-passed KIDS Act text — as the child-safety component of the trade, per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/congressional-stalemates-take-wind-out-of-us-digital-policy-sails). <!-- verified -->

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

**The July 20, 2026 Oval Office meeting.** Sen. Blackburn met with President Trump, joined by Senate Commerce Chairman Ted Cruz and National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett, to pitch a legislative framework combining a "very narrow" preemption of state AI laws with her children's online safety package (built around the Senate's KOSA), per [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/trump-discusses-ai-framework-with-blackburn-cruz-at-white-house) and [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-intelligence/ai-tech-brief/2026/07/21/ai-tech-brief-blackburns-ai-meeting-oval-office/). <!-- verified --> Cruz reportedly raised concerns during the meeting, and the White House team pulled a Truth Social post it had drafted to announce Trump's endorsement of the package — meaning the meeting ended without a presidential endorsement, per [The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-intelligence/ai-tech-brief/2026/07/21/ai-tech-brief-blackburns-ai-meeting-oval-office/) and [Punchbowl News](https://punchbowl.news/article/tech/cruz-blackburn-trump-meeting/). <!-- verified --> The Washington Post's AI & Tech Brief further reports that venture capitalists close to the White House appear to have shifted their preferred AI-preemption strategy away from a single omnibus deal and toward "chipping away" at individual state AI laws piecemeal — a signal that the comprehensive Blackburn bundle may be losing support inside the administration's own orbit. <!-- verified -->

**Senate Commerce Committee timeline slipping.** Chairman Cruz had targeted July 29, 2026 for a Senate Commerce Committee markup of AI and kids' online safety bills, per [Record of Record's July 15 report](https://recordofrecord.com/articles/2026-07-15-cruz-sets-july-29-target-for-senate-ai-markup-as-industry-states-brace-for-preem/), but by July 23, 2026 the Washington Times reported the committee was now aiming for "the first week of August," with Cruz telling reporters the exact agenda remains "still a work in progress," per [The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jul/23/senate-panel-take-kids-online-safety-ai-bills-early-august/). <!-- verified --> This updates and narrows the "expected... in July 2026" framing in Zwiad's July 17 update to the KIDS Act House-passage report. The Senate's KOSA (cosponsored by Blackburn and Blumenthal) — which would require default-strongest privacy/safety settings for minors' accounts, additional parental controls, and design-feature changes targeting compulsive use — is likely to be part of that agenda, per [The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jul/23/senate-panel-take-kids-online-safety-ai-bills-early-august/). <!-- verified -->

**The underlying policy standoff.** The core disagreement identified across all tracked sources is unchanged since June: the House-passed KIDS Act relies on a narrower list of enumerated harms and "reasonable policies" requirements, while the Senate's KOSA imposes an enforceable duty of care covering a broader set of harms (eating disorders, compulsive use, depression, anxiety, and more), per [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/29/kids-online-safety-push-clouded-by-house-senate-divide/). <!-- verified --> On the AI side, Democrats generally oppose preempting state AI regulation outright, while Republicans argue some preemption is necessary to avoid a 50-state compliance patchwork, per [The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/jul/23/senate-panel-take-kids-online-safety-ai-bills-early-august/). <!-- verified --> IAPP frames the calendar itself as an independent constraint: as of July 21, 2026, only a limited number of shared working days remain for the House and Senate before both a midterm election recess and the possibility of a change in chamber control heading into 2027, meaning any package not resolved in the next several working weeks is likely to be pushed into the next Congress or die entirely, per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/congressional-stalemates-take-wind-out-of-us-digital-policy-sails). <!-- verified -->

**Enforcement continues to fill the legislative gap.** Separately from the legislative stalemate, the FTC has continued to signal that children's privacy remains a top enforcement priority regardless of whether Congress acts — including its Take It Down Act enforcement responsibilities that began in May 2026 and continued age-verification-related guidance — meaning the compliance landscape for platforms serving minors is being shaped more by FTC and state action than by federal statute in the near term, per [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/childrens-online-privacy-in-2026-congress-stalls-again-ftc-signals-priorities). <!-- verified -->

## Official Legal Text [LOW confidence] [VERIFIED]

No new legal text was introduced by the July 20, 2026 Oval Office meeting itself — it was a negotiating session, not a bill introduction or committee action, and no legislative text specific to the "Blackburn omnibus" discussed at that meeting has been publicly filed as of this writing. The official texts of the two legislative vehicles referenced throughout remain: [S. 1748, Kids Online Safety Act, 119th Congress](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text) (Senate KOSA, duty-of-care version) and [H.R. 7757, KIDS Internet and Digital Safety Act, 119th Congress](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/7757/text) (House-passed omnibus). No Senate Commerce Committee markup text has been published as of July 24, 2026; the committee's own agenda and any bill text should be monitored at [commerce.senate.gov](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/) once the early-August markup is formally noticed. This section is tagged LOW confidence because the central new development in this report (the stalled Oval Office endorsement and the slipping markup date) is sourced entirely from news reporting rather than an official government record; no White House statement, press release, or committee notice confirming or denying the account has been located.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Organizations that have been monitoring the AI-preemption-for-KOSA trade as a compliance planning trigger should not treat the July 20 meeting as either a positive or negative resolution — it is, at most, evidence that the deal remains alive but not yet endorsed by the President, and that the venture-capital wing close to the administration may be favoring a piecemeal, state-by-state litigation and lobbying strategy over a single legislative bundle. Platforms operating under state comprehensive AI laws (Colorado, California, and others) should continue current compliance programs, since no preemption has been enacted or even formally introduced with agreed text. Platforms serving minors should continue preparing for the stricter, duty-of-care standard embodied in the Senate's KOSA rather than assuming the House's narrower KIDS Act language will prevail, given that the Senate Commerce Committee's own upcoming markup is expected to advance the Senate KOSA version. The compressed legislative calendar means that any further movement — a Commerce Committee markup, a floor vote, or a renewed presidential endorsement — is likely to happen (if at all) within a narrow window before the 2026 midterm recess season, and the near-term compliance landscape will continue to be driven primarily by the FTC's active enforcement posture (Take It Down Act, age verification) and by state children's-privacy and AI statutes rather than by new federal legislation.

## Action Items

- Do not treat the July 20 White House meeting as a signal that federal AI preemption is imminent; no legislative text has been agreed, and the President did not publicly endorse the package. Continue current state-level AI compliance programs (Colorado AI Act, California ADMT rules, etc.) without modification.
- Monitor the Senate Commerce Committee's early-August 2026 markup announcement closely, as this is the most concrete near-term event that could advance either the Senate's KOSA or an AI preemption vehicle; check commerce.senate.gov for the formal notice and any released bill text.
- For platforms serving minors, prioritize gap analysis against the Senate KOSA's enforceable duty-of-care standard over the House KIDS Act's narrower "reasonable policies" standard, since the Senate is the more likely near-term point of legislative movement and has historically held firmer on duty-of-care language.
- Continue to treat FTC enforcement (Take It Down Act notice-and-removal obligations, age-verification guidance) as the operative near-term compliance driver for children's online safety, independent of whether Congress passes new legislation this session.
- Track whether the "early August" Senate Commerce markup slips again; the calendar constraint identified by IAPP (limited shared working days before the midterms) suggests each further delay meaningfully reduces the odds either bill advances in the 119th Congress.
