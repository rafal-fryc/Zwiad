# Grassley Backs 'Clean' 18-Month Renewal of FISA Section 702 as April 20 Deadline Looms

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-14

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley (R-Iowa) is publicly backing an 18-month "clean" (reform-free) reauthorization of Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) ahead of the April 20, 2026 sunset, citing Department of Justice commitments to improve congressional oversight access to the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court (FISC). The White House has issued a Statement of Administration Policy urging the same clean 18-month extension, while a coalition of more than 130 civil society organizations and a bipartisan group of reform-minded lawmakers are demanding Congress close the so-called "data broker loophole" — the government's warrantless purchase of Americans' commercial data — as a condition of renewal.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Section 702 of FISA will sunset on April 20, 2026, two years after the Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act (RISAA) extended it in the [2024 reauthorization cycle](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48592).
- Sen. Grassley introduced [S.3696, the FISA Accountability and Extension Act of 2026](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3696/text), which would extend Section 702 and amend Section 5 of RISAA to guarantee congressional member and staff access to FISC and FISC of Review (FISCR) proceedings.
- Grassley and Ranking Member Dick Durbin (D-Ill.) have pressed Attorney General Pam Bondi to revise DOJ procedures — [first adopted under the Biden administration in November 2024](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/grassley-durbin-call-on-doj-to-amend-restrictions-on-congressional-oversight-of-foreign-intelligence-surveillance-courts) — that currently restrict congressional oversight attendance at FISC hearings, which they argue conflicts with Section 5(d) of RISAA.
- The White House Office of Management and Budget issued a Statement of Administration Policy [urging a clean extension](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/13/white-house-fisa-reauthorization-mike-johnson), stating "this cannot be accomplished without the reauthorization of Section 702," and President Trump has [publicly endorsed a clean 18-month extension](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5800566-donald-trump-fisa-702-extension-support/).
- A coalition of [over 130 civil society organizations](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/04/we-need-you-our-privacy-cannot-afford-clean-extension-section-702) — including EFF, the Brennan Center, EPIC, and CDT — sent a March 19, 2026 letter to congressional leadership urging that reauthorization include closing the data broker loophole, which permits intelligence and law enforcement agencies to purchase sensitive information on US persons from commercial brokers without a warrant.
- Sens. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) and Mike Lee (R-Utah), with Reps. Warren Davidson (R-Ohio) and Zoe Lofgren (D-Calif.), have introduced a [competing Government Surveillance Reform Act](https://www.wyden.senate.gov/news/press-releases/wyden-lee-davidson-and-lofgren-introduce-bill-to-reform-fisa-section-702-protect-americans-constitutional-rights-and-plug-data-broker-surveillance-loophole) that would reform Section 702 and plug the data broker loophole.
- [FBI queries of Americans' data under Section 702 rose 35% in 2025](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/03/fbi-queries-americans-data-under-fisa-702-rose-35-2025/412103/), according to ODNI's annual transparency report — a data point reform advocates cite as evidence that the 2024 RISAA guardrails are insufficient.
- House Speaker Mike Johnson faces a difficult floor math on a clean reauthorization, with hardline conservatives and progressive Democrats [threatening to sink the bill](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/13/politics/fisa-section-702-suveillance-law-expiration-congress); a vote is expected the week of April 14, 2026.

## Action Items

- **Privacy and compliance teams at US companies receiving FISA directives**: prepare for continuity planning in both scenarios — a clean 18-month extension (authorities unchanged through October 2027) or a sunset/lapse requiring transition to pre-702 procedures if Congress fails to act by April 20, 2026.
- **Data brokers and advertising-data companies**: monitor whether any final deal includes data-broker warrant provisions; such language would materially restrict existing government procurement channels and could trigger customer contract renegotiations.
- **In-house counsel at firms with national-security-adjacent workflows (cloud, telecom, AI)**: track amendment activity on S.3696 and the Wyden-Lee Government Surveillance Reform Act; any conference compromise likely lands in the next 7–10 days.
- **Civil-liberties and policy staff**: review the 130-organization coalition letter for the specific reform asks and be ready to brief leadership on which asks (warrant for US-person queries, data-broker ban, FISC transparency) are still live in the endgame.
