# Federal Education Privacy Update: GAO FAFSA IT Failures, Student Loan On-Ramp End, and Student Data Guidance (October 2024)

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-10-21

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

In October 2024, the House Education and the Workforce Subcommittee on Higher Education and Workforce Development held a hearing in conjunction with the release of two Government Accountability Office (GAO) reports examining the Department of Education's (ED) failed rollout of the simplified 2024–2025 FAFSA system. The GAO's IT and cybersecurity-focused report (GAO-24-107783) found systemic weaknesses in student financial aid data management — including 55 post-deployment defects, seven of them critical, and inadequate CIO oversight — raising substantial concerns about the security and integrity of sensitive student financial and tax data. Separately, the student loan on-ramp period ended on September 30, 2024, restoring credit-reporting consequences for missed payments and exposing millions of borrowers to potential negative financial records. Shortly after the report's primary October 2024 timeframe, on November 7, 2024, ED issued formal guidance on access, disclosure, and use of FAFSA data under FERPA and the Higher Education Act, establishing the framework governing how institutions may handle student personally identifiable information (PII). Compliance officers at institutions of higher education and state grant agencies should audit their FAFSA data-handling practices against the guidance immediately.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

### FAFSA Simplification Act and the 2024–2025 Rollout

The [FAFSA Simplification Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/116th-congress/house-bill/133), enacted as Title VII, Division FF of H.R. 133 (P.L. 116-260), the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021, directed ED to redesign the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) to reduce complexity and improve access. A central feature of the redesigned form is direct data transfer from the Internal Revenue Service — the so-called "IRS Direct Data Exchange" — allowing students and parents to consent to automatic import of federal tax information (FTI) into the FAFSA rather than manually entering tax data.

The new FAFSA system was originally scheduled to open in October 2023. ED delayed the launch by three months, opening the form in late December 2023. Further technical failures cascaded throughout the 2024–2025 award cycle: the new FAFSA Processing System (FPS) launched with major defects, processing errors affected approximately 30 percent of submitted forms in March and April 2024, and call center support was severely understaffed — approximately three-quarters of calls to ED's helpline went unanswered during peak periods.

### Student Privacy Legal Framework

Three federal statutes govern how FAFSA data — which contains some of the most sensitive financial and identifying information held by educational institutions — may be accessed, used, and disclosed:

- **The Higher Education Act (HEA)**: Authorizes the collection and use of FAFSA data for the application, award, and administration of Title IV federal student aid. HEA provisions establish the primary permissible purposes for data use and impose use restrictions on FTI.
- **The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA)**, [20 U.S.C. § 1232g](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1232g): Protects the privacy of student education records at institutions receiving federal funds. FERPA permits disclosure of education records without student consent only under specified exceptions. Importantly, where HEA or Internal Revenue Code restrictions are more restrictive than FERPA, they control.
- **The Privacy Act of 1974**, [5 U.S.C. § 552a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/552a): Governs federal agency collection and maintenance of records on individuals. Applies to ED's own handling of student records, as well as FTI obtained via IRS data transfer.

### The On-Ramp Period

When federal student loan repayments resumed after the pandemic-era payment pause in October 2023, ED established a 12-month "on-ramp" period running through September 30, 2024. During the on-ramp, loan servicers were not required to report missed, late, or partial payments to the three national credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion), even though interest continued to accrue. This temporary measure insulated borrowers from negative credit reporting consequences while they re-adjusted to repayment.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### GAO Report 1: IT and Cybersecurity Weaknesses (GAO-24-107783)

On September 24, 2024, the GAO released [GAO-24-107783](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-107783), titled "Department of Education: Preliminary Results Show Strong Leadership Needed to Address Serious Student Aid System Weaknesses." This report, produced by GAO's Information Technology and Cybersecurity team, examined the acquisition, testing, and oversight of the FAFSA Processing System.

Key findings with direct privacy and data security implications:

**System defects.** ED identified 55 defects in the FPS after deployment. Of these, seven remained unresolved and were categorized as "critical" as of documentation compiled March 6, 2024. The nature and remediation status of those critical defects was not fully disclosed in publicly available GAO materials, but the finding raises questions about whether student PII and federal tax information were adequately protected during the affected periods.

**Requirements gaps.** FSA moved 25 key requirements to a later delivery date in early 2023, and subsequently decided not to address 18 of those 25 requirements at FPS launch. Whether any of the deferred requirements related to data security or privacy controls was not specified in publicly available summaries.

**CIO oversight failure.** Although ED has established processes giving its Chief Information Officer a significant governance and oversight role for IT acquisitions, those processes were not followed for the FPS project. ED also lacked a permanent CIO, cycling through six CIOs since the FPS project commenced in February 2021. The absence of stable IT leadership is a recognized risk factor in federal IT security frameworks, including FISMA and NIST SP 800-37.

**Processing errors affecting student data.** In late March and early April 2024, processing and data errors were identified that affected approximately 30 percent of FAFSA forms. These errors — which included miscalculation of student aid eligibility — also implicated the integrity of IRS-sourced FTI embedded in affected submissions. GAO warned that until ED addresses these weaknesses, the 2025–2026 FAFSA cycle remains at elevated risk.

Testimony at the September 24 hearing was provided by Marisol Cruz Cain, GAO Director of Information Technology and Cybersecurity, and Melissa Emrey-Arras, GAO Director of Education, Workforce, and Income Security Issues, alongside witnesses from the Department of Education.

### GAO Report 2: Communications and Student Support Failures (GAO-24-107407)

The companion report, [GAO-24-107407](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-107407) — "FAFSA: Education Needs to Improve Communications and Support Around the Free Application for Federal Student Aid" — examined ED's outreach to students and institutions. While more focused on program administration than data security, the report has privacy-adjacent implications: ED's failure to communicate processing delays left students and institutions unable to determine whether their submitted data had been processed, introducing information asymmetry and potential vulnerability to confusion-based fraud.

The report found that the 2024–2025 rollout resulted in approximately 9 percent fewer high school seniors and first-time applicants submitting a FAFSA, with the largest declines among lower-income students. GAO issued seven recommendations to ED to improve communications and preparedness for future FAFSA cycles.

### End of Student Loan On-Ramp: Credit Reporting Resumes

The on-ramp period ended September 30, 2024. Beginning October 1, 2024, student loan servicers are required to resume standard credit reporting practices. Borrowers with missed or partial payments will now have those reported to credit bureaus after 90 days of delinquency. The practical privacy implication: credit reporting for student loans involves the transmission of student borrower PII to consumer reporting agencies, restoring the full scope of that data flow for approximately 43 million federal student loan borrowers.

The SAVE (Saving on a Valuable Education) income-driven repayment plan — which had enrolled approximately 7.5 million borrowers as of mid-2024 — remained blocked by court injunction following a July 18, 2024 federal court stay. The Eighth Circuit affirmed the injunction in August 2024. Borrowers in SAVE forbearance were not accruing interest but also were not making progress toward loan forgiveness, introducing ongoing uncertainty about their financial and data status with servicers.

### ED Guidance on FAFSA Data Handling (FERPA/HEA Intersection) — Issued November 7, 2024

**Note on timing:** Although this guidance is included in this report because it directly addresses the FAFSA data-handling issues raised by the October 2024 GAO hearing, ED formally published the guidance on **November 7, 2024** — approximately two weeks after the primary October 2024 developments covered in this report. It is not an October 2024 development; it is included here for topical continuity.

On November 7, 2024, ED issued [guidance on the permitted and prohibited access, disclosure, and use of FAFSA data](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2024-11-07/guidance-state-grant-agencies-and-institutions-higher-education-access-disclosure-and-use-fafsa-data-application-award-and-administration-student-aid-programs-updated-sept-30-2025) by institutions of higher education (IHEs) and state grant agencies. The guidance addresses the intersection of HEA, FERPA, and the Privacy Act as applied to FAFSA-derived PII.

Key compliance requirements established by the guidance:

- **Use restriction**: FAFSA data — including both FERPA-protected education records and IRS-derived FTI — may only be used for the application, award, and administration of Title IV student aid. Research uses, academic advising, or other institutional purposes require separate written consent from the student.
- **FTI handling**: Federal Tax Information imported via IRS Direct Data Exchange is categorized as Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI//SP-TAX) and subject to IRC Section 6103(l)(13) use restrictions in addition to HEA and FERPA requirements. FTI may not be used for research purposes even with student consent.
- **FERPA exception limits**: Standard FERPA exceptions (e.g., school official, legitimate educational interest) do not override HEA or IRC use restrictions. If the HEA prohibits a particular data use, a FERPA exception cannot authorize it.
- **Redisclosure**: IHEs may redisclose FAFSA data to contractors acting as school officials under FERPA, but those contractors are bound by all applicable use restrictions.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Institutions

All institutions of higher education participating in Title IV federal student aid programs — approximately 6,000 institutions — are affected by the FAFSA data use guidance and must ensure their student records and financial aid offices comply with the HEA/FERPA/Privacy Act framework. State grant agencies distributing state aid to students using FAFSA data face the same requirements.

### Students and Borrowers

An estimated 17 million students submitted a FAFSA for the 2024–2025 cycle (down from prior years due to the troubled rollout). All of those students had their financial and tax data processed through a system that GAO identified as having critical unresolved defects. Approximately 43 million federal student loan borrowers are subject to restored credit reporting as of October 1, 2024.

### Compliance Requirements

Institutions should:

1. Audit all current uses of FAFSA-derived data to confirm compliance with the HEA's use restriction. Any use beyond financial aid administration requires independent written consent.
2. Ensure contracts with third-party vendors handling FAFSA data include appropriate data use restrictions consistent with the HEA/FERPA/Privacy Act framework.
3. Implement separate handling protocols for FTI to maintain compliance with IRC Section 6103 — FTI cannot be commingled with other student data in ways that could lead to impermissible use.
4. Communicate with student loan borrowers affected by the end of the on-ramp period about resumed credit reporting and servicer obligations.

### Enforcement Outlook

ED's Student Privacy Policy Office (SPPO) oversees FERPA compliance through a complaint-based investigation process rather than proactive audits. However, violations of the HEA's use restriction or IRC Section 6103 can carry more severe consequences, including potential criminal penalties under 26 U.S.C. § 7213 for unauthorized disclosure of FTI. The GAO's public criticism of ED's IT oversight practices also increases the likelihood of congressional follow-up, including possible oversight hearings or subpoenas for additional information about the critical defects identified in the FPS.

## Action Items

- **Immediate**: Financial aid offices should review the ED guidance (issued November 7, 2024) on FAFSA data access, disclosure, and use and confirm that institutional data handling practices comply with HEA, FERPA, and IRC use restrictions. Pay particular attention to whether FAFSA data is being used for institutional research or advising without proper consent.
- **By end of Q4 2024**: Institutions should update data use agreements with third-party vendors who have access to FAFSA-derived PII to incorporate explicit HEA and FTI use restrictions.
- **Ongoing**: Monitor GAO follow-up reporting on ED's implementation of the recommendations from GAO-24-107783 and GAO-24-107407, which may surface additional compliance obligations if ED revises FPS operations or data governance policies.
- **Student loan servicer coordination**: Institutions with affiliated loan servicing operations should confirm that resumed credit reporting practices comply with Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) obligations and that borrower notification procedures are in place.
- **Watch**: SAVE plan litigation remains active; if the court injunction is lifted or the plan is terminated, additional income data-sharing consent mechanisms may change, affecting institutional and servicer obligations.
