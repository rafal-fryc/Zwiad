# Congress Passes 45-Day FISA Section 702 Extension, Punting Reauthorization to Mid-June

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-30

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Congress passed a 45-day short-term extension of FISA Section 702 on April 30, 2026, hours before the program was set to lapse, extending the warrantless surveillance authority through **June 12, 2026**. The stopgap — the second within two weeks — came after the Senate rejected the House's three-year reauthorization bill, which included an unrelated ban on central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) that Senate Majority Leader John Thune called a "poison pill." The Senate unanimously approved the 45-day extension, and the House followed with a 261–111 vote; the bill now goes to President Trump for signature. A key concession secured by Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) requires the declassification of a secret Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court (FISC) opinion within 15 days of the extension's enactment.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The **Senate passed the 45-day extension by unanimous consent** on April 30, 2026; the House followed with a **261–111 vote**, and the bill heads to President Trump for signature ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/29/g-s1-119094/congress-fisa-702)).
- The extension keeps Section 702 alive through **June 12, 2026**, the second stopgap in two weeks after Congress failed on April 17 to pass either a five-year or 18-month reauthorization ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/04/30/congress-clears-short-term-fisa-extension/)).
- The House passed a **three-year reauthorization on April 29 (235–191)** that included modest procedural safeguards (written query justifications, attorney approval for reviews of Americans' data, criminal penalties for misuse up to five years) but **no warrant requirement** — a central demand of privacy advocates ([The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/house/5856102-warrantless-spy-powers-fisa-702/)).
- The three-year bill also contained a provision banning the Federal Reserve from issuing a **central bank digital currency (CBDC)**, which Senate Majority Leader Thune declared dead on arrival in the Senate ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/29/house-passes-fisa-intelligence-bill-senate-opposition)).
- **Sen. Ron Wyden** agreed to support the 45-day extension after securing commitments from Senate Intelligence Committee leaders — Sen. Tom Cotton (R-Ark.) and Sen. Mark Warner (D-Va.) — that a secret **FISC opinion will be declassified within 15 days** of enactment ([Nextgov/FCW](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/04/house-passes-45-day-fisa-extension-after-senators-secure-declassification-deal/413250/)).
- The **warrant requirement for U.S.-person queries** remains the unresolved central dispute: privacy advocates, civil liberties organizations, and a bipartisan bloc of lawmakers continue to demand that the FBI obtain court approval before querying Americans' communications incidentally swept up under Section 702 ([ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/warrantless-surveillance-under-section-702-of-fisa)).
- Congress will be in **recess until mid-May**, leaving roughly three to four weeks of negotiating time before the June 12 deadline ([Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/05/congress-punts-fisa-section-702-renewal-to-june/)).
- Section 702, codified at [50 U.S.C. § 1881a](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48592), was last substantively reauthorized on April 20, 2024, via the Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act (RISAA). The FISC renewed annual certifications on April 11, 2026, meaning collection under existing directives continues even during the stopgap period — but new directives cannot be issued without a valid statutory authority.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA), added to the law in 2008 via the FISA Amendments Act, authorizes the FBI, NSA, CIA, and National Counterterrorism Center to compel U.S. electronic communications providers to collect the communications of non-U.S. persons located abroad. While the authority nominally targets foreign nationals, large volumes of Americans' communications are "incidentally" swept in when those Americans correspond with foreign targets. The law permits federal analysts to search this data using U.S.-person identifiers without obtaining a warrant — the so-called "backdoor search" that underpins most of the civil-liberties controversy.

Documented abuses of Section 702 query authorities include warrantless searches targeting a sitting U.S. senator, journalists, political donors, and over 19,000 individuals affiliated with a congressional campaign. The [ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/warrantless-surveillance-under-section-702-of-fisa) and [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://www.eff.org/702-spying) have long characterized these backdoor searches as a de facto warrant end-run. The 2024 RISAA imposed new FBI query limitations but did not add a warrant requirement.

## What Happens Next [MEDIUM confidence]

Congress must reach agreement on a longer-term reauthorization before **June 12, 2026**. The Senate is expected to develop its own reauthorization bill while the House considers whether to strip the CBDC provision from its three-year bill or introduce new legislation. The core legislative fault lines remain:

1. **Warrant requirement** — Privacy hawks in both chambers demand court approval for U.S.-person queries. Intelligence Community officials argue this would unacceptably impair operations.
2. **CBDC ban** — The House's attachment of an unrelated CBDC prohibition created the Senate impasse that forced the 45-day stopgap; it must be resolved before a bipartisan deal is possible.
3. **FISC declassification** — The Wyden-Cotton-Warner agreement creates a binding commitment to declassify a secret FISC opinion within 15 days; the contents of that opinion could reshape the debate if they reveal previously unreported surveillance activities.
4. **Provider definition** — The 2024 RISAA's expansion of "electronic communication service provider" remains contested; some lawmakers seek to narrow it to reduce potential compelled cooperation by businesses not historically subject to Section 702 directives.

## Action Items

- **Organizations managing EU-US data transfers** (Data Privacy Framework, Standard Contractual Clauses): Review transfer impact assessments in light of continued Section 702 authority and monitor the June 12 reauthorization for any material changes to program scope or oversight.
- **Technology companies and internet service providers** subject to Section 702 directives: Maintain existing compliance programs; directives issued under current certifications remain valid during the extension period.
- **Legal and compliance teams**: Monitor the declassification of the FISC opinion required within 15 days of enactment — if public, it may reveal program changes material to privacy risk assessments.
- **All affected organizations**: Set a calendar reminder for **June 12, 2026** — if Congress again fails to reauthorize, a lapse scenario (no new directives, carriers may halt collection) becomes a real operational risk. Model the impact of a short lapse on intelligence operations and any dependencies your organization has on related government access frameworks.
