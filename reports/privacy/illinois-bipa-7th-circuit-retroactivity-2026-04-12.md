# Seventh Circuit Holds 2024 BIPA Damages Amendment Applies Retroactively to Pending Cases

## Bottom Line [HIGH confidence]

On April 1, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit ruled unanimously in [*Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, No. 25-2185](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/25-2185/25-2185-2026-04-01.html) that the August 2024 amendment to the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) applies retroactively to cases that were pending when the amendment was enacted. The ruling caps recovery at a single violation per person — $5,000 for intentional/reckless violations or $1,000 for negligent violations — regardless of how many times a person's biometric identifier was scanned, reversing three federal district court decisions and dramatically reducing liability exposure for defendants in hundreds of active BIPA lawsuits.

## The Amendment at Issue [HIGH confidence]

Illinois [Senate Bill 2979](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/bipa-cases-7th-circuit-rules-change-illinois-laws-damages-provision-retroactively-limits-defendant-exposure), enacted as [Public Act 103-0769](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm) and signed by Governor Pritzker in August 2024, amended [Section 20 of BIPA (740 ILCS 14/20)](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K20.htm) to provide that a person whose biometric identifier or biometric information is collected in violation of Section 15(b) or disclosed in violation of Section 15(d) — "regardless of the number of times" — may recover "at most, one" liquidated-damages award.

The amendment was the Illinois General Assembly's direct legislative response to the Illinois Supreme Court's February 2023 decision in [*Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc.*](https://law.justia.com/cases/illinois/supreme-court/2023/128004.html), which held that a separate claim accrues on each scan or transmission — a reading that exposed White Castle to potentially $17 billion in damages from roughly 9,500 employees. The practical stakes are stark: a worker previously scanned 1,500 times could have sought $7.5 million in damages; under the amended framework, maximum recovery is $5,000 per person for intentional violations.

## The Seventh Circuit's Decision [HIGH confidence]

The consolidated appeal included three cases: *Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, *Gregg v. Central Transport LLC*, and *Willis v. Universal Intermodal Services*. In each, Illinois federal district courts had denied motions to dismiss or limit damages, holding the 2024 amendment did not reach pre-amendment conduct. The Seventh Circuit [reversed all three](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/04/03/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively-reversing-three-illinois-federal-court-decisions/).

Applying the Illinois retroactivity framework from *Commonwealth Edison Co. v. Will County Collector*, the panel concluded that the amendment is **remedial/procedural** rather than substantive, and therefore applies to pending cases. The court's reasoning rested on two principal points:

1. The amendment revised Section 20 (the damages/remedies provision) while leaving Section 15 (the substantive liability standards) untouched — signaling a remedial rather than rights-altering purpose.
2. The text of the amendment speaks directly to remedies, providing that an aggrieved person is "entitled to, at most, one recovery."

The panel also [distinguished the *Cothron* per-scan accrual rule](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/major-biometric-win-for-business-in-illinois) as addressing statute-of-limitations timing under Section 15, not the separate question of damages calculation under Section 20. The court expressly rejected the argument that plaintiffs' prior "per-scan" claims were vested property interests, since plaintiffs retain their cause of action and their ability to recover statutory damages — just not on a per-scan basis.

## Impact on Pending Litigation [HIGH confidence]

The ruling has three immediate consequences for the hundreds of pending BIPA class actions in Illinois federal courts:

- **Massive damages reduction.** Cases previously valued by reference to thousands of scans per class member now face a ceiling of one recovery per person.
- **Article III standing and CAFA jurisdiction challenges.** Many cases may now fail to meet CAFA's $5 million amount-in-controversy threshold and could be remanded to state court. Some individual claims may drop below Article III's concrete-injury threshold where no actual harm beyond the statutory violation is alleged.
- **Settlement recalibration.** Pending class settlements priced on per-scan exposure will be renegotiated; plaintiffs' leverage is sharply diminished.

This decision is binding on federal courts in Illinois, Indiana, and Wisconsin. Illinois state courts are not bound by it, and the Illinois Supreme Court has not yet addressed the retroactivity question.

## Open Questions [MEDIUM confidence]

- **State-court reception.** The Seventh Circuit's reading is a federal court's *Erie* prediction of Illinois law. The Illinois Supreme Court has not yet ruled on retroactivity, though lower Illinois appellate decisions have trended the same direction. A contrary Illinois Supreme Court ruling could disrupt the landscape and create a circuit split between state and federal courts.
- **Pre-*Cothron* conduct.** The decision addresses damages calculation, not whether claims accruing before *Cothron* remain viable under statute-of-limitations analyses that several Illinois trial courts have been wrestling with.
- **Section 15(a) and 15(e) claims.** The amendment's text focuses on Sections 15(b) and 15(d); treatment of other BIPA subsections may require further litigation.

## Action Items for Compliance and Litigation Teams

- **Reassess BIPA litigation reserves immediately.** Companies defending pending BIPA class actions in federal court within the Seventh Circuit should recalculate potential exposure using the per-person (not per-scan) damages model.
- **Consider removal to federal court.** Defendants in BIPA cases pending in Illinois state courts may benefit from removing eligible cases to federal court, where this binding Seventh Circuit precedent applies.
- **Evaluate settlement posture.** Pending settlement negotiations should be reassessed in light of the significantly reduced damages exposure. Existing settlement agreements tied to per-scan calculations may warrant renegotiation if not yet finalized.
- **Monitor Illinois state court rulings.** The Illinois Appellate Court and Illinois Supreme Court have not yet ruled on retroactivity; state court holdings may differ.
- **Preserve arguments for extension.** Preserve arguments for extending *Clay*'s reasoning to cases pending in Illinois state court and to other BIPA subsections.
- **Maintain BIPA compliance programs.** The amendment reduces damages but does not eliminate liability. Companies collecting biometric data in Illinois must still comply with BIPA Section 15 requirements for informed consent, written policy, and retention schedules.
