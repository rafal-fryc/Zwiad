# Seventh Circuit Holds 2024 BIPA Damages Amendment Applies Retroactively

## Bottom Line [HIGH confidence]

On April 1, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit ruled unanimously in [*Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, Nos. 25-2185 et al.](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/ph-privacy/7th-circuit-confirms-bipa-amendment-has-retroactive-application) that the August 2024 amendment to the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) applies retroactively to cases that were pending when the amendment was enacted. The amendment caps recovery at a single violation per person, regardless of how many times the person's biometric identifier was scanned — eliminating the 'per-scan' damages theory that had driven catastrophic exposure in BIPA class actions.

## The Amendment at Issue [HIGH confidence]

Illinois [Senate Bill 2979](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/bipa-cases-7th-circuit-rules-change-illinois-laws-damages-provision-retroactively-limits-defendant-exposure), enacted as Public Act 103-0769 and signed by Governor Pritzker in August 2024, amended Section 20 of BIPA (740 ILCS 14/20) to provide that a person whose biometric identifier or biometric information is collected in violation of Section 15(b) or disclosed in violation of Section 15(d) — 'regardless of the number of times' — may recover 'at most, one' liquidated-damages award ($1,000 for negligent violations, $5,000 for intentional/reckless).

The amendment was the Illinois General Assembly's direct legislative response to the Illinois Supreme Court's February 2023 decision in *Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc.*, which had held that a separate claim accrues on each scan or transmission — a reading that exposed White Castle to potentially $17 billion in damages from roughly 9,500 employees.

## The Seventh Circuit's Decision [HIGH confidence]

The consolidated appeal included three cases: *Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, *Gregg v. Central Transport LLC*, and *Willis v. Universal Intermodal Services*. In each, Illinois federal district courts had denied motions to dismiss or limit damages, holding the 2024 amendment did not reach pre-amendment conduct. The Seventh Circuit [reversed all three](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/04/03/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively-reversing-three-illinois-federal-court-decisions/).

Applying the Illinois retroactivity framework from *Commonwealth Edison Co. v. Will County Collector*, the panel concluded that the amendment is **remedial/procedural** rather than substantive, and therefore applies to pending cases. The court's reasoning rested on two principal points, per [Inside Privacy's analysis](https://www.insideprivacy.com/data-privacy/seventh-circuit-holds-that-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively/):

1. The amendment revised Section 20 (the damages/remedies provision) while leaving Section 15 (the substantive liability standards) untouched — signaling a remedial rather than rights-altering purpose.
2. The text of the amendment speaks directly to remedies, providing that an aggrieved person is 'entitled to, at most, one recovery.'

The panel also noted that the amendment does not impair any vested substantive right, since plaintiffs retain their cause of action and their ability to recover statutory damages — just not on a per-scan basis. Coverage from [Sidley's Data Matters blog](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/04/08/seventh-circuit-limits-potential-damages-under-bipa-holds-2024-amendment-applies-retroactively/) emphasizes that the court expressly rejected the argument that plaintiffs' prior 'per-scan' claims were vested property interests.

## Impact on Pending Litigation [HIGH confidence]

Per analyses from [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/illinois-damages-limitation-for-biometric-privacy-violations-applies-retroactively) and [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/privacy-counsel/damages-limiting-amendment-bipa-applies-retroactively), the ruling has three immediate consequences for the hundreds of pending BIPA class actions in Illinois federal courts:

- **Massive damages reduction.** Cases previously valued by reference to thousands of scans per class member now face a ceiling of one recovery per person.
- **Article III standing and CAFA jurisdiction challenges.** Many cases may now fail to meet CAFA's $5 million amount-in-controversy threshold and could be remanded to state court. Some individual claims may drop below Article III's concrete-injury threshold where no actual harm beyond the statutory violation is alleged.
- **Settlement recalibration.** Pending class settlements priced on per-scan exposure will be renegotiated; plaintiffs' leverage is sharply diminished.

## Open Questions [MEDIUM confidence]

- **State-court reception.** The Seventh Circuit's reading is a federal court's *Erie* prediction of Illinois law. The Illinois Supreme Court has not yet ruled on retroactivity, though lower Illinois appellate decisions have trended the same direction. A contrary Illinois Supreme Court ruling could disrupt the landscape.
- **Pre-*Cothron* conduct.** The decision addresses damages calculation, not whether claims accruing before *Cothron* remain viable under statute-of-limitations analyses that several Illinois trial courts have been wrestling with.
- **Section 15(a) and 15(e) claims.** The amendment's text focuses on Sections 15(b) and 15(d); treatment of other BIPA subsections may require further litigation.

## Action Items for Compliance and Litigation Teams

- Defendants in pending BIPA actions in the Seventh Circuit should promptly move to cap damages and reassess removal/remand posture.
- Reevaluate reserves, settlement authority, and insurance notice positions in light of the reduced exposure ceiling.
- Preserve arguments for extending *Clay*'s reasoning to cases pending in Illinois state court and to other BIPA subsections.
- Continue to maintain BIPA compliance programs (written policy, informed consent, retention schedule): the amendment reduces damages but does not alter Section 15 liability standards.
