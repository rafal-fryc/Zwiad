# 42-State AG Coalition Reaches $150 Million Settlement With 23andMe Over 2023 Genetic Data Breach

**Jurisdiction:** Multi-state (42 states and the District of Columbia, led by Connecticut) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 14, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On July 14, 2026, a coalition of 42 state attorneys general and the District of Columbia, led by Connecticut Attorney General William Tong, announced a settlement of bankruptcy claims against 23andMe (now Chrome Holding Co.) resolving multistate allegations that the company's unreasonable data security practices enabled the 2023 credential-stuffing breach that exposed the genetic and personal data of approximately 6.9 million customers worldwide ([Connecticut Attorney General press release](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-leads-multistate-settlement-of-bankruptcy-claims-against-23andme); [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-150-million-settlement-against-23andme-over-data-breach-exposed)). The settlement establishes $150 million in allowed claims against the 23andMe bankruptcy estate, but because bankruptcy-estate funds are finite and subject to competing creditor claims, participating states will actually recover approximately $18 million, to be distributed immediately from available estate funds ([Pennsylvania Attorney General press release](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/taking-action/ag-sunday-announces-18-million-national-settlement-with-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach/); [Delaware Attorney General press release](https://news.delaware.gov/2026/07/14/ag-jennings-announces-multistate-settlement-of-bankruptcy-claims-against-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach/)). This is the first coordinated multistate AG resolution of the 23andMe breach and is separate from both the $46.75 million federal bankruptcy class-action distribution approved in June 2026 and California Attorney General Rob Bonta's pending state-court GIPA/CCPA enforcement action, which remains unresolved.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The settlement was filed as a stipulation in the 23andMe Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceeding, *In re Chrome Holding Co.* (f/k/a 23andMe Holding Co.), Case No. 25-40976, U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Eastern District of Missouri, with the filing reported to have occurred the week of July 14, 2026, per [Regulatory Oversight (Troutman Pepper Locke)](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/state-ags-reach-novel-multistate-settlement-with-genetic-testing-company-23andme/).
- Forty-two jurisdictions joined the settlement: Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, the District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, New Hampshire, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, and Wisconsin, per [Connecticut AG press release](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-leads-multistate-settlement-of-bankruptcy-claims-against-23andme) and corroborating individual state releases from [South Carolina](https://www.scag.gov/about-the-office/news/attorney-general-alan-wilson-announces-multistate-settlement-of-bankruptcy-claims-against-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach/), [Maryland](https://oag.maryland.gov/News/pages/Attorney-General-Brown-Announces-Multistate-Settlement-of----Bankruptcy-Claims-Against-23andMe-Over-Genetic-Data-Breach-.aspx), [Georgia](https://law.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-07-14/carr-announces-multistate-settlement-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach), and [Minnesota](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/07/14_23andMe.asp).
- The states' investigation concluded that 23andMe engaged in unreasonable data security practices, specifically failing to: employ safeguards against credential-stuffing attacks; implement appropriate rate limiting or intrusion-prevention controls; deploy logging and monitoring tools capable of detecting a breach; investigate unusual login patterns; remediate known vulnerabilities; and adequately review and test design features — a reference to the "DNA Relatives" feature coding flaw that allowed attackers to pivot from roughly 14,000 compromised accounts to nearly 6.9 million exposed profiles, per [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/state-ags-reach-novel-multistate-settlement-with-genetic-testing-company-23andme/) and [Hunton Andrews Kurth Privacy and Cybersecurity Law Blog](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/connecticut-ag-leads-multistate-settlement-with-23andme-over-2023-data-breach).
- Of the $150 million allowed claim, the actual distributable recovery is $18 million, apportioned among participating states; individual state shares confirmed to date include Texas at $1,266,860, Connecticut at $887,729, Virginia at approximately $660,000, and Alabama at more than $260,000, per [Texas AG press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-150-million-settlement-against-23andme-over-data-breach-exposed), [Connecticut AG press release](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-leads-multistate-settlement-of-bankruptcy-claims-against-23andme), [WAVY.com](https://www.wavy.com/news/ag-jones-secures-660000-in-23andme-data-breach-settlement/amp/), and [Gulf Coast Media](https://gulfcoastmedia.com/stories/settlement-over-23andme-data-breach-to-bring-alabama-more-than-260000,339646).
- Beyond the monetary recovery, the resolution incorporates conduct terms negotiated as part of 23andMe's bankruptcy asset sale to TTAM Research Institute: enhanced data security requirements, a mandated comprehensive risk assessment, creation of an independent privacy/security advisory board, an agreement to be bound by applicable state privacy laws without exception, and continued availability of consumer data-deletion rights, per [Texas AG press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-150-million-settlement-against-23andme-over-data-breach-exposed) and [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/state-ags-reach-novel-multistate-settlement-with-genetic-testing-company-23andme/).
- The settlement is expressly described by participating AG offices and law-firm commentary as the largest multistate AG settlement to date involving a breach of consumer genetic data, and as a novel instance of state AGs negotiating consumer-protection conduct terms through a bankruptcy sale process rather than through traditional injunctive litigation, per [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/state-ags-reach-novel-multistate-settlement-with-genetic-testing-company-23andme/).
- The underlying breach ran from approximately April through September/October 2023 via credential stuffing that exploited reused login credentials, with the company publicly denying a breach had occurred before eventually confirming it and, according to the states, blaming affected consumers for their own password practices, per [Texas AG press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-150-million-settlement-against-23andme-over-data-breach-exposed).
- This multistate resolution is separate from the $46.75 million federal bankruptcy class-action distribution to individual breach victims approved on June 11, 2026 (see [related report](reports/privacy/enforcement-actions/federal-23andme-data-breach-settlement-2026-06-12.md)) and from California Attorney General Rob Bonta's still-pending state-court enforcement action, *People v. Chrome Holding Co.*, filed May 27, 2026 under GIPA, CCPA, and California's Reasonable Data Security Law (see [related report](reports/privacy/enforcement-actions/california-23andme-chrome-holding-breach-2026-05-29.md)); California's participation in this specific 42-state settlement could not be confirmed in the sources reviewed and appears to be proceeding on its separate enforcement track.

## Action Items

- **DTC genetic-testing and consumer-health-data companies** should treat the enumerated security failures (no credential-stuffing defenses, no rate limiting, inadequate logging/monitoring, unreviewed data-amplification features) as an explicit multistate AG enforcement checklist and remediate any matching gaps before an incident occurs, not after.
- **Compliance and privacy counsel for companies in bankruptcy or contemplating a distressed asset sale** should note that state AGs are now negotiating ongoing conduct terms (security standards, advisory-board oversight, deletion-rights preservation) directly into bankruptcy sale conditions — a template likely to recur in future distressed-company privacy matters.
- **Track the $18 million distribution process** against the 23andMe bankruptcy docket (Case No. 25-40976, E.D. Mo.) for confirmation of final per-state payouts and any court approval conditions.
- **Monitor the parallel California AG action** (*People v. Chrome Holding Co.*), which remains unresolved and could produce additional penalties or injunctive terms beyond what this multistate settlement covers.
- **Note the disclosure gap** between the $150 million headline figure and the $18 million actual recovery when advising clients or the public — multiple state releases and secondary coverage distinguish "allowed claims" from realizable bankruptcy-estate recovery, and client communications should preserve that distinction to avoid overstating the settlement's monetary value.
