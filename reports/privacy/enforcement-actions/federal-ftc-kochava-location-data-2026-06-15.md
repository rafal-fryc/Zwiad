# FTC v. Kochava: Proposed Order Imposes Restrictions on Sale of Sensitive Location Data

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-15

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 4, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission filed a [proposed stipulated final order](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc_v._kochava_-_proposed_stipulated_order.pdf) in the U.S. District Court for the District of Idaho resolving its nearly four-year enforcement action against data broker Kochava Inc. and its subsidiary Collective Data Solutions (CDS). The order prohibits Kochava and CDS from selling, licensing, or disclosing consumers' precise location data associated with sensitive venues — including healthcare facilities, places of worship, domestic violence shelters, LGBTQ+ venues, schools, and military installations — unless they first obtain the consumer's affirmative express consent and use the data only to provide a service the consumer directly requested. The settlement, which carries no monetary penalty, reinforces the FTC's position that selling sensitive location data without consent constitutes an unfair trade practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act and adds operational compliance requirements — supplier vetting, data deletion timelines, and consumer consent-withdrawal mechanisms — that will serve as an industry template.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Proposed order filed May 4, 2026:** The FTC filed the stipulated order in the District of Idaho (Case No. 2:22-cv-00377-BLW) on May 4, 2026; Kochava signed on March 2, 2026. The Commission approved the order 2-0. The order runs for ten years ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data); [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/ftc-v-kochava-inc)).

- **Original complaint (August 2022):** The FTC's complaint alleged Kochava sold customized geolocation data feeds derived from more than 61 million unique mobile device IDs in a single week, enabling purchasers to track individuals to reproductive health clinics, places of worship, homeless shelters, domestic violence shelters, and addiction recovery facilities ([FTC case timeline](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/ftc-v-kochava-inc-timeline-item-2026-05-04); [Justia docket](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/idaho/iddce/2:2022cv00377/50683)).

- **Litigation history:** Kochava preemptively filed its own suit against the FTC in August 2022. The Idaho District Court initially granted Kochava's motion to dismiss with leave to amend (May 2023), then unsealed an amended complaint in November 2023, and denied Kochava's motion to dismiss the amended complaint in 2024 ([Justia — Document 24](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/idaho/iddce/2:2022cv00377/50683/24/); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/enforcement-edge/2023/11/complaint-against-kochava)).

- **Legal basis:** The FTC proceeded entirely under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45(a), characterizing the sale of sensitive location data without consumer consent as an unfair trade practice. No HIPAA, CCPA, or other sector-specific statute was cited ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-bars-kochava-selling-sensitive-location-data)).

- **Prohibited sensitive location categories:** The order bars sale of location data revealing visits to medical and reproductive health facilities, places of religious worship, entities providing LGBTQ+ services or information, domestic violence shelters and rape crisis centers, schools and childcare providers, welfare and homeless shelters, correctional facilities, and military or federal law enforcement installations ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data); [Greenberg Traurig Data Privacy Dish](https://www.gtlaw-dataprivacydish.com/2026/05/ftc-bars-kochava-from-selling-sensitive-location-data/)).

- **Affirmative express consent requirement:** Outside the prohibited sensitive-location categories, Kochava and CDS may sell location data only if the consumer has given affirmative express consent and the data is used solely to provide a service the consumer directly requested ([GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/ftc-settlement-would-bar-kochava-from-selling-sensitive-location-data-without-consent)).

- **Operational compliance obligations:** Defendants must (1) implement a written Sensitive Location Data Program with quarterly accuracy assessments; (2) implement a Supplier Assessment Program to verify that all data suppliers obtained adequate consumer consent before collection; (3) provide consumers with a mechanism to withdraw consent and to request names of buyers who received their data; and (4) establish a data retention schedule. Two distinct deletion obligations apply and should not be conflated: (a) within **90 days** of the order's entry, defendants must de-identify or render non-sensitive all historical location data collected without verified consumer consent and certify compliance to the FTC; and (b) when a consumer submits a deletion request, defendants must complete deletion within **30 days** ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-remains-location-data-beat-agency-settles-long-running-kochava-enforcement-even); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/ftc-settles-data-broker-kochava-over-sale-sensitive-location-data-key-takeaways); [Proskauer New Media Law Blog](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2026/06/03/ftc-remains-on-the-location-data-beat-agency-settles-long-running-kochava-enforcement-even-as-state-regulation-has-already-changed-the-landscape/)).

- **No monetary penalty:** The FTC did not impose a civil monetary penalty. Kochava reportedly disclosed financial conditions that precluded a monetary settlement ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/privacy/the-ftc-bars-kochava-from-selling-sensitive-data-without-consent/)).

- **Enforcement pattern:** The order's terms closely parallel the FTC's 2024 consent orders against X-Mode/Outlogic, InMarket Media, Gravy Analytics/Venntel, and Mobilewalla, cementing a de facto regulatory standard for the location data broker industry ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20241216-ftc-continues-to-bring-enforcement-actions-against-data-brokers); [Proskauer New Media Law Blog](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2026/06/03/ftc-remains-on-the-location-data-beat-agency-settles-long-running-kochava-enforcement-even-as-state-regulation-has-already-changed-the-landscape/)).

## Action Items

- **Audit data supply chains now:** Any company purchasing, licensing, or receiving mobile location data must verify that upstream suppliers document consumer consent acquisition. The Supplier Assessment Program requirement applies directly to Kochava and CDS but signals the FTC expects supply-chain consent verification as a baseline industry practice.

- **Map sensitive location exposure:** Conduct a data inventory to identify whether any location datasets include signals from the FTC's prohibited sensitive location categories (medical, religious, LGBTQ+, shelter, school, military). Retain outside counsel to assess scope.

- **Review consent language and mechanisms:** Affirmative express consent — not implied, bundled, or buried consent — is the FTC's threshold. Review all consent flows in apps, SDKs, and partner agreements against this standard. Ensure users can withdraw consent and request disclosure of data buyers.

- **Establish data retention schedules:** The order sets a **90-day** deadline (from order entry) to de-identify or render non-sensitive all historical location data collected without verified consent, and requires **30-day** completion for consumer-initiated deletion requests. Companies should treat these windows as an emerging industry benchmark and build automated deletion workflows accordingly.

- **Monitor court approval:** The order is proposed and filed for court approval. Monitor the Idaho District Court docket (Case No. 2:22-cv-00377-BLW) for final entry. Terms are not legally binding on third parties until final, but FTC is expected to cite this order in future investigations.

- **Watch for state overlay:** Several states have enacted or are considering sensitive location data protections that independently require affirmative consent. The California AG ran a dedicated investigative sweep on the location data industry in early 2025. Compliance with the FTC order alone does not ensure compliance with state law.
