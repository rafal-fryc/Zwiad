# FTC Publishes FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan Prioritizing Children's Privacy and Data Security

## Overview [HIGH confidence]

On April 3, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission released its [FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/fy-2026-2030-strategic-plan), approved by a 2-0 Commission vote. The plan sets out three strategic goals covering consumer protection, competition enforcement, and agency operations, and restores language to the mission statement preserving enforcement "without unduly burdening legitimate business activity." Despite broader speculation about a retreat from privacy enforcement under the current Commission composition, the plan commits the agency to sustained privacy and data security work over the five-year horizon.

The plan describes children's online safety as "one of the most important consumer protection issues of our time" and highlights continuing enforcement of the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) together with newly delegated authorities under the TAKE IT DOWN Act and the Protecting Americans' Data from Foreign Adversaries Act (PADFAA).

## Key Enforcement Priorities [HIGH confidence]

The consumer protection goal identifies several enumerated priorities that will shape FTC enforcement through FY 2030:

- **Fraud and deception**, including imposter scams, subscription traps, and deceptive marketing.
- **Privacy and data security**, with continued Section 5 actions against unfair or deceptive data practices and security failures.
- **Unlawful telemarketing**, including Telemarketing Sales Rule and Do Not Call Registry enforcement.
- **Online harms to children**, including COPPA enforcement and the new TAKE IT DOWN Act regime.
- **Sensitive data collection and sale**, including data broker practices and cross-border data flows.

**Children's privacy (COPPA).** The plan signals ongoing COPPA enforcement, which prohibits covered operators from collecting, using, or disclosing personal information of children under 13 without verifiable parental consent. Analysis of the draft plan highlighted a material expansion of children's-safety language from the prior FY 2022-2026 plan, which has carried through to the final document.

**TAKE IT DOWN Act.** The [TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146/text) (S. 146), signed into law on May 19, 2025, criminalizes the nonconsensual publication of intimate images, including AI-generated "digital forgeries," and establishes a notice-and-removal regime for covered platforms. A platform's failure to reasonably comply with notice-and-removal obligations is treated as a violation of Section 18(a)(1)(B) of the FTC Act. Covered platforms must remove flagged depictions within 48 hours of notice, with a compliance deadline of **May 19, 2026**.

**PADFAA.** The [Protecting Americans' Data from Foreign Adversaries Act of 2024](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/7520/text) prohibits data brokers from selling, licensing, or otherwise transferring U.S. persons' personally identifiable sensitive data (health, financial, genetic, biometric, geolocation, sexual behavior, credentials, government IDs) to China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, or entities they control. Enforcement is through the FTC Act with civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation. In February 2026, the FTC sent warning letters to 13 data brokers, and the strategic plan formalizes PADFAA enforcement as a near-term priority.

## Compliance Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

Organizations within the FTC's consumer protection jurisdiction should treat the strategic plan as an operational roadmap rather than aspirational rhetoric:

- **Platforms hosting user-generated visual content** must stand up TAKE IT DOWN Act notice-and-removal procedures by **May 19, 2026**, including a designated intake channel, a 48-hour removal SLA, and documented identity-verification and good-faith review steps.
- **Operators of child-directed services** should revisit COPPA compliance programs, parental-consent mechanisms, data minimization, retention limits, and third-party disclosures in light of the amended COPPA Rule.
- **Data brokers and adtech intermediaries** trafficking in sensitive categories (geolocation, health, biometric, financial) should complete PADFAA diligence on downstream purchasers and contractually prohibit onward transfer to covered foreign adversaries.
- **All privacy-regulated businesses** should expect continued Section 5 enforcement in areas well-developed in the prior Commission: dark patterns, undisclosed data sharing, security misrepresentations, and subscription cancellation frictions.

The inclusion of "without unduly burdening legitimate business activity" in the mission statement likely signals proportionality in remedies and a narrower appetite for novel theories, but does not weaken existing Section 5 or statute-specific authorities.

## Timeline [HIGH confidence]

- **April 24, 2024** — PADFAA signed into law as part of H.R. 815.
- **June 23, 2024** — PADFAA effective date.
- **May 19, 2025** — TAKE IT DOWN Act signed into law.
- **October 2025** — FTC publishes draft FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan for public comment (Docket FTC-2025-0660).
- **February 2026** — FTC sends PADFAA warning letters to 13 data brokers.
- **April 3, 2026** — FTC approves and publishes final FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan, 2-0 vote.
- **May 19, 2026** — Covered-platform compliance deadline under the TAKE IT DOWN Act notice-and-removal regime.
- **FY 2030** — Strategic plan horizon closes; next cycle planning begins.
