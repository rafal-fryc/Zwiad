# FTC Bans Kochava and Subsidiary from Selling Sensitive Location Data Without Consent

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-05-08

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The Federal Trade Commission has obtained a stipulated final order barring data broker Kochava Inc. and its subsidiary, Collective Data Solutions (CDS), from selling, sharing, or disclosing consumers' precise location data associated with sensitive locations unless they first obtain affirmative express consent, concluding a nearly four-year federal lawsuit originally filed in August 2022. The order — filed May 4, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Idaho (Case No. 2:22-cv-00377-BLW) — also requires deletion or de-identification of all historical location data collected without consent and mandates a supplier assessment program, data retention schedule, and consumer-facing consent-withdrawal mechanism. The action cements the FTC's enforcement posture that selling sensitive location data without consumer consent constitutes an unfair trade practice under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, building on prior orders against X-Mode/Outlogic, InMarket, Gravy Analytics/Venntel, and Mobilewalla.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Order filed May 4, 2026:** The FTC filed the stipulated order in the District of Idaho on May 4, 2026; defendants signed on March 2, 2026. The Commission vote approving the order was 2-0. The order terminates in ten years ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data); [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/ftc-v-kochava-inc)).

- **Scope of original allegations:** The FTC's original August 2022 complaint alleged Kochava sold customized geolocation data feeds — derived from more than 61 million unique mobile devices in a single week — that enabled purchasers to track individuals' visits to reproductive health clinics, places of worship, homeless shelters, domestic violence shelters, and addiction recovery facilities ([FTC 2022 press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/08/ftc-sues-kochava-selling-data-tracks-people-reproductive-health-clinics-places-worship-other)).

- **Litigation history:** The case required three complaints. An Idaho district court initially ruled in May 2023 that the FTC needed stronger harm allegations, then denied Kochava's motion to dismiss in February 2024 after the FTC filed its first amended complaint (filed June 5, 2023) alleging two theories of consumer injury: increased risk of stigma, discrimination, and physical violence, and invasion of privacy. The FTC subsequently filed a second amended complaint on July 15, 2024, adding Collective Data Solutions (CDS) as a defendant after Kochava transferred its data broker business to that subsidiary. The case proceeded under Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45 ([EPIC opinion, Feb. 2024](https://epic.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/FTC-v-Kochava-22-377-opinion-mtd-020324.pdf); [FTC Second Amended Complaint, July 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/86-SecondAmendedComplaint.pdf); [ABA analysis](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/newsletters/ftc-v-kochava-whats-next/)).

- **Core prohibition — sensitive location data:** The order bars Kochava and CDS from selling, licensing, transferring, sharing, or disclosing "sensitive location data" — defined as precise location data associated with medical facilities, religious organizations, sites predominantly providing education or childcare to minors, homeless shelters, domestic violence shelters, and military or federal law enforcement sites — unless three conditions are simultaneously satisfied: (1) the defendant has a direct relationship with the consumer tied to that specific location, (2) the consumer has provided affirmative express consent, and (3) the data is used only to provide a service directly requested by the consumer ([Proposed Stipulated Order, p. 2 of 26](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc_v._kochava_-_proposed_stipulated_order.pdf)).

- **Affirmative express consent defined:** The order's consent standard requires a "freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication of agreement" following a clear and conspicuous disclosure of: the categories of information collected, the purposes for collection, and who will receive the data. Pre-checked boxes and bundled consent do not satisfy this definition ([Proposed Stipulated Order](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc_v._kochava_-_proposed_stipulated_order.pdf); [ppc.land analysis](https://ppc.land/ftc-closes-kochava-location-data-case-with-strict-consent-rules/)).

- **Historical data — 90-day de-identification deadline:** Within 90 days of the order's entry, defendants must de-identify or render non-sensitive all historical precise location data collected prior to the order without affirmative express consent. The narrow exception: defendants may retain historical data if they can produce records demonstrating the consumer consented to its collection, use, and disclosure ([Proposed Stipulated Order](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc_v._kochava_-_proposed_stipulated_order.pdf)).

- **Required compliance programs:** The order mandates: (1) a Sensitive Location Data Program establishing a comprehensive list of sensitive locations to prevent unauthorized disclosures; (2) a Supplier Assessment Program to verify that all location data acquired from third parties was collected with appropriate consumer consent; (3) a data retention schedule requiring deletion on an established timeframe; and (4) incident reporting to the FTC when third parties share consumers' precise location data in violation of contractual requirements ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data)).

- **Consumer rights:** The order grants consumers the right to request the names of any business or individual to whom their precise location data was sold, and requires an easy mechanism to withdraw consent for the sale of their device's precise location data ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data); [The Record from Recorded Future News](https://therecord.media/ftc-bans-kochava-location-data-sales)).

- **Enforcement pattern:** The Kochava order is the FTC's fifth major location data broker action in roughly two years, following orders against X-Mode/Outlogic (January 2024), InMarket (January 2024), Gravy Analytics/Venntel (December 2024), and Mobilewalla (December 2024). All orders use similar legal theory: selling precise location data without consent is an "unfair" practice under FTC Act Section 5 ([FTC blog post on X-Mode and InMarket](https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2024/03/ftc-cracks-down-mass-data-collectors-closer-look-avast-x-mode-inmarket); [WilmerHale analysis](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20240209-recent-enforcement-actions-signal-ftc-focus-on-protecting-location-data)).

## Action Items

- **Data brokers and location data companies:** Audit all location data inventory immediately. Any data associated with the sensitive categories defined in the order — medical, religious, education/childcare, shelters, military/law enforcement — requires documented affirmative express consent before it can be sold or shared. Pre-checked boxes and combined consent are insufficient.

- **Assess supplier chain:** If your business acquires location data from third parties, implement a supplier assessment process (as required of Kochava) to verify that upstream collection was consent-compliant. Contractual representations alone are insufficient without verification mechanisms.

- **Review historical data retention practices:** Implement a documented data retention schedule with deletion timelines. If you hold historical location data without consent records, begin de-identification planning now.

- **Consumer-facing consent and opt-out:** Ensure that your data collection disclosures clearly specify: the categories of data, the precise purposes, and all recipients. Build a consent-withdrawal mechanism that allows consumers to stop the sale of their location data.

- **Incident reporting policy:** Establish procedures to detect and report to appropriate regulators when third-party contractual recipients misuse location data. The FTC order foreshadows future enforcement requiring incident reporting as standard practice.

- **Monitor for court approval:** The stipulated order was filed but requires final court entry. Confirm status at the [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/ftc-v-kochava-inc) and track the 90-day historical data deadline from the entry date.
