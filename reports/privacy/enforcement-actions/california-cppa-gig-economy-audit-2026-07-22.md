# CalPrivacy Launches First Sectoral Privacy Audit, Targeting Gig Economy Platforms

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-22

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On July 21, 2026, the California Privacy Protection Agency's ("CalPrivacy" or "CPPA") newly formed Audits Division announced its first formal privacy audit, targeting gig economy platforms — app-based transportation, delivery, and task-services companies operating in California — for compliance with the CCPA's consumer rights provisions [California Privacy Protection Agency press release](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/07/california-privacy-protection-agency-launches-first-sectoral-audit-targets-gig-economy-platforms/). <!-- verified --> The audit is the first in what CalPrivacy describes as a planned series of industry-wide "sectoral" audits, modeled on the FTC's Section 6(b) market-study approach, and responds to hundreds of consumer complaints and comments received during CalPrivacy's public rulemaking process [Skadden client alert](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/07/calprivacys-new-audits-division). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch, including attribution of the "hundreds of consumer complaints and comments" framing to Executive Director Tom Kemp -- report does not over-attribute this framing to a specific speaker, so no discrepancy -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [NEEDS REVIEW]

- CalPrivacy's Audits Division — formed in February 2026 and led by Chief Privacy Auditor Sabrina Boyson Ross, formerly of Meta and Uber — opened the agency's first formal CCPA compliance audit on July 21, 2026, focused on gig economy platforms operating in California [privacy.ca.gov press release](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/07/california-privacy-protection-agency-launches-first-sectoral-audit-targets-gig-economy-platforms/); [CPPA Chief Auditor appointment announcement](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/02/california-privacy-protection-agency-names-sabrina-boyson-ross-as-chief-auditor/). <!-- verified: independent WebSearch confirms Feb. 3, 2026 appointment announcement and Ross's Meta (Director of Public Policy) and Uber (Global Head of Policy, Marketplace; Legal Director) background -->
- The audit examines whether gig platforms comply with CCPA consumer rights — particularly the right to access and control personal information — by testing whether platforms respond to access requests within the statutory timeframe, whether responses are complete, and whether platform systems let individuals meaningfully exercise their rights [Hunton Andrews Kurth Privacy and Information Security Law Blog](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/calprivacy-targets-gig-economy-tech-platforms-in-first-ccpa-compliance-audit); [Pillsbury Consumer Protection Dispatch](https://consumer-protection-dispatch.pillsburylaw.com/calprivacy-formal-privacy-audit-targeting-gig-economy-platforms/). <!-- verified -->
- Chief Auditor Sabrina Ross stated that "[c]ore to California's privacy rights is ensuring people have the power to access and understand the information that a company collects about them," and that "[f]or gig workers in California, the right of access is vital because it may directly impact their livelihood," adding that a worker "cannot contest a decision made by an algorithm without the underlying data" [privacy.ca.gov press release](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/07/california-privacy-protection-agency-launches-first-sectoral-audit-targets-gig-economy-platforms/); this quote is also reported by [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/news/calprivacy-launches-first-audit-gig-economy-delivery-ride-share-apps-under-scrutiny/). **These quotes are corroborated by independent search-result excerpts from multiple outlets, though to date only the second quote ("the right of access is vital...") has been independently verbatim-confirmed via WebSearch across multiple review passes; none of the three quotes have been confirmed against a direct read of the full press release text — see the verification note below.** <!-- needs-human-review: round-3 independent WebSearch verbatim-confirmed the second quote ("the right of access is vital because it may directly impact their livelihood"). - Gig platforms collect extensive categories of personal information relevant to the audit's scope, including precise geolocation data, behavioral and performance metrics, biometric identification data, financial information, and communications records [Skadden client alert](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/07/calprivacys-new-audits-division). <!-- verified -->
- The audit is expected to culminate in a public report on industrywide CCPA compliance in the gig sector, and findings may be referred directly to CalPrivacy's Enforcement Division, which holds broad subpoena power to compel documents and testimony [Pillsbury Consumer Protection Dispatch](https://consumer-protection-dispatch.pillsburylaw.com/calprivacy-formal-privacy-audit-targeting-gig-economy-platforms/). <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- Gig economy platforms (transportation, delivery, and task-based services) operating in California should conduct an internal review now of their CCPA right-of-access workflows: confirm that access requests are fulfilled within the statutory 45-day window (extendable by 45 days), that responses are complete (including algorithmically-derived performance and rating data used in worker-facing decisions), and that verification and submission mechanisms are functional for contractor users.
- Legal and compliance teams should prepare for the possibility of an unannounced or announced audit request, including document preservation practices consistent with CalPrivacy's Enforcement Division subpoena authority.
- Businesses outside the gig sector should treat this as an early signal of CalPrivacy's audit methodology (FTC 6(b)-style sectoral review) and benchmark their own access-request handling, especially where automated decision-making technology (ADMT) affects individuals' livelihood or opportunities, given CalPrivacy's parallel ADMT rulemaking.
- Monitor CalPrivacy's newsroom (privacy.ca.gov) and the Audits Division's public report on this sector for findings that may inform future enforcement priorities or referrals.
- **Verification follow-up:** A reviewer with working WebFetch/browser access should (1) directly open the CPPA press release to confirm the three Ross quotes verbatim (the second quote is independently corroborated; the first and third remain unconfirmed against the primary source), and (2) confirm or correct the $3.1 million CalPrivacy investigations budget figure against the Bloomberg Law article text or CPPA's FY2026-27 budget materials (Org 1703) before either claim is used in a verbatim client-facing deliverable.
