# Texas AG Gearing Up for Aggressive Enforcement as TDPSA Takes Effect July 1, 2024

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2024-06-10

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton announced on June 4, 2024, the creation of a dedicated Privacy and Technology enforcement team within the Consumer Protection Division of the Office of the Attorney General (OAG), less than four weeks before the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA) took effect on July 1, 2024. The team — [described by the OAG as poised to become "among the largest in the country"](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-launches-data-privacy-and-security-initiative-protect-texans-sensitive) focused on privacy law enforcement — was reported by the [Texas Tribune to comprise more than ten attorneys at launch](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/06/04/texas-ken-paxton-data-privacy-security/), with further growth anticipated. The team's mandate covers not only the TDPSA but also Texas's full suite of privacy-adjacent statutes: the Data Broker Law, the Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI), the Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act, the Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA), and concurrent enforcement of federal laws including COPPA and HIPAA. By framing its posture as "aggressive enforcement," the OAG signaled clearly that the TDPSA's effective date was an operational enforcement milestone, not merely a compliance deadline. Companies that process personal data of Texas consumers — particularly data brokers, ad tech firms, and businesses collecting sensitive or biometric data — face materially elevated enforcement risk.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Texas Legislative History

Texas enacted the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act as [House Bill 4 (88th Regular Session, 2023)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB00004F.htm), signed by Governor Greg Abbott in June 2023. The statute is codified at [Chapter 541 of the Texas Business and Commerce Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.541.htm), which took effect July 1, 2024 (with a carve-out for global opt-out mechanism recognition, which took effect January 1, 2025).

The TDPSA occupies a Virginia-model framework: it grants Texas residents rights to access, correct, delete, and port their personal data, and to opt out of processing for targeted advertising, sale, and consequential profiling. Enforcement is vested exclusively in the OAG, with no private right of action. As enacted, the statute requires the AG to issue a written notice of violation and allow a 30-day cure period before commencing a civil action — a cure right that the TDPSA makes permanent (no sunset), in contrast to the time-limited cure windows in Colorado, Connecticut, and other states.

Texas enacted the TDPSA against a backdrop of several pre-existing privacy statutes that would continue to run in parallel:

- **Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI):** Texas's biometric data law, which the OAG had already deployed in February 2022 when it [sued Meta for biometric data collection](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/paxton-sues-facebook-using-unauthorized-biometric-data) without consent. That case settled in July 2024 for [$1.4 billion](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-leads-nation-protecting-americans-data-privacy-and-security-big-tech), demonstrating the AG's willingness and capacity to pursue large-scale privacy litigation.
- **Texas Data Broker Law:** Requires data brokers to register with the Texas Secretary of State and pay an annual fee. Non-registration is itself an enforceable violation.
- **Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act:** A pre-existing general data security statute.
- **Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA):** A broad consumer protection statute the AG uses as a catch-all hook for data privacy and AI-related conduct.

### National Context

Texas's investment in enforcement infrastructure arrives at an inflection point in state privacy enforcement nationally. In 2024-2025, a wave of comprehensive state privacy laws took effect in Florida, Oregon, Texas, New Jersey, Montana, and other states, creating a landscape in which multiple AGs were simultaneously building or expanding dedicated enforcement teams. The Texas initiative was one of the most publicly prominent — the June 4, 2024 announcement named the team, described its scope, and used the phrase "aggressive enforcement" to characterize the OAG's intentions.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The Privacy and Technology Enforcement Team

On June 4, 2024, the [OAG announced the formation of the Privacy and Technology enforcement team](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-launches-data-privacy-and-security-initiative-protect-texans-sensitive) within the Consumer Protection Division. The press release described the team as:

- Tasked with "aggressive enforcement of Texas privacy laws"
- Poised to become "among the largest in the country" focused on privacy law enforcement
- Comprising attorneys, privacy analysts, technologists, and support staff

The [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/06/04/texas-ken-paxton-data-privacy-security/) reported that the unit exceeded ten attorneys at the time of the announcement. Later reporting from [The Texas Lawbook](https://texaslawbook.net/texas-emerges-as-a-leading-force-in-state-privacy-law-enforcement/) confirmed the team's size at roughly 20 personnel, citing Tyler Bridegan, Director of Privacy and Tech Enforcement at the OAG.

### Statutory Framework: TDPSA Key Provisions

The TDPSA's enforcement architecture establishes the legal basis for the team's work. Key provisions from [Chapter 541 of the Texas Business and Commerce Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.541.htm) include:

**Applicability.** The TDPSA applies to persons who conduct business in Texas or produce products or services consumed by Texas residents, and that during a calendar year (a) control or process personal data of at least 100,000 consumers, or (b) control or process personal data of at least 25,000 consumers and derive over 25% of gross revenue from the sale of personal data. Notably, small businesses as defined by the federal Small Business Administration are generally exempt — except that any small business that sells sensitive personal data must first obtain consumer consent.

**Consumer Rights.** The Act grants consumers rights to: (i) confirm whether a controller is processing their personal data; (ii) correct inaccuracies; (iii) delete personal data; (iv) obtain a portable copy; and (v) opt out of processing for targeted advertising, sale of personal data, and profiling in furtherance of significant decisions.

**Controller Obligations.** Controllers must: provide a clear and accessible privacy notice; respond to consumer requests within 45 days; limit data collection to what is adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary; conduct data protection assessments for high-risk processing (targeted advertising, sale, sensitive data, profiling with legal effect); and for sensitive data processing, obtain consumer consent. Controllers selling personal data must include a clear, conspicuous disclosure that they do so.

**Sensitive Data.** The TDPSA defines sensitive data to include precise geolocation data, racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health diagnosis, sexual orientation, citizenship or immigration status, genetic or biometric data, and personal data of a child.

**Enforcement and Penalties.** The OAG has exclusive enforcement authority. Before bringing a civil action, the AG must issue written notice of violation and allow 30 days to cure. If the violation is cured, the AG may not bring a civil action solely on that violation. If uncured, the AG may seek civil penalties of up to **$7,500 per violation**, injunctive relief, and reasonable attorney's fees. There is no private right of action.

**Processor Obligations.** Processors (entities processing personal data on behalf of a controller) must cooperate with controllers' compliance obligations. All controller-processor processing must be governed by a contract incorporating TDPSA-required privacy provisions.

### Early Enforcement Activity

The team's formation was quickly followed by enforcement activity. Within the first year of TDPSA operations:

- The OAG [conducted investigations into data practices of more than 200 companies](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-leads-nation-protecting-americans-data-privacy-and-security-big-tech), including data brokers.
- The OAG [notified over 100 companies](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-notifies-over-100-companies-their-apparent-failure-comply-texas-data) of apparent failure to register under the Texas Data Broker Law.
- The OAG issued violation notices to companies including SiriusXM, MyRadar, Miles, and Tapestri for unauthorized data sharing.
- The OAG opened a consumer privacy complaint portal that [received over 2,000 complaints](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-leads-nation-protecting-americans-data-privacy-and-security-big-tech) in its first year of operation.
- In August 2024, the OAG sued General Motors for collecting and selling drivers' private data.
- In October 2024, the OAG sued TikTok for violations of Texas parental consent laws involving minors' data.
- On January 13, 2025, the OAG filed the [first-ever enforcement action under a U.S. state comprehensive data privacy law](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-allstate-and-arity-unlawfully-collecting-using-and-selling-over-45), suing Allstate and subsidiary Arity for secretly harvesting geolocation data from 45 million consumers via SDKs embedded in third-party apps.

### Multi-Statute Enforcement Mandate

A key feature of the team's design — and a signal to businesses — is the deliberately broad statutory mandate. By assigning enforcement of CUBI, the Data Broker Law, the DTPA, and federal statutes alongside the TDPSA to the same team, the OAG structured its capacity to pursue privacy violations through multiple legal hooks simultaneously. A single set of facts (e.g., collection of geolocation data without consent) may give rise to liability under TDPSA (sensitive data consent violation), CUBI (biometric data), the Data Broker Law (unregistered operation), and the DTPA (deceptive trade practice). The Allstate/Arity complaint in January 2025 demonstrated exactly this approach, asserting claims under multiple Texas statutes in a single action.

[Perkins Coie's analysis](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/texas-ag-turns-heat-privacy-and-data-security) noted that the DTPA is particularly significant because it provides the AG with enforcement tools against data practices not squarely within the TDPSA's scope, such as AI-related data uses and deceptive disclosures.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Highest-Risk Industries and Practices

The team's enforcement targets since June 2024 reveal the OAG's priority areas:

**Data brokers** face near-term, concrete compliance obligations: the Data Broker Law requires registration with the Texas Secretary of State, and the OAG demonstrated at scale (100+ notices in 2024) that it will actively identify and pursue non-registrants. Any company deriving revenue from processing or transferring personal data of more than 50,000 individuals without direct collection must assess registration requirements.

**Ad tech, location data, and behavioral analytics companies** face heightened risk from the combination of TDPSA sensitive data consent requirements and the OAG's express focus on geolocation data. The Allstate/Arity action makes clear that SDK-embedded data harvesting is an enforcement priority.

**Companies collecting biometric data** face CUBI enforcement risk irrespective of TDPSA compliance status. The $1.4 billion Meta biometric settlement established that the OAG will pursue large-scale CUBI actions, and the multi-statute enforcement model means CUBI violations may be asserted alongside TDPSA claims.

**Consumer-facing technology companies with large Texas user bases** — particularly those with high complaint volumes — face investigation risk given the consumer complaint portal. With 2,000+ complaints received in year one, the portal is an active investigation pipeline.

**AI companies** face increasing attention: the OAG's use of the DTPA to pursue AI-related data practices (and the January 2025 lawsuit against LinkedIn for using data to train AI models without consent) signals that the team's mandate extends to AI data governance, not merely classical privacy compliance.

### Compliance Timeline Considerations

**July 1, 2024:** TDPSA effective date — the core obligations (consumer rights, privacy notices, controller-processor contracts, data protection assessments) were required on this date.

**January 1, 2025:** Global opt-out mechanism recognition requirement took effect under the TDPSA. Controllers processing personal data for targeted advertising or sale of personal data must honor consumer opt-out requests submitted via recognized universal opt-out mechanisms.

**Cure period:** The TDPSA's 30-day cure right is permanent (no sunset). Upon receipt of a written notice of violation, a company has 30 days to cure the violation and provide written attestation, consumer notification (where contact information is available), documentation of cure, and evidence of internal policy changes. Failure to cure within 30 days exposes the company to civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation.

### Enforcement Risk Assessment

The OAG's announcement of "aggressive enforcement" was not rhetorical. Within 18 months of the team's formation, the office: investigated 200+ companies, sent 100+ data broker notices, launched a public complaint portal, secured the $1.4 billion Meta CUBI settlement, sued General Motors, TikTok, and (first under any comprehensive state privacy law) Allstate/Arity. [Morrison Foerster's November 2024 analysis](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/241112-texas-privacy-enforcement-heats-up) characterized the pace as among the most active of any state AG privacy enforcement program in the country.

## Action Items

- **Complete TDPSA compliance immediately.** If not already done, audit controller obligations: privacy notice, data minimization, consumer rights request procedures (two or more methods; 45-day response window), data protection assessments for high-risk processing, and controller-processor contracts.
- **Audit sensitive data processing.** The TDPSA requires consent before processing sensitive data. Map all processing of precise geolocation, biometric, health, racial/ethnic origin, children's data, and other sensitive categories to ensure consent is obtained prior to processing.
- **Register as a data broker if applicable.** Any company deriving revenue from processing or transferring personal data of more than 50,000 individuals collected from a source other than the individuals directly should assess Texas Data Broker Law registration requirements urgently — the OAG has issued 100+ non-registration notices.
- **Audit biometric data practices under CUBI.** CUBI exposure is independent of TDPSA. Any collection, capture, or use of biometric identifiers (including facial geometry, voiceprint, retina/iris scan) requires consent and compliance with retention and destruction schedules.
- **Implement global opt-out mechanism recognition** (required since January 1, 2025) for targeted advertising and data sale.
- **Establish a violation notice response protocol.** Design and test a 30-day cure response workflow: intake, legal review, cure documentation, consumer notification, and written attestation to the OAG. The cure period is a procedural safeguard, not an indefinite extension — failure to cure within 30 days triggers civil penalty exposure.
- **Review SDK and third-party data relationships.** The Allstate/Arity action targets downstream use of data harvested by embedded SDKs. Controllers should audit data obtained via third-party SDKs, data brokers, and API integrations for TDPSA and Data Broker Law compliance.
- **Monitor enforcement actions.** Track OAG press releases and enforcement filings to identify newly emerging enforcement theories, especially in AI data use and geolocation data sectors.
