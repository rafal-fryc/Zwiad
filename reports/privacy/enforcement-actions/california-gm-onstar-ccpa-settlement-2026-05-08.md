# California Secures Record $12.75 Million CCPA Settlement with General Motors Over Driver Telematics Data

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** May 8, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 8, 2026, California Attorney General Rob Bonta, together with multiple county district attorneys and the California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy/CPPA), announced a [$12.75 million settlement](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general) with General Motors and its connected vehicle service OnStar. The settlement resolves allegations that GM sold precise geolocation and driving behavior data of hundreds of thousands of California consumers to data brokers LexisNexis Risk Solutions and Verisk Analytics — without adequate notice or consent — generating approximately $20 million in nationwide revenue. This is the largest CCPA penalty ever imposed and marks California's first enforcement action targeting the CCPA's data minimization and purpose limitation requirements, both added by the CPRA amendments effective January 1, 2023.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The settlement is subject to court approval and totals **$12.75 million in civil penalties** — nearly five times the prior record CCPA settlement of $2.75 million reached with Disney in February 2026, as reported by [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/california-authorities-announce-largest-ccpa-fine-to-date/).
- From **2020 through 2024**, GM collected driver names, contact information, precise geolocation data, and driving behavior metrics (speed, hard braking, rapid acceleration) through the OnStar platform and sold that data to [LexisNexis Risk Solutions and Verisk Analytics](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general), per the California AG press release.
- Both data brokers acquired the data to build **insurance risk-scoring products** marketed to auto insurers for setting premium rates — a use materially incompatible with GM's stated data collection purposes of emergency services and driving skill improvement, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations).
- The California AG's complaint alleged GM's own privacy notices **explicitly stated it would not sell driving or location data**, making the conduct a violation of the California False Advertising Law in addition to the CCPA, per [Calmatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/05/gm-record-california-penalty-onstar-data/).
- The enforcement was brought jointly by the Attorney General, **multiple county district attorneys**, and the **California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)**, reflecting a coordinated, multi-agency enforcement posture, per the [California AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general).
- The specific CCPA provisions at issue are **Civil Code § 1798.100(c)** (data minimization — personal information must be collected, used, retained, and shared only to the extent reasonably necessary and proportionate to the disclosed purposes) and the associated purpose limitation requirement, both enacted via CPRA in 2023, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations).
- Statutes violated also include the **California Unfair Competition Law** and the **California False Advertising Law**, per [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/california-announces-largest-ccpa-penalty-to-date-and-first-data-minimization-enforcement-action).
- The AG's complaint characterizes this as the **first CCPA enforcement action expressly targeting data minimization and purpose limitation**, per [DLA Piper Privacy Matters](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/05/californias-gm-settlement-has-data-minimization-finally-arrived/).

## Settlement Terms [HIGH confidence]

Under the final judgment, GM agreed to:

1. **Stop selling driving data** to any consumer reporting agency, including LexisNexis and Verisk, for **five years**, per [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/cgm-onstar-ccpa-settlement-12-75m-privacy-enforcement/).
2. **Delete retained driving data within 180 days** unless the consumer provides affirmative, express consent for its continued use, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations).
3. **Request deletion** of its driving data by LexisNexis and Verisk, per the [California AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general).
4. **Provide clear privacy notices** at OnStar enrollment and **obtain explicit, separate consent** for each distinct use or sharing of covered driving data, per [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/california-announces-largest-ccpa-penalty-to-date-and-first-data-minimization-enforcement-action).
5. **Build and maintain a privacy compliance program** for five years, with annual reports signed by the Chief Privacy Officer, reviewed by the General Counsel and CEO, and submitted to California regulators (DOJ, participating district attorneys, and CPPA), per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations).
6. **Conduct and submit ongoing privacy risk assessments** to California regulators, per [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/california-announces-multi-million-data-minimization-settlement-with-gm).

## Industry Implications [HIGH confidence]

This settlement has outsized significance beyond General Motors for three reasons:

**First enforcement of data minimization.** California's CPRA (Prop 24, enacted 2020, effective 2023) added data minimization and purpose limitation requirements to the CCPA that had not previously been the subject of a public enforcement action. This settlement establishes that regulators will evaluate the data collected against the purposes disclosed to consumers — and will not simply defer to a business's self-assessment of necessity, per [DLA Piper Privacy Matters](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/05/californias-gm-settlement-has-data-minimization-finally-arrived/).

**Connected vehicle and telematics sector put on notice.** The automotive industry's practice of collecting continuous telematics data through embedded systems like OnStar, then monetizing that data with insurers and data brokers, is directly implicated. Automakers, telematics service providers, and any business operating vehicle-connected systems that collect location or behavior data face materially elevated enforcement risk, per [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/cgm-onstar-ccpa-settlement-12-75m-privacy-enforcement/).

**Data brokers and downstream buyers.** The requirement that GM affirmatively request deletion from LexisNexis and Verisk signals that California views downstream data brokers as linked to the original violation. Companies that purchase vehicle or location data from OEM partners should assess whether that data was collected with consent adequate for the specific downstream use, per [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102msc8/from-paper-compliance-to-technical-verification-the-12-75m-gm-settlement-an).

**"Technical verification" vs. paper compliance.** Analysis from [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102msc8/from-paper-compliance-to-technical-verification-the-12-75m-gm-settlement-an) notes that the settlement's requirement for ongoing privacy assessments submitted to regulators suggests California is moving toward verifiable technical controls, not just policy representations — a significant shift in enforcement methodology.

## Action Items

Affected organizations — particularly those in the automotive, telematics, insurance, and data broker sectors — should take the following steps:

1. **Audit data collection against disclosed purposes.** Map every category of personal data your systems collect against the purposes stated in your privacy notice. Flag any data collected or retained for purposes that were not disclosed at the time of collection. Complete this audit within 60 days.

2. **Review third-party data-sharing agreements.** Identify all data broker, analytics, or insurance-sector partners who receive vehicle, location, or behavioral data. Confirm that the original consent collected is adequate for the downstream use. Terminate or renegotiate contracts where consent is insufficient.

3. **Implement purpose limitation controls.** Do not rely solely on contractual representations. Build technical controls — data tagging, access restrictions, automated retention limits — that enforce the stated purpose boundary. The "technical verification" framing in post-settlement commentary suggests regulators will probe these controls.

4. **Revise consent flows.** GM's consent failure stemmed in part from bundling multiple data uses into a single disclosure. Under this settlement's precedent, each distinct data use or sharing relationship requires separate, explicit consent. Review enrollment and onboarding flows accordingly.

5. **Assess data deletion obligations.** If your organization is retaining vehicle, location, or behavioral data beyond what is reasonably necessary for the original disclosed purpose, begin a data minimization remediation program. Under the CCPA's data minimization requirement (§ 1798.100(c)), retention beyond necessity is a standalone violation.

6. **Prepare for multi-agency inquiries.** The joint enforcement by the AG, CPPA, and county district attorneys signals that future CCPA investigations may involve multiple simultaneous regulators. Ensure your privacy governance structure (Chief Privacy Officer, board-level reporting, documented risk assessments) is defensible to multiple reviewers.

7. **Monitor settlement approval.** The settlement is subject to court approval. Track the docket for the final order, which will confirm effective dates for the compliance program and privacy assessment reporting requirements.
