# CalPrivacy Delete Act Enforcement Sweep: Registration Uptick, DROP Deadline, and $200/Day Penalties

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** May 8, 2026

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy / CPPA) announced at its [May 2026 board meeting](https://iapp.org/news/a/calprivacy-explores-data-broker-enforcement-uptick-eu-adequacy-prospects) that it is ramping up enforcement against unregistered data brokers ahead of the August 1, 2026 DROP compliance deadline. Despite an estimated thousands of data brokers operating in California, only approximately 575 have registered as required under [California's Delete Act (SB 362)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB362). The agency has issued demand letters to unregistered entities and confirmed it will "devote significant enforcement resources" to compel compliance, with willful non-registrants subject to penalties of up to $200 per day of non-registration.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Registration gap:** As of May 2026, only approximately 575 data brokers have registered with CPPA, up from 459 in June 2025, despite a much larger universe of businesses that meet the Delete Act's statutory definition — all subject to mandatory registration by January 31 each year ([IAPP, May 2026](https://iapp.org/news/a/calprivacy-explores-data-broker-enforcement-uptick-eu-adequacy-prospects)).

- **Demand letters issued:** CalPrivacy has issued demand letters to unregistered entities as part of the enforcement sweep, requiring registration and payment of accrued delinquency fees ([IAPP U.S. Privacy Digest, May 8, 2026](https://iapp.org/news/a/cppa-to-conduct-data-broker-registry-sweep)).

- **Penalty structure:** Non-registration carries a penalty of **up to $200 per day** per entity from the date the registration obligation arose. Failure to process DROP consumer deletion requests starting August 1, 2026 triggers an additional **up to $200 per day per consumer** ([Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.99.82](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB362)).

- **August 1, 2026 DROP deadline:** Beginning August 1, 2026, registered data brokers must access the [Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP)](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/) at least every 45 days, download consumer deletion requests, process them, and report status back to CalPrivacy. Failure to check DROP within the 45-day window is itself an independent violation ([CalPrivacy DROP information page](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/)).

- **Consumer uptake:** More than 285,000 California residents have already submitted DROP deletion requests since the platform launched January 1, 2026 ([IAPP, May 2026](https://iapp.org/news/a/calprivacy-explores-data-broker-enforcement-uptick-eu-adequacy-prospects)).

- **Enforcement track record:** As of January 2026, CalPrivacy had brought at least eleven enforcement actions resulting in fines, including against [Rickenbacher Data LLC d/b/a Datamasters ($45,000)](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2026/20260108.html), [S&P Global ($62,600)](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2026/20260108.html), and [ROR Partners LLC ($56,600)](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/california-privacy-agency-launches-data-broker-strike-force-amid-delete-act-crackdown).

- **Strike Force:** In November 2025, CalPrivacy [launched a dedicated Data Broker Enforcement Strike Force](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20251119.html) within its Enforcement Division to systematically investigate Delete Act and CCPA violations across the data broker industry.

- **Annual registration fee:** $6,000 per year (plus third-party payment processing fees) due by January 31 each calendar year ([CPPA Data Brokers page](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/)). The CPPA board initially voted in November 2024 to set the fee at $6,600 but subsequently adjusted it downward to $6,000, which is the operative 2026 figure ([CPPA final fee regulation, Dec. 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/20241227_dbfee_finaltext.pdf)).

## Action Items

- **Assess broker status immediately:** Any business that knowingly collects and sells personal information of California consumers with whom it has no direct relationship must determine whether it meets the Delete Act's definition of "data broker" (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.99.80). Passive data collection through cookies, tracking pixels, or purchased lead lists can qualify.

- **Register without delay if unregistered:** Registration is accepted year-round through [DROP](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/). Penalties of up to $200 per day can accrue from the date registration was first required (January 31 each year). Self-initiated registration substantially reduces exposure compared to enforcement-initiated registration.

- **Build DROP access into operational calendar:** Registered brokers must set up automated workflows to access DROP at least every 45 days beginning August 1, 2026, download consumer deletion lists, delete matching personal information (including inferences), and submit status reports within the 45-day window. Each missed cycle is a separate, chargeable violation.

- **Audit vendor and partnership agreements:** Brokers that share data with marketing partners, affiliates, or analytics vendors should review downstream data flows. If the business qualifies as a data broker itself, contractual pass-through obligations may extend to those data flows.

- **Respond to demand letters promptly:** If your organization has received a CPPA demand letter, engage California privacy counsel immediately. The CPPA has demonstrated willingness to settle cases in exchange for timely registration, payment of back penalties, and injunctive compliance terms.

- **Monitor DROP fine exposure:** With 285,000+ Californians having already submitted DROP deletion requests, unregistered brokers that have not processed those requests face compounding per-consumer exposure beginning August 1, 2026.
