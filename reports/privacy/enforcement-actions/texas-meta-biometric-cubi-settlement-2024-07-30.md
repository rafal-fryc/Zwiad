# Meta Settles Texas Biometric Data Lawsuit for Record $1.4 Billion Under CUBI

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 30, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On July 30, 2024, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton [announced](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-14-billion-settlement-meta-over-its-unauthorized-capture) a $1.4 billion settlement with Meta Platforms, Inc. to resolve a lawsuit alleging that Meta systematically captured and used the facial geometry of millions of Texas residents without their consent, in violation of the [Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.503.htm), Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Chapter 503, and the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA). The settlement is the largest privacy enforcement recovery ever obtained by a single state attorney general and the first enforcement action resolved under CUBI since the law's enactment in 2009. Meta agreed to pay the $1.4 billion over five years, with an initial $500 million payment due within 30 days. This settlement signals an aggressive enforcement posture by the Texas AG's newly established data privacy and security enforcement unit and carries material compliance implications for any company collecting biometric data of Texas residents.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI)

The [Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.503.htm), codified at Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Chapter 503, was enacted in 2009. CUBI prohibits any person from capturing a biometric identifier of an individual for a commercial purpose unless the person: (1) informs the individual before capturing the biometric identifier; and (2) receives the individual's consent to the capture. The statute defines "biometric identifier" as a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or record of hand or face geometry.

CUBI further requires that a person who captures a biometric identifier:

- Store, transmit, and protect the identifier using reasonable care and in a manner at least as protective as the manner in which the person protects other confidential information;
- Refrain from selling, leasing, or otherwise disclosing the identifier unless the individual consents or the disclosure is required by law; and
- Destroy the identifier within a reasonable time, but no later than the first anniversary of the date the purpose for collection expires.

Critically, CUBI does not provide a private right of action. The Texas Attorney General has exclusive authority to enforce the statute and may seek civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation. This enforcement-only model contrasts sharply with the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA), which grants individuals a private right of action and has generated thousands of class action lawsuits.

### Meta's Tag Suggestions Feature and the DeepFace Program

Beginning in December 2010, Meta introduced a facial recognition feature on Facebook called "Tag Suggestions." The feature analyzed photographs uploaded by users, identified faces depicted in those photographs using facial recognition technology — specifically Meta's DeepFace program — and suggested the names of individuals whose faces appeared in the images. The DeepFace program captured records of the facial geometry of individuals depicted in uploaded photographs, including non-users of Facebook whose images appeared in photos uploaded by others.

According to the [Texas AG's February 2022 complaint](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/paxton-sues-facebook-using-unauthorized-biometric-data), Meta ran this facial recognition software on virtually every face contained in photographs uploaded to Facebook for more than a decade, capturing billions of biometric identifiers from Texas residents. Meta never informed Texas residents that it was capturing their facial geometry, nor did it obtain their consent prior to capture — both mandatory prerequisites under CUBI.

Meta discontinued the Tag Suggestions program in November 2021, shortly before the lawsuit was filed.

### Prior Enforcement History and Parallel Proceedings

The Texas AG's suit against Meta was filed on [February 14, 2022](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/paxton-sues-facebook-using-unauthorized-biometric-data) and represented the first-ever litigation brought under CUBI. Meta had previously settled a class action lawsuit under Illinois BIPA for [$650 million in 2020](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202407/metas-1-4b-settlement-in-texas-biometric-data-privacy-lawsuit-approved), which covered approximately 1.6 million Illinois Facebook users and paid affected individuals approximately $345 each. That Illinois settlement was negotiated and finalized without any individual payments to affected Texans, which the Texas AG's office cited as further justification for independent state enforcement.

In June 2024, shortly before the settlement announcement, Attorney General Paxton [announced the creation of a new data privacy and security enforcement unit](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/06/alerts-practices-dpc-texas-new-privacy-law-goes-into-effect-enforcement) within the AG's office, dedicated specifically to enforcing Texas data protection laws including CUBI and the newly effective Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Settlement Terms and Payment Structure

The settlement resolves all claims asserted in the February 2022 complaint. Under its terms:

- Meta will pay Texas **$1.4 billion over five years**;
- The first installment of **$500 million** is due within 30 days of settlement approval;
- Remaining payments are made annually over the subsequent four years;
- Meta does not admit liability or wrongdoing as part of the settlement.

The $1.4 billion figure is the [largest privacy settlement ever obtained by a single state attorney general](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-14-billion-settlement-meta-over-its-unauthorized-capture), substantially exceeding Meta's $650 million BIPA settlement, which involved a comparable factual record but a private class action mechanism rather than state enforcement. No portion of the settlement is distributed to individual affected Texans — the funds go to the state.

### Causes of Action

The Texas AG's complaint asserted four causes of action arising from Meta's conduct:

1. **Illegal capture of biometric data** — capturing facial geometry of Texas residents without prior notice or consent in violation of CUBI § 503.001(b);
2. **Unauthorized use of biometric data** — using captured identifiers for facial recognition processing without consent;
3. **Failure to destroy biometric data** — retaining identifiers beyond the permissible period under CUBI § 503.001(c);
4. **Deceptive trade practices** — misrepresenting to Texas consumers the scope and nature of Meta's biometric data collection, in violation of the Texas DTPA.

The DTPA claim was particularly significant because it expanded the theory of liability beyond the consent-and-notice requirements of CUBI to encompass affirmative misrepresentations Meta made to users about its privacy practices.

### CUBI vs. BIPA: Structural Comparison

The Meta settlement highlights the structural differences between Texas and Illinois biometric privacy regimes:

| Feature | Texas CUBI | Illinois BIPA |
|---|---|---|
| Private right of action | No — AG only | Yes — individuals may sue |
| Civil penalty cap | $25,000 per violation | $1,000 (negligent) or $5,000 (intentional) per violation per person |
| Consent requirement | Prior informed consent before capture | Written release required |
| Destruction requirement | Within 1 year of purpose expiration | Earlier of when purpose is satisfied or 3 years |
| Enforcement to date | One settlement ($1.4B, 2024) | 2,000+ class actions (2017–2023) |

Despite lacking a private right of action — which critics have cited as a structural weakness — the Texas AG's exclusive enforcement authority produced a settlement more than double the Illinois class action settlement arising from the same conduct. The [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/07/texas-wins-14-billion-biometric-settlement-against-meta-it-would-have-happened) argued that had CUBI provided a private right of action from its 2009 enactment, Meta's liability exposure would have materialized far sooner.

### The TDPSA Overlay

Effective July 1, 2024 — one month before the Meta settlement announcement — the [Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA)](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/06/alerts-practices-dpc-texas-new-privacy-law-goes-into-effect-enforcement) came into force. The TDPSA designates "biometric data" as sensitive personal data and requires data controllers to obtain opt-in consent before processing biometric data. While CUBI remains on the books and governs the capture of biometric identifiers specifically, the TDPSA adds a broader layer of consent, data minimization, and consumer rights obligations applicable to biometric data processors.

Compliance with CUBI alone is therefore no longer sufficient for entities operating in Texas. Organizations that capture biometric data must satisfy both CUBI's notice-and-consent requirements for the initial capture and the TDPSA's broader obligations for subsequent processing, disclosure, and retention.

### Novel Aspects and Enforcement Signals

Several aspects of this settlement are notable beyond the dollar amount:

- **First CUBI enforcement action**: The settlement establishes proof of concept that the AG's exclusive enforcement model can generate substantial deterrence. Prior uncertainty about whether the AG would pursue CUBI claims aggressively is now resolved.
- **AI training liability**: Legal commentators, including [Bracewell LLP](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/billion-dollar-liability-understanding-your-obligations-under-the-texas-capture-or-use-of-biometric-identifier-act/) and [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/ftc-state-ag/dont-mess-with-texas-cubi-settlement-marks-state-tough-biometric-privacy-enforcer/), noted that biometric data captured to train AI or large language models may itself constitute a CUBI violation even where the resulting product does not deploy facial recognition against Texas residents.
- **Non-user exposure**: The complaint's allegations covered both Facebook users and non-users whose faces appeared in uploaded photos — significantly expanding the population of individuals whose biometric data Meta captured without consent.
- **Cross-state spillover risk**: [Spencer Fane LLP](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/billion-dollar-biometric-bust-meta-reaches-largest-ag-settlement-in-history-1-4-billion-for-alleged-violations-of-texas-biometric-privacy-law/) observed that companies resolving BIPA class actions should expect the Texas AG to investigate whether that same conduct reached Texas residents, given the AG's stated enforcement priorities.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Entities

The settlement has immediate compliance implications for:

- **Social media platforms and photo-sharing services** that use facial recognition, tagging suggestions, or automated photo analysis involving Texas residents;
- **Employers** using biometric time-and-attendance systems, access control, or employee monitoring tools involving face geometry;
- **Retailers** deploying facial recognition for loss prevention, customer analytics, or age verification;
- **Technology companies** training AI or machine learning models on datasets that include photographs or video of Texas residents;
- **Any entity** that captures "records of hand or face geometry" — the CUBI definition of biometric identifier — for a commercial purpose without prior informed consent.

### Compliance Requirements Under CUBI and TDPSA

Organizations must assess compliance across both frameworks:

**CUBI (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Chapter 503):**
1. **Prior notice and consent**: Before capturing any biometric identifier for a commercial purpose, inform the individual and obtain consent;
2. **Storage safeguards**: Use reasonable care in storing and transmitting biometric identifiers — at minimum, equivalent to safeguards applied to other confidential data;
3. **Disclosure prohibition**: Do not sell, lease, or disclose biometric identifiers without individual consent or legal compulsion;
4. **Timely destruction**: Destroy biometric identifiers no later than one year after the purpose for collection expires.

**TDPSA (effective July 1, 2024):**
1. **Sensitive data consent**: Obtain opt-in consumer consent before processing biometric data;
2. **Data protection assessments**: Conduct privacy impact assessments for processing activities involving biometric data;
3. **Consumer rights**: Provide rights of access, deletion, correction, and portability for biometric data subject to TDPSA.

### Enforcement Outlook

The Meta settlement and the AG's establishment of a dedicated data privacy enforcement unit signal that Texas is no longer a passive regulatory environment for biometric privacy. The AG's office has explicitly stated its intent to enforce both CUBI and the TDPSA aggressively. The [Faegre Drinker analysis](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2024/8/texas-attorney-generals-office-zeroes-in-on-data-privacy-and-entities-using-biometric-data) warned that any entity that collects biometric data of Texas residents — whether for authentication, analytics, advertising, or AI model training — is now a potential enforcement target.

The AG's track record suggests cross-referencing BIPA settlements: companies that settled Illinois BIPA claims should evaluate whether their Texas exposure was addressed or whether a parallel Texas enforcement action is likely.

## Action Items

- **Audit biometric data practices immediately**: Identify all systems that capture face geometry, fingerprints, voiceprints, iris scans, or hand geometry from Texas residents and map them against CUBI's notice-and-consent requirements.
- **Review AI training datasets**: Determine whether any datasets used for AI model training include photographs or biometric data derived from Texas residents; ensure consent was obtained prior to capture under CUBI.
- **Update consent mechanisms**: Where biometric capture occurs, implement explicit prior-consent workflows — not post-hoc notices or bundled terms-of-service — to satisfy CUBI's consent standard.
- **Assess TDPSA compliance**: For biometric data processing activities that extend beyond mere capture, conduct a TDPSA compliance assessment to identify additional obligations that took effect on July 1, 2024.
- **Cross-check BIPA settlements**: If your company resolved Illinois BIPA claims for conduct involving facial recognition or biometric data, evaluate whether the same conduct involved Texas residents and consult counsel about Texas AG exposure.
- **Implement retention and destruction schedules**: Establish documented policies for destroying biometric identifiers no later than one year after the stated collection purpose expires, per CUBI § 503.001(c).
- **Monitor AG enforcement activity**: Track the Texas AG's data privacy enforcement unit for further CUBI or TDPSA enforcement actions, which are expected based on the AG's public statements.
