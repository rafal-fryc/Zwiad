# Indiana AG Sues Roblox and Discord for Child Safety Misrepresentations Under State UDAP Law

**Jurisdiction:** Indiana | **Category:** Privacy — Children's Online Safety | **Date:** May 6, 2026 (filed); May 7, 2026 (announced)

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Indiana Attorney General Todd Rokita filed a 70-page complaint on May 6, 2026 in Hamilton County Circuit/Superior Court against Roblox Corp. and Discord Inc., publicly announcing the action on May 7. Both companies are alleged to have violated Indiana's [Deceptive Consumer Sales Act (I.C. § 24-5-0.5)](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-24/article-5/chapter-0-5/) ("DCSA") by marketing their platforms as safe for children while knowingly allowing predatory contact, child sexual abuse material ("CSAM"), and other systemic harms to minors. The suit seeks injunctive relief, disgorgement of profits, and civil penalties of up to $5,000 per knowing violation. Indiana filed approximately two weeks after Roblox reached a combined $35.8 million settlement with Alabama, Nevada, and West Virginia, becoming the latest state in a rapidly expanding wave of AG enforcement targeting both platforms.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Filing date and venue:** AG Rokita filed the complaint on May 6, 2026 in Hamilton County Circuit/Superior Court; the AG's office publicly announced the action on May 7. The [official press release](https://events.in.gov/event/to-protect-hoosier-children-from-online-predators-attorney-general-todd-rokita-sues-roblox-corp-and-discord-inc) characterizes the action as part of a broader effort to protect "Hoosier families" from online predators.

- **Statutory basis:** Both companies are sued under the [Indiana Deceptive Consumer Sales Act, I.C. § 24-5-0.5](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-24/article-5/chapter-0-5/), which prohibits unfair, abusive, or deceptive acts by suppliers in connection with consumer transactions. The DCSA authorizes the AG to seek injunctive relief, disgorgement, and civil penalties. Per [I.C. § 24-5-0.5-4](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-24/article-5/chapter-0-5/section-24-5-0-5-4/), civil penalties may reach $5,000 per knowing violation.

- **Triggering case — Hailey Buzbee:** The complaint prominently cites the death of 17-year-old Hailey Buzbee of Fishers, Indiana, who was allegedly lured from her home in January 2026 after months of interaction with 39-year-old Tyler Thomas through Roblox and Discord. According to the [AG's office](https://events.in.gov/event/to-protect-hoosier-children-from-online-predators-attorney-general-todd-rokita-sues-roblox-corp-and-discord-inc), Buzbee is one of at least three Indiana girls who were groomed or enticed from their homes using Roblox. A copy of the complaint is available at the [Indiana subscription archive (PDF)](https://image.subscription.in.gov/lib/fe2e11747364047b721071/m/1/9a278bec-f19f-4bac-adcd-eb7847688ff3.pdf).

- **Allegations against Roblox:** The complaint alleges that despite publicly advertising robust child safety features, Roblox fails to require meaningful age verification when accounts are created — even though the company has access to AI-powered biometric age verification software (government ID or real-time selfie verification). The complaint also alleges Roblox's virtual currency system ("Robux") is weaponized by predators to offer Robux to minors in exchange for photos. According to [WRTV/ABC affiliate reporting](https://www.wrtv.com/news/local-news/indiana-attorney-general-sues-roblox-and-discord-over-child-safety-concerns), sexually explicit content is accessible on the platform despite Roblox's stated content policies.

- **Allegations against Discord:** The complaint accuses Discord of creating an environment in which "the sexual exploitation of children is rampant and thriving." Specifically, the filing alleges Discord has "chosen not to implement easy measures that would protect children but stunt user growth, such as age verification to ensure that children under 13 cannot create accounts." CSAM is alleged to be available on Discord and accessible to both adults and minors. ([WANE News](https://www.wane.com/top-stories/indiana-ag-sues-roblox-discord-over-child-safety/))

- **Relief sought:** Injunctive and declaratory relief to prevent further harm; disgorgement of all profits obtained through the alleged unlawful conduct; civil penalties of up to $5,000 per knowing violation; and reimbursement of the AG's investigative costs. ([Consumer Notice](https://www.consumernotice.org/news/indiana-becomes-latest-state-to-sue-roblox-as-child-safety-lawsuits-grow/))

- **Roblox multistate settlement context:** In April 2026, Roblox settled with Alabama ($12.2M), Nevada ($12.5M), and West Virginia ($11.1M) for a combined [$35.8 million](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/04/23/866879.htm), announced April 21-22, 2026. Those settlements include commitments to age-verify all users before granting chat access. Indiana's suit was filed approximately two weeks after that settlement was announced.

- **Broader enforcement trend:** Prior state AG actions against Roblox include Texas (November 2025), Nebraska (March 2026), Iowa (December 2025), Louisiana (August 2025), and Kentucky (October 2025). Discord has faced a separate [lawsuit filed by New Jersey in April 2025](https://www.njoag.gov/ag-platkin-sues-messaging-app-discord-for-unlawful-practices-that-expose-nj-kids-to-child-predators-and-violent-sexual-content/) and investigations or subpoenas from Texas and Florida. ([Cozen O'Connor / JDSupra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/indiana-takes-on-roblox-discord-over-3596067/))

## Action Items

- **Platforms operating children-facing services in Indiana:** Review whether your platform's safety representations (in terms of service, marketing materials, and app store listings) align with actual technical controls. A mismatch between advertised child safety features and implemented safeguards is the core liability theory in this suit — applicable in any state with a UDAP statute.

- **Age verification gap:** The complaint specifically targets the gap between available age verification technology (biometric / government-ID-based) and actual deployment. Platforms that market to minors but do not deploy available age verification technology face heightened litigation exposure in Indiana and other DCSA-analogue states.

- **Monitor the $5,000-per-violation penalty structure:** Unlike statutory damage caps tied to individual data subjects, the DCSA's per-knowing-violation framing (I.C. § 24-5-0.5-4) could generate substantial aggregate penalties given the scale of minor-user interactions alleged. Platforms should quantify their exposure under this theory.

- **Discord-specific:** The complaint singles out Discord as a vector for escalating grooming from Roblox because Discord enables private encrypted messaging. Platforms that serve as downstream communication tools for predator contact may face independent DCSA liability even where the initial predatory contact originated elsewhere.

- **Watch for multistate coordination:** Indiana is part of a growing AG coalition targeting both Roblox and Discord. Future coordinated AG actions (similar to the Alabama/Nevada/West Virginia Roblox settlement) remain a near-term probability. Legal teams should evaluate settlement posture in light of the $35.8M precedent.
