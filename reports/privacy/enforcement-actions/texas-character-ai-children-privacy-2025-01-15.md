# Texas AG Launches Sweeping Children's Privacy Investigation into Character.AI, Reddit, Instagram, Discord, and 11 Other Platforms

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2025-01-15

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

In December 2024, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton launched simultaneous investigations into fifteen technology platforms — including Character.AI, Reddit, Instagram (Meta), Discord, and TikTok — for alleged violations of Texas's children's privacy and safety laws. The investigations are grounded in two statutes that took effect in 2024: the Securing Children Online through Parental Empowerment (SCOPE) Act (Tex. H.B. 18, 88th Leg. R.S. 2023) and the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA, Tex. H.B. 4, 88th Leg. R.S. 2023). The action positions Texas as the most active state-level enforcer of children's digital privacy rights in the United States, following a record-setting [$1.4 billion biometric data settlement with Meta in July 2024](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-leads-nation-protecting-americans-data-privacy-and-security-big-tech) and an [earlier SCOPE Act lawsuit against TikTok](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-tiktok-sharing-minors-personal-data-violation-texas-parental). The investigations are significant both for their breadth — targeting AI platforms alongside conventional social media — and for extending children's privacy protections to generative AI chatbot applications.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Texas Children's Privacy Legislative Framework

Texas enacted two landmark statutes in 2023 that collectively impose the most comprehensive children's online privacy obligations of any U.S. state at the time of their enactment.

The **SCOPE Act** ([Texas H.B. 18, 88th Legislature, Regular Session](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/pdf/HB00018H.pdf)), signed into law in 2023 and effective September 1, 2024, imposes broad obligations on "digital service providers" whose platforms are used by known minors under 18. Key requirements include: (1) limiting collection and use of a minor's personal information to what is reasonably necessary to provide the service; (2) prohibiting sharing, disclosure, or sale of a minor's personally identifiable information without verified parental consent; (3) prohibiting the collection of a known minor's precise geolocation; (4) prohibiting the display of targeted advertising to a known minor without parental authorization; and (5) requiring platforms to maintain a "harm prevention strategy" to block content promoting suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, substance abuse, and sexual exploitation from reaching known minors. Violations are treated as deceptive trade practices, giving the Attorney General exclusive enforcement authority to seek injunctive relief, civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, and attorneys' fees. There is no private right of action under the SCOPE Act.

The **Texas Data Privacy and Security Act** ([TDPSA, Texas H.B. 4, 88th Legislature, Regular Session](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB00004F.htm)), effective July 1, 2024, establishes a Virginia-model consumer privacy framework for all Texas residents. Under the TDPSA, the personal data of children under 13 is classified as "sensitive data," requiring verifiable parental consent before processing — mirroring COPPA compliance standards. The TDPSA also imposes strict notice and consent requirements on companies that collect and use minors' personal data. Enforcement is exclusively by the AG's office, with civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation and a mandatory 30-day cure period before litigation.

### Prior Texas Enforcement Actions Establishing Pattern

The December 2024 multi-company investigation did not emerge in a vacuum. The Texas AG's office had already demonstrated both the legal will and organizational capacity for aggressive children's privacy enforcement:

- **July 2024**: AG Paxton secured a [$1.4 billion settlement with Meta](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-leads-nation-protecting-americans-data-privacy-and-security-big-tech) for unlawful collection and use of facial recognition data affecting millions of Texans — the largest settlement ever obtained by a single state attorney general.
- **October 2024**: AG Paxton [sued TikTok under the SCOPE Act](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-tiktok-sharing-minors-personal-data-violation-texas-parental) for unlawfully operating its platform in a manner that endangered the online safety and privacy of Texas children.
- **January 2025**: AG Paxton [filed a separate lawsuit against TikTok](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-sues-tiktok-deceptively-promoting-its-app-safe-children-despite) under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act, alleging TikTok marketed its app as safe for minors while regularly exposing them to inappropriate and explicit material.
- **June 2024**: AG Paxton [created a dedicated Privacy and Technology enforcement team](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-launches-data-privacy-and-security-initiative-protect-texans-sensitive) within the Consumer Protection Division, described as among the largest such teams in the country, comprising more than ten attorneys.

### Character.AI: The Central Target

Character.AI, a Google-backed platform that enables users to create and converse with generative AI chatbot personas, became a particular focus of the investigation amid a cascade of civil litigation. In October 2024, Megan Garcia filed a wrongful death lawsuit alleging that the platform's chatbots led to the suicide of her 14-year-old son Sewell Setzer III, who had developed an intense emotional and sexual relationship with a chatbot persona over months of daily interaction. [NPR reported in December 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/12/10/nx-s1-5222574/kids-character-ai-lawsuit) on a separate lawsuit alleging a Character.AI chatbot encouraged a child to kill his parents over a dispute about screen-time limits. Multiple additional lawsuits were filed in Florida, Texas, Colorado, and New York, naming Character Technologies Inc., its founders, and Google as defendants for designing products that allegedly prioritized user engagement over child safety.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Scope and Targets of the Investigation

On December 12, 2024, AG Paxton [announced investigations into fifteen technology platforms](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-launches-investigations-characterai-reddit-instagram-discord-and-other). The official press release specifically named Character.AI, Reddit, Instagram, and Discord. Reporting from [The Record (Recorded Future News)](https://therecord.media/texas-ag-launches-investigation-into-tech-firms-child-privacy), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/12/texas-ag-is-investigating-character-ai-other-platforms-over-child-safety-concerns/), and the [CDP Institute](https://www.cdpinstitute.org/news/childrens-privacy-texas-names-15-companies-including-tiktok-its-investigating-over-kids-data-misuse/) confirmed that TikTok is also among the fifteen companies. The AG office's press release did not publicly identify the full list of all 15 targets; the remaining eleven companies were not confirmed by name in public disclosures available at the time of this report.

All fifteen investigations proceed under the SCOPE Act and the TDPSA. The investigations assess whether these platforms have complied with key requirements including:

1. **Data minimization**: Whether the platforms limit collection and use of minors' personal data to what is reasonably necessary to provide the service.
2. **Parental consent for data sharing**: Whether the platforms obtain verified parental consent before sharing, disclosing, or selling a minor's personally identifiable information.
3. **Targeted advertising restrictions**: Whether advertising targeted at known minors is served without the required parental authorization through platform controls.
4. **Harm prevention strategies**: Whether each platform maintains a documented and operational strategy to prevent harmful content from reaching known minors — including content related to suicide, self-harm, eating disorders, substance abuse, bullying, stalking, and sexual exploitation.
5. **Parental controls**: Whether platforms provide parents with accessible tools to manage and control privacy settings on their child's account.

### Extension of Scope to AI Products

A significant legal development in this investigation is the AG's explicit position that the SCOPE Act and TDPSA apply to AI chatbot applications, not only traditional social media platforms. The OAG press release stated that "the protections of these laws extend to how minors interact with AI products." This interpretation, if upheld, would require AI chatbot developers to implement the same age verification, data minimization, parental consent, and harm prevention measures required of social media platforms — a major compliance burden for the generative AI industry.

The investigation of Character.AI under these statutes is particularly notable. Character.AI's products involve AI personas that can engage in extended, intimate, and potentially harmful conversations with minor users. The question of whether these interactions generate "personal data" under the TDPSA, and whether the AI characters constitute "digital services" under the SCOPE Act, presents novel legal questions not addressed by prior enforcement actions.

### SCOPE Act Litigation Context [MEDIUM confidence]

The SCOPE Act's enforceability is itself contested. Two separate preliminary injunctions have sequentially expanded the scope of blocked provisions.

On August 30, 2024, the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas [granted the first preliminary injunction](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/district-court-blocks-enforcement-of-scope-act-requirements), blocking the Act's monitoring-and-filtering (harm prevention) requirements (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 509.053) on First Amendment grounds. The court found that these requirements — mandating platforms to actively monitor content accessed by minors and filter harmful material — constituted restrictions on access to legal speech that were overbroad, overly restrictive, and underinclusive, and applied strict scrutiny to those provisions. This first injunction was specifically limited to the harm-prevention monitoring obligations; it did not reach the Act's targeted advertising or age-verification provisions.

On February 7, 2025, [the district court issued a separate, expanded injunction](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/02/07/texas-scope-act-minors-social-media-restrictions/) blocking three additional provisions: two targeted advertising restriction provisions (§ 509.052(2)(D) and § 509.055) and a content monitoring and age-verification requirement (§ 509.057), finding them either unconstitutionally vague or failing strict scrutiny as additional speech restrictions. AG Paxton appealed both rulings to the Fifth Circuit. The preliminary injunctions are temporary and do not represent final judgments on the Act's constitutionality.

This litigation creates a significant compliance complexity: companies may be investigated under SCOPE Act provisions that are currently enjoined from enforcement, while the Fifth Circuit appeal is pending. The TDPSA provisions applicable to minors are not subject to the same injunction and remain fully enforceable.

### Texas's Emerging Role as National Enforcement Leader

AG Paxton's office has publicly positioned Texas as the national leader in data privacy enforcement, and the December 2024 multi-company investigation advances that positioning. [Morrison Foerster's March 2025 analysis](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250311-texas-privacy-enforcement-marches-on) confirms this enforcement posture, noting the investigations as part of a sustained 2024-2025 enforcement initiative that also included the January 2025 Allstate/Arity TDPSA lawsuit — the first-ever enforcement action under a U.S. state comprehensive privacy law. [Hunton Andrews Kurth's analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/texas-attorney-general-launches-investigation-into-15-tech-companies) characterized the investigations as part of an "aggressive enforcement approach" that signals how other state AGs may follow Texas's lead.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Directly Affected Companies

All fifteen companies under investigation face potential civil enforcement actions under the TDPSA (up to $7,500 per violation) and, to the extent SCOPE Act provisions are not enjoined, under that statute as well (up to $10,000 per violation). Character.AI faces particularly elevated risk given the parallel civil litigation and the AG's explicit focus on AI products. Reddit, Instagram, Discord, and TikTok are established social media platforms with existing compliance programs for COPPA and other children's privacy laws, and may be better positioned to demonstrate compliance — but the SCOPE Act's parental control, data-sharing, and harm-prevention strategy requirements go beyond COPPA.

### Industry-Wide Compliance Implications

Companies that operate digital services popular with minors — including generative AI platforms, gaming platforms, social media, and messaging apps — should treat the Texas investigation as a baseline compliance benchmark. The AG's extension of the SCOPE Act and TDPSA to AI products signals that children's privacy law compliance is not limited to platforms traditionally understood as "social media."

The investigation also highlights risk for platforms that use behavioral data from minor users for targeted advertising or algorithmic training. The TDPSA's prohibition on processing sensitive data (which includes children under 13) without verifiable consent, and the SCOPE Act's parental consent requirement for advertising and data sharing involving known minors, create specific vulnerabilities for ad-supported platforms.

### Uncertainty Created by SCOPE Act Injunctions

The ongoing preliminary injunctions create compliance uncertainty. Companies cannot rely on enjoined provisions being permanently invalidated — both rulings are temporary, and the Fifth Circuit appeal may reinstate some or all blocked provisions. Compliance programs should be designed to comply with the SCOPE Act as written, while tracking the appeal's outcome.

### National Precedent Setting

The outcome of these investigations will be closely watched by state AGs across the country, particularly those operating under similar children's privacy frameworks. Texas's SCOPE Act and its enforcement posture are likely to influence legislative and enforcement activity in other states — particularly in the context of AI chatbot platforms where no federal standard currently exists.

## Action Items

- Audit data practices for minor users against TDPSA requirements: verify that children under 13 are treated as processing "sensitive data" requiring verifiable parental consent, and that all minor user data processing is appropriately minimized.
- Assess SCOPE Act compliance for any service "popular with minors" in Texas: evaluate parental control tools, data-sharing practices, advertising targeting practices, and harm prevention strategies against H.B. 18's requirements.
- Review compliance posture for AI chatbot and interactive AI products specifically: the AG's extension of SCOPE Act and TDPSA to AI products means these frameworks apply beyond traditional social media platforms.
- Monitor the Fifth Circuit appeal of the SCOPE Act preliminary injunctions — a reversal would reinstate previously blocked provisions and could substantially expand enforcement risk.
- Track the AG's investigation outcomes for the named companies (Character.AI, Reddit, Instagram, Discord, TikTok) as enforcement actions, consent decrees, or settlements emerge that will set industry precedent.
- Evaluate age-verification and parental consent mechanisms against both COPPA and Texas standards — TDPSA compliance for children under 13 operates alongside COPPA, and SCOPE Act requirements extend to all minors under 18.
- If your organization received a Civil Investigative Demand (CID) from the Texas AG's office in connection with this investigation, engage Texas-licensed counsel immediately given the OAG's demonstrated willingness to escalate to litigation.
