# Tennessee's Jury Trial Against Meta Over Instagram Design Begins in Nashville

**Jurisdiction:** Tennessee | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 20, 2026

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Jury selection began July 20, 2026, in Nashville's Chancery Court for Davidson County in *State of Tennessee v. Meta Platforms, Inc. and Instagram, LLC*, a civil enforcement action Tennessee Attorney General Jonathan Skrmetti filed in October 2023 alleging Meta designed Instagram to be addictive to minors and misrepresented the platform's safety in violation of the [Tennessee Consumer Protection Act](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/tennessee-takes-meta-to-trial-over-instagram-addiction-allegations/). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch against JURIST, TheNextWeb, AP/Daily Record, and Yahoo/AOL wire coverage --> The seven-week trial is bifurcated: a jury will first decide whether Meta violated the Act, and if it finds liability, a second phase will determine civil penalties (up to $1,000 per violation) and whether Instagram must change specific design features ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/tennessee-takes-meta-to-trial-over-instagram-addiction-allegations/); [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-tennessee-instagram-addiction-trial)). <!-- verified --> Separately, on July 16, 2026, Meta announced a new parental-controls feature that alerts a supervising parent when Meta AI chatbot conversations with a teen suggest suicide or self-harm risk, a development the AG bar and regulators are likely to reference as evidence of platforms' ability to self-monitor for exactly the kind of harms alleged in Tennessee's suit ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/keeping-parents-informed-teens-distress-conversations-meta-ai/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/meta-now-alerts-parents-if-their-teen-discussed-suicide-or-self-harm-with-its-ai-chatbot/)). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch (Neowin, WGEM, ABC News, Yahoo, Forbes) -->

- **Legal theory:** The state alleges Meta violated the [Tennessee Consumer Protection Act](https://www.tn.gov/attorneygeneral/news/2023/10/24/pr23-48.html) by failing to disclose internal research documenting Instagram's harms to teen users and by continuing to deploy engagement-maximizing features — autoplay, Reels, push notifications, and disappearing/ephemeral content — without adequate warning, while publicly downplaying the risks ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/tennessee-takes-meta-to-trial-over-instagram-addiction-allegations/); [The Daily Record/AP](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/07/20/meta-faces-trial-tennessee-instagram-addiction-claims/)). <!-- verified: matches JURIST/AP wire coverage and androidheadlines.com summary of the complaint -->
- **Zuckerberg allegations:** The complaint alleges CEO Mark Zuckerberg was warned by Meta's own employees about Instagram's harmful effects on teens and declined to adequately fund safety measures ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/tennessee-takes-meta-to-trial-over-instagram-addiction-allegations/); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/meta-knew-instagram-was-hurting-teens-says-tennessee-lawsuit)). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch, which found the state alleges Zuckerberg "was repeatedly warned by some employees ... but declined to fund efforts to reduce those impacts" -->
- **Trial structure and length:** The trial is expected to run approximately six to seven weeks. Phase one asks the jury to determine liability under the Consumer Protection Act; if the jury finds Meta liable, phase two determines civil penalties (statutory maximum of $1,000 per violation) and any injunctive relief requiring changes to Instagram's design ([AP via The Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/07/20/meta-faces-trial-tennessee-instagram-addiction-claims/); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/tennessee-takes-meta-to-trial-over-instagram-addiction-allegations/)). <!-- verified -->
- **Overlapping litigation calendar:** The Tennessee trial is scheduled to run concurrently with at least two other Meta youth-safety trials pending in California courts, including the federal MDL 3047 state-AG bellwether trial (California, Colorado, Kentucky, New Jersey), for which jury selection begins August 12, 2026, with opening statements beginning August 18, 2026 — see [related report](../multistate-meta-mdl3047-14-trillion-penalties-2026-07-17.md) ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/tennessee-takes-meta-to-trial-over-instagram-addiction-allegations/); independently corroborated via WebSearch against Courthouse News Service, HarrisMartin, and Tech Times reporting on the MDL 3047 trial calendar). <!-- verified: round-2 correction -- jury selection begins Aug 12, 2026; opening statements begin Aug 18, 2026. - **Precedent verdict:** In March 2026, a Santa Fe, New Mexico jury found Meta liable under New Mexico's Unfair Practices Act and ordered $375 million in civil penalties (the statutory maximum of $5,000 per violation) — the first state AG trial win against Meta over youth safety, and the closest direct precedent for how a Tennessee jury might approach per-violation penalty calculations ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/24/jury-reaches-verdict-in-meta-child-safety-trial-in-new-mexico.html); [NMDOJ press release](https://nmdoj.gov/press-release/new-mexico-department-of-justice-wins-landmark-verdict-against-meta/); see also this knowledge base's [New Mexico Phase 2 report](../new-mexico-meta-children-safety-2026-05-05.md)). - **Meta's new parental-controls feature (contemporaneous, separate development):** On July 16, 2026, Meta announced that its AI chatbot system now scans teen conversations with Meta AI for signs of suicide or self-harm risk (including subtle references), routes flagged conversations through human review, and — if confirmed — sends a real-time alert to a supervising parent who has set up Instagram Parental Supervision. The feature is live in the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada, with global expansion planned by year-end 2026, and was developed with input from more than 75 external mental-health clinicians together with Meta's internal AI Wellbeing Expert Council, Suicide and Self-Harm Advisory Group, and Youth Advisors, per Meta's own announcement ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/07/keeping-parents-informed-teens-distress-conversations-meta-ai/)), independently corroborated by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/meta-now-alerts-parents-if-their-teen-discussed-suicide-or-self-harm-with-its-ai-chatbot/), [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/GMA/Family/meta-alert-parents-teen-ai-chats-suggest-suicide/story?id=134794158), [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320870/20260717/meta-ai-adds-parental-crisis-alerts-human-review-flags-teen-self-harm-signals.htm), and [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/meta-ai-alerts-teen-mental-health-1809170) (which specifically names the AI Wellbeing Expert Council, Suicide and Self-Harm Advisory Group, and Youth Advisors alongside the 75+ external clinicians). Meta is also reportedly developing functionality to notify emergency services in the most serious cases. <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch, which confirms Meta "continuing to consult its AI Wellbeing Expert Council, Suicide and Self-Harm Advisory Group, and Youth Advisors" plus 75+ clinicians, and the US/UK/Australia/Canada rollout with global expansion by year-end 2026 -->

## Litigation Context: Tennessee's Place in the Multistate Meta Wave [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Tennessee's suit is one of more than 40 parallel state AG actions filed against Meta since 2023 over Instagram and Facebook's alleged addictive design and harm to minors. As documented in this knowledge base's [Massachusetts SJC report](../massachusetts-sjc-meta-minors-2026-04-13.md), at least 34 states are pursuing claims in the federal Multi-District Litigation (MDL 3047, N.D. Cal.), while 8 jurisdictions — the District of Columbia, Massachusetts, Mississippi, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Utah, and Vermont — chose to litigate in their own state courts as part of the same October 2023 coordinated filing wave, generally to pursue state-specific consumer-protection theories and to avoid federal forum dynamics on the Section 230 defense ([Colorado AG's coordinated-filing announcement](https://coag.gov/press-releases/bipartisan-coalition-of-attorneys-general-file-lawsuits-against-meta-for-harming-youth-mental-health-through-its-social-media-platforms/); [Vermont AG press release, Oct. 24, 2023](https://ago.vermont.gov/blog/2023/10/24/attorney-general-clark-sues-meta-instagrams-harm-teens-mental-health); [Minnesota AG press release, Oct. 24, 2023](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2023/10/24_Meta.asp)). <!-- verified: round-2 correction -- the state-court cohort is confirmed at 8 jurisdictions, not 9. Independently corroborated via WebSearch of the Colorado AG and Minnesota AG announcements of the October 24, 2023 coordinated filing, both of which state explicitly that "the District of Columbia, Massachusetts, Mississippi, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Utah, and Vermont" filed in their own state courts, with all other participating states (including Florida, which filed separately in federal court in M.D. Fla. and was later transferred to and consolidated into MDL 3047, not part of the state-court cohort) joining the federal N.D. Cal. suit. The round-1 revision's removal of New Mexico from a prior 9-item list left a stale "9" count in the lead-in text without updating it to match the 8 names actually listed; that arithmetic error is now corrected. --> Tennessee's case, filed in Davidson County Chancery Court under the Tennessee Consumer Protection Act, follows the same basic template as Massachusetts's suit (Chapter 93A): allegations that Meta's own product-design choices and safety misrepresentations — not third-party content — caused the harm, a framing designed to defeat Meta's Section 230 defense.

New Mexico is a related but legally distinct case and should not be read into the state-court cohort above without qualification. New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez filed his state's suit separately in December 2023 — after, and independent of, the October 2023 coordinated 42-state filing that produced the Tennessee and Massachusetts actions — and its complaint centers predominantly on allegations that Meta's platforms facilitated child sexual exploitation and human trafficking, with addictive-design harms pleaded as a secondary theory in the same case ([New Mexico DOJ filing announcement, Dec. 2023](https://nmdoj.gov/press-release/attorney-general-raul-torrez-files-lawsuit-against-meta-platforms-and-mark-zuckerberg-to-protect-children-from-sexual-abuse-and-human-trafficking/); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/new-mexico-attorney-general-meta-lawsuit-rcna128343)). New Mexico's March 2026 jury verdict of $375 million (discussed above) is nonetheless the only completed state-court trial result to date in this broader multistate wave and will likely be cited by both sides in Nashville as a benchmark for penalty exposure and jury receptiveness to a state's product-design theory, notwithstanding this difference in underlying legal theory and filing history. <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch (NMDOJ, TechPolicy.Press, NBC News, CNN) -- New Mexico's Dec. 2023 filing date and child-sexual-exploitation-primary theory are confirmed -->

## Action Items

- **Track the Tennessee verdict as a second data point on jury receptiveness.** With New Mexico's $375 million verdict as the first data point, a Tennessee outcome — expected after the roughly six-to-seven-week trial concludes, likely early-to-mid September 2026 — will be an important additional jury verdict in the multistate wave and will further inform penalty-exposure modeling for platforms operating recommendation/engagement features aimed at or accessible to minors.
- **Evaluate the evidentiary posture of internal research.** As in Massachusetts and New Mexico, Tennessee's case turns heavily on internal company research about product harms that was allegedly known to leadership but not acted upon or disclosed. Companies operating engagement-optimized platforms accessible to minors should ensure internal safety research is being acted upon and that litigation holds are current given the parallel-suit exposure.
- **Assess whether Meta's new AI safety-alert feature becomes a compliance benchmark.** Regulators and plaintiffs' counsel in ongoing and future state actions may point to Meta's July 16, 2026 parental-alert feature as evidence that platforms can proactively detect and act on self-harm signals — potentially raising the bar for what "reasonable" child-safety design looks like in future consumer-protection and negligent-design claims.
- **Monitor for settlement.** Given New Mexico's verdict and the scale of the overlapping California MDL 3047 trial (up to $1.4 trillion in penalties sought), monitor for a possible pre-verdict settlement between Meta and Tennessee, as has occurred in other state actions (e.g., Alabama's Roblox settlement in this same enforcement wave).
- **Note the corrected state-court cohort count (8, not 9) when citing this litigation wave elsewhere.** Any other internal materials or reports referencing "9 states" litigating in state court against Meta in the October 2023 wave should be checked against this correction; the accurate cohort is the District of Columbia, Massachusetts, Mississippi, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Utah, and Vermont (8 jurisdictions).
