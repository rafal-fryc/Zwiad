# RentGrow to Pay $2.25 Million to Settle FTC Allegations of FCRA and FTC Act Violations

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-10

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The Federal Trade Commission announced on July 9, 2026 that RentGrow, Inc., a Massachusetts-based tenant-screening company, will pay $2.25 million and adopt new accuracy and dispute-handling procedures to settle allegations that it violated the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and the FTC Act. The complaint, filed by the Department of Justice on the FTC's behalf in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, alleges RentGrow's background reports contained duplicate criminal and eviction records that overstated applicants' histories, and that the company mishandled consumer disputes and misrepresented dispute outcomes to landlords ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/rentgrow-pay-225-million-settle-ftc-allegations-company-violated-fair-credit-reporting-act-ftc-act); [FTC case docket](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/222-3002-rentgrow-inc-us-v)). This is the FTC's latest in a series of FCRA enforcement actions targeting the tenant/consumer-screening industry and reinforces the agency's continued focus on background-check accuracy and dispute-process integrity under Chairman Andrew Ferguson.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- RentGrow will pay a $2.25 million civil penalty to resolve the FTC's allegations, per the proposed stipulated order filed with the court ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/rentgrow-pay-225-million-settle-ftc-allegations-company-violated-fair-credit-reporting-act-ftc-act)).
- The complaint alleges four counts against RentGrow: (1) failure to use reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy of consumer report information, in violation of FCRA Section 607(b); (2) failure to disclose to consumers, upon request, certain information in their files and its sources, in violation of FCRA Section 609(a); (3) failure to comply with reseller dispute-handling procedures required by FCRA Section 611; and (4) deceptive representations to property managers about whether consumers' tenant-screening reports had been updated following a dispute, in violation of FTC Act Section 5(a) ([FTC Complaint, RentGrow, Inc.](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/RentGrow-Complaint.pdf), filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia).
- The FTC alleges RentGrow's reports frequently contained duplicate entries for the same criminal or eviction case, creating the false impression that rental applicants had more convictions or eviction filings than they actually did ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/rentgrow-pay-225-million-settle-ftc-allegations-company-violated-fair-credit-reporting-act-ftc-act); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2499408/rentgrow-to-pay-2-25-million-to-settle-us-ftc-s-fcra-allegations)).
- The FTC also alleges RentGrow at times dismissed consumer disputes as "invalid" without meaningful investigation, provided landlords and disputing consumers inconsistent information about dispute outcomes, and failed to disclose Accurint (a LexisNexis data product) as one of its underlying data sources ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/rentgrow-pay-225-million-settle-ftc-allegations-company-violated-fair-credit-reporting-act-ftc-act)).
- Beyond the monetary penalty, the proposed stipulated order requires RentGrow to implement reasonable procedures to prevent duplicate criminal/eviction entries, to comply with FCRA's other accuracy, disclosure, and dispute-handling requirements going forward, and to stop misrepresenting to property managers whether reports have been updated post-dispute ([Consent Motion for Entry of Stipulated Order](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/RentGrow-OrderMtn.pdf)).
- The case was filed by the Department of Justice, at the FTC's request, in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ([FTC case docket, 222-3002](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/222-3002-rentgrow-inc-us-v)). Independent legal/news coverage corroborates the settlement amount and core allegations ([MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2499408/rentgrow-to-pay-2-25-million-to-settle-us-ftc-s-fcra-allegations); [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/news/rentgrows-2-25-million-fcra-settlement-when-bad-data-costs-people-housing-and-costs-companies-millions/)).
- The $2.25 million is structured as a civil penalty payable to the U.S. Treasury; reporting to date indicates no direct consumer redress fund was established as part of this settlement ([MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2499408/rentgrow-to-pay-2-25-million-to-settle-us-ftc-s-fcra-allegations)).

## Action Items

- Tenant-screening and consumer-reporting companies should audit matching logic used to associate court records (criminal, eviction) with individual consumers to eliminate duplicate or over-inclusive entries — a defect the FTC treated as an FCRA Section 607(b) "maximum possible accuracy" violation.
- Companies that resell third-party data (e.g., LexisNexis/Accurint feeds) should confirm their consumer disclosures identify all data sources used to compile reports, consistent with FCRA Section 609(a).
- Review dispute-handling workflows to ensure disputes are substantively reinvestigated rather than summarily marked "invalid," and that consumers and downstream users (landlords, employers) receive consistent, accurate information about dispute outcomes and any resulting report corrections.
- Property managers and landlords using tenant-screening vendors should confirm their vendor's compliance posture in light of this enforcement action, given the FTC's continued scrutiny of the sector.
- Monitor the case docket (FTC Matter No. 222-3002) for entry of the final stipulated order and any court modifications before the settlement is treated as final.
