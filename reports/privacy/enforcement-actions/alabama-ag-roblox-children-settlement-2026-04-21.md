# Alabama AG Secures $12.2 Million Settlement With Roblox Over Children's Online Safety

**Jurisdiction:** Alabama, Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-21

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 21, 2026, Alabama Attorney General Steve Marshall [announced a $12.2 million settlement](https://www.alabamaag.gov/attorney-general-marshall-announces-12-2-million-settlement-with-roblox-to-ensure-child-safety/) with online gaming platform Roblox Corporation, resolving pre-litigation consumer protection claims that Roblox failed to protect children under 16 from predatory contact and explicit content. The settlement — an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance — requires Roblox to implement government ID and facial scan age verification for chat access, ban unverified adult-to-minor communications, and maintain unencrypted minor-user chats to facilitate law enforcement access. Settlement funds are directed entirely to the Attorney General's Safe School Initiative Fund for school resource officers statewide. The agreement is part of a coordinated multistate effort: Alabama, West Virginia, and Nevada announced settlements totaling approximately $35 million on or around the same date, with active litigation by at least seven additional states ongoing.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Alabama AG Steve Marshall announced the settlement on April 21, 2026 via an [official press release](https://www.alabamaag.gov/attorney-general-marshall-announces-12-2-million-settlement-with-roblox-to-ensure-child-safety/) filed as an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance; the [term sheet is publicly available](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/ALAG/2026/04/21/file_attachments/3624490/2026-04-21_-_Roblox_AL_-_Final_Term_Sheet_-_for_Roblox_signature.pdf) on the Alabama AG's website
- The $12.2 million payment is deposited entirely into the [Alabama Safe School Initiative Fund](https://www.wsfa.com/2026/04/21/alabama-ag-announces-122m-child-safety-settlement-with-online-gaming-platform-roblox/) to fund school resource officers across the state
- **Legal basis:** The settlement resolves pre-litigation claims under Alabama's consumer protection and deceptive trade practices statute; the agreement also references compliance with [COPPA](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa), per the publicly filed term sheet; no Alabama Personal Data Protection Act (APDPA/HB 351) claims are cited
- **Age verification:** Roblox must require all users to verify age using either government-issued ID or facial age estimation technology before accessing chat features; behavioral monitoring will flag users whose ages may have been incorrectly entered, per the [Alabama AG press release](https://www.alabamaag.gov/attorney-general-marshall-announces-12-2-million-settlement-with-roblox-to-ensure-child-safety/)
- **Chat restrictions:** Adult users and users under 16 may not chat unless designated as a mutually verified "trusted friend"; trusted-friend designations for users under 13 require parental consent; users 13-15 may add trusted friends via QR code or phone contact importer unless a parent opts out, per [Alabama Reflector coverage](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/04/21/alabama-reaches-12-2-million-settlement-with-roblox-over-parental-lawsuits/)
- **Encryption ban:** Roblox will not encrypt communications involving minor users, preserving law enforcement access for child exploitation investigations; Roblox must also establish a direct law enforcement liaison position accessible to Alabama agencies, per [WSFA reporting](https://www.wsfa.com/2026/04/21/alabama-ag-announces-122m-child-safety-settlement-with-online-gaming-platform-roblox/)
- **Parental controls:** Settlement expands controls enabling parents to restrict who their children communicate with, which games they can play, and Robux (in-platform currency) transfers from non-trusted adults
- **Safe content default:** All users under 16 and all unverified users are defaulted to safe content mode
- **Data minimization:** Roblox will not retain data collected solely for the age verification process, per [GovPing summary](https://changeflow.com/govping/courts-legal/alabama-ag-12-2m-roblox-settlement-for-child-safety-2026-04-21)
- **MFN clause:** The settlement includes a most-favored-nation provision entitling Alabama to any more favorable terms Roblox later agrees to with any other state, per the [term sheet](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/ALAG/2026/04/21/file_attachments/3624490/2026-04-21_-_Roblox_AL_-_Final_Term_Sheet_-_for_Roblox_signature.pdf)
- **Platform-wide effect:** Settlement reforms apply to all Roblox users nationally, not only Alabama-based users, per [Alabama Daily News](https://aldailynews.com/alabama-attorney-general-12-million-dollar-roblox-settlement/)
- **Implementation timeline:** Some requirements are already in effect as of announcement; remaining changes roll out between May 1 and September 1, 2026, per [Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/04/21/alabama-reaches-12-2-million-settlement-with-roblox-over-parental-lawsuits/)

## Multistate Context [HIGH confidence]

Alabama's settlement is one of three announced in the same week as part of a coordinated state AG enforcement campaign:

- **Nevada** ($12 million): Nevada AG Aaron Ford [announced his settlement on April 15, 2026](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/roblox-gaming-platform-reaches-12-million-settlement-with-nevada-enhancing-youth-protections), with $10 million over three years directed to programs such as the Boys and Girls Club and other non-digital activities; includes facial age estimation technology and government ID verification requirements on materially identical terms to the Alabama settlement. Note: Biometric Update [reported the figure as $12.5 million](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202604/roblox-on-the-hook-for-12-5m-in-nevada-online-child-safety-settlement), apparently reflecting a granular component tally ($10M + $1M + $1.5M); the official AG headline figure and all other authoritative reporting — including [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/roblox-gaming-platform-reaches-12-million-settlement-with-nevada-enhancing-youth-protections), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/roblox-agrees-to-a-12-million-settlement-with-nevada-142842421.html), [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/04/15/roblox-gaming-platform-reaches-us12m-settlement-with-nevada-enhancing-youth-protections/), and [Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/apr/15/roblox-gaming-platform-reaches-12-million-settleme/) — consistently states "$12 million" or "more than $12 million."
- **West Virginia** ($11.1 million): WV AG JB McCuskey [announced simultaneously with Alabama on April 21, 2026](https://ago.wv.gov/article/west-virginia-reaches-11-million-settlement-roblox-prompts-major-child-safety-overhaul); funds are allocated to safety workshops, a public safety campaign, and a dedicated internet safety specialist
- **Combined total:** The three settlements total approximately [$35.8 million](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2026/04/23/866879.htm) in monetary relief, per Insurance Journal (figure uses the $12.5M Biometric Update component tally for Nevada; if the official $12M figure is used, the aggregate is approximately $35.3 million)

At least seven additional state AGs have active child safety suits against Roblox that have not yet settled:

- **Tennessee, Texas, Iowa, Florida, and Nebraska** — per [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/roblox-alabama-settlement-attorney-general-rcna341128); as of March 2026, Nebraska filed suit alleging Roblox [enabled child exploitation and engaged in deceptive safety practices](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2026/03/04/nebraska-sues-roblox-for-what-ag-calls-enabling-child-exploitation-deceptive-safety-practices/)
- **Kentucky** — AG Russell Coleman filed suit in October 2025 in Madison Circuit Court, alleging Roblox knowingly enabled child sexual exploitation and failed to implement effective age verification, per the [official Kentucky AG press release](https://www.kentucky.gov/Pages/Activity-stream.aspx?n=AttorneyGeneral&prId=1832) and [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/kentucky-sues-roblox-citing-charlie-kirk-assassination-simulators-child-safety-lawsuit); the complaint additionally cites Roblox's failure to remove violent simulation games accessible to children
- **Louisiana** — AG Liz Murrill filed suit in August 2025, calling Roblox ["the perfect place for pedophiles"](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/louisiana-attorney-general-sues-roblox-calling-platform-perfect-place-rcna225144) and alleging violations of the Louisiana Unfair Trade Practices Act through design choices that enabled adult predators to reach children; additional coverage at [WAFB](https://www.wafb.com/2026/03/07/louisiana-ag-sues-roblox-alleging-platform-exposes-children-sex-predators/) and [Louisiana Illuminator](https://lailluminator.com/briefs/louisiana-files-lawsuit-against-roblox-calling-it-a-platform-for-sexual-predators/)

The NBC News report cited at time of initial publication covered only five states; Kentucky and Louisiana filed suit prior to the Alabama settlement announcement but were not included in that outlet's April 2026 coverage.

There are additionally [146 Roblox cases](https://www.consumernotice.org/legal/roblox-lawsuit/) consolidated in multidistrict litigation brought by individual families.

At the federal level, the FTC holds [FOIA-disclosed records on Roblox child safety](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/frequently-requested-foia-records/roblox/2026-00096-2026-00096_roblox_child_safety_redacted), and in February 2026 issued a [COPPA Policy Statement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children) encouraging platforms to adopt age verification technologies — a regulatory backdrop that contextualizes the age verification mandates in these settlements.

## Legal Basis and Significance [MEDIUM confidence]

The Alabama settlement is framed as an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance under Alabama's consumer protection and deceptive trade practices statute. The settlement does not cite Alabama's newly enacted Personal Data Protection Act (HB 351, signed April 2026), which is prospective with an effective date of January 1, 2027.

The key legal theory underlying the pre-litigation claims — as reflected in contemporaneous civil suits from Alabama families — is that Roblox's design choices (permitting unverified adult-to-child contact, failing to enforce age gating, monetizing a platform it knew exposed children to predators) constituted deceptive and unfair trade practices. At least three Alabama families filed suits against Roblox alleging the platform failed to protect children from grooming, per [Alabama Reflector](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/04/21/alabama-reaches-12-2-million-settlement-with-roblox-over-parental-lawsuits/).

The facial-scan age verification requirement is significant in the broader context of state children's online safety legislation: it operationalizes an approach the FTC flagged as acceptable under COPPA (collecting biometric age signals without parental consent, under specific conditions, solely for age determination). The settlement's data minimization requirement — no retention of age verification data — tracks the FTC's February 2026 policy statement conditions.

The MFN clause has structural implications for Roblox's multistate exposure: it converts each future settlement into a ratchet that may automatically benefit earlier-settling states if Roblox agrees to stronger terms elsewhere.

## Action Items

- **Platform operators with users under 16** should treat the Roblox settlement terms as a practical benchmark for state AG expectations on age verification, adult-to-minor chat restrictions, and parental controls — particularly in states with active AG investigations (Tennessee, Texas, Iowa, Florida, Nebraska, Kentucky, and Louisiana)
- **Age verification program design:** The settlement endorses facial age estimation plus government ID as compliant age assurance approaches; operators should document that age verification data is not retained beyond the verification event to satisfy both the settlement model and the FTC's February 2026 COPPA policy statement conditions
- **Encryption policy audit:** The settlement's prohibition on encrypting minor-user communications is a significant operational requirement; platforms currently offering end-to-end encryption for minor accounts should assess how this enforcement posture intersects with their architecture
- **Parental control scope:** The settlement's expanded parental controls framework — covering contact restrictions, content access, and in-platform currency transfers — establishes a checklist for operators to evaluate against their own parental consent mechanisms
- **MFN exposure modeling:** Companies in multistate AG settlement negotiations should model MFN clause risks: agreeing to stronger terms in any state may automatically improve terms for earlier-settling states
- **Alabama APDPA readiness (separate track):** Although the Roblox settlement did not invoke Alabama's new Personal Data Protection Act (HB 351), that law takes effect January 1, 2027; operators with Alabama customers should begin compliance work on that statute in parallel
