# New Jersey and Texas AG Offices Build Out Dedicated Privacy Enforcement Teams (2024)

**Jurisdiction:** New Jersey, Texas | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2024-05-28

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

In mid-2024, the attorneys general of both New Jersey and Texas made significant investments in dedicated data privacy enforcement capacity, signaling that state-level enforcement of comprehensive privacy statutes is moving from aspiration to operational reality. Texas AG Ken Paxton announced on June 4, 2024, the creation of a specialized Privacy and Technology enforcement team — reported to exceed ten attorneys at launch — within the Consumer Protection Division, timed to coincide with the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA) taking effect on July 1, 2024. Separately, the New Jersey AG's Office expanded its pre-existing Data Privacy and Cybersecurity (DPC) Section to a reported 11 full-time attorneys ahead of the New Jersey Data Protection Act (NJDPA) effective date of January 15, 2025. Together, these developments mark a national inflection point: two major states with distinct legal regimes are building enforcement infrastructure that rivals, and in Texas's case purports to exceed, the scale of any other state privacy enforcement unit in the country. Companies operating in either jurisdiction — or processing the personal data of residents of either state — face materially elevated enforcement risk and should treat these developments as a trigger for compliance prioritization.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Texas: From Statute to Enforcement Machine

Texas enacted the Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA) as [House Bill 4 (88th Regular Session)](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/HB00004F.htm), which was signed into law in 2023 and became effective July 1, 2024. The TDPSA is codified at [Chapter 541 of the Texas Business and Commerce Code](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/file-consumer-complaint/consumer-privacy-rights/texas-data-privacy-and-security-act) and grants the Office of the Attorney General exclusive enforcement authority, with no private right of action.

The TDPSA did not emerge in a vacuum. Texas already maintained several complementary privacy statutes that the new team would enforce in parallel: the Texas Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act, the Texas Data Broker Law, the Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI), and the Deceptive Trade Practices Act. In February 2022, AG Paxton's office had already demonstrated its enforcement appetite when it [filed suit against Meta for biometric data collection](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/paxton-sues-facebook-using-unauthorized-biometric-data) without consent under CUBI — a case that would ultimately settle in July 2024 for $1.4 billion, one of the largest privacy settlements in US history.

### New Jersey: Expanding a Pre-Existing Unit

New Jersey's enforcement trajectory differs from Texas's in that the AG's Office did not build its privacy team from scratch. The Data Privacy and Cybersecurity (DPC) Section, housed within the Division of Law's Affirmative Civil Enforcement Practice Group, predates the NJDPA and had already been active in investigating data practices. Former AG Gurbir Grewal originally announced the creation of the DPC Section and its takeover of New Jersey's Facebook investigation. By 2024, ahead of the NJDPA's enactment, the section had grown to 11 full-time attorneys, as [noted by BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerdatacounsel.com/blogs/new-jersey-attorney-generals-office-ramping-up-data-privacy-and-cybersecurity-enforcement-efforts/).

New Jersey Governor Phil Murphy signed [Senate Bill 332 (the NJDPA)](https://www.njleg.state.nj.us/bill-search/2022/S332/bill-text?f=S0500&n=332_R6) on January 16, 2024, making New Jersey the 13th state to enact a comprehensive consumer privacy law. The law became effective January 15, 2025, with the DPC Section assigned as the primary enforcement body.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Texas: Team Scope and Statutory Authority

On June 4, 2024 — less than four weeks before the TDPSA's effective date — the [Texas AG's Office announced the formation of its Privacy and Technology enforcement team](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-launches-data-privacy-and-security-initiative-protect-texans-sensitive) within the Consumer Protection Division. The team was explicitly framed as poised to become "among the largest in the country focused on enforcing privacy laws" and was reported to already exceed ten attorneys at the time of the announcement, with further growth anticipated.

The team's enforcement mandate is broad, covering at minimum:

- **TDPSA (Chapter 541, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code):** The comprehensive consumer privacy statute effective July 1, 2024, covering controllers and processors of personal data. Enforcement requires a 30-day cure period after written notice of violation before an action may be filed.
- **Texas Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act:** Pre-existing general data security statute.
- **Texas Data Broker Law:** Requires registration of data brokers; the AG notified over 100 companies of apparent registration failures shortly after enforcement capacity was established.
- **Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI):** Texas's biometric law, under which the Meta case was brought.
- **Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA):** A broad consumer protection statute that the AG has since relied upon to bring data privacy and AI-related cases not squarely covered by sector-specific statutes.
- **Federal laws:** COPPA and HIPAA enforcement through concurrent state authority.

In the months following the team's formation, the Texas AG pursued aggressive enforcement. By late 2024, the office had [conducted investigations into data practices of more than 200 companies](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-leads-nation-protecting-americans-data-privacy-and-security-big-tech), including data brokers, issued violation notices to companies including SiriusXM, MyRadar, Miles, and Tapestri for unauthorized data sharing, and opened a consumer privacy complaint portal that received over 2,000 complaints in its first year of operation. The [Texas Tribune reported](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/06/04/texas-ken-paxton-data-privacy-security/) that privacy abuses would meet the "full force of the law" from the new unit.

Goodwin Law's [June 28, 2024 client alert](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/06/alerts-practices-dpc-texas-new-privacy-law-goes-into-effect-enforcement) noted the team's formation against the backdrop of TDPSA's simultaneous effective date, characterizing the combination as a meaningful escalation in enforcement risk for businesses with Texas consumer data.

### New Jersey: Expanded Team and NJDPA Enforcement Mandate

New Jersey's DPC Section expansion to 11 full-time privacy enforcement attorneys, as [reported by BakerHostetler's Data Counsel blog](https://www.bakerdatacounsel.com/blogs/new-jersey-attorney-generals-office-ramping-up-data-privacy-and-cybersecurity-enforcement-efforts/), is significant in context. Most state privacy enforcement units are staffed by attorneys who also handle non-privacy matters. A dedicated team of 11 attorneys signals the AG's intent to be a primary mover in enforcement nationally.

The NJDPA (SB 332) grants the AG exclusive enforcement authority with no private right of action. Violations of the NJDPA are treated as violations of New Jersey's Unfair Deceptive Acts and Practices (UDAP) framework, enabling the AG to seek:

- Civil penalties of up to **$10,000 per violation** for first violations
- Civil penalties of up to **$20,000 per violation** for second and subsequent violations
- Injunctive relief
- Disgorgement of unjust enrichment

Notably, NJDPA enforcement is sequenced through a notice-and-cure regime: [P.L. 2023, c.266](https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2022/PL23/266_.HTM) provides that "until the first day of the 18th month next following the effective date," the Division of Consumer Affairs must issue a notice of violation and allow 30 days to cure before commencing a civil action. Counting from the January 15, 2025 effective date, the first day of the 18th month is **July 1, 2026**, after which the AG may proceed directly to civil penalties without mandatory notice. The [NJ Division of Consumer Affairs FAQ page](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/ocp/Pages/NJ-Data-Privacy-Law-FAQ.aspx) documents these enforcement parameters.

[Cozen O'Connor's August 2024 Law360 analysis](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2024/considerations-as-state-ags-step-up-privacy-enforcement) placed New Jersey and Texas within a broader national trend of state AGs ramping enforcement infrastructure simultaneous with the wave of comprehensive state privacy laws taking effect in 2024-2025. The firm identified the DPC Section's staffing level as exceptional among state enforcement bodies.

### Comparison and National Significance

The simultaneous buildout in two populous states — with combined populations exceeding 30 million consumers and significant presence of major technology companies, financial institutions, and healthcare entities — represents the maturation of the state AG privacy enforcement model. Key distinctions between the two programs:

| Factor | Texas | New Jersey |
|---|---|---|
| Team home | Consumer Protection Division | Division of Law (Affirmative Civil Enforcement) |
| Reported size (2024) | 10+ attorneys, growing | 11 attorneys |
| Primary statute | TDPSA (eff. July 1, 2024) | NJDPA (eff. Jan. 15, 2025) |
| Notice-and-cure | 30 days | 30 days (through July 1, 2026) |
| Private right of action | None | None |
| Enforcement breadth | Multi-statute (TDPSA, CUBI, Broker, DTPA, federal) | Multi-statute (NJDPA, UDAP, prior DPC mandates) |
| Notable 2024 action | $1.4B Meta biometric settlement | Expansion of pre-existing Facebook investigation |

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

The expansion of enforcement capacity in both states directly elevates risk for the following categories of businesses:

**Highest immediate risk:**
- Data brokers operating in Texas (the AG notified 100+ companies in 2024 and has included Data Broker Act violations in broader TDPSA actions)
- Companies collecting biometric data in Texas (CUBI enforcement is active; the Meta settlement demonstrates willingness to pursue large-scale actions)
- Businesses processing sensitive data about New Jersey residents who have not yet completed NJDPA compliance work ahead of the January 2025 effective date

**Elevated ongoing risk:**
- Any company with a consumer-facing data practice that generates the volume of complaints sufficient to trigger AG investigation (the Texas complaint portal received 2,000+ complaints in year one)
- Ad tech and data sharing companies, given the Texas notice letters to SiriusXM, MyRadar, Miles, and Tapestri
- Companies with AI data practices — the Texas AG has signaled AI as a core enforcement focus, and the DTPA provides a broad hook

**Compliance timeline considerations:**
Both states have 30-day cure periods, but these are not indefinite shields. In Texas, the TDPSA cure period applies per violation notice; in New Jersey, the mandatory cure right expires on July 1, 2026 (the first day of the 18th month following the January 15, 2025 effective date, per [P.L. 2023, c.266](https://pub.njleg.state.nj.us/Bills/2022/PL23/266_.HTM)). Companies that receive violation notices in either state should treat the notice as a serious compliance event, not merely a procedural step.

The Cozen O'Connor analysis notes that the patchwork of state AG enforcement programs creates cumulative compliance obligations that businesses must navigate proactively, rather than reactively.

## Action Items

- **Texas-facing businesses:** Confirm TDPSA compliance status for any processing of Texas consumers' personal data. Verify Data Broker Law registration if applicable. Audit biometric data collection and consent practices under CUBI.
- **New Jersey-facing businesses:** Complete NJDPA gap analysis and remediation before January 15, 2025 effective date. Establish a process to receive and respond to AG notice-of-violation letters within the 30-day cure window — this mandatory cure window expires on July 1, 2026.
- **Both jurisdictions:** Implement consumer complaint intake processes to allow self-identification of potential violations before they become AG investigation triggers. The Texas portal demonstrates that consumer complaints are an active enforcement pipeline.
- **Data brokers:** Prioritize Texas Data Broker Law registration compliance; the AG has shown it will issue notices to non-registered entities at scale.
- **Monitor:** Watch for the first NJDPA enforcement actions, expected no sooner than mid-2025. Track Texas TDPSA enforcement actions closely — the office has been active in issuing notices since the law took effect.
- **Review multi-statute exposure:** Companies with operations in both states should map all applicable statutes (not just the omnibus privacy laws) given both AGs' multi-statute enforcement mandates.
