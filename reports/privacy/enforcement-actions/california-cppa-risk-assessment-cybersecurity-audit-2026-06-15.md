# California CPPA Risk Assessment and Cybersecurity Audit Certification Requirements

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-15

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) finalized regulations on September 23, 2025 — effective January 1, 2026 — imposing mandatory privacy risk assessments and annual cybersecurity audit certifications on qualifying businesses subject to the CCPA. These obligations represent the most demanding proactive compliance regime of any US state privacy law, requiring independent audit certifications submitted to the CPPA and complete risk assessments prior to initiating high-risk data processing. Businesses have until phased deadlines beginning April 1, 2028 to file initial certifications, but underlying compliance work must begin now.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The [California Office of Administrative Law approved the final CPPA regulations on September 23, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html), with an effective date of January 1, 2026. The regulations cover three new compliance areas: privacy risk assessments, cybersecurity audits, and automated decision-making technology (ADMT).

- **Cybersecurity audit applicability thresholds** apply to businesses that: (1) derive 50% or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing consumers' personal information; or (2) have annual gross revenues over $25 million (adjusted biennially; currently $26,625,000) AND process personal information of more than 250,000 California consumers or households, or sensitive personal information of more than 50,000 consumers. ([Wilson Elser analysis](https://www.wilsonelser.com/publications/californias-ccpa-new-cybersecurity-audit-rules-applicability-thresholds-audit-scope-and-compliance-timelines))

- **Phased certification deadlines** for cybersecurity audits are based on annual revenue: businesses with revenues over $100 million (measured in 2026) must submit their first certification by **April 1, 2028**; businesses with $50–$100 million in 2027 revenue by **April 1, 2029**; businesses under $50 million in 2028 revenue by **April 1, 2030**. ([Ropes & Gray alert](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/californias-ccpa-cybersecurity-audit-rule-takes-effect-what-businesses-need-to-know))

- **Audit independence and standards** (Cal. Code Regs., tit. 11, §§ 7122–7123): Audits must be conducted by a qualified, objective, independent professional using recognized auditing standards such as AICPA, PCAOB, ISACA, or ISO frameworks. Auditor findings cannot rely primarily on management assertions — auditors must use evidence including documents, sampling, testing, and interviews. ([Cornell LII, § 7102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/california/11-CCR-7102))

- **Annual audit reports** must describe the business's full cybersecurity program, including security measures and policies, information systems covered, evidence examined, and audit findings. A written **certification of completion** signed by the auditor must be submitted to the CPPA by April 1 of each year following a covered period. All audit-related records must be retained for at least **five years**. ([Skadden October 2025 analysis](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/california-finalizes-cppa-regulations))

- **Privacy risk assessment triggers** (effective January 1, 2026): Businesses must complete a risk assessment before initiating any processing presenting a "significant risk to consumers' privacy," including: selling or sharing personal information; processing sensitive personal information; using ADMT for significant decisions; using automated processing to infer sensitive personal traits from employee/job applicant activity or presence in sensitive locations; and continuing legacy processing activities that began before January 1, 2026. ([Workplace Privacy Report — Part 1](https://www.workplaceprivacyreport.com/2025/10/articles/california-consumer-privacy-act/understanding-ccpas-new-risk-assessment-requirements-part-1/))

- **Risk assessment update obligations**: Assessments must be updated at least every **three years** and within **45 days** of any material change in an applicable processing activity. ([Loeb & Loeb December 2025 alert](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/california-privacy-regulations-requiring-cybersecurity-audits-and-risk-assessments-what-to-know))

- **First risk assessment filing deadline**: Businesses must submit an attestation and summary of completed risk assessments to the CPPA by **April 1, 2028**, covering assessments conducted during 2026 and 2027. The attestation must identify the types and number of assessments conducted and the categories of personal information involved. ([BCLP analysis](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/risk-assessments-under-the-ccpa-key-triggers-timelines-and-compliance-steps.html))

- **Skadden June 2026 analysis** (the primary Lexology source for this finding) identifies these as among the most demanding proactive compliance obligations under any US state privacy law, specifically because they require independent third-party certifications — not mere self-attestation — filed directly with a state regulator. ([Skadden June 2026 alert](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/californias-mandatory-risk-assessment-and-cybersecurity-audit-certification-requirements))

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The CPPA's authority to promulgate cybersecurity audit and risk assessment regulations derives from the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), which amended the CCPA in 2020 and directed the newly created CPPA to adopt such regulations. The CPPA initiated the formal rulemaking process in November 2024, published proposed regulations in early 2025, held public hearings, and adopted final regulations at its board meeting on July 24, 2025. The California Office of Administrative Law approved the package on September 23, 2025.

The official statute text (including all amendments effective January 1, 2026) is published at [CPPA's regulations page](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_statute_eff_20260101.pdf). The Notice of Approval for the cybersecurity, risk assessment, and ADMT regulations is available at [cppa.ca.gov](https://www.cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_noa.pdf). The agency's Final Statement of Reasons is published at [cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_fsor_and_uid.pdf](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_fsor_and_uid.pdf).

The cybersecurity audit obligation is codified at Cal. Code Regs., tit. 11, §§ 7120–7123. The risk assessment framework appears at tit. 11, §§ 7101 et seq. (Article 8). These are the first cybersecurity audit certification requirements imposed by any US state data privacy law. No other state has yet adopted comparable independent-auditor certification requirements filed directly with a privacy regulator.

## Compliance Gap Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

Many businesses in scope face two overlapping gap risks. First, risk assessments were required to be initiated as of January 1, 2026 for pre-existing processing activities and before initiating any new high-risk processing after that date. Businesses that have not begun this work are already out of compliance with the triggering obligation — not merely behind on the filing deadline. Second, audit preparation timelines are longer than many compliance teams anticipate: identifying a qualified independent auditor, scoping the cybersecurity program, gathering evidentiary documentation, and completing the audit report is typically a 6–18 month process for large organizations.

The [Alston & Bird October 2025 alert](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/ccpa-cybersecurity-audits-admt-risk-assessments) and [Faegre Drinker September 2025 analysis](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/9/cybersecurity-audits-under-the-california-consumer-privacy-act-ccpa) each note that the independence requirement is a potential stumbling block: businesses that have historically used internal audit teams for privacy or security assessments may need to restructure their audit arrangements to satisfy the objective-and-independent standard under § 7122. The regulations permit internal auditors only if they are sufficiently independent from the business functions being audited — a standard that many security teams embedded within business units will not meet.

The CPPA has not yet announced an enforcement moratorium or safe harbor for early-phase compliance. Businesses should not assume the filing deadlines in 2028–2030 also delay the underlying substantive obligations.

## Action Items

- **Immediately identify scope**: Determine whether the business meets either cybersecurity audit threshold (50% revenue from selling/sharing PI, or $26.6M+ revenue + 250K consumers/50K sensitive consumers). Use 2026 annual revenue figures for Group 1 (>$100M) threshold determination.

- **Begin risk assessment inventories now**: Map all processing activities that trigger the risk assessment obligation (selling/sharing PI, sensitive PI processing, ADMT for significant decisions, employee/applicant automated profiling, legacy processing continuing from before January 1, 2026). Document these assessments with the specificity required by Article 8 of Title 11.

- **Build a 45-day material change protocol**: Establish an internal change management procedure to identify and document when processing activity changes require a risk assessment update within 45 days.

- **Engage an independent auditor early**: For businesses in Group 1 (>$100M revenue), the first audit period begins January 1, 2027 and the report is due April 1, 2028. Auditor selection, scoping, and engagement should begin in 2026 to allow adequate lead time. Assess whether existing internal audit resources satisfy the independence standard under § 7122.

- **Develop a five-year records retention program**: All audit reports, documentation, evidence, and certifications must be retained for at least five years from creation. Ensure document retention policies cover audit-related materials.

- **Track CPPA enforcement signals**: The CPPA has issued enforcement actions under the CCPA (most recently the $1.1M PlayOn Sports fine in 2026) and is actively building its enforcement capacity. Monitor CPPA board meetings and enforcement announcements at [cppa.ca.gov](https://cppa.ca.gov) for early enforcement signals related to audit and risk assessment compliance.

- **Monitor the CPPA's DROP audit rulemaking**: The separate data broker audit rulemaking under the Delete Act (DROP audits, for which CPPA opened a preliminary comment period in April 2026) imposes additional audit requirements on registered data brokers. Businesses qualifying as data brokers face a second, overlapping audit obligation track.
