# OCR's 50th HIPAA Right of Access Enforcement: $70,000 CMP Against Maryland Dental Practice

**Jurisdiction:** Federal, Maryland | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-11-08

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On October 17, 2024, the HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) issued a [Notice of Final Determination](https://www.hhs.gov/about/news/2024/10/17/hhs-office-civil-rights-imposes-70000-civil-monetary-penalty-against-gums-dental-care-failure-provide-timely-access-patient-records.html) imposing a $70,000 civil monetary penalty (CMP) against Gums Dental Care, LLC, a solo dental practice in Silver Spring, Maryland, for failure to provide a patient with timely electronic access to protected health information (PHI). The action marks OCR's 50th enforcement action under its HIPAA Right of Access Initiative, which the agency launched in fall 2019 to focus enforcement on timely and affordable patient record access. Unlike the majority of Right of Access enforcement actions — which result in negotiated settlements with corrective action plans — this case proceeded through contested administrative adjudication, with an Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) imposing the penalty in September 2023 and the Departmental Appeals Board (DAB) affirming it in March 2024. The milestone underscores OCR's sustained commitment to right-of-access enforcement across all covered entity types, including small and solo practices.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The HIPAA Privacy Rule, at [45 C.F.R. § 164.524](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.524), grants individuals the right to access their PHI maintained in a designated record set. Covered entities must act on a valid access request no later than 30 calendar days after receipt, with a single permissible extension of up to 30 additional days upon written notice and explanation of the delay. When an individual requests an electronic copy of PHI maintained electronically, the covered entity must provide it in the electronic form and format the individual requests if readily producible, or in another readable electronic format otherwise.

Fee limitations are a central compliance requirement. Under [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html), when PHI is transmitted electronically to a personal health application or third party, covered entities may not charge any fee. For other electronic delivery, the fee may cover only labor for copying. A flat administrative fee not to exceed $6.50 is a recognized safe harbor for providing electronic records. Flat fees for paper copying or postal delivery cannot lawfully be applied to electronic access requests.

OCR launched its Right of Access Initiative in fall 2019 in response to persistent noncompliance in this area. The initiative targets covered entities that fail to provide access in a timely manner or charge fees exceeding what the rule permits. By October 2024, OCR had brought 50 enforcement actions under this initiative, including [settlements with three dental practices in September 2022](https://www.hhs.gov/about/news/2022/09/20/ocr-settles-three-cases-dental-practices-patient-right-access-under-hipaa.html) ranging from $30,000 to $80,000, and numerous other actions across hospital systems, physician practices, and specialty clinics.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The Gums Dental Care Case

Gums Dental Care, LLC is a solo practice owned and operated by Anna Gumbs, DMD, in Silver Spring, Maryland. The enforcement action originated from a complaint filed in May 2019, in which a patient alleged that she had submitted a written request on April 8, 2019, for electronic copies of her own PHI and the PHI of her minor children. Gums Dental responded the same day by providing information about the number of office visits — not the actual records — and did not provide the requested PHI.

OCR sent technical assistance to the practice, advising it of HIPAA's right-of-access requirements and encouraging workforce training. Notwithstanding that guidance, the patient submitted a second written request on June 26, 2019. When no records were forthcoming, the patient filed a second complaint with OCR on August 2, 2019.

OCR's investigation identified two compounding compliance failures. First, Gums Dental asserted that it could not send records electronically because it lacked a secure website — but this rationale does not relieve a covered entity of its obligation. OCR noted that Gums Dental made no apparent effort to provide the records in any alternative electronic form or format. Second, Gums Dental sought to charge the patient a flat fee of $25 to mail records via certified mail. OCR found this fee impermissible for an electronic access request: the $6.50 flat-fee safe harbor applies to electronic delivery, and charging a separate postal fee for a request explicitly directed at electronic delivery violates the fee cap provisions.

The practice's records were not produced until May 2022, more than three years after the initial request, and only after OCR issued a Notice of Proposed Determination signaling intent to pursue a CMP.

### Administrative Adjudication and the CMP

OCR issued a Notice of Proposed Determination seeking a $70,000 CMP. Gums Dental exercised its right to contest the penalty before an HHS Administrative Law Judge. On September 29, 2023, the [ALJ issued a decision](https://www.hhs.gov/about/agencies/dab/decisions/alj-decisions/2023/alj-cr6367/index.html) finding that Gums Dental violated 45 C.F.R. § 164.524(b)(2) — the timely-action provision — and affirmed the $70,000 penalty. Gums Dental appealed to the Departmental Appeals Board. On March 22, 2024, the [DAB affirmed the ALJ's ruling](https://www.hhs.gov/about/agencies/dab/decisions/board-decisions/2024/board-dab-3132/index.html). OCR then issued a Notice of Final Determination on October 17, 2024, rendering the $70,000 penalty final and collectible.

This trajectory is notable. Most Right of Access cases are resolved as voluntary settlements with corrective action plans. When a covered entity elects to contest a Notice of Proposed Determination and the penalty is ultimately affirmed through the full administrative appellate process, no corrective action plan is imposed — the CMP stands alone. Research from enforcement data indicates that organizations that settled their Right of Access cases paid approximately 18% less than those against whom a CMP was ultimately imposed, suggesting a financial incentive to engage with OCR's settlement process in good faith.

### The 50th Action Milestone

OCR's Right of Access Initiative has produced a steady accumulation of enforcement actions since 2019. The total financial penalties under the initiative now exceed $2.8 million across 50 cases. Penalty amounts have ranged from $3,500 to $240,000, with an average of approximately $56,794. The dental sector has been disproportionately represented in enforcement activity, reflecting a pattern of noncompliance with access request procedures in smaller practices that may lack dedicated compliance infrastructure.

Just weeks after the Gums Dental announcement, OCR announced its 51st Right of Access action in November 2024 involving a mental health center, signaling that the initiative's pace of enforcement shows no sign of slowing. OCR's press release on the Gums Dental case included a pointed statement from the agency that the message of the initiative should now be "loud and clear."

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Affected Entities

This enforcement action is directly relevant to all HIPAA-covered entities, with heightened salience for:

- **Solo and small group practices**: The Gums Dental case demonstrates that OCR pursues enforcement against the smallest providers, not only large institutions with dedicated compliance teams. The $70,000 penalty on a solo dentist is a substantial financial consequence.
- **Dental practices**: The dental sector has been a repeated enforcement target under the Right of Access Initiative. OCR's September 2022 batch of three dental practice settlements and the Gums Dental CMP make clear that dental practices are a focus population.
- **Any covered entity without electronic records delivery capability**: OCR has rejected the argument that the absence of a "secure website" excuses non-delivery. Providers must identify alternative electronic delivery methods, such as encrypted email with consent.

### Compliance Requirements

The regulatory requirements under 45 C.F.R. § 164.524 are unambiguous and enforced strictly:

1. **30-day response deadline**: Covered entities must fulfill or formally deny access requests within 30 days of receipt. A single 30-day extension requires written notice and stated reasons.
2. **Electronic format**: If the patient requests electronic delivery, the provider must deliver electronically unless a requested format is not readily producible, in which case an alternative readable electronic format must be offered.
3. **Fee caps**: Providers cannot charge a flat administrative fee for postal delivery on a request for electronic records. The $6.50 flat-fee safe harbor applies to electronic delivery scenarios. Labor-based fees must be cost-justified.
4. **Technical assistance is not a safe harbor**: Receiving OCR technical assistance — and failing to comply — is an aggravating factor that can influence both the decision to impose a CMP and the penalty amount.

### Potential Consequences of Non-Compliance

The penalty scale for Right of Access violations runs from $127 to $1,919,173 per violation category per year, calibrated by culpability tier (unknowing, reasonable cause, willful neglect — corrected, willful neglect — not corrected). The $70,000 CMP in this case reflects a multi-year pattern of noncompliance following OCR's technical assistance. Providers who contest a CMP and lose through the full adjudicative process sacrifice the opportunity for a corrective action plan and the statistical reduction in penalty amount that settlements tend to produce.

## Action Items

- Review access request intake procedures and assign a responsible individual to track response deadlines against the 30-day requirement.
- Audit fee schedules for records requests and confirm that electronic delivery is not being billed at postal or flat administrative rates exceeding $6.50.
- Develop and document at least one electronic delivery mechanism (e.g., encrypted email with patient consent, patient portal, or third-party delivery service) so that "no secure website" cannot be asserted as grounds for non-delivery.
- Train clinical and front-office staff on the right-of-access requirements, including the prohibition on conditioning records release on outstanding balances or fees not authorized by the fee cap rule.
- Respond cooperatively and promptly to any OCR technical assistance or data requests; lack of response is an aggravating factor in CMP calculations.
- Monitor OCR's [Resolution Agreements and CMPs page](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/index.html) for new Right of Access enforcement patterns as the initiative continues.
