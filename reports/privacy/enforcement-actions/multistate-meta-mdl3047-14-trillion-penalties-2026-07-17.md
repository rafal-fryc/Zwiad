# States Seek Up to $1.4 Trillion From Meta in August MDL 3047 Youth Safety Trial

**Jurisdiction:** California, Colorado, Kentucky, New Jersey (Multi-state) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 9, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [DISPUTED]

Meta disclosed in a July 7, 2026 court filing that attorneys general from California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey are seeking as much as $1.4 trillion in civil penalties ahead of an August 2026 trial in *In re: Social Media Adolescent Addiction/Personal Injury Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 3047, pending before Chief District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/07/07/338654.htm); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/four-states-seeking-1-4-trillion-penalties-child-social-media-addiction-trial-meta-says)). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch across multiple wire outlets --> The figure — roughly equivalent to Meta's entire market capitalization — stems from the four states' calculation of per-violation statutory penalties under their consumer-protection and children's-privacy statutes, multiplied by the estimated number of affected teen and child users ([The National Desk](https://thenationaldesk.com/news/americas-news-now/states-seek-14-trillion-from-meta-in-landmark-youth-social-media-trial-facebook-instagram-teens-social-media-mental-health-lawsuit)). <!-- disputed: independent verification found Meta's market cap as of July 2026 was approximately $1.69-1.73 trillion (companiesmarketcap.com, macrotrends.net), roughly 20-24% higher than the $1.4 trillion penalty figure -- "roughly equivalent to" overstates the comparison and is not tied to a specific cited source --> The trial is scheduled to proceed in Oakland, California, beginning August 18, 2026, shortly after Judge Gonzalez Rogers, in a 38-page June 30, 2026 decision, denied Meta's motion to dismiss the states' deception, unfair-practices, and federal COPPA claims — finding material factual disputes over whether Meta's platforms are addictive by design and whether Meta misrepresented that fact — and separately granted the states partial summary judgment on a discrete COPPA issue: Meta's failure to comply with COPPA's notice and parental-consent requirements ([U.S. News/AP](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-06-30/meta-loses-bid-to-dismiss-us-states-claims-that-facebook-instagram-addict-children); [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-orders-trial-for-meta-over-social-media-harms/); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/judge-lets-states-pursue-claims-meta-designed-facebook-instagram-addict-children)). <!-- verified: independently confirmed via WebSearch (OANN, Law.com, US News/AP, Courthouse News) that the June 30 ruling denied a motion to dismiss and separately granted partial summary judgment to the states on the COPPA notice/consent issue; 38-page decision length also confirmed -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- **Case and forum:** *In re: Social Media Adolescent Addiction/Personal Injury Products Liability Litigation*, MDL No. 4:22-md-03047-YGR, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, before Chief District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers ([official case docket, N.D. Cal.](https://cand.uscourts.gov/cases-e-filing/cases/422-md-03047-ygr/re-social-media-adolescent-addictionpersonal-injury-products); [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/65407433/in-re-social-media-adolescent-addictionpersonal-injury-products-liability/)). <!-- verified: case number 4:22-md-03047-YGR independently confirmed via WebSearch (CourtListener, govinfo.gov docket filings) -->
- **Penalty figure:** Meta's July 7, 2026 filing states that California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey are seeking up to $1.4 trillion in civil penalties if the states prevail at trial — an amount Meta called having "no analog in the history of consumer protection enforcement" ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/07/07/338654.htm); [Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/meta-says-us-states-are-seeking-14-trillion-in-penalties-in-august-youth-safety-trial-034217946.html)). <!-- verified: independently corroborated by numerous wire outlets via WebSearch -->
- **States and claims proceeding to trial:** The August 18, 2026 trial will hear two distinct sets of claims together in the same proceeding: (1) state-law consumer-protection and children's-privacy claims brought solely by California, Colorado, Kentucky, and New Jersey — the basis for the $1.4 trillion penalty figure — and (2) federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) claims brought by 29 states. Only the four named states are pursuing state-law claims and the associated penalty demand; the other 25 states with claims in MDL 3047 are participating only through the federal COPPA track at this trial ([JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/meta-says-state-ags-seek-1-4t-over-youth-safety-claims/); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/lawsuit-news/meta-social-media-addiction-lawsuits-cleared-for-august-bellwether-trial/); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/four-states-seeking-1-4-trillion-penalties-child-social-media-addiction-trial-meta-says)). <!-- verified: "29 states" COPPA figure independently corroborated via WebSearch (RT, Law.com "29 States Head to Trial") -->
- **Penalty methodology:** Because the states' penalty filings are sealed, the methodology is known only from public court hearings: the states are multiplying the number of alleged statutory violations (based on estimated affected teen/child users on Facebook and Instagram) by the per-violation fine amounts set under each state's law ([The National Desk](https://thenationaldesk.com/news/americas-news-now/states-seek-14-trillion-from-meta-in-landmark-youth-social-media-trial-facebook-instagram-teens-social-media-mental-health-lawsuit)). <!-- verified: methodology description consistent with independent WebSearch summaries -->
- **Pre-trial motion-to-dismiss and partial summary judgment rulings:** On June 30, 2026, in a 38-page decision, Judge Gonzalez Rogers denied Meta's **motion to dismiss** the states' deception, unfair-practices, and federal COPPA claims, finding material factual disputes over whether Meta's platforms are addictive by design and whether Meta misrepresented that fact ("a jury could reasonably find the statements were untrue to a reasonable person"). In the same ruling, the court separately **granted the states partial summary judgment** on a discrete COPPA issue — Meta's failure to comply with COPPA's notice and parental-consent requirements ([U.S. News/AP](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-06-30/meta-loses-bid-to-dismiss-us-states-claims-that-facebook-instagram-addict-children); [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/judge-orders-trial-for-meta-over-social-media-harms/); [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/judge-lets-states-pursue-claims-meta-designed-facebook-instagram-addict-children); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/06/30/875848.htm)). Note: earlier drafts of this report and some initial wire summaries mischaracterized this as a summary-judgment denial rather than a motion-to-dismiss denial; the correction above reflects independent verification across multiple outlets. <!-- verified: independently re-confirmed via WebSearch (OANN "Meta loses bid to toss," Law.com "29 States Head to Trial ... Judge Refuses to Toss Claims") that the ruling is a motion-to-dismiss denial plus a separate COPPA partial summary judgment grant; note that the California AG's own press release characterizes the ruling more loosely as denying Meta's "attempt to obtain summary judgment," which is the AG office's own framing rather than the more precise procedural posture reported by legal press -- flagged for awareness but does not change the verdict since the specialized legal press (Law360, Law.com, Courthouse News) consistently use "motion to dismiss" -->
- **Trial structure:** The August 18, 2026 trial will be conducted with an eight-member advisory jury to assist Judge Gonzalez Rogers, who will make the final findings on the state attorneys general claims; the trial covers the four states' state-law claims together with the 29 states' federal COPPA claims ([Law360](https://www.law360.com/colorado/articles/2482427); [Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/meta-says-us-states-are-seeking-14-trillion-in-penalties-in-august-youth-safety-trial-034217946.html); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/meta-says-state-ags-seek-1-4t-over-youth-safety-claims/)). <!-- verified: eight-member advisory jury and August 18 trial date independently corroborated via WebSearch -->
- **Broader litigation context:** MDL 3047 also encompasses approximately 800 school-district lawsuits and personal-injury claims against Meta, Snap, TikTok/ByteDance, YouTube, Google, and Alphabet, filed since October 2022 (one source, MediaNama, cites a higher figure of over 1,200 school districts as part of the broader MDL, so this count should be treated as approximate); the state AG track is a distinct advisory-jury bellwether within the same MDL before the same judge ([official case docket](https://cand.uscourts.gov/cases-e-filing/cases/422-md-03047-ygr/re-social-media-adolescent-addictionpersonal-injury-products); [MDL Update](https://mdlupdate.com/mdl/3047-social-media-adolescent-addiction/); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/07/223-us-states-meta-pay-1-4-trillion-youth-addiction-case/)). <!-- verified: "nearly 800 school district lawsuits" figure independently corroborated via WebSearch; disclosed range vs. MediaNama's higher figure is appropriate given source disagreement -->
- **Meta's defense theory:** Meta argues the AGs have no evidence it misled consumers because "social media addiction" is not a recognized psychiatric diagnosis, and that statements it made about its platforms' safety therefore cannot be shown false ([Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/meta-says-us-states-are-seeking-14-trillion-in-penalties-in-august-youth-safety-trial-034217946.html)). <!-- verified: consistent with independent WebSearch summaries of Meta's defense -->

## Action Items

- **Track the August 18, 2026 trial calendar.** The advisory-jury proceeding before Judge Gonzalez Rogers will be the first federal bellwether resolution of the state AG track in MDL 3047, combining the four states' state-law claims with 29 states' federal COPPA claims, and will inform penalty exposure calculations for the other state AGs with parallel claims pending in the same MDL.
- **Monitor the partial summary judgment order for legal reasoning on COPPA notice/consent.** The court's finding that Meta failed to satisfy COPPA's notice-and-parental-consent requirements is a discrete, already-decided liability point that other platforms with child-directed features should assess against their own COPPA compliance posture.
- **Assess statutory-penalty stacking exposure.** The $1.4 trillion figure illustrates how per-violation state consumer-protection statutes can produce extraordinary aggregate exposure when multiplied across large user bases; companies operating platforms used by minors should model similar exposure under applicable state UDAP/consumer-protection statutes.
- **Watch for a possible settlement before trial.** Given the scale of exposure disclosed, monitor for settlement announcements between Meta and the four trial-track states in the weeks before the August 18 trial date.
