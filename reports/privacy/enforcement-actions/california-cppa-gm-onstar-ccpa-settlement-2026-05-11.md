# California Secures Record $12.75 Million CCPA Settlement Against GM/OnStar for Connected Vehicle Data Sales

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-05-11

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 8, 2026, California Attorney General Rob Bonta, the California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA), and district attorneys from San Francisco, Los Angeles, Napa, and Sonoma counties announced a [$12.75 million settlement](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general) with General Motors LLC and its OnStar connected vehicle service — the largest CCPA penalty in California history and the state's first enforcement action targeting data minimization and purpose limitation violations. From 2020 to 2024, GM sold precise geolocation and driving behavior data of hundreds of thousands of California consumers to data brokers LexisNexis Risk Solutions and Verisk Analytics for use in insurance risk-scoring products, without adequate consumer notice or consent. The settlement requires GM to cease selling driving data to consumer reporting agencies for five years, delete retained driving data within 180 days, and build a comprehensive privacy compliance program.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Announcement date:** May 8, 2026; announced jointly by California AG Rob Bonta, the CPPA, and district attorneys from San Francisco, Los Angeles, Napa, and Sonoma counties. ([CA AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general))
- **Penalty amount:** $12.75 million — the largest CCPA fine ever imposed, surpassing the prior record $2.75 million Disney settlement. ([Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations))
- **Data at issue:** Names, contact information, precise geolocation (including parking location), and driving behavior signals (hard braking, rapid acceleration, speeds) collected through the OnStar connected vehicle service from 2020 to 2024. ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/05/gm-record-california-penalty-onstar-data/))
- **Revenue from data sales:** GM earned approximately $20 million nationwide from selling driving data to LexisNexis and Verisk, both of which intended to develop driver-rating products for auto insurance underwriting. ([Spectrum News / CA AG press release](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/us/snplus/business/2026/05/08/gm-driver-data-california-lawsuit-settlement))
- **Statutes alleged violated:** California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA), California Unfair Competition Law (Bus. & Prof. Code § 17200), and California False Advertising Law. ([Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/california-announces-largest-ccpa-penalty-to-date-and-first-data-minimization-enforcement-action))
- **Core CCPA provision:** Civil Code § 1798.100(c) — a business's collection, use, retention, and sharing of personal information must be "reasonably necessary and proportionate" to the purposes for which it was collected; using data collected to operate a vehicle service for third-party insurance scoring violates this requirement. ([leginfo.legislature.ca.gov § 1798.100](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100.))
- **First data minimization enforcement action:** Regulators specifically characterized the settlement as California's first enforcement of CCPA's data minimization and purpose limitation principles. ([Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/california-announces-largest-ccpa-penalty-to-date-and-first-data-minimization-enforcement-action))
- **California consumer impact:** California consumers were not directly harmed by the insurance rate-setting use, because California's insurance laws independently prohibit insurers from using telematics driving data to set insurance rates. ([CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/05/gm-record-california-penalty-onstar-data/))

## Settlement Terms [HIGH confidence]

In addition to the $12.75 million civil penalty, the consent judgment (subject to court approval) requires GM to:

1. **Stop selling driving data** to consumer reporting agencies for **five years**, including to LexisNexis and Verisk. ([Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations))
2. **Delete retained driving data** within **180 days** unless GM obtains affirmative, express consumer consent for continued retention. ([Clark Hill / JDSupra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/california-announces-record-12-75-9447635/))
3. **Request deletion** of driving data already held by LexisNexis and Verisk. ([Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-ag-announces-record-12-75m-settlement-with-gm-over-ccpa-data-minimization-and-purpose-limitation-violations))
4. **Develop and maintain a privacy program** to assess, mitigate, and document privacy risks. ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/cgm-onstar-ccpa-settlement-12-75m-privacy-enforcement/))
5. **Complete privacy assessments** and submit compliance reports to regulators. ([McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/california-announces-multi-million-data-minimization-settlement-with-gm))

## Regulatory Context [HIGH confidence]

The GM settlement is the most recent and significant action in an ongoing California enforcement campaign against connected vehicle manufacturers. The CPPA's Enforcement Division launched a formal review of connected vehicle data practices in 2023 — the agency's first such review since acquiring full enforcement authority on July 1, 2023. Prior actions in the same cluster include:

- **Honda settlement (2025):** American Honda Motor Co. paid $632,500 and was required to reform its advertising technology vendor contracts for failing to include CCPA-required privacy safeguards. ([Proskauer / National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/new-aged-automakers-beware-cppas-enforcement-action-against-honda-results-agencys))
- **Ford settlement (March 2026):** The CPPA Board required Ford to pay $375,703 for creating "unnecessary friction" in consumers' opt-out process by requiring email verification before honoring opt-out requests. Ford was also required to audit its tracking technologies and honor Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals. ([CPPA press release](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/03/ford-to-change-practices-pay-fine-for-adding-unnecessary-friction-to-opt-out-process/))

The GM case escalates the enforcement ladder significantly: earlier actions targeted opt-out mechanics; the GM action targets the substantive decision to share data with third parties for incompatible purposes — a harder-to-cure compliance failure.

The CPPA had previously signaled its data minimization enforcement priority in its April 2024 Enforcement Advisory (No. 2024-01), which stated that data minimization requirements under CCPA apply to how businesses handle consumer access and deletion requests — a precursor to the full-scale data-sharing theory applied against GM. ([CPPA Enforcement Advisory 2024-01](https://cppa.ca.gov/pdf/enfadvisory202401.pdf))

## Action Items

- **Audit connected-product data flows immediately.** Any product that collects location, behavioral, biometric, or sensor data — not just vehicles — must verify that each downstream data sharing arrangement is necessary and proportionate to the purpose disclosed at collection.
- **Map all secondary uses of operational data.** Data collected to operate a service (navigation, diagnostics, insurance telematics) cannot be repurposed for unrelated revenue streams (data broker sales, insurance risk scoring) without obtaining fresh, affirmative, express consumer consent.
- **Review data retention schedules against purpose.** GM's liability included retaining driving data longer than was reasonably necessary. Businesses should set and enforce automated retention limits tied to the specific disclosed purpose.
- **Assess data broker and vendor contracts.** If your business sells or shares personal information to any consumer reporting agency or data broker, immediately evaluate whether those flows comply with CCPA's data minimization, purpose limitation, and disclosure requirements.
- **Audit consent mechanisms for adequacy.** The GM case turns in part on lack of adequate consumer consent for the insurance-scoring use. "Buried" disclosures in privacy policies are unlikely to satisfy CCPA's affirmative consent standard for secondary uses.
- **Request third-party deletion proactively.** The settlement requires GM to claw back data already sold to LexisNexis and Verisk. Businesses should consider whether contracts with data recipients include deletion rights exercisable on regulatory demand.
- **Monitor California insurance sector separately.** California Insurance Code § 1861.02 already restricts telematics use in rate-setting. The GM settlement may prompt additional regulatory scrutiny of insurance-adjacent data flows across sectors.
