# Florida AG and Roku Resolve First Digital Bill of Rights Enforcement Action Over Children's Data

**Jurisdiction:** Florida | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** June 29, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier announced on June 26, 2026 that his office resolved its enforcement action against Roku, Inc. under the Florida Digital Bill of Rights (FDBR), with Roku agreeing to commit approximately $25 million to enhance parental controls and children's-data safeguards rather than pay a civil fine [Florida AG press release](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/newsrelease/florida-attorney-general-james-uthmeier-and-roku-announce-resolution-digital-privacy-0). <!-- verified --> The resolution closes what was the first major enforcement action brought under the FDBR's children's-data provisions, filed against Roku in October 2025 [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/roku-alleged-to-improperly-process-childrens-data), and includes no finding of wrongdoing [Roku newsroom statement](https://newsroom.roku.com/news/2026/06/florida-attorney-general-james-uthmeier-and-roku-announce-resolution-of/eqab3fij-1782433974). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- Florida filed its enforcement action against Roku on October 13, 2025 in state court in Collier County, alleging violations of the FDBR's children's-data provisions codified at [Fla. Stat. § 501.1735](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/0501.1735) — the statute's "Protection of Children in Online Spaces" section, which prohibits processing a minor's personal data in a way that a platform knows, or willfully disregards, may cause substantial harm, and bars profiling children without safeguards or use of dark patterns to extract broader consent [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/roku-alleged-to-improperly-process-childrens-data), [Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2025/10/14/florida-attorney-general-alleges-roku-sold-kids-information/). <!-- verified -->
- The complaint alleged Roku collected children's viewing habits, voice recordings, and precise geolocation without parental notice or consent, and sold or shared this data with data brokers including Kochava, which allegedly used it to reidentify individuals and build profiles linking household Roku accounts to specific mobile devices [Florida Phoenix](https://floridaphoenix.com/2025/10/14/florida-attorney-general-alleges-roku-sold-kids-information/), [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/10/roku-accused-of-selling-childrens-data-to-advertisers-and-brokers). <!-- verified -->
- The FDBR authorizes civil penalties of up to $50,000 per violation, tripled to $150,000 per violation where the platform has actual knowledge the affected consumer is a minor [Fla. Stat. § 501.1735](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/0501.1735). <!-- verified -->
- Under the June 26, 2026 resolution, Roku agreed to invest an estimated $25 million in engineering resources to give parents greater control over their children's streaming experience, with implementation beginning immediately and full nationwide deployment expected within 12 months; the agreement includes no civil fine and no finding of wrongdoing [Florida AG press release](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/newsrelease/florida-attorney-general-james-uthmeier-and-roku-announce-resolution-digital-privacy-0), [Roku newsroom statement](https://newsroom.roku.com/news/2026/06/florida-attorney-general-james-uthmeier-and-roku-announce-resolution-of/eqab3fij-1782433974). <!-- verified -->
- Bloomberg Law and other outlets note the settlement structure — an operational/product-investment commitment rather than a monetary penalty — may serve as a template for future cooperative resolutions of FDBR children's-data claims, and observe that many of the required changes (parental controls, consent flows, restrictions on data-broker sharing, opt-out accuracy) are not strictly limited to minors' data [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/roku-strikes-deal-to-end-floridas-privacy-suit-on-minors-data), [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/07/florida-ag-and-roku-resolve-childrens-data-privacy-enforcement-action/). <!-- verified -->
- This is the first enforcement resolution under the FDBR, which took effect July 1, 2024 [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2023/06/florida-digital-bill-of-rights-data-privacy), and IAPP frames it as a signal that Florida intends to actively enforce its comprehensive privacy statute going forward [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/florida-enters-the-privacy-chat-why-roku-should-be-a-wake-up-call). <!-- verified -->

## Other State AG Activity Against Roku [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel filed a separate suit against Roku on April 29, 2025, but it is a distinct and still-unresolved matter, not a parallel "precedent" to Florida's completed settlement. The Michigan action proceeds in federal court on federal COPPA claims (and originally state consumer-protection theories), not under FDBR-style state comprehensive-privacy provisions [Michigan AG complaint](https://www.michigan.gov/ag/-/media/Project/Websites/AG/releases/2025/April/Roku-Complaint.pdf), [Natural Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/michigan-ag-sues-roku-over-alleged-privacy-violations). On March 31, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan dismissed five of the seven claims in the Michigan case (for lack of parens patriae standing to bring non-COPPA claims in federal court), leaving only the COPPA claims to proceed; the court has since set a 20-day jury trial for November 2027 [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/04/michigan-federal-court-narrows-roku-privacy-suit-to-coppa-claims/), [MLex: Roku must face Michigan's COPPA claims, judge rules](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2460739/roku-must-face-michigan-s-coppa-claims-judge-rules), [MLex: Michigan gets November 2027 trial date in litigation against Roku](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2476040/michigan-gets-november-2027-trial-date-in-litigation-against-roku). As of this writing, the Michigan matter remains active, unresolved litigation headed toward trial rather than a concluded enforcement action.

## Action Items

- Connected-TV, streaming, and other platforms that may process Florida minors' data should audit age-verification, parental-consent, and data-broker-sharing practices against FDBR § 501.1735, given this is now a tested enforcement priority for the Florida AG's office.
- Companies working with data brokers such as Kochava for cross-device identity resolution or "reidentification" of de-identified data should review those arrangements for FDBR compliance, since re-identification of de-identified data was a central theory in the complaint.
- Monitor Roku's 12-month nationwide parental-controls rollout as a potential compliance benchmark/reference implementation for children's-data safeguards under state comprehensive privacy laws.
- Track whether other state AGs bring parallel actions against connected-TV or streaming platforms. Michigan's AG has a related but distinct COPPA-based suit against Roku pending trial in November 2027, which remains unresolved and is not a direct FDBR analog; it should be monitored as a bellwether for federal children's-privacy litigation risk against streaming platforms rather than treated as an established state-law "precedent" alongside Florida's resolved action (the non-COPPA claims were dismissed March 31, 2026 for lack of standing, leaving only COPPA claims pending trial) [MLex: Michigan gets November 2027 trial date](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2476040/michigan-gets-november-2027-trial-date-in-litigation-against-roku), [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2026/04/michigan-federal-court-narrows-roku-privacy-suit-to-coppa-claims/).
