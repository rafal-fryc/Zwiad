# Arkansas AG Sues Roblox and Discord Over Child Safety Under State Deceptive Trade Practices Act

**Jurisdiction:** Arkansas | **Category:** Privacy — Children's Online Safety | **Date:** June 11, 2026 (filed); June 22, 2026 (publicly announced)

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin filed a civil complaint on June 11, 2026 against Roblox Corporation and Discord Inc. in the Superior Court of Los Angeles County, California, alleging the two platforms function as a "two-stage predatory pipeline" that exposes children to sexual predators, per the [official Arkansas AG press release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-roblox-and-discord-for-knowingly-profiting-from-putting-children-at-risk/) and the [publicly filed complaint](https://media.ark.org/ag/2026.06.11-Complaint.pdf). The suit alleges violations of the [Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-4/subtitle-7/chapter-88/subchapter-1/section-4-88-107/), Arkansas public nuisance law, and common-law unjust enrichment, claiming predators first contact children on Roblox before moving conversations to Discord to conduct grooming, sextortion, and in-person meeting arrangements. Arkansas joins a rapidly expanding wave of state AG enforcement against Roblox and Discord that already includes settlements in Alabama, Nevada, and West Virginia and active litigation in Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Nebraska, Tennessee, Texas, Iowa, and Florida.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Filing date and venue:** The complaint was filed June 11, 2026 in the Superior Court of Los Angeles County, California — not an Arkansas state court — per the [publicly filed complaint document](https://media.ark.org/ag/2026.06.11-Complaint.pdf) and confirmed by multiple outlets including [the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette](https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2026/jun/22/arkansas-attorney-generals-lawsuit-says-gaming/) and [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/arkansas-lawsuit-links-roblox-discord-as-two-stage-predatory-pipeline). The action was publicly announced June 22, 2026 via the [Arkansas AG's official press release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-roblox-and-discord-for-knowingly-profiting-from-putting-children-at-risk/).
- **Statutory basis:** The complaint invokes the [Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act, Ark. Code Ann. § 4-88-107](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-4/subtitle-7/chapter-88/subchapter-1/section-4-88-107/) (prohibiting deceptive and unconscionable trade practices), Arkansas public nuisance law, and common-law unjust enrichment, per [KARK](https://www.kark.com/news/local-news/arkansas-ag-sues-roblox-discord-over-child-safety/) and the [Arkansas AG press release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-roblox-and-discord-for-knowingly-profiting-from-putting-children-at-risk/).
- **Civil penalty exposure:** Under [Ark. Code Ann. § 4-88-113](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-4/subtitle-7/chapter-88/subchapter-1/section-4-88-113/), the Attorney General may seek civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, plus recovery of investigation and litigation expenses and attorney's fees; the statute also permits joint and several liability for controlling persons, officers, and directors who knew or should have known of a violation.
- **"Two-stage predatory pipeline" theory:** The complaint alleges Roblox and Discord function together as a pipeline in which predators first make contact with children on Roblox and then move conversations to Discord, where grooming, sextortion, and in-person meeting arrangements allegedly occur, per [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/arkansas-lawsuit-links-roblox-discord-as-two-stage-predatory-pipeline) and [the Arkansas AG press release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-roblox-and-discord-for-knowingly-profiting-from-putting-children-at-risk/).
- **Roblox-specific allegations:** The complaint alleges Roblox failed to implement age verification measures it already possessed, rejected internal proposals to warn children about grooming behavior, and allowed sexually explicit content to proliferate. It cites Roblox's own mandatory reports to the [National Center for Missing and Exploited Children](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/06/22/arkansas-sues-roblox-and-discord-accusing-platforms-of-putting-children-at-risk/), which documented approximately 675 instances of suspected child sexual exploitation in 2019, rising to more than 13,000 by 2023 — a nearly twentyfold increase, per the [Arkansas AG press release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-roblox-and-discord-for-knowingly-profiting-from-putting-children-at-risk/) and corroborated by [KAIT8](https://www.kait8.com/2026/06/22/arkansas-attorney-general-sues-roblox-discord-over-child-safety-concerns/).
- **Discord-specific allegations:** The complaint alleges Discord "deliberately engineered" its product to expose children to risk, and that its "Family Center" parental-monitoring feature places minors — not parents — in control of whether monitoring occurs at all. The complaint cites Discord's own transparency reporting showing child-safety violations rose approximately 150% in a single quarter, per [Arkansas Advocate](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/06/22/arkansas-sues-roblox-and-discord-accusing-platforms-of-putting-children-at-risk/) and [KARK](https://www.kark.com/news/local-news/arkansas-ag-sues-roblox-discord-over-child-safety/).
- **Relief sought:** The state seeks injunctive relief, monetary damages, and disgorgement of profits the state alleges were wrongfully obtained from Arkansas consumers, per the [official press release](https://arkansasag.gov/news-release/attorney-general-griffin-sues-roblox-and-discord-for-knowingly-profiting-from-putting-children-at-risk/).
- **Platform scale:** Roblox reports approximately 144 million daily active users, with roughly 40% under age 13; Discord reports over 200 million monthly active users with an average user age of 16, per [the complaint as reported by Arkansas Advocate](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/06/22/arkansas-sues-roblox-and-discord-accusing-platforms-of-putting-children-at-risk/).
- **Company responses:** Roblox stated it "strongly dispute[s] the claims" and said the suit "fundamentally misrepresents how our platform works," pointing to its recent rollout of mandatory age checks for chat access. Discord said the lawsuit "does not reflect the platform we have built," citing its safety systems and investment in user safety, per statements collected by [THV11](https://www.thv11.com/article/news/local/arkansas-attorney-general-lawsuit-roblox-discord/91-131d735e-b43a-4bbb-9f51-87cc4884a091).

## Multistate Context [HIGH confidence]

Arkansas's lawsuit is the latest entrant in a coordinated, rapidly expanding wave of state AG enforcement against Roblox and, increasingly, Discord as a co-defendant:

- **Settlements:** Alabama ($12.2 million), Nevada ($12 million, per the official figure — see the [Alabama AG Roblox settlement report](reports/privacy/enforcement-actions/alabama-ag-roblox-children-settlement-2026-04-21.md) for sourcing on the discrepancy with a $12.5 million component tally reported elsewhere), and West Virginia ($11.1 million) reached Assurances of Voluntary Compliance in April 2026, totaling approximately $35 million.
- **Active litigation naming both Roblox and Discord:** Indiana filed a 70-page complaint on May 6, 2026 under its Deceptive Consumer Sales Act (see the [Indiana AG Roblox/Discord report](reports/privacy/enforcement-actions/indiana-roblox-discord-child-safety-dcsa-2026-05-07.md)), applying a similar "two-platform" theory to the one now advanced by Arkansas.
- **Roblox-only active litigation:** Kentucky (filed October 2025), Louisiana (filed August 2025), Nebraska (filed March 2026), and, per earlier reporting, Tennessee, Texas, Iowa, and Florida have pending suits or investigations against Roblox.
- Arkansas's choice to file in the Superior Court of Los Angeles County — rather than an Arkansas state court — is notable and, per available reporting, is not fully explained; it may reflect forum considerations related to Roblox's and Discord's California headquarters, but the complaint and press materials reviewed do not state an explicit rationale. This procedural detail should be verified against the complaint's jurisdictional allegations by counsel evaluating venue strategy in comparable multistate actions.

## Legal Basis and Significance [MEDIUM confidence]

Arkansas's use of its general consumer-protection Deceptive Trade Practices Act — rather than a children's-privacy-specific statute — mirrors the legal theory used in the Alabama, Nevada, and West Virginia settlements and the Indiana litigation: that design choices permitting unverified adult-to-child contact, combined with public safety marketing claims, constitute deceptive and unconscionable trade practices. The $10,000-per-violation penalty structure under [Ark. Code Ann. § 4-88-113](https://law.justia.com/codes/arkansas/title-4/subtitle-7/chapter-88/subchapter-1/section-4-88-113/) means that, given the scale of alleged exploitation reports (13,000+ incidents cited for Roblox alone in 2023), aggregate penalty exposure could be substantial if per-incident violations are found, though the complaint's specific per-violation theory has not been independently verified from the full complaint text beyond secondary reporting. The addition of Discord as a co-defendant — following Indiana's precedent — signals that state AGs are increasingly treating cross-platform "pipeline" dynamics (contact on one platform, grooming continuation on another) as a unified theory of liability rather than pursuing each platform separately.

This section is tagged MEDIUM confidence because, while the official press release and the publicly posted complaint PDF were located and cited, the full complaint text was not independently parsed line-by-line for the specific counts and prayer for relief beyond what secondary reporting (Arkansas Advocate, Biometric Update, KARK, KAIT8) and the AG's own press release corroborate; readers requiring counts, paragraph-level citations, or exact statutory subsections should consult the [complaint PDF](https://media.ark.org/ag/2026.06.11-Complaint.pdf) directly.

## Action Items

- **Platform operators serving minors** should treat the Arkansas complaint as further confirmation that state AGs are standardizing on a "cross-platform pipeline" theory (Roblox-to-Discord contact continuation) as a basis for joint liability; operators offering both a gaming/social discovery surface and a separate messaging surface should assess whether their own architecture creates similar cross-platform contact-continuation risk.
- **Age verification and parental control design** should be benchmarked against the age-checks-for-chat-access model Roblox has begun rolling out and cited in its own defense; operators should document proactive safety measures contemporaneously, since both companies invoked recent safety investments in their public responses to the suit.
- **Monitor for amended or consolidated proceedings:** Given that Indiana, and now Arkansas, have sued both Roblox and Discord jointly, companies should watch for coordinated multistate litigation strategies (e.g., potential future MDL consolidation of state AG claims, distinct from the existing 146-case private-plaintiff MDL) and monitor the docket in the Superior Court of Los Angeles County for scheduling and any motions to dismiss or transfer venue.
- **Track the Arkansas DTPA per-violation penalty theory:** Given the $10,000-per-violation exposure under Ark. Code Ann. § 4-88-113 and the scale of alleged incidents, companies should monitor how Arkansas frames its per-violation damages theory as the case develops, since this could set persuasive precedent for other states' per-violation penalty calculations.
