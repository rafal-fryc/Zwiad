# FTC Chairman Ferguson Signals Surge in Privacy Enforcement Actions for Late 2026

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-28

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson stated in a June 18, 2026 interview with [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2491539/ferguson-says-ftc-poised-for-jump-in-us-privacy-enforcement-in-late-2026) that the agency is preparing a significant increase in data privacy enforcement cases for the second half of 2026, remarking that reporters covering the FTC "will have a hard time keeping up with the number of cases we're gonna be bringing." The announcement comes as the FTC operates with an unusually lean two-commissioner structure and builds on a 2026 enforcement pace that has already included high-profile settlements against OkCupid/Match Group, Kochava, and Cox Media Group. Organizations with gaps in children's privacy compliance, health-related data practices, or inadequate cybersecurity programs face materially elevated enforcement risk through year-end.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Chairman Ferguson made the surge announcement in a [June 18, 2026 interview with MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2491539/ferguson-says-ftc-poised-for-jump-in-us-privacy-enforcement-in-late-2026), directly stating the FTC would be bringing a high volume of privacy cases in the second half of the year.
- The FTC currently operates with only [two commissioners](https://www.axios.com/2025/11/17/ftc-two-commissioners) — Chairman Ferguson and Commissioner Mark Meador — after President Trump removed two Democratic commissioners in March 2025 and Commissioner Holyoak [subsequently departed in November 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/11/ftc-commissioner-melissa-holyoak-appointed-interim-us-attorney) to serve as Interim U.S. Attorney for the District of Utah. The resulting 2-0 decision structure streamlines case authorization.
- Ferguson identified four enforcement focus areas: children's privacy (COPPA), cybersecurity failures, deceptive data practices, and health-related claims, consistent with the agency's [FY 2026-2030 Strategic Plan](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/fy-2026-2030-ftc-strategic-plan.pdf) published April 3, 2026.
- The FTC has brought [more than 90 cybersecurity enforcement actions since 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/protecting-consumer-privacy-security/privacy-security-enforcement), demonstrating substantial baseline enforcement capacity in this area.
- Recent 2026 enforcement actions preceding Ferguson's announcement include: the [OkCupid/Match Group settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-takes-action-against-match-okcupid-deceiving-users-sharing-personal-data-third-party) (March 30, deceptive data sharing with AI company); the [Kochava settlement](https://compliancehub.wiki/ftc-kochava-location-data-ban-2026/) (May 4, prohibition on selling sensitive location data without express consent); and the [Cox Media Group combined settlement of $930,000](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-require-cox-media-group-two-other-firms-pay-nearly-1-million-settle-charges-they-deceived) (May 21, deceptive "active listening" AI advertising claims — Cox's own share is $880,000, with two co-respondents paying $25,000 each).
- **COPPA compliance deadline:** The amended COPPA Rule — requiring new parental consent flows, written information security programs, and stricter data retention limits — became fully enforceable on [April 22, 2026](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect). Any operator of a website or service directed at children who has not yet updated its practices is now subject to immediate enforcement.
- Ferguson has publicly stated that protecting children online is ["one of the most important consumer protection issues of our time"](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/overview-ftcs-2026-childrens-privacy-focus-expect-ongoing-if-not-more-attention) and the FTC issued a COPPA [age verification policy statement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children) on February 25, 2026 promoting age-gating as a compliance mechanism.
- Analysis by [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/publication/future-privacy-enforcement-under-ferguson-ftc/) notes that novel Section 5 legal theories are less prominent under Ferguson, who relies instead on established statutory authorities — COPPA, the Health Breach Notification Rule, and Section 5 deception/unfairness standards — making enforcement outcomes more predictable but the targets broader.
- The [FTC's 2026-2030 Strategic Plan](https://natlawreview.com/article/whats-next-ftc-look-2026-2030-strategic-plan) formally lists children's data, Big Tech, data broker practices, and deceptive advertising as cross-cutting priorities through FY 2030.

## Action Items

- **Children's privacy (immediate):** Audit COPPA compliance against the April 2025 amended rule. The April 22, 2026 compliance deadline has passed — any gap is a live enforcement risk. Review parental consent flows, update written information security programs, and verify data retention schedules.
- **Health-related data claims:** Review all advertising and marketing claims touching health outcomes, wellness, or fitness. Evaluate applicability of the Health Breach Notification Rule to direct-to-consumer health apps and wearables not covered by HIPAA.
- **Privacy policy accuracy:** The OkCupid settlement (20-year consent order) turned on a gap between the published privacy policy and actual data sharing. Audit third-party data flows and confirm that privacy policy representations accurately describe current practices — particularly regarding AI vendor access to user data.
- **Location and sensitive data practices:** Following Kochava, any sale or transfer of precise location data requires affirmative express consumer consent. Data brokers and app publishers should audit downstream sharing of location, health, and biometric identifiers.
- **Cybersecurity posture:** With 90+ cybersecurity actions already on record and a promised acceleration, organizations should evaluate their data security programs against FTC Safeguards Rule requirements and prior FTC consent order terms for comparable industries. Incident response plans and vendor security assessments warrant review.
- **Monitor FTC docket closely:** Ferguson's public signaling of volume means the late-2026 enforcement wave is likely to span multiple industries simultaneously. Compliance teams should subscribe to FTC press release alerts and review new case announcements for pattern signals.
