# CPPA Launches Data Broker Enforcement Era: Growbots Settlement and the Delete Act Compliance Imperative

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Development Type:** Enforcement | **Date:** March 13, 2025

---

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) is conducting an aggressive, sustained enforcement campaign against unregistered data brokers under California's Delete Act ([SB 362](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB362)). Beginning with the [first settlements against Growbots, Inc. and UpLead LLC in November 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241114.html), the Agency has brought at least six enforcement actions imposing daily fines of $200 per day of non-registration. The March 2025 CPPA Board meeting advanced formal rulemaking for the DELETE Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP), which will create a new wave of compliance obligations beginning August 1, 2026. Any company that collects and sells California consumer personal information without a direct consumer relationship must register with the CPPA or face material enforcement risk.

---

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The California Delete Act (SB 362)

California enacted [Senate Bill 362](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB362), the Delete Act, in 2023 (Chapter 709, Statutes of 2023). The law fundamentally restructured how California regulates the data broker industry in two respects.

First, it transferred administration and enforcement of the Data Broker Registry from the California Department of Justice to the newly empowered CPPA, effective January 1, 2024. Second, it required the CPPA to build the [Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/drop.html) — a centralized mechanism allowing consumers to submit a single deletion request that propagates to every registered data broker simultaneously.

**Registration obligations.** Under the Delete Act, any business that knowingly collects and sells to third parties the personal information of consumers with whom it has no direct relationship must register annually with the CPPA by January 31, pay an annual fee of $6,600, and disclose specified information about its data practices. Failure to register by the deadline triggers an automatic penalty of **$200 per day** of continued non-compliance, enforceable through CPPA administrative action.

**"Direct relationship" defined narrowly.** The CPPA's proposed DROP regulations, advanced in March 2025, clarify that a business does not have a "direct relationship" simply because it collects information directly from a consumer. The consumer must intentionally interact with the business for the purpose of obtaining its products or services. CPPA staff noted that passive collection through cookies or geolocation capture during an unrelated transaction does not constitute a direct relationship — a position that significantly expands the universe of companies subject to data broker registration.

### CPPA Enforcement Authority

The CPPA Enforcement Division has independent authority to investigate and settle enforcement actions. Settlements require Board approval, typically obtained in closed session. The Enforcement Division is led by Michael Macko, who has publicly signaled that the Agency intends to pursue unregistered data brokers systematically rather than case by case.

## What Happened [HIGH confidence]

### Wave 1: Growbots and UpLead Settlements (November 2024)

On [November 14, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241114.html), the CPPA announced its first-ever enforcement settlements under the Delete Act against two California-based data brokers:

- **Growbots, Inc.** — Fined **$35,400** for 177 days of non-registration (February 1 – July 26, 2024). Growbots is a sales automation and lead generation platform that sells contact data on business professionals.
- **UpLead LLC** — Fined **$34,400** for 172 days of non-registration (February 1 – July 21, 2024).

Both companies agreed to injunctive terms requiring them to maintain future compliance and to pay the Enforcement Division's attorney fees and costs resulting from any future non-compliance. The Board voted unanimously to approve both settlements during a closed session of its November 8, 2024 meeting.

The penalty calculation is transparent: $200/day × days of non-registration = total fine. Both companies registered mid-year 2024 after the Enforcement Division made contact during its investigative sweep.

### Wave 2: Infillion and The Data Group (December 2024)

On [December 23, 2024](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2024/20241223.html), the CPPA announced two additional settlements:

- **PayDae, Inc. (d/b/a Infillion)**, a New York-based data broker — fined **$54,200** for non-registration from February 1 – November 4, 2024.
- **The Data Group, LLC** — also settled for failure to register.

### Wave 3: KMA Settlement (January 2025)

On [January 29, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250129.html), the CPPA settled with a fifth data broker:

- **Key Marketing Advantage, LLC (KMA)**, a Connecticut-based data broker — fined **$55,800** for non-registration from February 1 – November 5, 2024.

Michael Macko, CPPA's head of enforcement, characterized this as the fifth action taken against unregistered data brokers in a few months, calling it part of a continuing investigative sweep announced October 30, 2024.

### National Public Data Enforcement (February 2025)

On [February 20, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250220.html), the CPPA filed an enforcement action against **Jerico Pictures, Inc. (d/b/a National Public Data)**, a Florida-based data broker that suffered a major data breach in 2024. The Agency sought a **$46,000 fine** for 230 days of non-registration. National Public Data eventually registered on September 18, 2024 — only after the Enforcement Division had already contacted the company.

### Background Alert Settlement (February 2025)

On [February 27, 2025](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250227.html), the CPPA reached a notable settlement with **Background Alert, Inc.**, a California-based data broker. Background Alert agreed to **cease operations through 2028** or face a $50,000 fine — a significant escalation beyond registration penalties, showing the CPPA's willingness to pursue operational shutdowns where data broker practices pose heightened consumer harm.

### March 2025 Board Meeting: DROP Regulations Advanced

At its [March 6-7, 2025 board meeting](https://cppa.ca.gov/meetings/materials/20250306_07.html), the CPPA Board voted unanimously to authorize staff to advance proposed DROP regulations to formal rulemaking. Key requirements under the proposed DROP framework:

- Data brokers must **access DROP at least every 45 days** beginning August 1, 2026, to retrieve and process consumer deletion requests.
- Deletion requests must be processed within the 45-day cycle unless a CCPA deletion exception applies.
- Starting with 2026 registration, data brokers must first create a business account in DROP before completing registration.
- The platform was made available to consumers on January 1, 2026.

## Legal Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Penalty Structure Is Self-Enforcing

The $200/day penalty structure is mechanically severe. A company that misses the January 31 registration deadline and operates through the rest of the calendar year faces up to $73,000 in fines from registration failures alone — before any CCPA or substantive privacy violations are counted. The Growbots and UpLead settlements confirm the CPPA applies the full statutory rate without apparent mitigation for first-time offenders.

### Scope of "Data Broker" Is Broader Than Many Companies Assume

The Delete Act defines a data broker as a business that "knowingly collects and sells to third parties the personal information of consumers with whom it does not have a direct relationship." The CPPA's March 2025 DROP regulations propose a narrow definition of "direct relationship" that would capture many B2B SaaS platforms, advertising technology companies, and analytics providers that aggregate and resell data derived from passive consumer interactions.

Enforcement targets to date — Growbots (sales automation), UpLead (B2B leads), National Public Data (public records aggregator), Background Alert (background check) — illustrate the breadth of affected industries.

### The Investigative Sweep Model

Rather than responding reactively to complaints, the CPPA's Enforcement Division proactively cross-referenced its public Data Broker Registry against companies it identified as likely data brokers through its own investigation. Companies that had not registered received contact from the Enforcement Division, then faced formal enforcement action if they did not promptly comply. This model differs from most US state AG enforcement, which typically requires a triggering complaint.

### DROP Creates a New Tier of Obligations

The DROP framework will layer substantive processing obligations on top of registration requirements. Data brokers that are registered and paying fees but fail to correctly implement DROP-based deletion workflows beginning August 1, 2026 will face a new category of enforcement exposure. The CPPA has signaled this is an enforcement priority.

## What This Means for Businesses

### Immediate Actions Required

1. **Determine whether your company is a "data broker" under SB 362.** If your business collects personal information about California consumers and sells, licenses, or otherwise provides that data to third parties — and those consumers did not interact directly with your company to obtain your products or services — you likely meet the definition. The CPPA's narrow interpretation of "direct relationship" is the operative standard.

2. **Register by January 31 each year.** The CPPA Data Broker Registry is available at [cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers/). Failure to register by the deadline begins the $200/day clock automatically.

3. **Prepare for DROP compliance by August 1, 2026.** Registered data brokers must build workflows to access the DROP platform at least every 45 days, match consumer deletion requests against their records, and process confirmed matches within the cycle. Begin technical assessment of your data infrastructure against DROP requirements now.

4. **Review trade names and disclosure completeness.** The CPPA's enforcement priorities for its Data Broker Strike Force (formed November 2025) include brokers that use trade names not disclosed in their registration. Ensure your registration covers all operating names.

5. **Assess CCPA compliance in parallel.** The CPPA has signaled it will review data broker compliance with substantive CCPA obligations — not just the registration requirement. Data brokers handling sensitive categories (minors' data, precise geolocation, reproductive health) face heightened scrutiny.

### Penalty Mitigation

Based on the settlement pattern to date, the CPPA does not appear to offer substantial penalty reduction for first-time offenders who register after contact from the Enforcement Division. The Growbots fine of $35,400 represents the full statutory rate ($200 × 177 days) with no apparent mitigation. Companies contacted during an investigative sweep should consult counsel promptly — but registration after contact has not, to date, eliminated enforcement exposure for the prior non-registration period.
