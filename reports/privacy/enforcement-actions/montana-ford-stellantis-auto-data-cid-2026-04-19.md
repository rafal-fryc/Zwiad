# Montana AG Launches Civil Investigative Demands Against Ford and Stellantis Over Connected-Car Data Sharing

**Jurisdiction:** Montana | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-15

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Montana Attorney General Austin Knudsen issued [Civil Investigative Demands (CIDs)](https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-launches-investigation-into-major-auto-companies/) on April 8, 2026 to Ford Motor Company and Stellantis N.V., alleging that both automakers secretly collected sensitive driving and telematics data from Montana consumers and sold it to third-party data brokers — including LexisNexis Risk Solutions and Verisk Analytics — who then licensed the data to insurance carriers to adjust premiums and deny coverage, all without consumers' knowledge or consent. AG Knudsen stated that if the allegations are verified, "this deceitful practice raises serious consumer protection concerns and is also a violation of state law." The investigation invokes Montana's Consumer Protection Act (MCA §30-14-101 et seq.) and the Montana Consumer Data Privacy Act (MCA §30-14-2801 et seq.), which was recently amended in 2025 to remove the cure period and add up to $7,500 per-violation civil penalties.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- AG Knudsen issued CIDs to Ford Motor Company and Stellantis N.V. on April 8, 2026, demanding that both companies produce documents related to the collection, sale, and sharing of driving data, connected vehicle data, telematics data, and personal information ([Montana DOJ press release](https://dojmt.gov/attorney-general-knudsen-launches-investigation-into-major-auto-companies/)).
- The CIDs specifically name LexisNexis Risk Solutions and Verisk Analytics as the third-party recipients of interest — the same data brokers implicated in General Motors' data-sharing scandal exposed in 2023–2024 ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/04/14/montana-investigates-ford-stellantis-for-selling-driver-data/)).
- Ford's deadline to respond to the CID is May 8, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. MDT — a one-month response window ([Montana Right Now coverage](https://www.montanarightnow.com/montana/montana-ag-investigates-auto-companies-over-selling-customer-data-claims/article_7ea4b88e-82d5-4c90-bd8b-cdcf215bc9d4.html)).
- The CIDs demand disclosure of "all products, services, or platforms that collect driving data or personal information" and the scope of data-sharing arrangements, including third-party recipients and licensing terms ([ppc.land report](https://ppc.land/montana-subpoenas-ford-and-stellantis-over-secret-vehicle-driving-data-deals/)).
- The legal basis includes Montana's Consumer Protection Act (MCA §30-14-101 et seq.) and the Montana Consumer Data Privacy Act (MCDPA), codified at MCA §30-14-2801 et seq. The CID authority flows from MCA §30-14-113 (investigative demand provision) and MCA §30-14-2817 (MCDPA enforcement) ([Montana MCA §30-14-2817](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0300/chapter_0140/part_0280/section_0170/0300-0140-0280-0170.html)).
- Montana's MCDPA was amended in 2025 by SB 297 (effective October 1, 2025), which removed the 60-day cure period for violations and introduced civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation, plus reasonable attorney fees and costs ([Perkins Coie analysis](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/montanas-consumer-data-privacy-law-update-sb-297s-sweeping-changes-glance)).
- "Telematics Data" is defined in the CIDs as connected vehicle data used to "infer or score driving behavior," bringing in scope raw sensor outputs that underpin usage-based insurance (UBI) programs — data that Verisk has recently stopped accepting from automakers, while LexisNexis continues to promote its driver behavior data products ([ppc.land report](https://ppc.land/montana-subpoenas-ford-and-stellantis-over-secret-vehicle-driving-data-deals/)).
- Ford estimates automotive data collection could generate up to $100 per vehicle per year in additional revenue — illustrating the commercial incentives driving this practice ([KBB report](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/your-car-may-snitch-on-your-driving-raise-your-insurance-costs/)).

## Context: A Wave of State and Federal Enforcement [HIGH confidence]

Montana's investigation follows a multi-year pattern of state AG and federal enforcement against automakers' connected-vehicle data practices:

- **Texas v. General Motors (August 2024):** Texas AG Ken Paxton sued GM and its OnStar subsidiary, alleging it collected and sold driving data from more than 14 million vehicles — including 1.8 million Texas drivers — to LexisNexis and Verisk without consent. The data was transmitted as frequently as every three seconds ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/gm-selling-driver-data-car-insurers-texas-lawsuit/)). GM had previously entered "exploratory partnerships" with both brokers and publicly stated it had stopped sharing data in April 2023.
- **Texas multi-automaker probes (2024–2025):** Beyond GM, Texas expanded investigations to include Ford, Hyundai, Toyota, Fiat Chrysler, Kia, Subaru, and Mitsubishi — making it the most active state on connected-car data enforcement ([Texas AG announcement](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-opens-investigation-car-manufacturers-collection-and-sale-drivers-data); [The Record](https://therecord.media/texas-probes-four-more-car-companies-data-collection-sharing)).
- **FTC v. GM/OnStar (finalized January 2026):** The Federal Trade Commission finalized a 20-year consent order against GM, banning the automaker from sharing driver geolocation and behavior data with consumer reporting agencies for five years — the first major federal action of this kind ([Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/news/ftc-finalizes-settlement-with-gm-over-unauthorized-driver-data-sales-as-texas-ag-targets-connected-car-privacy-violations/)).
- **California enforcement:** California fined Honda $632,500 for inadequate consumer data rights processes, and the California Privacy Protection Agency has an active Data Broker Enforcement Strike Force issuing penalties as recently as January 8, 2026 ([Nelson Mullins analysis](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/driving-forward-developments-in-transportation-law-and-innovation/all/privacy-regulation-of-auto-industry-to-accelerate-in-2026-part-1)).
- **Connecticut AG:** In 2025, Connecticut's AG issued dozens of violation notices and warning letters focused specifically on connected vehicles and driving habit location data ([Nelson Mullins analysis](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/driving-forward-developments-in-transportation-law-and-innovation/all/privacy-regulation-of-auto-industry-to-accelerate-in-2026-part-1)).

## Legal Framework [MEDIUM confidence]

The Montana AG's investigation rests on two overlapping statutory bases:

**Montana Consumer Protection Act (MCA §30-14-101 et seq.):** The AG's broad consumer protection authority authorizes civil investigative demands whenever the AG has reasonable cause to believe a person has engaged in deceptive or unfair trade practices. MCA §30-14-113 sets out the investigative demand procedure, including service requirements and a petition mechanism allowing targets to challenge demands within 20 days of service ([Justia — MCA §30-14-113](https://law.justia.com/codes/montana/2021/title-30/chapter-14/part-1/section-30-14-113/)).

**Montana Consumer Data Privacy Act (MCA §30-14-2801 et seq.):** Enacted in 2023 and substantially amended by SB 297 (effective October 1, 2025), the MCDPA grants consumers rights over their personal data — including the right to opt out of sales and to consent before processing of "sensitive data." Driving and telematics data inferring behavior likely constitutes sensitive data under the statute. The MCDPA:
- Grants the AG exclusive enforcement authority
- Authorizes CIDs pursuant to MCA §30-14-113
- Allows the AG to require controllers to submit data protection assessments relevant to investigations
- Imposes civil penalties of up to $7,500 per violation (added by SB 297)
- Eliminated the 60-day cure period as of October 1, 2025

Official MCDPA text is available at the [Montana Legislature MCA table of contents for Title 30, Chapter 14, Part 28](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0300/chapter_0140/part_0280/sections_index.html).

Note: The AG's press release does not specify whether the investigation proceeds exclusively under the MCDPA or also invokes the older Consumer Protection Act's unfair/deceptive acts standard. The CPA route would not require proving a MCDPA "sale of personal data" violation, giving the AG flexibility if strict MCDPA elements cannot be established.

## Why It Matters

**For automakers and OEM suppliers:** Ford and Stellantis face exposure under two overlapping Montana statutes. At $7,500 per violation, penalties can scale rapidly across a large number of affected consumers. The investigation also signals that the wave of auto data enforcement is no longer limited to Texas and federal regulators — smaller-state AGs are now actively pursuing connected-car cases using their general consumer protection authority in combination with state-specific data privacy laws.

**For data brokers (LexisNexis, Verisk):** Montana's CIDs name these intermediaries explicitly, reinforcing the trend of holding the entire data supply chain — not just the OEM — accountable. Verisk's decision to stop accepting automaker data shows industry self-correction in progress, but LexisNexis remains exposed.

**For insurers:** Insurance carriers using driver behavior scores derived from telematics data face growing regulatory scrutiny at both the state AG and insurance regulatory level. Montana's investigation will likely surface information about downstream insurer use of Montana driver data.

**For compliance and privacy counsel:** The elimination of Montana's cure period means a CID or enforcement notice no longer comes with a grace window. Companies receiving a Montana AG inquiry on data practices must respond promptly and substantively, as any enforcement action can be initiated immediately without a prior opportunity to remedy.

## Action Items

- **Ford and Stellantis (and their supply chains):** Engage Montana-licensed counsel immediately; the May 8, 2026 CID response deadline for Ford is firm. Identify all telematics data flows involving Montana consumer data and any agreements with LexisNexis Risk Solutions or Verisk Analytics. Assess whether any data sharing meets the MCDPA "sale of personal data" definition triggering opt-out rights.
- **Other automakers:** Evaluate whether data collection and sharing practices involving LexisNexis, Verisk, or comparable data brokers are legally defensible under Montana (and other state) law. The Montana investigation is likely to be followed by other state AGs using similar CID authority.
- **Insurance carriers:** Review whether driving behavior scores or telematics-derived risk assessments used in underwriting are sourced from data that was collected and shared in compliance with state privacy laws in all 50 states.
- **Privacy and compliance teams generally:** Update data mapping to reflect telematics data flows. Under MCDPA SB 297, the absence of a cure period means any gap identified by a regulator goes directly to enforcement.
- **Monitor:** Track whether Montana progresses to formal enforcement action after the CID response deadline. The Texas GM and FTC GM consent orders provide a template for what monetary and operational relief Montana might seek.
