# Oklahoma AG Sues Temu for Undisclosed Data Collection and China-Linked Data Sharing

**Jurisdiction:** Oklahoma | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** May 18, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Oklahoma Attorney General Gentner Drummond filed suit on May 6, 2026 in Cleveland County District Court against Temu's operating entities — Whaleco Inc. and parent PDD Holdings Inc. — alleging systematic violations of the [Oklahoma Consumer Protection Act (Okla. Stat. tit. 15, §§ 751–763)](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-15/section-15-751/). The complaint alleges Temu's mobile app functions as spyware: harvesting precise geolocation, microphone and camera access, biometric identifiers, and cross-app behavioral data without adequate consumer disclosure or consent, then routing that data to servers accessible by entities tied to the Chinese Communist Party. Oklahoma seeks injunctive relief, civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, and consumer restitution — making this part of a multi-state enforcement campaign against the platform spanning 2024–2026.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Drummond filed suit on **May 6, 2026** in Cleveland County District Court, naming [Whaleco Inc. (the Delaware-incorporated Temu operator) and PDD Holdings Inc.](https://oklahoma.gov/oag/news/newsroom/2026/may/drummond-files-lawsuit-against-temu-for-stealing-oklahomans-data-and-deceiving-consumers.html) (the Chinese parent) as defendants.
- The complaint alleges Temu's app **covertly accesses** users' precise physical location, microphone, camera, and activity on other installed applications, all without meaningful disclosure — conduct Drummond characterized as "functionally malware and spyware." ([Oklahoma AG press release](https://oklahoma.gov/oag/news/newsroom/2026/may/drummond-files-lawsuit-against-temu-for-stealing-oklahomans-data-and-deceiving-consumers.html))
- Data allegedly collected includes **biometric identifiers and precise geolocation** — categories also treated as sensitive under Oklahoma's newly enacted Consumer Data Privacy Act (SB 546, signed March 20, 2026, effective January 1, 2027). ([Oklahoma House press release on SB 546](https://www.okhouse.gov/posts/news-20260323_2))
- The complaint further alleges Temu harvested data was **transmitted to servers controlled by PDD Holdings in China**, raising national security concerns under U.S. data protection standards. ([State AG Report](https://www.stateagreport.com/news/oklahoma-says-temus-data-deals-are-no-bargain/))
- Additional allegations include: **selling counterfeit goods** bearing Oklahoma brands (OKC Thunder, Oklahoma State University, University of Oklahoma), deploying **bait-and-switch referral schemes** that promise prizes that never arrive, and failing to disclose the use of **forced labor** from Chinese ethnic minorities in its supply chain. ([KXII reporting](https://www.kxii.com/2026/05/11/drummond-accuses-temu-stealing-oklahomans-data-new-lawsuit/))
- The Oklahoma Consumer Protection Act authorizes civil penalties of **up to $10,000 per violation** in civil actions. ([Okla. Stat. tit. 15, § 761.1](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-15/section-15-761-1/))
- Temu denied the allegations and stated it "intends to defend itself vigorously." ([News9](https://www.news9.com/oklahoma-city-news/oklahoma-attorney-general-drummond-temu-lawsuit))
- Oklahoma's action joins similar suits filed by attorneys general in **Arkansas** (filed 2024; ongoing), **Nebraska** (June 2025), **Kentucky** (July 2025), **Arizona** (December 2025), and **Texas** (February 2026) — all alleging illegal data harvesting and CCP-linked data sharing by Temu/PDD Holdings. ([Captain Compliance analysis](https://captaincompliance.com/education/oklahoma-ag-sues-temu-unlawful-data-harvesting-spyware-allegations-and-ip-theft-in-latest-blow-to-chinese-e-commerce-giant/))

## Legal Framework [MEDIUM confidence]

The Oklahoma complaint relies on the **Oklahoma Consumer Protection Act (OCPA)**, Okla. Stat. tit. 15, §§ 751–763, which prohibits deceptive and unfair trade practices in the course of commerce. The AG is authorized under § 761.1 to seek injunctive relief, civil penalties up to $10,000 per violation, and restitution. The OCPA does not require a private right of action to be triggered; the AG brings suit in the state's name.

The complaint does not appear to invoke the **Oklahoma Consumer Data Privacy Act (SB 546)**, which was signed into law on March 20, 2026 but does not take effect until January 1, 2027. Instead, the AG relies on general consumer protection authority — the same approach used in parallel suits by Texas ([Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code §§ 17.41–17.63](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/images/press/Petition_12.pdf)) and Arkansas (Deceptive Trade Practices Act). This reflects a broader trend of state AGs using pre-existing consumer protection statutes to fill enforcement gaps before new data privacy laws become effective.

Oklahoma's OCPA does not define "biometric data" or "precise geolocation" as standalone protected categories, but the allegations frame the covert collection of such data as deceptive because it was not disclosed to consumers — bringing it within the OCPA's prohibition on materially misleading omissions and unfair practices.

## Action Items

- **App operators with broad data collection practices:** Audit your mobile app's data collection against state consumer protection law standards in every jurisdiction where users are located — not just states with formal privacy statutes. Oklahoma demonstrates that general consumer protection statutes can reach undisclosed data practices.
- **Retail platforms with China-based parent entities:** Review data flows to offshore parent companies and assess whether existing privacy notices accurately describe cross-border data transfers. Failure to disclose transfers to Chinese-controlled entities is now an explicit enforcement trigger in multiple states.
- **Companies relying on lengthy terms-of-service disclosures:** The multi-state Temu complaints suggest that burying data-sharing disclosures in app terms of service will not defeat an OCPA "deceptive omission" claim. Consider whether consumer-facing disclosures at onboarding are prominent and specific.
- **Monitor Oklahoma OCDPA compliance calendar:** Oklahoma's new Consumer Data Privacy Act (SB 546) takes effect January 1, 2027. Businesses processing data of Oklahoma residents should begin gap analysis now, particularly regarding biometric data, precise geolocation, and consent requirements for sensitive data — the same categories at issue in this enforcement action.
- **Watch for coordination:** With 6+ states suing Temu on similar theories across a two-year span (2024–2026), a coordinated multi-state settlement is plausible. Track docket developments in Cleveland County District Court and in parallel state proceedings.
