# FTC Sends Warning Letters to 12 Nudify Websites Over TAKE IT DOWN Act Noncompliance

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-05-20

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 20, 2026 — the day after the TAKE IT DOWN Act's platform compliance deadline — the Federal Trade Commission sent warning letters to 12 "nudify" website operators, AI tools that generate nonconsensual sexualized images from clothed photographs, notifying them that they appeared to be violating the Act by failing to offer any mechanism for victims to request removal of such imagery. The letters directed the companies to immediately come into compliance; failure to do so risks civil penalties of up to **$53,088 per violation** under Section 5 of the FTC Act. The FTC did not publicly name the 12 recipients but published a template of the letter it sent.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The FTC sent warning letters to 12 companies operating "nudify" AI services on May 20, 2026 — one day after the TAKE IT DOWN Act's notice-and-removal obligations took effect on May 19, 2026. The letters are distinct from Chairman Ferguson's simultaneous pre-deadline advisory letters to major mainstream platforms (Amazon, Alphabet, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, TikTok, X, and others). ([FTC Press Release: FTC Sends Warning Letters to Companies About Compliance with the TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-sends-warning-letters-companies-about-compliance-take-it-down-act))

- The 12 recipient companies were not publicly identified by the FTC. The FTC published only a [template of the nudify warning letter (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/tida-nudify-warning-letter-template_0.pdf), which states that the recipient's service "appears to be in violation of [the TAKE IT DOWN Act] because it does not provide individuals with a process through which they can request removal of nonconsensual intimate images." ([FTC Warning Letter Template](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/warning-letters/take-it-down-act-nudify-warning-letter-template))

- Nudify tools are software applications or websites that use AI to digitally remove clothing from photographs of real individuals without their consent, generating fabricated sexualized imagery. The TAKE IT DOWN Act's definition of "digital forgery" expressly covers images "digitally created or altered using software, an app, or artificial intelligence" to falsely depict an individual in an intimate visual depiction. ([Congress.gov, S.146 text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146/text))

- The letters order recipients to "immediately come into compliance" with Section 3 of the TAKE IT DOWN Act (47 U.S.C. § 223a), which requires covered platforms to establish a clearly accessible process for removal requests and to honor valid requests within **48 hours**, including removal of known identical copies. ([FTC Business Blog, May 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/05/take-it-down-act-enforcement-starts-now-what-know-about-ftc-tida))

- Noncompliance is treated as a violation of a rule defining an unfair or deceptive act or practice under Section 18(a)(1)(B) of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 57a(a)(1)(B)), and the FTC may seek civil penalties under Sections 5(l) and 5(m) — currently set at **$53,088 per violation**. ([FTC Press Release: FTC Sends Warning Letters](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-sends-warning-letters-companies-about-compliance-take-it-down-act); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/05/us-federal-trade-commission-warns-12-nudify-websites-to-comply-with-take-it-down-act/))

- Chairman Ferguson issued a broader statement on May 19 applying to all TAKE IT DOWN Act enforcement: "Today we're demonstrating just how serious we are about protecting the public, especially children, from abusive online conduct. Platforms no longer have any excuses — they must comply with their obligations under the TAKE IT DOWN Act or face the consequences." ([The Record / Recorded Future News](https://therecord.media/ftc-warns-12-firms-of-violating-take-it-down-act))

- The FTC simultaneously launched a consumer reporting portal at [TakeItDown.ftc.gov](https://takeitdown.ftc.gov) where victims can report platforms that fail to honor removal requests, providing an ongoing investigative intake mechanism. ([FTC Consumer Advice](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/05/what-will-ftcs-enforcement-take-it-down-act-mean-you))

- The Ogletree Deakins analysis notes the FTC's parallel enforcement activity includes monitoring guidance, consumer complaint intake, and letters to nudify platforms as part of a coordinated enforcement launch rather than isolated one-off actions. ([Ogletree Deakins](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/ftc-begins-enforcement-of-the-take-it-down-act-new-risks-and-tools-for-businesses/))

## Action Items

- **If operating a generative AI image tool:** Conduct an immediate assessment of whether your service creates or hosts "digital forgeries" as defined in 47 U.S.C. § 223a. Nudify-style tools are squarely within scope. The FTC's letters targeted services that lacked a removal request mechanism entirely — remediation must begin now.
- **Establish a compliant removal request process:** The process must be clearly accessible (conspicuously displayed on the platform), allow any depicted individual or their representative to submit a request, and support processing within 48 hours including removal of known identical copies.
- **Do not assume the FTC will only send one letter.** Warning letters are a precursor to formal enforcement actions. The FTC has a consumer complaint portal actively receiving reports; companies that ignore warning letters face civil penalty exposure accumulating per-violation.
- **Review platform scope carefully.** The "nudify" characterization in the FTC's letters is not a formal legal term — any AI tool that generates, alters, curates, or hosts nonconsensual intimate imagery in the course of its regular business is a covered platform under the statute, regardless of how it markets itself.
- **Document compliance steps with timestamps.** If a formal investigation follows, demonstrable good-faith efforts taken immediately after receipt of the FTC letter will matter significantly in any civil penalty negotiation.
