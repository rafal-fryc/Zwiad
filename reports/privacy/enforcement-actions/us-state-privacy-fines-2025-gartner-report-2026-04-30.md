# U.S. State Privacy Enforcement Fines Surpass $3.4 Billion in 2025, Nearly Doubling Year-Over-Year

**Jurisdiction:** Multi-state (US) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** April 30, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

A [Gartner report released April 28, 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-28-gartner-estimates-us-states-privacy-fines-totaled-3-point-425-billion-dollars-in-2025-trend-expected-to-accelerate-through-2028) found that U.S. state regulators collected $3.425 billion in privacy-related fines in 2025 — nearly doubling the $1.827 billion recorded in 2024, and exceeding the combined total from the prior five years. Gartner analyst Nader Henein stated that regulators are "shifting their efforts away from spreading awareness to full-scale enforcement," characterizing this as "increasingly the standard in 2026 and beyond." The acceleration is expected to continue through 2028 as new automated decision-making obligations activate across multiple states.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **$3.425 billion** in U.S. state privacy fines collected in 2025, per [Gartner's April 28, 2026 press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-04-28-gartner-estimates-us-states-privacy-fines-totaled-3-point-425-billion-dollars-in-2025-trend-expected-to-accelerate-through-2028) — a 87% year-over-year increase from $1.827 billion in 2024.
- The 2025 total exceeds the **combined privacy fine total from the prior five years**, marking a decisive shift from regulatory infancy to sustained enforcement.
- **Texas** accounts for a disproportionate share: [Texas AG Ken Paxton secured a $1.4 billion settlement with Meta](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-14-billion-settlement-meta-over-its-unauthorized-capture) for unauthorized biometric data capture under the Texas CUBI statute, and separately secured a [$1.375 billion settlement with Google](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-historic-1375-billion-settlement-google-related-texans-data) over data privacy rights.
- **California AG** announced a [$2.75 million CCPA settlement with Disney](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/california-wont-let-it-go-attorney-general-bonta-announces-275-million) — the largest CCPA settlement in California history — for failure to honor opt-out signals including Global Privacy Control (GPC) across all devices and streaming services.
- **California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)** issued a [$1.35 million fine against Tractor Supply Company](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250930.html) on September 30, 2025, then its largest penalty, for multiple CCPA violations including broken opt-out mechanisms and failure to process opt-out preference signals.
- **CPPA** also fined [Ford Motor Company $375,703](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/03/ford-to-change-practices-pay-fine-for-adding-unnecessary-friction-to-opt-out-process/) in March 2026 for adding unnecessary friction to the opt-out process — specifically, requiring email verification before honoring opt-out requests.
- As of early 2026, **20 states** have comprehensive consumer privacy laws in effect, with Indiana, Kentucky, and Rhode Island joining the landscape on January 1, 2026, per [MultiState's tracker](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/2/4/all-of-the-comprehensive-privacy-laws-that-take-effect-in-2026).
- Gartner's Henein noted that new privacy law amendments are "primarily focused on automated decision-making technologies," with California's ADMT compliance requirements activating January 1, 2027, per [CPPA's September 2025 rulemaking announcement](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html).
- Regulators are expanding attention beyond large platforms to **mid-sized companies across tech, automotive, consumer products, and mobile gaming** — evidenced by actions against Tractor Supply, Ford, Jam City (mobile gaming), and Background Alert (data broker).

## Key Enforcement Actions in 2025–2026 [HIGH confidence]

### Texas
- **Meta — $1.4 billion** (2024, payments over 5 years): Biometric data capture without consent under the Texas CUBI statute. First enforcement action under that law, per [Texas AG announcement](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-14-billion-settlement-meta-over-its-unauthorized-capture).
- **Google — $1.375 billion**: Settlement over Texans' data privacy rights, per [Texas AG announcement](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-historic-1375-billion-settlement-google-related-texans-data).

### California
- **Disney — $2.75 million** (CA AG, February 2026): Opt-out signals not honored across all devices and streaming services (Disney+, Hulu, ESPN+). GPC signals limited to the device used; webform opt-outs did not stop third-party tracking pixels. Required corrective actions include system-level opt-out implementation. Per [CA AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/california-wont-let-it-go-attorney-general-bonta-announces-275-million) and [WilmerHale analysis](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260309-california-attorney-general-announces-largest-ccpa-settlement).
- **Tractor Supply — $1.35 million** (CPPA, September 30, 2025): Four categories of violations: broken opt-out mechanisms, failure to process opt-out preference signals, outdated privacy policy, and deficient service provider contracts. Required quarterly digital property scans and annual compliance certification by a corporate officer. Per [CPPA announcement](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250930.html) and [Orrick analysis](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/10/CPPA-Imposes-the-Largest-Administrative-Fine-to-Date-What-Companies-Need-to-Know).
- **Ford Motor Company — $375,703** (CPPA, March 2026): Email verification requirement added unnecessary friction to the opt-out process, violating CCPA. Part of CPPA's broader connected vehicle investigation running since 2023. Per [CPPA announcement](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/03/ford-to-change-practices-pay-fine-for-adding-unnecessary-friction-to-opt-out-process/).
- **Jam City — $1.4 million** (CA AG): Failure to provide opt-out methods in mobile gaming apps.

## Enforcement Trajectory [HIGH confidence]

Gartner characterizes the current enforcement environment as a permanent regime shift, not a one-time spike. Three structural factors point toward continued escalation:

1. **New obligations activating**: California's Automated Decision-Making Technology (ADMT) regulations took effect January 1, 2026. Businesses using ADMT for significant decisions must comply with opt-out and pre-use notice requirements by January 1, 2027. Risk assessments for activities initiated before 2026 must be completed by December 31, 2027, with executive attestation due April 1, 2028, per [CPPA's rulemaking](https://cppa.ca.gov/announcements/2025/20250923.html) and [Greenberg Traurig's compliance summary](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/9/revised-and-new-ccpa-regulations-set-to-take-effect-on-jan-1-2026-summary-of-near-term-action-items).

2. **Geographic expansion**: 24 additional states are expected to pass comprehensive privacy legislation within five years, per Gartner's report, effectively foreclosing the strategy of limiting compliance to California and Texas.

3. **Enforcement sophistication**: The CPPA's Tractor Supply settlement established a template for multi-violation enforcement (opt-out mechanics, privacy notices, service provider contracts), while the Disney settlement extended GPC obligations to a cross-device, cross-service scope. Gartner noted that many organizations "built their privacy programs in 2020 and have allowed them to atrophy," leaving them poorly positioned for this enforcement environment.

## Action Items

- **Audit opt-out signal handling immediately**: Both the Disney and Tractor Supply enforcement actions turned on failures to honor GPC signals and cross-device opt-outs. Organizations must verify that opt-out requests apply at the account level, not the device level, and that third-party tracking pixels are covered.
- **Eliminate friction from opt-out workflows**: The Ford settlement established that email verification before honoring an opt-out request is an independent CCPA violation. Review every step in the opt-out flow.
- **Update and review service provider contracts**: Tractor Supply's fine included violations for contracts with advertising technology companies that lacked required CCPA provisions. Audit all contracts with ad-tech and data-sharing partners.
- **Prepare for ADMT compliance deadlines**: California's automated decision-making obligations activate January 1, 2027 for new processing; December 31, 2027 for existing activities. Begin risk assessments and pre-use notice drafting now.
- **Extend compliance to mid-market and all verticals**: Enforcement actions against Tractor Supply (retail), Ford (automotive), Jam City (mobile gaming), and data brokers demonstrate that regulators are not limiting enforcement to large tech platforms.
- **Conduct a privacy program audit**: Gartner explicitly flagged organizations that built privacy programs in 2020 and have not updated them as high-risk. Review against current CCPA/CPRA regulations and applicable state law requirements.
