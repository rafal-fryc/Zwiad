# FTC Takes Enforcement Action Against Match Group and OkCupid for Sharing User Photos and Data With AI Startup Without Consent

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On March 30, 2026, the FTC filed a complaint and stipulated final order against Match Group Americas and OkCupid (Humor Rainbow, Inc.) for sharing nearly three million user photos and related personal data -- including location information -- with AI facial recognition startup Clarifai without user consent, in violation of OkCupid's own privacy policy. <!-- verified --> The data sharing was driven by the personal financial interests of OkCupid's founders, who were investors in Clarifai, and the companies subsequently attempted to obstruct the FTC's investigation. <!-- verified --> The settlement permanently bars the companies from misrepresenting their data practices but imposes no monetary penalty ([FTC Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-takes-action-against-match-okcupid-deceiving-users-sharing-personal-data-third-party)). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- **Data shared:** In September 2014, OkCupid provided Clarifai with access to nearly three million user photos plus location and demographic data, with no contractual restrictions on use ([FTC Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-takes-action-against-match-okcupid-deceiving-users-sharing-personal-data-third-party)). <!-- verified -->

- **Motivation -- conflict of interest:** OkCupid's founders were financial investors in Clarifai. There was no formal business relationship, vendor agreement, or service contract between the companies. Clarifai's founder requested the data based on the personal investment relationship ([FTC Case Page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/okcupidmatch)). <!-- verified -->

- **Privacy policy violation:** OkCupid's privacy policy at the time stated it only shared personal information with service providers, business partners, or affiliated companies, or after notifying users and providing an opt-out. Clarifai fit none of these categories, and users were never notified ([FTC Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-takes-action-against-match-okcupid-deceiving-users-sharing-personal-data-third-party)). <!-- verified -->

- **Obstruction:** When media reports revealed that Clarifai had obtained large OkCupid datasets, OkCupid publicly denied involvement. The companies also obstructed the FTC's Civil Investigative Demand, which the FTC ultimately had to enforce in federal court ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202603/ftc-order-bars-okcupid-from-misleading-users-about-biometric-data-sharing)). <!-- verified -->

- **Legal basis:** The FTC's complaint alleges deceptive acts or practices in violation of Section 5 of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. Sec. 45 ([FTC Case Page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/okcupidmatch)). <!-- verified -->

- **Case filed:** Case No. 3:26-cv-00996-K, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Dallas Division. The Commission voted 2-0 to authorize filing ([FTC Case Page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/okcupidmatch)). <!-- verified -->

- **Settlement terms:** The stipulated final order permanently prohibits misrepresentation of data collection, use, disclosure, and deletion practices, as well as misrepresentation of privacy controls. No monetary penalty was imposed. The companies must file compliance reports for ten years, distribute the order to relevant personnel, and notify the FTC within 14 days of corporate changes affecting compliance ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-enforcement-alert-match-group-okcupid-privacy-promises-have-consequences)). <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- **Audit investor and affiliate data-sharing arrangements.** Review whether any personal data flows to entities connected to company founders, executives, or investors. Ensure all third-party data sharing is covered by a legitimate business purpose and documented in a written agreement with use restrictions.

- **Align privacy policies with actual practices.** Confirm that every category of data sharing currently occurring is accurately described in your published privacy policy. Any gap between policy language and practice creates Section 5 deception risk.

- **Prepare for FTC investigative demands.** If contacted by the FTC, respond accurately and completely. This case demonstrates that obstruction and public denial can become separate bases for enforcement action and can extend the duration and severity of an investigation.

- **Implement conflict-of-interest controls for data access.** Establish internal policies requiring disclosure and approval when data sharing involves entities in which officers, founders, or directors hold financial interests.
