# FTC Secures $35 Million Settlement Against Shutterstock for Deceptive Subscription and Negative Option Practices

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy / Consumer Protection | **Date:** May 13, 2026

---

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 13, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission simultaneously filed and settled a lawsuit against Shutterstock, Inc. in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, alleging that the stock media platform employed deceptive "negative option" subscription practices in violation of Section 5 of the FTC Act and the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA). Shutterstock agreed to pay [$35 million in consumer redress](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-cancellation-practices) and to implement permanent injunctive relief governing its subscription enrollment, disclosure, and cancellation practices. The company does not admit liability. Shutterstock has stated it will pay the full settlement from cash on hand in Q2 2026.

This action is the FTC's most recent high-value enforcement against subscription services and signals continued aggressive use of ROSCA and Section 5 as the agency's primary tools in the absence of a durable codified Negative Option Rule — the FTC's revised rule having been vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 on procedural grounds.

---

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

**Filing and forum:** The complaint and stipulated order were filed simultaneously on May 13, 2026, in the Southern District of New York ([FTC Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-cancellation-practices)). Shutterstock did not admit liability.

**Monetary penalty:** A $35 million monetary judgment for consumer redress, to be paid in full from company cash in Q2 2026 ([Shutterstock SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001549346/000114036126021319/ef20073632_8k.htm)).

**Legal basis:** Violations of Section 5 of the FTC Act (deceptive and unfair acts or practices) and ROSCA, 15 U.S.C. §§ 8401–8405, which requires online sellers using negative option features to (1) clearly and conspicuously disclose all material terms before obtaining billing information, (2) obtain express informed consent before charging, and (3) provide a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges ([FTC Complaint (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ShutterStock-Complaint.pdf)).

**Products at issue:** Two primary product categories were implicated:
- **Annual Paid Monthly (APM) plans** — annual subscriptions billed monthly, advertised in ways the FTC alleged obscured the annual commitment and associated early termination fees.
- **On-demand content packs** — marketed as "best for a one-time project" with "no commitment," but structured with auto-refill triggers and, for a period, automatic renewal of expired packs ([Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/05/ftc-targets-shutterstocks-negative-option-subscriptions-in-35-million-settlement/)).

**Cancellation fee structure:** Consumers cancelling APM plans before the end of the annual term were charged cancellation fees equal to 50% of remaining annual obligations — ranging in practice from approximately $29 to thousands of dollars, depending on the subscription tier ([All About Advertising Law](https://www.allaboutadvertisinglaw.com/2026/05/shutterstock-settles-with-ftc-for-35-million-for-subscription-and-negative-option-marketing-practices.html)).

**Cancellation process:** The FTC described a multi-step, eight-screen online cancellation flow as an unlawful barrier under ROSCA's "simple mechanism" requirement. Phone and email cancellation channels were also alleged to be difficult to locate or dysfunctional ([Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/05/ftc-targets-shutterstocks-negative-option-subscriptions-in-35-million-settlement/)).

**Internal documentary evidence:** The FTC complaint cited internal Shutterstock emails demonstrating the company's awareness of consumer confusion. A Senior Manager of Customer Experience stated in October 2023 that the cancellation fee was a "constant source of confusion/frustration for customers." Other internal communications acknowledged that "many of [our] customers don't realize what they have signed up for," and that the cancellation fee was deployed as a "powerful retention tool" ([FTC Complaint (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ShutterStock-Complaint.pdf)).

---

## Specific Practices Alleged [HIGH confidence]

The FTC complaint identifies four distinct categories of deceptive or unfair conduct:

**1. APM plan disclosure failures.** Shutterstock's enrollment flow for annual-paid-monthly plans failed to clearly disclose: (a) that the plan constituted an annual commitment rather than a true monthly subscription; (b) the existence, amount, and timing of early termination fees; and (c) that the plan would auto-renew at the end of the annual term. The cancellation fee — amounting to 50% of outstanding obligations — was buried or absent from key enrollment screens.

**2. On-demand pack auto-refill and auto-renewal.** Packs marketed as "best for a one-time project" with "no commitment" automatically refilled once all included download licenses were used, generating a new charge. Disclosure language about refills appeared in small gray text below the call-to-action button, contradicting more prominent "one-time project" marketing language. For a period, Shutterstock also renewed expired packs without adequate advance notice.

**3. Obstruction of cancellation.** The online cancellation path required consumers to navigate a multi-step, eight-screen flow. Phone and email support channels were hard to locate or inoperative, failing ROSCA's requirement that the cancellation mechanism be "at least as easy" as enrollment.

**4. Charges without informed consent.** By failing to disclose the full terms at the point of billing information collection, Shutterstock charged consumers without the express informed consent ROSCA requires before any charge.

## Settlement Terms [HIGH confidence]

**Monetary relief:** $35 million payable to the FTC for consumer redress. Shutterstock intends to satisfy the judgment from existing cash in Q2 2026 ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001549346/000114036126021319/ef20073632_8k.htm)).

**Permanent injunction — disclosure requirements:** Before collecting any billing information for a negative-option offer, Shutterstock must clearly and conspicuously disclose: all material subscription terms; billing frequency; the existence, amount, and timing of any cancellation or early termination fees; and how consumers can stop charges. Disclosures must appear in close proximity to the payment button.

**Permanent injunction — consent requirements:** Shutterstock must obtain express, informed consent to all material terms before any charge. The order prohibits misrepresenting any material term, including whether a product is a one-time purchase or a recurring subscription, the nature of any auto-renewal, and the presence or amount of cancellation fees.

**Permanent injunction — cancellation mechanism:** Shutterstock must provide a cancellation method that is at least as simple as enrollment. This includes a functional one-click online cancellation option and a working customer-service telephone line. The mechanism must immediately halt further charges upon consumer request.

**Compliance monitoring:** The order imposes detailed compliance, recordkeeping, and reporting obligations, and authorizes the FTC to conduct ongoing monitoring of Shutterstock's compliance ([FTC Shutterstock Case Page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/shutterstock-inc)).

## Regulatory Context: The Negative Option Enforcement Landscape [MEDIUM confidence]

The Shutterstock settlement is the latest in a sustained FTC campaign against subscription trap practices, prosecuted primarily through ROSCA and Section 5 in the absence of a currently operative codified rule:

- **Eighth Circuit vacatur (July 2025):** The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's revised Negative Option Rule ("Click-to-Cancel" rule), finding the agency failed to conduct a required preliminary regulatory analysis, thereby depriving stakeholders of the opportunity to comment on cost-benefit alternatives ([Eighth Circuit decision coverage — Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/click-to-cancelled-eighth-circuit-vacates-federal-trade-commissions-revised-negative-option-rule); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-negative-option-rule/)).

- **Rulemaking restart (January 2026):** On January 30, 2026, the FTC submitted a draft Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) to OIRA, restarting the rulemaking process. A public comment period is expected to follow ANPRM publication. Given the vacatur, the FTC is likely to take extra care to satisfy procedural requirements, which means a final rule remains years away ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/); [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur)).

- **Parallel enforcement actions:** During the rulemaking gap, the FTC continues to enforce directly under ROSCA and Section 5. Recent comparable actions include: FTC v. JustAnswer LLC (N.D. Cal., January 2026, alleging hidden subscription enrollment); FTC v. Instacart ($60 million settlement, December 2025); United States v. Adobe, Inc. (N.D. Cal., 2024); and the Amazon ROSCA summary judgment in September 2025 ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/02/ftc-and-state-ags-continue-to-scrutinize-subscription-practices-amidst-a-possible-click-to-cancel-rule-revival); [InfoLawGroup](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2026/2/6/from-justanswer-to-instacart-the-ftcs-latest-subscription-enforcement)).

- **State-level amplification:** California's Automatic Renewal Law (ARL) imposes disclosure and consent requirements that exceed federal ROSCA standards, including mandatory online cancellation for subscriptions initiated online and annual renewal reminders. State AGs — particularly in California, New York, and Texas — are coordinating with FTC investigations and launching independent sweeps. Class-action plaintiffs routinely follow FTC enforcement announcements within weeks.

## Why It Matters

The $35 million Shutterstock penalty is significant for three reasons:

**Scale of monetary judgment.** At $35 million, this is among the larger subscription-practice settlements against a single company outside the Amazon action. It signals that the FTC views even mid-market subscription platforms as worth substantial enforcement resources.

**Internal evidence as litigation risk.** The FTC's heavy reliance on internal Shutterstock emails — documenting known consumer confusion and deliberate use of cancellation fees as a retention tool — follows the pattern from Adobe and JustAnswer. Companies generating internal communications that describe subscription design as a retention mechanism should treat those documents as potential enforcement exhibits. Privilege review and litigation-hold procedures should be calibrated accordingly.

**ROSCA enforcement without a rule.** The vacatur of the Click-to-Cancel rule has not slowed enforcement — it has instead pushed the FTC toward case-by-case actions using existing statutory authority. This means that ROSCA's three core requirements (conspicuous disclosure, express informed consent, simple cancellation) are judicially tested obligations today, not pending regulatory requirements. Companies cannot defer compliance on the premise that a final rule has not been issued.

## Action Items

Companies offering subscription services — in any sector, not only media or software — should audit current practices against the Shutterstock settlement's specific findings:

1. **Disclosure audit.** Map every subscription enrollment flow. Confirm that all of the following appear clearly and conspicuously before the payment button: the subscription period (monthly vs. annual), billing frequency, auto-renewal terms, and any cancellation or early termination fee including its amount and the conditions under which it applies.

2. **Cancel-to-enroll parity.** Count the steps required to cancel. If cancellation requires more steps than enrollment, the process likely fails ROSCA's "simple mechanism" requirement. Implement one-click online cancellation for subscriptions initiated online.

3. **Pack and bundle product review.** If any product is marketed as "one-time," "no commitment," or project-based, verify that auto-refill or auto-renewal features are either absent or disclosed with equal or greater prominence than the "no commitment" language.

4. **Email and retention audit.** Review internal communications relating to cancellation fee design, subscription renewal optimization, or "saves" programs. Communications that document consumer confusion alongside intentional retention design create substantial enforcement exposure.

5. **California ARL compliance check.** The California Automatic Renewal Law imposes requirements beyond ROSCA, including online cancellation for online enrollments, annual reminder notices for subscriptions over $5/month, and specific consent-language requirements. Multi-state operators must satisfy both regimes.

6. **Incident-response plan for FTC outreach.** Civil Investigative Demands (CIDs) in subscription investigations have preceded complaints in recent FTC cases. Companies in the SaaS, media, stock-content, and e-commerce sectors should ensure legal teams are prepared to respond promptly and cooperatively, given that obstruction (as alleged in the OkCupid/Match case) has drawn heightened sanctions.

## What To Watch

- **Court approval of the stipulated order.** The order was filed simultaneously with the complaint on May 13, 2026. Formal court entry is pending; any modification by the SDNY would alter the binding injunctive terms.
- **Consumer redress distribution.** The FTC will administer the $35 million fund. The methodology for identifying and compensating affected consumers — particularly those who paid cancellation fees — has not yet been publicly detailed.
- **Negative Option rulemaking.** Once the ANPRM is published by OIRA, the comment period will open. Industry participants should monitor for proposed rule text and submit comments on compliance feasibility, particularly regarding the "simple mechanism" standard.
- **Follow-on state AG actions.** Several state attorneys general have coordinated with recent FTC subscription enforcement; similar coordination around the Shutterstock facts is possible.
- **Class-action litigation.** Shutterstock subscribers who paid cancellation fees may file class actions relying on the FTC's factual findings, even though Shutterstock did not admit liability. California's Automatic Renewal Law and CLRA provide private rights of action that align with the alleged conduct.
