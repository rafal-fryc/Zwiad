# Iowa AG Brenna Bird Amends TikTok Lawsuit, Adds China Data Access and Child Addiction Claims

**Jurisdiction:** Iowa | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** April 14, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird filed a 113-page amended petition on April 14, 2026 in the ongoing state court action against TikTok, Inc. and related ByteDance entities, adding two significant new claim clusters: (1) that TikTok deceives Iowa consumers into believing their personal data is safe from the Chinese government when it is not, in violation of the [Iowa Consumer Fraud Act, Iowa Code § 714.16](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/714.16.pdf); and (2) that TikTok knowingly designed the platform to addict children and teenagers through compulsive-use mechanisms, despite internal awareness of resulting harms including sleep deprivation, depression, and anxiety. The amended complaint also names TikTok's newly created U.S.-based ownership entity as a defendant, following Congress's forced-divestiture legislation.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Original lawsuit filed January 2024:** AG Bird originally sued TikTok in [Iowa District Court for Polk County](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/newsroom/attorney-general-bird-sues-tiktok-for-misleading-parents) alleging the platform misled parents about the prevalence of inappropriate content, including drugs, alcohol, nudity, and profanity, despite maintaining a 12+ app store rating. Defendants included TikTok Inc. (incorporated in California), TikTok Ltd. (Cayman Islands), TikTok PTE Ltd., ByteDance Ltd., and ByteDance Inc., per the [original complaint](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/IACIO/2024/01/17/file_attachments/2752222/As-Filed%20TikTok%20Complaint.pdf).

- **Iowa Supreme Court affirmed jurisdiction January 23, 2026:** In [State ex rel. Attorney General Brenna Bird v. TikTok, Inc., No. 24-1566 (Iowa Jan. 23, 2026)](https://law.justia.com/cases/iowa/supreme-court/2026/24-1566.html), the Iowa Supreme Court ruled that Iowa courts have specific personal jurisdiction over all TikTok and ByteDance defendants because they actively serve content to and collect data from Iowa users' devices, and use Iowa-specific location data to target advertisements generating revenue from Iowa.

- **New Claim 1 — China Data Access Deception:** The amended petition alleges TikTok falsely represented to Iowa consumers that their personal data is protected from access by the Chinese government, when in fact the Chinese government can readily obtain TikTok users' personal information, per the [Iowa AG's press release](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/newsroom/attorney-general-brenna-bird-amends-tiktok-lawsuit-asserting-risk-of-china-access-to-iowans-data). This claim proceeds under the Iowa Consumer Fraud Act, [Iowa Code § 714.16](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/714.16.pdf), which prohibits deception, false promises, and material misrepresentations in the advertisement or sale of consumer merchandise.

- **New Claim 2 — Addictive Design Targeting Minors:** The amended petition alleges that TikTok knowingly designed app features that prey upon minors' psychological vulnerabilities and override their autonomy to control time spent on the platform through compulsive-use mechanisms, per [KCRG reporting](https://www.kcrg.com/2026/04/14/iowa-attorney-general-expands-tiktok-lawsuit-claiming-children-are-risk/). The state's investigators created test accounts set up as 13-year-old users and documented the For You Page serving videos with profane music, alcohol use, sexual themes, and mature content.

- **Mental Health Harms:** The amended complaint alleges TikTok targeted young users despite internal awareness that heavy use contributes to sleep deprivation, depression, anxiety, and other harms, according to [reporting by We Are Iowa](https://www.weareiowa.com/article/news/local/iowa-tiktok-lawsuit-attorney-general-bird-data-breach-personal-information-china/524-e67c51cd-7fdb-4162-afd8-10e34993e5b9).

- **New Defendant Added:** TikTok's newly created U.S.-based ownership entity — created following the federal forced-divestiture legislation — is named as a defendant in the amended complaint, per [The State AG Report](https://www.stateagreport.com/news/iowa-ag-adds-claims-to-tiktok-lawsuit/).

- **Relief Sought:** The state seeks a permanent injunction compelling TikTok to cease deceptive conduct, plus civil penalties, disgorgement, and attorney fees under the Iowa Consumer Fraud Act, per the [Iowa AG press release](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/newsroom/attorney-general-brenna-bird-amends-tiktok-lawsuit-asserting-risk-of-china-access-to-iowans-data).

- **Broader State AG Landscape:** Iowa's action sits within a multistate enforcement wave. As of early 2025, at least 23 states had filed individual or coalition actions against TikTok. Minnesota's AG [defeated a motion to dismiss in March 2026](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2026/03/23_TikTok.asp). A bipartisan 14-state coalition led by California and New York [filed coordinated actions in October 2024](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-attorney-general-james-lead-coalition-suing-tiktok-for-exploiting-young-users-deceiving-public).

## Legal Framework [HIGH confidence]

The amended complaint relies primarily on two Iowa statutory provisions:

**Iowa Consumer Fraud Act, Iowa Code § 714.16** — This provision authorizes the Attorney General to bring enforcement actions against any person who engages in deception, fraud, false pretense, false promise, or material misrepresentation in connection with the advertisement or sale of consumer merchandise. The AG can seek civil penalties, injunctive relief, and restitution. The [Iowa AG's original complaint PDF](https://www.iowaattorneygeneral.gov/media/cms/AsFiled_TikTok_Complaint_AD07A587C6BC2.pdf) confirms this as the primary enforcement vehicle.

**Iowa Consumer Fraud — Private Actions Act, Iowa Code Chapter 714H** — Iowa Code [Chapter 714H](https://law.justia.com/codes/iowa/title-xvi/chapter-714h/) separately provides a private right of action for consumers who suffer ascertainable loss due to prohibited deceptive practices, paralleling but distinct from the AG's enforcement authority under § 714.16. The AG's action proceeds under § 714.16, not Chapter 714H.

The state's China-data-access theory presents a novel application of consumer fraud law to geopolitical data security representations — a legal theory that other state AGs have not yet widely adopted in TikTok litigation. Its viability will likely turn on whether TikTok's data security representations constitute actionable "material misrepresentations" under Iowa law, and on TikTok's ability to challenge the factual premise (i.e., whether Chinese government access is actually available).

## Action Items

- **Social media platforms with Chinese parent company affiliations** should audit consumer-facing representations about data security, particularly any statements assuring users that data is protected from foreign government access. Iowa's consumer fraud theory could be replicated by other state AGs.

- **Companies with addictive-design features targeting minors** should review internal documentation about platform engagement metrics and known harms — such records become potent enforcement tools when states obtain them through discovery.

- **TikTok specifically**: The addition of the new U.S.-based ownership entity as a defendant signals that the AG views the divestiture restructuring as insufficient to insulate TikTok from Iowa's claims. Assess whether the corporate restructuring triggers any successor liability arguments by the state.

- **Monitor the Iowa district court docket** for TikTok's anticipated motion to dismiss the new claims. The Iowa Supreme Court's January 2026 jurisdiction ruling narrows the viable dismissal grounds to substantive legal defenses, which could shape similar litigation nationally.

- **Compliance teams operating apps for minors** should benchmark against the AG's test: creating 13-year-old test accounts and documenting the content served. If a 13-year-old account quickly surfaces profane, adult-themed, or harmful content, that gap in enforcement is likely to attract similar AG scrutiny.
