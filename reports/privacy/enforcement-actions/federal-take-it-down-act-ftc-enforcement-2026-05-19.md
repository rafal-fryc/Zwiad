# FTC Begins Enforcement of TAKE IT DOWN Act, Launches Consumer Complaint Website

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-05-19

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 19, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission began enforcing the platform compliance obligations of the TAKE IT DOWN Act (S. 146), the federal law signed by President Trump on May 19, 2025 that requires covered online platforms to establish a notice-and-removal process for nonconsensual intimate imagery (NCII), including AI-generated deepfakes. The FTC simultaneously launched a consumer complaint portal at [TakeItDown.ftc.gov](https://takeitdown.ftc.gov) where victims can report platforms that fail to establish or honor the mandated removal process. FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson warned that companies not in compliance face civil penalties of up to $53,088 per violation, stating: "We stand ready to monitor compliance, investigate violations, and enforce the Take It Down Act."

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The TAKE IT DOWN Act (Pub. L. No. 119-___; S. 146, 119th Congress) was signed into law May 19, 2025, giving covered platforms exactly one year to comply with the notice-and-removal requirement. The FTC began enforcing Section 3 on the one-year compliance deadline: May 19, 2026. ([Congress.gov, S.146 text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146/text))

- Covered platforms must: (1) establish an accessible process through which individuals can request removal of NCII; (2) remove the reported imagery and **known identical copies** within **48 hours** of a valid request. The law covers real images, digitally altered imagery, and AI-generated deepfakes. ([FTC Business Blog, May 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/05/take-it-down-act-enforcement-starts-now-what-know-about-ftc-tida))

- Failure to comply is treated as a violation of Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act, subjecting companies to civil penalties of up to **$53,088 per violation** and potential FTC legal action. ([FTC Press Release: FTC Begins Enforcing the TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act))

- Chairman Ferguson sent warning letters to major platforms — including Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Automattic, Bumble, Discord, Match Group, Meta, Microsoft, Pinterest, Reddit, SmugMug, Snapchat, TikTok, and X — reminding them of their compliance obligations. ([FTC Press Release: FTC Chairman Ferguson Advises Companies](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-chairman-ferguson-advises-companies-comply-take-it-down-act))

- The FTC separately sent warning letters to **12 "nudify" websites** — AI tools that generate nonconsensual sexualized images from clothed photos — notifying them that they appeared to be in violation of the Act by failing to provide a removal request process. These companies were not publicly named. ([FTC Warning Letters Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-sends-warning-letters-companies-about-compliance-take-it-down-act); [Jurist News](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/05/us-federal-trade-commission-warns-12-nudify-websites-to-comply-with-take-it-down-act/))

- A template of the FTC's nudify warning letter is publicly available: [TIDA Nudify Warning Letter Template (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/tida-nudify-warning-letter-template_0.pdf).

- Chairman Ferguson stated: "In the age of AI, anyone can be targeted, and that becomes even more appalling if children are involved. The TAKE IT DOWN Act empowers families and provides the FTC with an effective tool to protect minors against this form of abuse." ([FTC Press Release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-begins-enforcing-take-it-down-act))

- Consumers can file complaints about non-compliant platforms at [TakeItDown.ftc.gov](https://takeitdown.ftc.gov) or [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov). The FTC's consumer guidance explains rights under the Act at [consumer.ftc.gov](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/05/what-will-ftcs-enforcement-take-it-down-act-mean-you).

## Legal Background [HIGH confidence]

The TAKE IT DOWN Act — an acronym for "Tools to Address Known Exploitation by Immobilizing Technological Deepfakes on Websites and Networks Act" — was championed by First Lady Melania Trump, passed by the Senate on February 13, 2025, passed the House on April 28, 2025, and signed by President Trump on May 19, 2025. ([Congress.gov Legislative History, S.146](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146); [Congressional Research Service, LSB11314](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11314))

The law has two distinct enforcement tracks:

**Criminal provisions (47 U.S.C. § 223(h)) — effective immediately upon enactment.** Any person who knowingly uses an interactive computer service to publish an "intimate visual depiction" or "digital forgery" of an identifiable individual without consent faces fines and up to two years' imprisonment, with enhanced penalties where minors are depicted. A "digital forgery" covers AI-generated or machine-modified imagery falsely depicting an individual in an intimate visual depiction.

**Platform notice-and-removal obligations (47 U.S.C. § 223a) — effective May 19, 2026.** A "covered platform" is a website, online service, online application, or mobile application that serves the public and either (a) primarily provides a forum for user-generated content, or (b) publishes, curates, hosts, or makes available nonconsensual intimate visual depictions in the regular course of business. Excluded are: broadband internet access service providers, email services, and websites/apps consisting primarily of provider-selected (non-user-generated) content where any interactive functionality is incidental — unless the platform is in the business of hosting NCII. ([Congress.gov, S.146 text](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146/text))

Compliance enforcement under the civil track is delegated exclusively to the FTC under Section 5 of the FTC Act. ([FTC statute page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/tools-address-known-exploitation-immobilizing-technological-deepfakes-websites-networks-act-take-it))

## Platform Compliance Requirements [HIGH confidence]

Per the [FTC's compliance guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act), covered platforms must, at minimum:

1. **Establish a takedown request mechanism** — a clear, accessible process through which depicted individuals (or authorized representatives, including for minors) can submit removal requests.
2. **Remove reported content within 48 hours** — including not just the reported item but known identical copies on the same platform.
3. **Avoid re-posting removed content** — the Act prohibits restoring removed NCII without the depicted individual's consent.
4. **Maintain the process on an ongoing basis** — the compliance obligation is not one-time; platforms must keep the mechanism operational.

The FTC's IAPP analysis notes that the "covered platform" definition sweeps broadly across social media, video hosting, image sharing, messaging, and gaming platforms. ([IAPP: How to Comply as FTC Begins Enforcement](https://iapp.org/news/a/take-it-down-act-how-to-comply-as-the-ftc-begins-enforcement))

## Action Items

- **Assess coverage immediately.** Any operator of a public-facing platform that hosts or enables user-generated content should determine whether it qualifies as a "covered platform" under 47 U.S.C. § 223a. The FTC's definition is broad and will capture many social media, image-sharing, video, messaging, and gaming services.

- **Build or audit the NCII removal process.** Covered platforms must have a documented, accessible mechanism for removal requests. Review whether the current process meets the 48-hour removal window, extends to known identical copies, and prevents re-posting.

- **Review "nudify" and AI image-generation services.** If your business operates any AI tool that generates sexualized imagery from user-supplied photos, expect heightened FTC scrutiny. The 12 warning letters to nudify sites signal these services are enforcement priorities.

- **Train operations and trust-and-safety teams.** Frontline teams handling content reports need to understand the 48-hour mandatory removal clock and internal escalation procedures.

- **Monitor the FTC's TakeItDown.ftc.gov portal.** Consumer complaints filed there will drive the FTC's enforcement triage. High complaint volumes against a platform increase enforcement risk.

- **Document compliance proactively.** In the event of an FTC inquiry, platforms should be prepared to demonstrate the date their removal mechanism went live, the volume of requests processed, and average response times.
