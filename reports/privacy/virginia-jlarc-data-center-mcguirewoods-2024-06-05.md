# With JLARC Study, Virginia Continues Review of Data Center Industry

**Jurisdiction:** Virginia | **Category:** Privacy / Regulatory | **Date:** 2024-06-05

> **Note:** A comprehensive report on this topic — covering the full arc from the May 2024 kickoff through the December 2024 final JLARC report and 2025–2026 legislative consequences — is available at [reports/privacy/virginia-jlarc-data-center-study-sjr17-2024-05-30.md](virginia-jlarc-data-center-study-sjr17-2024-05-30.md). This memo covers the McGuireWoods June 2024 client alert, which marked the public launch of the JLARC study and framed the regulatory questions that the Commission ultimately answered in December 2024.

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

On May 21, 2024, the Virginia Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission (JLARC) held its initial public discussion to open a legislative study of the state's data center industry, examining opportunities, challenges, and the possible reform of incentives and regulations for a sector that had become a dominant force in Virginia's economy and energy grid. [McGuireWoods LLP](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2024/5/with-jlarc-study-virginia-continues-review-of-data-center-industry/) published a client alert on June 5, 2024 summarizing the session and previewing the topics JLARC would analyze through the remainder of the year. The study was formally directed by [Senate Joint Resolution 17](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/resolutions/2024_Data%20centers_JLARC.pdf), passed by the 2024 Virginia General Assembly, which chose to defer most proposed data center legislation pending JLARC's findings. The Commission released its final report — *Data Centers in Virginia* (Report 598) — on December 9, 2024, containing findings and recommendations on economic impacts, energy infrastructure, land use, noise, water use, and the state sales tax exemption.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

### Virginia's Dominant Position in Global Data Center Market

Northern Virginia — specifically the corridor often called "Data Center Alley" running through Loudoun, Fairfax, and Prince William Counties — hosts the world's largest concentration of data center capacity. By 2024, the industry was generating approximately $9.1 billion annually in Virginia GDP and supporting an estimated 74,000 jobs (primarily during the construction phase). The industry's growth has been closely tied to a suite of state incentives, chief among them the [Data Center Retail Sales and Use Tax (DCRSUT) exemption](https://www.vedp.org/incentive/data-center-retail-sales-use-tax-exemption), which has been in effect since July 1, 2010, and is currently set to expire in 2035 for most operators. Operators who entered qualifying memoranda of understanding (MOUs) with VEDP on or after January 1, 2023 may have extensions to 2040 (requiring $35 billion in new capital investment and 1,000 new jobs) or 2050 (requiring $100 billion and 2,500 new jobs), so the effective expiration date varies by operator.

By fiscal year 2024, the DCRSUT exemption cost Virginia approximately $1 billion annually — up from $685 million in FY2023 — and between fiscal years 2021 and 2024 the cumulative tax benefit to the industry totaled $2.73 billion, accounting for approximately 53% of the state's total economic incentive spending across all industries during that period, per [JLARC's own analysis](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/reports/Rpt598-2.pdf).

### The 2024 Legislative Session: Deferral Pending Study

During the 2024 Virginia General Assembly session, legislators introduced a significant number of bills targeting the data center industry — addressing energy costs imposed on non-data-center ratepayers, noise mitigation near residential areas, siting restrictions, and environmental compliance. Rather than enacting these bills directly, the General Assembly largely deferred action, directing JLARC to conduct a comprehensive study and report back with findings before the 2025 session. [Senate Joint Resolution 17](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/resolutions/2024_Data%20centers_JLARC.pdf) formally authorized the study. According to the [Piedmont Environmental Council's legislative tracking](https://www.pecva.org/work/energy-work/data-center-legislation-in-the-2024-virginia-general-assembly/), the principal remaining data center bill from the 2024 session was HB 338, allowing localities to conduct site assessments for water usage and carbon emission impacts.

### Prior JLARC Study

JLARC had previously reviewed data center incentives in a narrower 2019 study ([Data Center and Manufacturing Incentives](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/presentations/RptPres518.pdf), Commission Briefing dated June 2019), but the 2024 study directed by SJR 17 was substantially broader in scope — covering not just tax incentives but also energy infrastructure impacts, land use and zoning, environmental and community effects, and long-term growth projections.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### The May 21, 2024 Initial Public Discussion

The JLARC kickoff session on May 21, 2024 was a scoping exercise, not a findings presentation. According to [McGuireWoods' summary](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2024/5/with-jlarc-study-virginia-continues-review-of-data-center-industry/), the Commission's opening discussion addressed five broad topic clusters that would frame the study:

1. **Economic development benefits** — the industry's contributions to GDP, employment, state and local revenues, and the continued relevance of tax incentives as a competitive tool against other states
2. **Taxation** — the structure, cost, and potential reform of the DCRSUT exemption; whether the exemption was properly calibrated against economic benefits received
3. **Energy and power considerations** — load growth driven by data centers, the adequacy of Virginia's grid, and the relationship between data center expansion and utility rate increases for all customers
4. **Noise and visual impact mitigation** — community impacts from cooling equipment and emergency diesel generators, and the adequacy of existing noise ordinances and local authority to address them
5. **Locational considerations** — siting of data centers relative to residential areas, historic resources, natural landscapes, and the scope of local zoning authority

### Study Methodology

Over the course of the study (May through December 2024), JLARC staff conducted more than 300 interviews across a wide array of stakeholders, including data center operators and hyperscalers, Dominion Energy and other utilities, county governments, state agencies, environmental organizations, and residents living near data center facilities. JLARC also commissioned two independent technical analyses: an energy grid modeling study from energy consulting firm E3, and an economic impact study. This mixed-methods approach was designed to provide the General Assembly with data-driven recommendations rather than purely policy-driven opinions.

### Study Resolution Scope

The [SJR 17 study resolution](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/resolutions/2024_Data%20centers_JLARC.pdf) directed JLARC to examine:

- Factors affecting future growth of the data center industry in Virginia
- Impacts on natural, historic, and cultural resources
- Impacts on energy demand and supply, including infrastructure investment and customer cost allocation
- Effects on local revenue (property taxes, local business taxes)
- Effects on residents (noise, visual impact, community character)
- State and local policies and incentives, including the DCRSUT exemption

### Key Findings of the December 2024 Final Report

Although the McGuireWoods June 2024 alert was written at the study's launch rather than its conclusion, the final report — released December 9, 2024 — addressed all five scoping areas in depth. Headline findings included:

- **Energy demand**: E3's commissioned model found that unconstrained data center energy demand could double Virginia's total electricity consumption within ten years. A separate UVA Weldon Cooper Center analysis projected demand could triple under fully unconstrained growth scenarios. ([JLARC Report 598](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/reports/Rpt598-2.pdf))
- **Rate impacts**: Data center load growth will require significant new generation and transmission investment, costs that will be distributed across all Virginia electricity customers through rate increases ([JLARC Commission Briefing, December 9, 2024](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/presentations/Rpt598Pres-1.pdf))
- **Residential proximity**: Nearly one-third of Virginia data centers are within 200 feet of residential homes; 70% are within 500 feet ([WRIC reporting](https://www.wric.com/news/taking-action/jlarc-report-data-centers-virginia/))
- **Tax exemption cost**: The DCRSUT exemption cost the state approximately $1 billion in FY2024, representing 79% of Virginia's total economic incentive spending for that year ([Virginia Business](https://virginiabusiness.com/virginia-data-centers-tax-exemption-2-7-billion/))
- **Three policy paths**: JLARC presented the General Assembly with options to extend, allow to expire, or condition the DCRSUT exemption

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### For Data Center Operators and Developers

The JLARC study opened a structured governmental review of whether Virginia's regulatory and fiscal posture toward the data center industry remained appropriate. Companies operating or planning Virginia data centers faced heightened scrutiny beginning with the May 2024 kickoff and ongoing through the 2025 and 2026 legislative sessions that followed the December report. Key pressure points include:

- **Tax exemption uncertainty**: The DCRSUT exemption — the single most valuable state incentive for the industry — was formally put under legislative review
- **Siting and noise**: The scoping of noise and locational topics signaled likely new standards for proximity to residential areas
- **Energy obligations**: The study explicitly examined whether data centers bear their fair share of the infrastructure costs they drive

### For Local Governments

Northern Virginia counties — particularly Loudoun, Fairfax, Prince William, and Fauquier — were direct stakeholders in the study. The study examined whether current local zoning authority was adequate to address community impacts, potentially leading to new state-level mandates or guidance on what localities could require of data center developers.

### For Utilities

Dominion Energy, as Virginia's primary electric utility, faced scrutiny of how it plans for and recovers the costs of serving large industrial loads. The study's energy modeling directly addressed how much new generation capacity would be required and who would pay for it.

### Legislative Aftermath

The study's December 2024 release directly shaped Virginia's 2025 and 2026 General Assembly sessions. In 2026, the legislature considered 61 data center bills and passed 15, touching generator emissions standards, demand flexibility programs, and siting requirements. The sales and use tax exemption itself remained contested as of early 2026, with its future structure unresolved between chambers. ([Virginia Mercury, March 2026](https://virginiamercury.com/2026/03/17/data-center-bills-dominated-this-years-general-assembly-heres-what-passed/))

## Action Items

- **Monitor JLARC study outputs**: The December 2024 final report is now available. Review [Report 598](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/reports/Rpt598-2.pdf) and the [recommendations summary](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/summary/Rpt598Rec.pdf) in full
- **Track DCRSUT exemption legislation**: Any reform or extension of the sales and use tax exemption in 2025 or 2026 directly affects the financial model for Virginia data center investment; monitor bill introductions closely
- **Evaluate generator compliance posture**: JLARC recommendations on Tier IV generator standards have advanced into legislation; assess whether current backup generation equipment meets emerging requirements
- **Engage in stakeholder processes**: JLARC's methodology included stakeholder interviews; future state rulemakings (at DEQ and the State Corporation Commission) will likely include comment periods where operators and affected communities can participate
- **Coordinate with local governments**: Given the study's emphasis on local land use authority and community impacts, data center operators in Virginia should proactively engage with county governments on noise mitigation and siting standards before regulatory requirements are codified
