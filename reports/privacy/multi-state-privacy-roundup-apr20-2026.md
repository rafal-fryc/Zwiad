# State Privacy Law Update: Kentucky and Virginia Finalize Amendments, Nebraska Enacts Chatbot Law (Week of April 20, 2026)

**Jurisdiction:** Multi-state (Kentucky, Virginia, Nebraska, Tennessee, Oklahoma) | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-22

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

The week ending April 20, 2026 saw the final legislative actions of the 2026 session for several state legislatures, producing a cluster of enacted privacy and AI laws. [Troutman Pepper Locke's April 20, 2026 state privacy tracker](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/proposed-state-privacy-law-update-april-1018509/) reported that Alabama became the twenty-first state to enact a comprehensive consumer data privacy law (covered separately in this knowledge base), while Kentucky and Virginia finalized amendments to their existing privacy statutes. Simultaneously, Nebraska enacted the [Conversational AI Safety Act](https://legiscan.com/NE/bill/LB525/2025) as part of a broader omnibus bill, becoming the fourth state in 2026 to enact a chatbot-specific law. The developments reflect continued momentum in state-level privacy and AI legislation, with multiple additional states (Tennessee, Oklahoma) advancing related legislation.

**Note on scope:** Kentucky [HB 692](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26rs/hb692.html) and Virginia [SB 338](https://lis.virginia.gov/bill-details/20261/SB338) are each the subject of dedicated reports in this knowledge base. This roundup memo provides comparative context for the April 20, 2026 legislative cycle and covers Nebraska LB 525 and broader state landscape developments not addressed elsewhere.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The spring 2026 state legislative season saw an unusually concentrated period of privacy enactments, as legislatures across the country approached their sine die adjournment deadlines. Key session-end dates relevant to this report's period include Kentucky (April 15), Alabama (April 16), Nebraska (April 17), and Tennessee (April 24).

By April 2026, the U.S. had seen the enactment of comprehensive consumer data privacy statutes in at least [20 states](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/2/4/all-of-the-comprehensive-privacy-laws-that-take-effect-in-2026), with Indiana, Kentucky, and Rhode Island laws taking effect January 1, 2026. This legislative activity builds on years of state-led privacy policymaking in the absence of federal comprehensive privacy legislation.

States were also acting to layer targeted data-category-specific restrictions on top of existing omnibus frameworks — a pattern exemplified by Virginia's geolocation ban and Kentucky's ACR classification. Simultaneously, AI-specific regulation of conversational AI ("chatbot") platforms emerged as a distinct legislative category in 2026, with at least four states enacting such laws by the date of this report.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Kentucky HB 692 — ACR Data as Sensitive Data

Governor Andy Beshear signed [Kentucky HB 692](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26rs/hb692.html) on April 13, 2026. The bill amends KRS 367.3611, the Kentucky Consumer Data Protection Act (KCDPA), to:

- Define **"automatic content recognition" (ACR)** as technology embedded in internet-connected smart TVs or monitors that identifies displayed content in real time by analyzing audio or video fingerprints, including broadcast, cable, satellite, streaming, and external inputs, using digital fingerprinting or watermark detection techniques.
- Define **"smart monitor"** as a digital display device with integrated hardware and software enabling independent internet connectivity, app execution, and media streaming.
- Classify ACR data as **sensitive data** under the KCDPA, requiring consumer consent before a controller may collect it — aligning ACR data with existing sensitive categories such as health information, biometric data, and precise geolocation data.

HB 692 passed the Kentucky House of Representatives [unanimously on March 16, 2026](https://nationaltoday.com/us/ky/frankfort/news/2026/03/17/kentucky-house-passes-bill-to-protect-smart-tv-data-privacy/), and passed the Senate 38-0. The effective date is **July 1, 2027**, providing manufacturers and streaming-service operators approximately fourteen months to implement consent mechanisms for ACR data collection. See the dedicated report in this knowledge base for full compliance analysis.

### Virginia SB 338 — Outright Ban on Sale of Precise Geolocation Data

Governor Abigail Spanberger signed [Virginia SB 338](https://lis.virginia.gov/bill-details/20261/SB338) on April 13, 2026, amending § 59.1-578 of the Code of Virginia (the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act, VCDPA). The amendment:

- **Prohibits controllers from selling or offering for sale** a consumer's precise geolocation data — replacing the VCDPA's prior consent-based treatment of geolocation as sensitive data with an outright ban on commercialization.
- Defines the scope of "precise geolocation data" as data that can identify a consumer or device within **1,750 feet**.
- Adds new **child-specific restrictions**: controllers may not collect precise geolocation data from a known child unless reasonably necessary to provide a service, must signal collection to the child throughout the collection period, and must obtain parental consent in accordance with COPPA.
- **Effective date: July 1, 2026** — one of the shortest implementation windows in recent state privacy legislation.

Virginia joins [Oregon and Maryland](https://natlawreview.com/article/virginia-expands-vcdpa-ban-sale-precise-consumer-geolocation-data) in imposing an outright ban on geolocation data sales, signaling a potential shift from consent-based to prohibition-based approaches for this data category. The bill passed the Virginia Senate 39-0 and the House of Delegates unanimously. See the dedicated report in this knowledge base for full compliance analysis.

### Nebraska LB 525 — Agricultural Data Privacy Act and Conversational AI Safety Act [MEDIUM confidence]

Governor Jim Pillen signed [Nebraska LB 525](https://legiscan.com/NE/bill/LB525/2025) on April 14, 2026. The omnibus bill contains two distinct components:

**Agricultural Data Privacy Act:** The act gives farmers sole control over their agronomic, climate, weather, land, livestock, management, and sustainability data, prohibiting the sale of this raw data by any party other than the producer. Nebraska is [described as the first state to enact this type of agricultural data privacy law](https://nationaltoday.com/us/ne/north-platte/news/2026/03/30/nebraska-passes-landmark-agricultural-data-privacy-law/).

**Conversational AI Safety Act:** Nebraska became the fourth state in 2026 to enact chatbot-specific regulation. The law requires AI chatbot operators to:
- Disclose to minors that they are interacting with AI rather than a human.
- Institute measures to prevent the AI service from producing sexually explicit content or statements simulating romantic or sexual innuendo.
- Adopt a protocol for responding to user prompts regarding suicidal thoughts or self-harm, including referring users to crisis service providers such as suicide hotlines.
- Refrain from representing the service as designed to provide professional mental or behavioral health care.

Both components of LB 525 carry an **effective date of July 1, 2027**.

Official text of LB 525 could not be retrieved from the Nebraska Legislature website at time of writing. The expected URL for manual verification is `https://nebraskalegislature.gov/bills/view_bill.php?DocumentID=LB525`. The factual summary above is drawn from secondary reporting by [Frankfort Today/NationalToday](https://nationaltoday.com/us/ne/north-platte/news/2026/03/30/nebraska-passes-landmark-agricultural-data-privacy-law/) and Troutman Pepper Locke's tracker summary; the relevant provisions have been independently corroborated by the [State of Surveillance tracker](https://stateofsurveillance.org) and [LegiScan's bill record](https://legiscan.com/NE/bill/LB525/2025).

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

**Organizations affected by the Kentucky and Virginia amendments** must layer new compliance obligations on top of existing KCDPA and VCDPA programs:

- **Smart TV manufacturers and ACR technology vendors** operating under the KCDPA will need to implement consent workflows for ACR data collection by July 1, 2027. This requires identifying where ACR is currently active without explicit consumer opt-in and retrofitting consent mechanisms.
- **Data brokers, location intelligence firms, and adtech companies** that monetize precise geolocation data under the VCDPA have until July 1, 2026 — a compressed window — to cease selling or offering for sale precise geolocation datasets of Virginia consumers or devices. Downstream purchasers of such datasets also face direct compliance obligations.
- **Children's service operators** subject to the VCDPA face the most immediate risk: the combination of the geolocation ban for known children and COPPA-consent requirements applies as of July 1, 2026 and should be treated as a hard deadline.

**Organizations affected by Nebraska LB 525:**
- **Agricultural data processors** (precision agriculture platforms, farm management software vendors) will need to assess whether they handle covered agronomic or sustainability data of Nebraska producers and restructure data-sharing practices accordingly.
- **AI chatbot developers** deploying consumer-facing conversational AI must assess whether their platforms are accessible to minors in Nebraska and implement the mandatory disclosure, content, and crisis-response protocols by July 1, 2027.

**The broader legislative trend** — outright bans on data commercialization rather than consent-based permissions, and AI-specific overlay legislation — signals that compliance programs calibrated only to Virginia's original VCDPA framework or foundational KCDPA structure will require ongoing adjustment as amendments layer new restrictions.

## Action Items

- **Geolocation data (Virginia):** Audit all data pipelines that involve the sale or transfer of precise location data (within 1,750-foot precision) for Virginia consumers or devices. Implement cessation of any commercial sales by June 30, 2026.
- **VCDPA child geolocation:** Review products and services where precise geolocation data is collected from known child users in Virginia; implement COPPA-compliant parental consent and collection-notification requirements by July 1, 2026.
- **Smart TV / ACR (Kentucky):** Map all ACR data collection touchpoints in products subject to KCDPA; design consent UI/UX workflows and prepare for July 1, 2027 implementation.
- **Nebraska chatbot operators:** If any consumer-facing conversational AI product may be accessed by minors in Nebraska, begin gap analysis of disclosure, content-moderation, and crisis-response protocols against LB 525 requirements; target implementation by July 1, 2027.
- **Agricultural data (Nebraska):** Assess whether precision-agriculture or farm-data platforms collect covered data from Nebraska producers; engage legal counsel on the Agricultural Data Privacy Act's data-sale restrictions.
- **Monitor Tennessee and Oklahoma:** Track session outcomes for Tennessee SB 1700, SB 837, and Oklahoma SB 1521, HB 3544, and HB 3546 as these chambers approach adjournment. Enactment could add additional state obligations within the same compliance cycle.
