# Vermont Overhauled Money Transmission Law Adds Virtual Currency Licensing and Kiosk Rules (Act 110 / H.659)

**Jurisdiction:** Vermont | **Category:** Privacy / Financial Regulation | **Date:** 2024-08-19

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Effective July 1, 2024, Vermont enacted [Act 110 (H.659)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2024/H.659), a comprehensive overhaul of its money transmission law that adopts the [Conference of State Bank Supervisors (CSBS) Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA)](https://www.csbs.org/csbs-money-transmission-modernization-act-mtma) in full. Vermont joins approximately half of U.S. states that have enacted some or all of the MTMA framework. The legislation creates an entirely new licensing regime for "virtual-currency business activity" under [8 V.S.A. Chapter 79](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/fullchapter/08/079), covering exchanges, custodians, wallet providers, and sellers of virtual currency. Virtual currency kiosk operators face a moratorium prohibiting new deployments until July 1, 2026, daily transaction caps of $1,000 per customer, and fee limits of the greater of $5 or 3% per transaction. Existing money transmission licensees had a transition deadline of July 1, 2025 to update authorized delegate contracts to comply with the new framework.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The MTMA Model Law

The [Conference of State Bank Supervisors](https://www.csbs.org/csbs-money-transmission-modernization-act-mtma) developed the Money Transmission Modernization Act as a model statute intended to harmonize the patchwork of state money transmission licensing laws across the country. Prior to the MTMA, the regulatory landscape varied significantly by state, creating compliance burdens for multi-state money transmitters and virtual currency businesses that must hold licenses in each jurisdiction where they operate.

The MTMA addresses core licensing elements including: standardized definitions of covered activity, clearer exemptions, uniform change-of-control requirements, common permissible investment categories, and enhanced authorized delegate management rules. The model law was designed to reduce the administrative burden for the money transmission industry, which includes traditional payment processors, remittance companies, and virtual currency-related businesses.

As of the August 2024 enactment of Vermont's legislation, [nine states had passed MTMA-based legislation in 2024 alone](https://www.csbs.org/state-pending-enacted-mtma-legislation) — Maryland, South Dakota, Wisconsin, Kansas, Maine, Vermont, South Carolina, Missouri, and Connecticut — [according to CSBS tracking](https://www.csbs.org/sites/default/files/external-link-files/MTMA%20legislative%20update%205-15-2024.pdf). Vermont's adoption of a "full MTMA" (incorporating the entire model act, not just portions) placed it among the more comprehensive adoptions.

### Vermont's Prior Regulatory Framework

Vermont's pre-Act 110 money transmission law was established under the original Chapter 79 framework, which addressed traditional money services but did not explicitly regulate virtual currency businesses. Virtual currency operators in Vermont had historically existed in a regulatory gray area, either relying on informal guidance or arguing that their activities fell outside the definition of money transmission under the older statute.

Vermont's [Department of Financial Regulation (DFR)](https://dfr.vermont.gov/) had issued a [Virtual Currency Kiosk Report pursuant to 8 V.S.A. § 2577(g)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/assets/Legislative-Reports/Virtual-Currency-Kiosk-Report-Pursuant-to-8-V.S.A.-2577g.pdf) as part of the legislative process, which informed the targeted consumer protection provisions in the new law.

Vermont's legislature had separately considered and ultimately vetoed a comprehensive consumer data privacy bill (H.121, the Vermont Data Privacy Act) in 2024, signaling a legislative environment that was active on digital economy regulation even where approaches diverged.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Core MTMA Provisions Adopted

Vermont's Act 110 amended [8 V.S.A. Chapter 79](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/chapter/08/079) across multiple subchapters. The [Act Summary (ACT110)](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/Docs/ACTS/ACT110/ACT110%20Act%20Summary.pdf) identifies the law as an act relating to banking, insurance, and securities that brings Vermont's money services framework in line with the CSBS model.

Key MTMA elements adopted by Vermont include:

- **Definitions of regulated activity**: Modernized and expanded definitions that explicitly encompass virtual currency operations
- **Standardized exemptions**: Harmonized exemptions for certain categories of entities that would otherwise require a license
- **Change of control requirements**: Procedures for obtaining regulatory approval when a licensee's ownership or control structure changes
- **Permissible investments**: Updated rules on what assets licensees may hold to back outstanding payment obligations
- **Receipting and recordkeeping**: Enhanced transaction documentation requirements

Notably, Vermont's adoption deviates from the full MTMA template in at least one area: it adopts a broader definition of individuals who may be deemed "in control" of a licensee and thus subject to background checks, and it does not incorporate a key Model Act provision that exempts from the change-of-control approval process an internal reorganization of a licensee that does not change its ultimate ownership. [According to Cooley LLP analysis](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-08-20-us-states-adopt-model-money-transmission-act-but-harmonization-remains-elusive), these state-level modifications illustrate why full national harmonization remains elusive even among MTMA-adopting states.

### New Virtual Currency Licensing Subchapter

The most significant innovation in Act 110 is the creation of an entirely new subchapter under Chapter 79 specifically addressing virtual currency businesses. Under [8 V.S.A. § 2576](https://law.justia.com/codes/vermont/title-8/chapter-79/section-2576/), any person engaged in "virtual-currency business activity" in Vermont must obtain a money transmission license unless a specific exemption applies.

"Virtual-currency business activity" is defined broadly to include:

- Exchanging virtual currency for fiat currency or other virtual currency
- Storing or administering virtual currency
- Buying or selling virtual currency as a service to the public
- Receiving virtual currency or control of virtual currency on behalf of customers
- Certain virtual currency issuance activities

This captures a wide range of industry participants: cryptocurrency exchanges (both centralized and potentially some decentralized structures), digital custodians, wallet service providers, and operators of virtual currency kiosks (cryptocurrency ATMs).

The law expressly requires licensees conducting virtual currency business activity to maintain records of all such activity for at least five years, including individual transaction records, aggregate transaction data, bank statements, and business-call reports required by the DFR. Fraud prohibitions applicable to money transmitters are extended to virtual currency businesses, making it unlawful to employ any device, scheme, or artifice to defraud or make untrue statements of material fact in connection with virtual currency activity.

### Virtual Currency Kiosk Rules (8 V.S.A. § 2577)

[8 V.S.A. § 2577](https://law.justia.com/codes/vermont/title-8/chapter-79/section-2577/) establishes a dedicated regulatory scheme for virtual currency kiosk operators (cryptocurrency ATMs) that is among the most consumer-protective in the country. Key provisions include:

**Licensing requirement**: All kiosk operators must obtain and maintain a Vermont money transmission license and must register each physical kiosk with the DFR and receive approval before commencing operations.

**New deployment moratorium**: Virtual currency kiosks are prohibited from operating in Vermont prior to July 1, 2026. The moratorium does not apply to kiosks that were duly licensed and operational in Vermont on or before June 30, 2024 — creating a protected class of pre-existing operators.

**Transaction limits**: Cash purchases of virtual currency through a kiosk are capped at $1,000 per customer per day.

**Fee caps**: Fees charged per kiosk transaction are limited to the greater of $5 or 3% of the USD-equivalent value of the virtual currency involved in the transaction.

**Consumer disclosures**: Kiosk operators must display conspicuous disclosures at each kiosk providing information about the business, applicable fees, and transaction receipts.

The kiosk-specific rules reflect a legislative finding that virtual currency ATMs have been disproportionately used as instruments of fraud targeting elderly and vulnerable consumers. Vermont's DFR noted this concern in the [Virtual Currency Kiosk Report](https://legislature.vermont.gov/assets/Legislative-Reports/Virtual-Currency-Kiosk-Report-Pursuant-to-8-V.S.A.-2577g.pdf) that informed the legislative drafting.

### Transition Provisions

Act 110 includes a transition framework for entities already licensed under the prior subchapter 3 of Chapter 79:

- Such licensees and their authorized delegates are not subject to new or different requirements until July 1, 2025.
- By July 1, 2025, all licensees must amend their authorized delegate written contracts to comply with the requirements in the new [8 V.S.A. § 2525](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/fullchapter/08/079), provided that the licensee and delegate otherwise operate in full compliance with the updated chapter.

This one-year transition window gave established money transmitters time to audit and update their compliance programs, delegate agreements, and internal policies before the full requirements became applicable.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Businesses

The following categories of businesses face material compliance obligations under Act 110:

**Virtual currency exchanges and platforms**: Any exchange offering services to Vermont residents — including major platforms like Coinbase, Kraken, and Gemini — must hold or confirm they hold a Vermont money transmission license under the updated requirements. Many large exchanges already held pre-Act 110 Vermont licenses, but the new virtual currency subchapter may impose additional conditions.

**Digital custodians and wallet providers**: Custodial wallet services that hold virtual currency on behalf of customers are explicitly covered by the "storing or administering virtual currency" prong of the definition. Non-custodial wallets (where the user controls private keys) are generally not covered, but businesses operating any form of custodial arrangement should analyze their exposure.

**Virtual currency kiosk operators**: Operators of cryptocurrency ATMs in Vermont face the strictest requirements. Those without existing operational kiosks as of June 30, 2024, cannot deploy new machines until July 1, 2026. Those already operating must comply with transaction caps, fee limits, and disclosure rules immediately.

**Payment processors and traditional money transmitters**: Businesses already licensed under the prior framework had until July 1, 2025 to update authorized delegate contracts and come into full compliance with the MTMA provisions adopted by Act 110.

### Enforcement Outlook

Vermont's DFR is the primary regulator with authority to examine licensees, issue guidance, and take enforcement action under Chapter 79. The DFR has historically been an active regulator in the money services space, and the expanded licensing requirements provide it with jurisdiction over categories of virtual currency businesses that previously operated without state oversight in Vermont.

Given the consumer protection focus of the kiosk provisions, enforcement attention is likely to focus initially on unlicensed kiosk operators and violations of transaction limits and fee caps. The one-year transition for existing licensees (with a July 1, 2025 deadline) means DFR enforcement on authorized delegate compliance issues was likely deferred until mid-2025.

### National Significance

Vermont's adoption adds to the growing list of states that have incorporated the MTMA framework. According to [Cooley analysis](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2024/2024-08-20-us-states-adopt-model-money-transmission-act-but-harmonization-remains-elusive), while harmonization remains elusive due to state-level variations, the adoption wave reduces fragmentation. For multi-state money transmitters and virtual currency businesses, Vermont's MTMA adoption may simplify certain compliance elements compared to the prior framework. However, the virtual currency kiosk moratorium and the Vermont-specific consumer protection rules represent affirmative new compliance obligations that have no direct analog in many other states.

## Action Items

- **Virtual currency kiosk operators**: Confirm whether your kiosks qualify for the pre-July 1, 2026 moratorium exemption by verifying they were duly licensed and operational in Vermont on or before June 30, 2024. If not, suspend Vermont kiosk operations until July 1, 2026.
- **All virtual currency businesses**: Assess whether operations fall within "virtual-currency business activity" under 8 V.S.A. § 2576 and apply for a Vermont money transmission license if not already licensed. Contact Vermont DFR for license application requirements.
- **Existing money transmission licensees**: Confirm that authorized delegate written contracts were updated to comply with 8 V.S.A. § 2525 by the July 1, 2025 deadline. If the deadline was missed, prioritize remediation and engage with DFR.
- **All new kiosk deployments**: Ensure each kiosk is registered with DFR and approved prior to operation; build in transaction limit controls ($1,000/day per customer in cash) and fee caps (greater of $5 or 3%) into kiosk software.
- **Multi-state operators**: Consult the [CSBS MTMA legislative tracker](https://www.csbs.org/state-pending-enacted-mtma-legislation) to monitor which other states have adopted the MTMA and assess whether Vermont's transition approach sets a useful compliance template.
- **Monitor moratorium expiration**: Track the July 1, 2026 end date of the new kiosk deployment moratorium for planning purposes if expansion into Vermont is anticipated.
