# Treasury Secretary Signals Executive Order Requiring Banks to Collect Consumer Citizenship Data

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy / Financial Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-15

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed on April 14–15, 2026, that a Trump administration executive order requiring U.S. banks and credit unions to collect citizenship documentation from customers is "in process." The order would mandate that financial institutions request passports, birth certificates, or other proof of citizenship — documents not currently required under existing Customer Identification Program (CIP) rules — and would represent a significant expansion of the data banks must collect about their customers. The proposal raises layered concerns: it conflicts with existing financial privacy frameworks, would impose substantial compliance costs on covered institutions, and could expose collected citizenship data to immigration enforcement agencies.

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence]

- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent stated the executive order is "in process" during remarks on April 14, 2026, per [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-14/bessent-says-bank-citizenship-verification-order-is-in-process) and [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/administration/5830266-trump-executive-order-banking-system-citizenship/).
- The draft would require banks to collect citizenship documents — passports or birth certificates — at account opening, going beyond the name, date of birth, address, and Social Security number/ITIN already required under [Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) Customer Identification Program rules](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/cfrpart103), per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/15/banks-citizenship-data-collection-customer-accounts.html).
- REAL ID credentials would not satisfy the proposed requirement because they do not establish citizenship, only identity, per [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/04/16/trump-executive-order-bank-citizenship-information-bessent-immigration/).
- Senator Tom Cotton (R-AR) introduced the "Know Your American Customer Act" in March 2026, a companion legislative measure that would require FDIC- and NCUA-insured institutions to verify citizenship or lawful immigration status of new customers and make it a federal crime for undocumented individuals to open or maintain an account, per [Cotton's press release](https://www.cotton.senate.gov/news/press-releases/cotton-introduces-bill-to-ban-illegal-migrants-from-american-banking-system).
- The American Action Forum estimates a citizenship verification requirement would add 33.1–73.3 million hours of industry paperwork and cost banks $2.6 billion–$5.6 billion, per [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/04/16/trump-executive-order-bank-citizenship-information-bessent-immigration/).
- The U.S. banking industry has pushed back, with industry representatives calling the proposed requirements "impractical and costly," per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/15/banks-citizenship-data-collection-customer-accounts.html).
- Credit union trade associations have also raised concerns: America's Credit Unions president/CEO Scott Simpson raised questions about the compliance burden the proposed order would impose on credit unions, per [America's Credit Unions](https://www.americascreditunions.org/news-media/news/simpson-raises-concerns-about-potential-burdens-citizenship-eo).
- The order is part of a broader pattern of using financial data infrastructure for immigration enforcement. The administration previously issued an executive order to share IRS taxpayer data with immigration enforcement agencies; a federal judge blocked that order but ICE subsequently executed a Memorandum of Understanding with the IRS for a data-sharing framework, per [oltarsh.com](https://www.oltarsh.com/immigrant-bank-account-rights-2026/).
- Advocacy groups and legal experts have signaled lawsuits are likely, with arguments focusing on Privacy Act violations and Treasury's statutory authority, per [oltarsh.com](https://www.oltarsh.com/immigrant-bank-account-rights-2026/).
- An estimated millions of U.S. citizens do not hold passports and may lack other qualifying citizenship documents, creating the risk that legal residents and U.S. citizens — not only undocumented individuals — would face barriers to banking access, per [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/04/16/trump-executive-order-bank-citizenship-information-bessent-immigration/).

## Legal Framework and Privacy Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

### Existing Customer Identification Program Requirements

Current CIP requirements under the [Bank Secrecy Act, 31 U.S.C. § 5318(l)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title31-section5318&num=0&edition=prelim), and implementing regulations at 31 C.F.R. § 1020.220 (for banks) require financial institutions to collect name, date of birth, address, and an identification number (Social Security number or, for non-U.S. persons, an ITIN or passport number). Citizenship is not a required data element. The proposed executive order would amend or supplement these requirements by adding citizenship documentation as a mandatory collection element.

Official text of the proposed executive order could not be retrieved at time of writing. The order has not yet been signed or published in the Federal Register. The expected publication point upon signing would be the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov) and the [White House briefing room](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/). **This section is tagged LOW confidence for the specific order provisions pending official text.**

### Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) Considerations [MEDIUM confidence]

Title V of [GLBA, 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801–6809](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/gramm-leach-bliley-act), governs how financial institutions handle "nonpublic personal information" (NPI). If banks are required to collect citizenship documentation, that documentation — including copies of passports or birth certificates — would constitute NPI subject to GLBA's notice and opt-out requirements. Banks would face obligations to:

- Disclose to customers how citizenship data is collected, used, and shared, in annual and initial privacy notices under 16 C.F.R. Part 313 (FTC Safeguards Rule) and equivalent banking agency rules.
- Comply with the opt-out right for sharing NPI with nonaffiliated third parties — though the law enforcement exception at 15 U.S.C. § 6802(e)(8) would likely permit sharing with government agencies pursuant to legal process.
- Secure citizenship documents under Safeguards Rule information security requirements, 16 C.F.R. Part 314.

A concurrent House discussion draft by Rep. Bill Huizenga would modernize and potentially preempt state financial privacy laws governing NPI — this could affect how state-level financial privacy protections apply to citizenship data collected under any new federal mandate. See the [related report on the Huizenga GLBA discussion draft](reports/privacy/financial-privacy/glba-reform-huizenga-discussion-draft-2026-04-12.md).

### Right to Financial Privacy Act [MEDIUM confidence]

The [Right to Financial Privacy Act (RFPA), 12 U.S.C. §§ 3401–3422](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title12-section3401&num=0&edition=prelim), restricts the federal government's ability to access financial records held by financial institutions without customer notice and an opportunity to challenge access, subject to exceptions for national security, law enforcement, and regulatory examinations. If citizenship data collected by banks is subsequently requested by ICE or other immigration enforcement agencies, the RFPA would govern the access process — but the broad law enforcement exceptions could facilitate access without customer notice in many enforcement contexts.

### Fourth Amendment Considerations [MEDIUM confidence]

Under the third-party doctrine as articulated in [United States v. Miller, 425 U.S. 435 (1976)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/425/435/), financial records voluntarily conveyed to a bank generally lack Fourth Amendment protection. However, compelled collection of citizenship documents — documents that may reveal national origin, religion (in some passport photo contexts), and immigration history — at the behest of the government could raise novel constitutional questions about whether the government, by mandating collection of sensitive personal data through a private intermediary, has effectively effected a warrantless seizure of that data. Courts have not definitively addressed this configuration.

### Financial Exclusion Risk [MEDIUM confidence]

Civil liberties advocates and banking access experts have flagged that requiring citizenship documentation at account opening — or retroactively for existing accountholders — could effectively exclude large populations from the banking system. According to advocacy groups cited by [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/04/16/trump-executive-order-bank-citizenship-information-bessent-immigration/), millions of U.S. citizens lack ready access to passports or birth certificates. For undocumented individuals currently holding accounts via ITIN-based identification, the order would likely force account closure, pushing them toward unbanked cash-only status, which advocates note is associated with reduced economic mobility and increased vulnerability to crime. Eric Rodriguez of UNIDOS, a Latino civil rights organization, stated that "the government may go after that and use it abusively to track down and harass people in communities," per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/15/banks-citizenship-data-collection-customer-accounts.html).

## Compliance Considerations for Financial Institutions [MEDIUM confidence]

Financial institutions should monitor this development closely but need not take immediate compliance action as no executive order has been signed. When — and if — the order is signed, key compliance questions will include:

1. **Scope of coverage**: Whether the order applies only to new account openings or also requires retroactive verification of existing account holders. (The Cotton bill grandfathers existing customers; the executive order's treatment of existing accounts is unknown pending release of official text.)
2. **Acceptable documentation**: The draft reportedly requires passports or birth certificates, not REAL ID. Institutions will need updated CIP policies and procedures specifying acceptable document types.
3. **Data security**: Copies of passports and birth certificates are highly sensitive identity documents. Existing Safeguards Rule compliance programs may need updating to address document storage, access controls, and retention/destruction policies for citizenship documents.
4. **GLBA privacy notice updates**: Privacy notices will require amendment to describe the new data element, its purpose, and sharing practices.
5. **Retroactive verification costs**: If the order requires verification of existing customers, institutions must assess the operational capacity to re-verify potentially millions of existing accountholders.
6. **Legal challenge timeline**: Given the industry opposition and anticipated litigation, institutions should preserve records of compliance costs and monitor court proceedings before incurring major system investments.

## Action Items

- **Monitor**: Track Treasury and White House announcements for the executive order's signing date and official text. Subscribe to [Federal Register alerts](https://www.federalregister.gov/sign-in) for FinCEN and Treasury rulemaking in the Bank Secrecy Act program area.
- **Engage trade associations**: Coordinate with ABA, CUNA, NAFCU, and other trade groups on comment letters and lobbying if the order enters a notice-and-comment period (executive orders may not, but implementing FinCEN regulations likely would).
- **Legal review**: Engage outside counsel to assess the executive order's interaction with GLBA, RFPA, BSA/CIP requirements, and state law upon order publication.
- **Gap analysis**: Identify whether current CIP systems and data infrastructure can accommodate passport/birth certificate storage and what modifications would be needed.
- **Cost documentation**: Begin documenting the projected compliance costs now, both to inform internal business planning and to support industry comment processes.
- **Privacy notice inventory**: Identify all GLBA privacy notices that would require amendment and establish a timeline for updates upon order finalization.
- **Watch litigation**: ACLU, National Immigration Law Center, and other advocacy groups are expected to challenge the order in federal court. An injunction could delay or block implementation.
