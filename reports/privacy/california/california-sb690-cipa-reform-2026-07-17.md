# California Legislature Advances SB 690 to Narrow CIPA Wiretapping Litigation

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-01 (committee action); ongoing legislative session

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

California Senate Bill 690 (SB 690, introduced by Senator Anna Caballero), a proposal to rein in the explosion of website-tracking lawsuits filed under the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA), passed the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection unanimously on [July 1, 2026](https://www.cyberadviserblog.com/2026/07/california-assembly-committee-advances-cipa-reform/) and was referred to the Assembly Committee on Appropriations. <!-- verified: independently corroborated by WebSearch (14-0 committee vote, referred to Appropriations) --> The bill has been substantially narrowed since its 2025 introduction: rather than the broad "commercial business purpose" exemption originally proposed for all of CIPA, the amended version applies only to [Penal Code Sections 638.50 and 638.51](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB690) (California's pen register and trap-and-trace statute), strips private plaintiffs of the right to sue under those sections for conduct occurring on websites, apps, or mobile applications, and shifts enforcement exclusively to the California Attorney General. <!-- verified: dual-section scope (638.50 and 638.51) and AG-exclusive enforcement independently corroborated via WebSearch across multiple law firm summaries; ## Background [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

CIPA was enacted in 1967 to prohibit unauthorized wiretapping of telephone communications, and its pen register/trap-and-trace provisions -- definitions at Penal Code Section 638.50 and the operative prohibition at Section 638.51 -- originally targeted devices that record the numbers dialed on, or addressing information transmitted over, a telephone line without recording the communication's content. Beginning around 2022-2023, plaintiffs' firms began applying these provisions to modern website technologies -- cookies, tracking pixels, session-replay software, and live-chat widgets -- theorizing that such tools function as unlawful pen registers or trap-and-trace devices when they capture URL or addressing information without the "consent of all parties" that CIPA requires. This theory produced a wave of class actions and individual suits against website operators across virtually every industry, a trend previously documented in Zwiad's litigation reports on [Rounds v. Development Dimensions International](../litigation/california-cipa-trap-trace-rounds-v-ddi-2026-04-28.md), [Blaker v. NetScout](../litigation/california-cipa-blaker-netscout-pen-register-2026-05-27.md), and the CIPA pen-register/IP-address line of cases.

SB 690 was introduced in the 2025-2026 regular session by Senator Anna Caballero as a response to this litigation wave. <!-- verified: author (Sen. Anna Caballero, D-Merced) and 2025-2026 session independently confirmed via WebSearch --> As originally drafted, the bill would have amended Sections 631, 632, 632.7, and 638.50/638.51 to preclude liability when the tracking technology at issue was used in a manner consistent with a "commercial business purpose," defined by reference to the "business purpose" and consumer opt-out framework under the CCPA. That original approach drew opposition from privacy and consumer advocates, who argued it would gut CIPA's application to modern surveillance technologies well beyond what the bill's proponents intended (see [Skadden's original coverage of the reform effort](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/cipa-is-still-a-mess), which is the source article underlying this finding).

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

On [July 1, 2026, the Assembly Committee on Privacy and Consumer Protection held a hearing](https://apcp.assembly.ca.gov/media/1212) on SB 690 and passed it as amended by unanimous committee vote, referring it to the Assembly Committee on Appropriations. <!-- verified: WebSearch independently confirms a 14-0 committee vote and referral to Appropriations; direct WebFetch of apcp.assembly.ca.gov not possible this round (tool permission unavailable) --> According to law firm analyses of the amended bill text ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/sb-690-amended-california-moves-to-strip-private-right-of-action-for-pen-register-claims/); [Shook, Hardy & Bacon](https://www.shb.com/intelligence/newsletters/pds/straus-pedersen-july-2026-california-sb-690); [National Law Review / cipaworld.com syndication](https://natlawreview.com/article/senate-bill-690-amended-california-scales-back-its-proposed-cipa-overhaul); [CyberAdviser](https://www.cyberadviserblog.com/2026/07/california-assembly-committee-advances-cipa-reform/)), the July 1-2, 2026 amendments made two consequential changes:

1. **Narrowed scope.** The bill abandoned its original broad "commercial business purpose" exemption applicable across CIPA and instead targets only Penal Code Sections 638.50 and 638.51 -- California's pen register and trap-and-trace statute. As amended, the bill would authorize only the California Attorney General to bring an enforcement action for a Section 638.50 or 638.51 violation where the alleged conduct occurs "on an internet website, online application, or mobile application" -- eliminating the private right of action for this specific theory of liability in the online context. This narrowing of scope (from all of CIPA to just Sections 638.50/638.51) is independently confirmed by [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/sb-690-amended-california-moves-to-strip-private-right-of-action-for-pen-register-claims/), [Shook, Hardy & Bacon](https://www.shb.com/intelligence/newsletters/pds/straus-pedersen-july-2026-california-sb-690) (also syndicated via [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1814170/california-lawmakers-narrow-sb-690-leaving-potential-relief-for-pen-register-and-trap-and-trace-claims)), and [Mullen Coughlin](https://www.mullen.law/ca-senate-bill-690-amendments/). <!-- verified: reviewer independent WebSearch corroborates dual-section (638.50/638.51) scope and AG-only enforcement across multiple independent law-firm sources -->

2. **Retroactive application to pending claims.** The amendment would apply retroactively to pending Section 638.50/638.51 claims. As currently drafted, the bill would take effect January 1, 2027 and reach claims filed on or after January 1, 2025 -- i.e., claims filed within the two years preceding the operative date -- meaning it would reach the large existing docket of pen-register/trap-and-trace theory cases rather than only future filings ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/sb-690-amended-california-moves-to-strip-private-right-of-action-for-pen-register-claims/); [Mullen Coughlin](https://www.mullen.law/ca-senate-bill-690-amendments/)). Reporting citing Senator Caballero's office states that the number of Section 638.51 suits grew from an estimated 600 cases when SB 690 was introduced to more than 4,000 cases by mid-2026, with a concentration of filings from a small number of plaintiffs' firms and repeat named plaintiffs ([National Law Review/cipaworld.com](https://natlawreview.com/article/senate-bill-690-amended-california-scales-back-its-proposed-cipa-overhaul)). <!-- verified: 600-to-4,000+ litigation growth figure and Caballero attribution independently confirmed via WebSearch across multiple sources -->

Notably, the amended bill leaves CIPA Sections 631 (wiretapping) and 632 (eavesdropping) untouched. Multiple sources emphasize that the "overwhelming majority" of website-tracking lawsuits are actually brought under Sections 631 and 632, not the pen register/trap-and-trace provisions -- meaning SB 690 in its current, narrowed form addresses only a subset of the litigation wave it was originally intended to curb ([National Law Review/cipaworld.com](https://natlawreview.com/article/senate-bill-690-amended-california-scales-back-its-proposed-cipa-overhaul); [Stoel Rives Global Privacy & Security Blog](https://www.stoelprivacyblog.com/2026/07/articles/privacy/two-steps-forward-one-step-back-amending-the-california-invasion-of-privacy-act-cipa/)). Industry groups, including the News/Media Alliance, publicly supported the committee advancement as a partial win for defendants facing pen-register/trap-and-trace suits ([News/Media Alliance](https://www.newsmediaalliance.org/nma-member-advocacy-secures-cipa-bill-advancement/) -- presence in search-result listings corroborated but not independently re-fetched this round; see confidence note below). <!-- unverifiable: source unavailable -- WebFetch tool permission unavailable this round; content confirmed only via WebSearch listing, not direct fetch -->

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

If enacted in its current, narrowed form, SB 690 would:

- **Eliminate private CIPA Section 638.50/638.51 claims** against businesses for online tracking conduct (cookies, pixels, chat widgets, session-replay tools used in a pen-register or trap-and-trace theory), leaving enforcement to the California Attorney General only.
- **Retroactively affect the pending litigation docket**, reaching claims filed on or after January 1, 2025 if the bill takes effect January 1, 2027 as currently drafted -- potentially mooting or requiring dismissal of a substantial share of the 4,000+ currently pending Section 638.50/638.51 suits within that window, pending final bill language and effective date.
- **Leave undisturbed the larger volume of CIPA Section 631/632 wiretapping and eavesdropping suits**, meaning businesses should not expect broad relief from CIPA-based website litigation generally -- only from the pen-register/trap-and-trace theory specifically.
- **Affect any business operating a website, mobile application, or online application** that uses third-party tracking, analytics, chat, or session-recording tools and has been a defendant (or is at litigation risk) under Section 638.50/638.51 theories.

The bill must pass the Assembly Appropriations Committee, a full Assembly floor vote, and (since it originated in the Senate and was amended in the Assembly) return to the Senate for concurrence, before the [August 31, 2026 end-of-session floor vote deadline](https://www.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2025-10/2026-senate-legislative-deadlines.pdf) for signature or veto by the Governor. Further amendments addressing Sections 631/632 remain possible before final passage.

## Action Items

- Monitor SB 690's progress through the Assembly Appropriations Committee and subsequent floor votes; the retroactivity provision (claims filed on or after January 1, 2025, effective date January 1, 2027) makes the bill's final language directly relevant to any pending Section 638.50/638.51 defense strategy.
- Businesses currently defending Section 638.50/638.51 (pen register/trap-and-trace) claims arising from website tracking technology should track the bill's operative date and retroactivity window, as it may provide a basis for dismissal or stay of pending claims.
- Do not treat SB 690 as addressing CIPA Section 631/632 wiretapping and eavesdropping exposure -- that litigation risk is unaffected by the current bill language and remains the dominant source of CIPA website-tracking suits.
