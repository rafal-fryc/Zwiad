# EDPB Adopts Standardized DPIA Template to Harmonize GDPR Compliance

**Jurisdiction:** EU | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-14

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 14, 2026, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) adopted a standardized Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) template, implementing a core commitment of its December 2025 Helsinki Statement to simplify GDPR compliance and strengthen cross-border consistency. The template — accompanied by a plain-language explainer document — is open for public consultation until June 9, 2026, after which national Data Protection Authorities (DPAs) are expected to adopt it as their standard or align national templates to it as a "meta-template."

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The EDPB [announced adoption of the DPIA template on April 14, 2026](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2026/enhancing-compliance-and-consistency-edpb-adopts-dpia-template_en), framing it as a direct deliverable of its Helsinki Statement commitments on clarity, support, and engagement.
- The [DPIA template and explainer are available on the EDPB public consultations page](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/documents/public-consultations/2026/edpb-dpia-template_en), with stakeholder comments due by **June 9, 2026**.
- Use of the EDPB template is **not mandatory**, but it provides predefined fields designed to prompt complete, structured responses under Article 35 GDPR and reduce documentation errors, per the [EDPB announcement](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2026/enhancing-compliance-and-consistency-edpb-adopts-dpia-template_en).
- The accompanying **explainer document** breaks down key DPIA concepts in plain language and addresses common controller questions and knowledge gaps, per the [EDPB announcement](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2026/enhancing-compliance-and-consistency-edpb-adopts-dpia-template_en).
- Following consultation, **all EU DPAs will take steps to adopt the template either as their sole standard or as a "meta-template"** to which national-specific templates align, per the [EDPB announcement](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2026/enhancing-compliance-and-consistency-edpb-adopts-dpia-template_en) — a significant step toward ending the current patchwork of divergent national DPIA forms.
- The template builds on the EDPB's existing [Guidelines on DPIAs and "likely to result in high risk" processing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/data-protection-impact-assessments-high-risk-processing_en) under Article 35 GDPR, which remain the governing substantive standard.
- The Helsinki Statement, adopted in December 2025, committed the EDPB to [enhanced clarity, support, and engagement](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/statements/helsinki-statement-enhanced-clarity-support-and-engagement_en) with stakeholders — including production of practical compliance tools such as this template.

## Action Items

- **Review the draft template and explainer before June 9, 2026**, and consider submitting comments via the EDPB public consultation portal, especially if your organization runs DPIAs across multiple EU member states.
- **Benchmark internal DPIA templates against the EDPB draft** — map current fields to the EDPB structure so that a transition (or dual-template maintenance) can be planned in Q3/Q4 2026 once DPAs begin adopting it.
- **Flag jurisdictional divergence risk**: track announcements from your lead supervisory authority (and any concerned DPAs) on whether they will adopt the EDPB template wholesale or maintain national add-ons as a meta-template overlay.
- **Update DPO and privacy engineering workflows** to anticipate the standardized structure — in particular, fields requiring documented necessity/proportionality assessments, risk scoring methodologies, and mitigation evidence.
- **Monitor the EDPB April 15-16 plenary** for related guidance on scientific research processing and generative AI best practices, which may intersect with DPIA obligations for AI and research programs.
