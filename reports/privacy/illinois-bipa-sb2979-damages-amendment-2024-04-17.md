# Illinois Amends BIPA to Limit Per-Person Damages and Permit Electronic Consent (SB 2979)

**Jurisdiction:** Illinois | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-04-17

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 11, 2024, the Illinois Senate voted 46-13 to pass [Senate Bill 2979](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/BillStatus.asp?DocNum=2979&GAID=17&DocTypeID=SB&LegId=152094&SessionID=112&GA=103), the first amendment to the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) since its enactment in 2008. The bill limits BIPA damages to a single recovery per person per violation, regardless of the number of times biometric data was collected or disclosed, and permits electronic signatures to satisfy BIPA's "written release" consent requirement. The Illinois House subsequently passed the bill 81-30 in May 2024, and Governor J.B. Pritzker [signed it into law on August 2, 2024](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/bipa-legislative-update-governor-pritzker-signs-amendment-limiting-damages-to-a-single-recovery-1.html) as [Public Act 103-0769](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/publicacts/103/103-0769.htm), effective immediately. The amendment was a direct legislative response to the Illinois Supreme Court's 2023 ruling in *Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc.*, which had held that a separate claim accrues with every scan or transmission -- a standard that exposed one employer to potentially $17 billion in damages.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### BIPA's Statutory Framework

Illinois enacted BIPA in 2008, making it one of the first state laws in the nation to regulate the collection and use of biometric identifiers such as fingerprints, retina scans, voiceprints, and facial geometry. BIPA's [Section 15](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004&ChapterID=57) imposes consent and notice requirements on private entities that collect biometric data, and its private right of action under [Section 20](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K20.htm) authorizes liquidated damages of $1,000 per negligent violation and $5,000 per intentional or reckless violation, plus reasonable attorneys' fees and injunctive relief.

BIPA's private right of action made it an outlier among biometric privacy laws and generated a surge of class action litigation, particularly after the Illinois Supreme Court's 2019 decision in [*Rosenbach v. Six Flags Entertainment Corp.*](https://law.justia.com/cases/illinois/supreme-court/2019/123186.html) held that a plaintiff need not allege actual injury beyond a statutory violation to have standing.

### The *Cothron v. White Castle* Decision

The stakes escalated dramatically with the Illinois Supreme Court's February 17, 2023 decision in [*Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc.*, 2023 IL 128004](https://law.justia.com/cases/illinois/supreme-court/2023/128004.html). The case involved White Castle employees who were required to scan their fingerprints to access pay stubs and computers. The court held that "a claim accrues under the Act with every scan or transmission of biometric identifiers or biometric information without prior informed consent." Under this per-scan accrual interpretation, White Castle faced potential liability of [up to $17 billion in damages](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2023-may/illinois-supreme-court-finds-white-castle-could-face-up-to-17b-in-damages/) from approximately 9,500 employees, each of whom may have scanned fingerprints hundreds or thousands of times.

The *Cothron* majority acknowledged the potential for "annihilative" damages but stated that courts retain discretion over damage awards and noted that "the legislature is in a superior position to assess the policy implications of the various approaches" to damages. The court [denied rehearing](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2023/07/20/illinois-supreme-court-refuses-to-reconsider-per-scan-bipa-accrual-ruling-in-cothron-v-white-castle/) on July 18, 2023.

### Legislative Response

Business groups and industry organizations immediately pressed the Illinois General Assembly to intervene. Senator Bill Cunningham introduced SB 2979 on January 31, 2024, directly responding to the *Cothron* court's invitation for legislative action on the damages question.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Damages Limitation (Sections 15(b) and 15(d))

SB 2979 amends [Section 20 of BIPA (740 ILCS 14/20)](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K20.htm) to provide that:

- Under **Section 15(b)** (collection): A private entity that "in more than one instance, collects, captures, purchases, receives through trade, or otherwise obtains the same biometric identifier or biometric information from the same person using the same method of collection" in violation of Section 15(b) has committed **a single violation** for which the aggrieved person is entitled to "at most, one recovery." ([Greenberg Traurig analysis](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/8/bipa-update-illinois-limits-liability-and-clarifies-electronic-consent-for-biometric-data-collection))

- Under **Section 15(d)** (disclosure): A private entity that discloses or rediscloses the same biometric information to the same recipient using the same method has committed **a single violation** entitling the aggrieved person to "at most, one recovery," regardless of the number of times the information was disclosed. ([Nixon Peabody analysis](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2024/05/20/bipa-reform-bill-addressing-one-time-claim-accrual-and-electronic-signature-passess))

This overrides the *Cothron* per-scan accrual standard. Under the amended statute, an employee whose fingerprint was scanned 1,500 times without consent can recover a maximum of $5,000 (for intentional/reckless violations) or $1,000 (for negligent violations) -- not $7.5 million as the pre-amendment *Cothron* framework would have allowed.

### Electronic Signature Consent

SB 2979 also amends the definition of "written release" in BIPA to include an **electronic signature**, defined as "an electronic sound, symbol, or process attached to or logically associated with a record and executed or adopted by a person with the intent to sign the record." This modernizes BIPA's consent mechanism and [eliminates compliance friction](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/illinois-bipa-reform-takes-effect) for employers and businesses that collect biometric data through digital workflows, time-clock kiosks, or mobile applications. Previously, the requirement of a "written release" created ambiguity about whether digital consent mechanisms satisfied the statute.

### Retroactive Application

The amendment's text does not explicitly address retroactivity, which became a contested question in pending BIPA class actions. Several federal district courts initially held that the amendment applied only prospectively. However, on April 1, 2026, the Seventh Circuit Court of Appeals unanimously held in [*Clay v. Union Pacific Railroad Co.*, No. 25-2185](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/04/08/seventh-circuit-limits-potential-damages-under-bipa-holds-2024-amendment-applies-retroactively/) that the amendment applies retroactively to cases pending at the time of enactment, reasoning that the amendment was a "remedial change" focused on damages rather than a substantive alteration of rights. This ruling [reversed three federal district court decisions](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/04/03/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-amendment-applies-retroactively-reversing-three-illinois-federal-court-decisions/) and dramatically reduced exposure for defendants in hundreds of active BIPA lawsuits.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Reduced Litigation Exposure

The amendment fundamentally reshapes the economics of BIPA class actions. Under the prior per-scan framework established by *Cothron*, class-wide damages could reach billions of dollars, creating settlement pressure that many defendants characterized as [extortive](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2024/08/illinois-bipa-biometrics-law-amended-for-damages). Under the amended statute, maximum statutory damages per class member are capped at $5,000 (intentional) or $1,000 (negligent), bringing total class-wide exposure to manageable levels for most employers and technology companies.

### Impact on Pending and Future Litigation

Following the Seventh Circuit's retroactivity ruling, the amendment applies to both prospective and pending cases. This has significant implications for the [hundreds of active BIPA class actions](https://www.sgrlaw.com/client-alerts/seventh-circuit-holds-bipa-damages-amendment-applies-retroactively-reshaping-liability-for-hundreds-of-pending-cases/) in Illinois state and federal courts. Defendants in pending cases can now use the amendment to argue for reduced damages or to renegotiate settlement terms. Plaintiffs' attorneys face diminished incentives to file per-scan damages claims.

### Affected Industries

The amendment benefits any private entity that collects or uses biometric data in Illinois, including:

- **Employers** using fingerprint or facial recognition time clocks
- **Technology companies** offering biometric authentication products
- **Retailers** deploying facial recognition or biometric-based security
- **Social media platforms** utilizing facial geometry tagging
- **Financial institutions** using biometric identity verification

### Electronic Consent Modernization

The electronic signature provision removes a practical compliance barrier, particularly for companies operating digital-first workflows. Organizations can now obtain BIPA consent through electronic onboarding systems, mobile apps, and digital kiosks without risking challenges to the validity of the consent mechanism.

### Ongoing Risks

While the amendment significantly reduces damages exposure, BIPA's private right of action, notice requirements, and substantive consent obligations remain intact. Companies must still:

- Provide written notice of biometric data collection purposes and retention schedules
- Obtain informed consent before collecting biometric identifiers
- Comply with data retention and destruction obligations
- Refrain from profiting from biometric data

Violations still carry per-person statutory damages, attorneys' fee awards, and injunctive relief -- meaningful exposure in large class actions even under the amended framework.

## Action Items

- **Review biometric consent workflows** to confirm they satisfy BIPA's notice and consent requirements. Transition to electronic signature-based consent if not already implemented, leveraging the new statutory authorization.
- **Reassess pending BIPA litigation exposure** in light of the per-person damages cap and the Seventh Circuit's retroactivity holding. Consider whether settlement terms should be renegotiated.
- **Update biometric data retention policies** to ensure compliance with BIPA's retention and destruction requirements, which remain unchanged by SB 2979.
- **Audit all biometric data collection points** to identify any collections, disclosures, or transmissions that lack proper consent, as per-person liability still applies.
- **Monitor Illinois state court rulings** on retroactivity. While the Seventh Circuit has ruled, Illinois state courts may reach their own conclusions on the amendment's temporal scope.
