# Federal Court Blocks Arkansas Social Media Safety Act Amendments Targeting Addictive Design Features

**Jurisdiction:** Arkansas | **Category:** Privacy — Children's Online Safety | **Date:** April 21, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

U.S. District Court Judge Timothy Brooks (W.D. Ark.) granted a preliminary injunction on April 20, 2026, blocking enforcement of Arkansas Act 900 of 2025 — the second attempt by the state to regulate minors' social media access — hours before key provisions were set to take effect on April 21, 2026. The court found that NetChoice was likely to succeed on its vagueness challenge to the statute's "addictive practices" prohibition and on its First Amendment challenges to provisions mandating default privacy settings and parental monitoring dashboards. The ruling is the latest chapter in a protracted three-year dispute between Arkansas and the tech industry, and adds to a growing body of federal decisions striking down or enjoining state social media minor-protection statutes on constitutional grounds.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Case:** *NetChoice, LLC v. Griffin et al*, No. 5:25-cv-05140-TLB (W.D. Ark., Fayetteville Div.), Judge Timothy L. Brooks presiding. NetChoice filed its complaint on June 27, 2025, challenging Act 900 shortly after its enactment; a first preliminary injunction against separate provisions was granted December 15, 2025. The April 20, 2026 order blocked the remaining provisions — specifically the "addictive practices" ban, default-settings mandate, and parental dashboard requirement — that were not covered by the December 2025 order. ([NetChoice case page](https://netchoice.org/netchoice-v-griffin-arkansas-2025/))

- **Act 900 background:** Arkansas Act 900 (enacted April 21, 2025, as SB 611) amended the original Social Media Safety Act (SB 396/Act 689 of 2023), which had itself been permanently enjoined on First Amendment grounds in March 2025. Act 900 narrowed the age threshold to users under 16 (down from 18), retained age verification requirements, and added three new categories of obligations: (1) a prohibition on "addictive practices," (2) mandatory privacy-protective default settings, and (3) a parental monitoring dashboard. ([Arkansas Legislature — Act 900 text](https://arkleg.state.ar.us/Home/FTPDocument?path=/ACTS/2025R/Public/ACT900.pdf))

- **Vagueness holding:** The "addictive practices" provision (Act 900 § 4) prohibited platforms from engaging in practices "to evoke any addiction or compulsive behaviors" in minors, listing notifications, recommended content, "artificial sense of accomplishment," and engagement with human-appearing bots as examples. The statute did not define "addictive" or "compulsive behaviors." Judge Brooks found the provision imposed strict civil liability — with penalties of $10,000 per violation — for any practice that caused addiction in even a single child, even if the platform had no reasonable prior knowledge of that effect. The court noted that when asked at the hearing to identify even two or three examples of non-speech-tied "addictive" features, the state's attorney could not name a single one. ([MediaPost coverage of ruling](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414449/arkansas-addictive-practices-law-likely-unconsti.html))

- **First Amendment holding on default settings:** Act 900 also required platforms to apply the most privacy-protective default settings to any user under 16, restrict overnight notifications (10 p.m. to 6 a.m.) by default, and build parental dashboards enabling monitoring even of minors who had no account on the platform. Judge Brooks found these provisions impose "small burdens on vast quantities of speech for no appreciable benefit" and that "Arkansas cannot sentence speech on the internet to death by a thousand cuts." The court found NetChoice likely to succeed under intermediate First Amendment scrutiny. ([NetChoice press release](https://netchoice.org/first-amendment-to-arkansas-you-cannot-sentence-speech-on-the-internet-to-death-by-a-thousand-cuts-in-netchoice-court-victory/))

- **Nature of relief:** The April 20, 2026 order is a **preliminary injunction**, not a final judgment on the merits. The state retains the ability to appeal to the Eighth Circuit and to seek a stay of the injunction. Arkansas AG Tim Griffin has stated the state's office is considering its options and "will continue to vigorously defend Act 900." ([UALRPublicRadio report](https://www.ualrpublicradio.org/local-regional-news/2026-04-20/federal-judge-blocks-reworked-arkansas-law-restricting-minors-social-media))

- **Appellate posture:** A separate Eighth Circuit appeal from the December 2025 injunction (No. 25-1889) was already pending; the state filed its opening brief arguing the revised statute should be upheld. The new April 2026 injunction likely joins or parallels that appeal. ([Eighth Circuit opening brief](https://netchoice.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ARs-Opening-Brief_CA8-AR1.pdf))

- **Broader context:** This is the third consecutive Arkansas social media law to be enjoined or struck down. Act 689 (2023) was preliminarily enjoined in August 2023 and permanently enjoined March 31, 2025; Act 900 (2025) has now been preliminarily enjoined twice. Similar laws in Louisiana and other states have met comparable fates in federal courts. ([Biometric Update coverage](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202604/netchoice-wins-in-arkansas-but-faces-forever-war-against-age-assurance))

## Action Items

- **Social media platforms operating in Arkansas:** Maintain the status quo — Act 900's addictive-practices ban, default-settings mandate, and parental-dashboard requirement are preliminarily enjoined and currently unenforceable. Monitor the Eighth Circuit docket (No. 25-1889 and any consolidated appeal) for any motion to stay the injunction, which could restore enforcement obligations rapidly.

- **Compliance teams:** Do not implement Act 900 compliance measures for the enjoined provisions in reliance on a permanent victory — preliminary injunctions can be reversed or stayed. Prepare contingency compliance plans for the addictive-practices and default-settings obligations so they can be activated if the injunction is lifted.

- **Legal counsel:** Evaluate whether Act 900's remaining provisions (age verification obligations) were addressed by the December 2025 injunction or remain operative. The April 2026 order focused on specific provisions; the full scope of enjoined sections should be confirmed by reviewing the court's order directly on PACER (Case No. 5:25-cv-05140-TLB).

- **Policy teams:** Monitor legislative activity in Arkansas for a potential third amendment to the Social Media Safety Act. The pattern of amendment-and-re-litigation suggests the state will attempt further revisions. Track the Eighth Circuit's reception of the state's constitutional arguments, which may signal whether any narrower statute could survive review.

- **Multi-state compliance:** Audit similar "addictive design" or "compulsive-use" prohibitions in other state legislation (e.g., Missouri HB 2392). Courts applying the vagueness doctrine to Arkansas's statute may set precedent affecting analogous laws elsewhere.
