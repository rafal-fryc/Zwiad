# Sixth Circuit Revives Ohio's Social Media Parental Notification Act, Reverses District Court Injunction

**Jurisdiction:** Ohio | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-01

## Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

On June 18, 2026, a divided panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit reversed a district court's permanent injunction and held that Ohio may enforce its Parental Notification by Social Media Operators Act (the "Act"), which requires operators to obtain verifiable parental consent before minors under 16 can create or maintain social media accounts. The decision, *NetChoice, LLC v. Yost*, No. 25-3371 (6th Cir. June 18, 2026), reverses U.S. District Judge Algenon Marbley's April 16, 2025 ruling that had declared the Act unconstitutional and permanently enjoined its enforcement. The panel held that NetChoice — the industry trade association representing Google/YouTube, Meta, and other platforms — lacked prudential third-party standing to assert the First Amendment rights of minor users, and that NetChoice's own facial challenges (strict scrutiny and vagueness) failed on the merits [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/sixth-circuit-revives-ohios-parental-consent-law-for-kids-social-media/), [Justia case summary](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/25-3371/25-3371-2026-06-18.html), [Pearl Cohen client alert](https://www.pearlcohen.com/sixth-circuit-upholds-ohios-parental-notification-social-media-law/). <!-- verified: case name, docket 25-3371, decision date, standing/merits holdings independently corroborated via WebSearch -->

## Key Facts [MEDIUM confidence] [NEEDS REVIEW]

- **Case:** *NetChoice, LLC v. Yost*, No. 25-3371 (6th Cir.), decided June 18, 2026 [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/25-3371/25-3371-2026-06-18.html). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch -->
- **Panel:** Circuit Judge Clay announced the judgment; Circuit Judge Batchelder concurred in the judgment; Circuit Judge Ritz dissented [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/sixth-circuit-revives-ohios-parental-consent-law-for-kids-social-media/). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch (panel = Batchelder, Clay, Ritz; Clay authored lead opinion, Batchelder concurred in judgment, Ritz dissented) -->
- **Underlying law:** Ohio's Parental Notification by Social Media Operators Act, enacted July 2023, requires social media operators to obtain verifiable parental consent before allowing a user under 16 to create or maintain an account [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/sixth-circuit-revives-ohios-parental-consent-law-for-kids-social-media/). <!-- verified: HB 33 signed by Gov. DeWine on July 5, 2023, per independent WebSearch (Hunton, Lexology, NatLawReview) -->
- **Procedural history:** U.S. District Judge Algenon Marbley (S.D. Ohio) declared the Act unconstitutional and permanently enjoined its enforcement on April 16, 2025, finding it not narrowly tailored to the state's interest in protecting minors' mental health [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/sixth-circuit-revives-ohios-parental-consent-law-for-kids-social-media/). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch (statenews.org, mediapost.com report same April 16-17, 2025 ruling) -->
- **Standing holding:** The Sixth Circuit held NetChoice lacks prudential third-party standing to assert the First Amendment rights of its members' minor users, reasoning that the platforms' commercial/profit motive conflicts with minors' interests [Pearl Cohen client alert](https://www.pearlcohen.com/sixth-circuit-upholds-ohios-parental-notification-social-media-law/), [CaseMine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/netchoice-llc-v-yost-no-third-party-standing-for-platforms-to-litigate-minors-speech-rights-ohios-parental-consent-social-media-contracting-law-survives-facial-strict-scrutiny/view). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch -->
- **Merits holding:** Even considering NetChoice's own claims, the court found the Act — though content-based and subject to strict scrutiny — is narrowly tailored to the state's compelling interest in protecting minors from "predatory contracting" and engagement-maximizing design features, and is not impermissibly vague in all its applications, so it survives facial challenge [CaseMine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/netchoice-llc-v-yost-no-third-party-standing-for-platforms-to-litigate-minors-speech-rights-ohios-parental-consent-social-media-contracting-law-survives-facial-strict-scrutiny/view). <!-- verified: "predatory contracting" / engagement-maximizing design language independently corroborated via WebSearch -->
- **Dissent:** Judge Ritz dissented, calling the Act a "breathtakingly blunt instrument" that unconstitutionally supplants parental authority and cuts off minors from "vast democratic forums" [Courthouse News Service](https://www.courthousenews.com/sixth-circuit-revives-ohios-parental-consent-law-for-kids-social-media/). <!-- needs-human-review: WebFetch could not reach the primary opinion or Courthouse News directly (tool permission denied); one independent WebSearch summary suggested the "breathtakingly blunt instrument" phrase may originate with the district court (Marbley) rather than Ritz's dissent specifically. Ritz's dissent and general themes (parental-authority displacement, cutting off minors from online speech) are otherwise corroborated, but the exact quote attribution should be confirmed against the primary opinion at opn.ca6.uscourts.gov before citing it. -->
- **Practical effect:** The ruling vacates the district court's permanent injunction, clearing the way for Ohio to enforce the Act against covered social media operators pending any further appeal (e.g., rehearing en banc or certiorari) [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/court-lifts-block-on-ohios-age-assurance-law-for-social-media-in-defeat-for-netchoice). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch -->
