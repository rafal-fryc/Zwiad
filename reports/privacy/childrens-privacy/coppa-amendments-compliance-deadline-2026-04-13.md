# COPPA Rule Amendments: Compliance Deadline April 22, 2026

## Overview [HIGH confidence]

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) finalized sweeping amendments to the Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (16 CFR Part 312) in January 2025. The final rule was [published in the Federal Register on April 22, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule) and became effective on June 23, 2025. The general compliance deadline for regulated operators is **April 22, 2026** — one year from Federal Register publication.

These are the most significant changes to COPPA in over a decade, tightening consent requirements for third-party disclosures, mandating a written information security program, imposing explicit data retention limits, and expanding the definition of "personal information" to cover biometric identifiers and government-issued ID numbers.

## Key New Requirements [HIGH confidence]

### 1. Separate Verifiable Parental Consent for Third-Party Disclosures
Operators must obtain a **separate, specific opt-in VPC** before disclosing a child's personal information to third parties — most notably for targeted advertising or any purpose unrelated to the service. Marketing/disclosure consent cannot be bundled into a general consent to collection and use.

### 2. Written Information Security Program (WISP)
The amendments replace COPPA's prior general "reasonable security" obligation with a prescriptive requirement to establish, implement, and maintain a **written information security program** with safeguards appropriate to the sensitivity of the data and operator's size. Operators must designate a program coordinator, perform annual risk assessments, implement safeguards, and obtain written assurances from service providers.

### 3. Data Retention Policy and Public Retention Schedule
Operators may retain children's personal information only **for as long as reasonably necessary** for the specific collection purpose, must establish a written retention policy, and must **publicly disclose** the retention schedule (business purpose, timeframe, deletion process) in the online notice. Indefinite retention is prohibited.

### 4. Expanded Definition of "Personal Information"
16 CFR 312.2 is expanded to include:
- **Biometric identifiers** usable for automated/semi-automated recognition (fingerprints, voiceprints, retina/iris patterns, genetic data, facial templates, gait).
- **Government-issued identifiers** beyond SSN (state ID, passport numbers).

### 5. Narrowed "Support for Internal Operations" Exemption
Operators relying on the internal-operations exemption must now **publicly list the specific internal operations** for which persistent identifiers are used, and cannot use the data to contact individuals, build profiles, or serve targeted ads.

### 6. Ed-Tech Clarifications
The amendments codify a school-authorization pathway, allowing schools to authorize collection of student personal information for a **school-authorized education purpose only**, under written agreements and with no commercial use.

### 7. Safe Harbor Program Transparency
FTC-approved Safe Harbor programs face heightened transparency and membership-disclosure obligations, with **earlier compliance deadlines** (90 days–6 months after publication).

## Compliance Checklist [HIGH confidence]

Before **April 22, 2026**, operators of child-directed services (or general-audience services with actual knowledge of under-13 users) should:

- [ ] Update privacy notices for the expanded PI definition, public retention schedule, and internal-operations disclosures.
- [ ] Re-paper parental consent flows with a separate, unbundled VPC for third-party disclosures (especially targeted ads).
- [ ] Adopt a WISP with designated coordinator, annual risk assessment, documented safeguards, and service-provider assurances.
- [ ] Adopt a written data retention policy and purge data no longer necessary.
- [ ] Inventory biometric and government-ID data flows; confirm VPC coverage.
- [ ] Review K-12 ed-tech contracts against the new school-authorization framework.
- [ ] Update vendor/service-provider agreements for written data-protection assurances.
- [ ] Train product, engineering, and marketing teams on new consent and disclosure rules.

## Risk Outlook [MEDIUM confidence]

FTC has historically prioritized COPPA enforcement, with settlements against YouTube, TikTok, Epic Games, Amazon, and Microsoft ranging from tens to hundreds of millions of dollars. The new WISP and retention requirements provide concrete, documentable checkpoints that will streamline post-April 2026 enforcement. State attorneys general, who share COPPA enforcement authority, are also expected to act under the amended rule.
