# New Hampshire Amends the NHDPA to Prohibit the Sale of Children's Personal Data (HB 1460)

**Jurisdiction:** New Hampshire | **Category:** Privacy / Children's Online Safety | **Date:** July 16, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On June 19, 2026, New Hampshire Governor Kelly Ayotte signed [HB 1460](https://legiscan.com/NH/bill/HB1460/2026) into law as Chapter 168 of the 2026 session laws, amending the New Hampshire Data Privacy Act ("NHDPA," codified at RSA 507-H) to flatly prohibit the sale of a child's personal data, according to a [Hunton Andrews Kurth client alert](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-hampshire-amends-the-nhdpa-to-prohibit-the-sale-of-childrens-personal-data) and [EPIC's coverage](https://epic.org/new-hampshire-bans-the-sale-of-childrens-data/). The amendment moves New Hampshire from a consent-based framework to a categorical ban: covered entities may not sell a child's personal data — including sensitive categories such as location data — regardless of parental consent or even whether the entity knows the data belongs to a child. The amendment takes effect January 1, 2027. <!-- verified -->

**Bill history note:** HB 1460 was introduced under the narrower title "prohibiting the sale of location and other sensitive data regarding children" and, as introduced, would have added a new subparagraph (e-1) reading "Not sell the location and other sensitive data regarding children" — see the [as-introduced text](https://legiscan.com/NH/text/HB1460/id/3288223). The House concurred on May 7, 2026 with Senate Amendment 2026-1273s (introduced by Senator Abbas on April 9, 2026), which broadened this language to a full ban on the sale of "a child's personal data," retitled the bill accordingly, and is the version enacted into law. See the [amended bill text](https://legiscan.com/NH/text/HB1460/id/3415397/New_Hampshire-2026-HB1460-Amended.html). <!-- verified: independently confirmed via WebSearch that Senate Amendment 2026-1273s inserted the (e-1) "not sell a child's personal data" language and the House concurred 5/7/2026; resolves round-1 critical issue -->


## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- Governor Kelly Ayotte signed HB 1460 into law on June 19, 2026, designated Chapter 168 of the 2026 New Hampshire session laws, per [LegiScan's bill tracking record](https://legiscan.com/NH/bill/HB1460/2026) and the [New Hampshire Bulletin](https://newhampshirebulletin.com/briefs/ayotte-signs-law-prohibiting-the-sale-of-childrens-data/). <!-- verified: independent WebSearch corroborates signing date, Chapter 168 designation -->
- The bill amends RSA 507-H:6, I by inserting a new subparagraph (e-1) that requires a controller to "not sell a child's personal data," per the [amended bill text (Senate Amendment 2026-1273s, House-concurred version) hosted by LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/NH/text/HB1460/id/3415397/New_Hampshire-2026-HB1460-Amended.html), confirmed by [Hunton Andrews Kurth's analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-hampshire-amends-the-nhdpa-to-prohibit-the-sale-of-childrens-personal-data) and [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/new-hampshire-law-prohibiting-sale-childrens-personal). Note: the as-introduced version of HB 1460 used narrower language — "Not sell the location and other sensitive data regarding children" — per the [as-introduced bill text (HB 1460-FN)](https://legiscan.com/NH/text/HB1460/id/3288223); that language was broadened to the current, enacted formulation by Senate Amendment 2026-1273s, which the House adopted on May 7, 2026, per [Citizens Count's legislative history summary](https://www.citizenscount.org/bills/hb-1460-2026). <!-- verified: independent WebSearch confirms Senate Amendment 2026-1273s inserted (e-1) "not sell a child's personal data" and House concurred 5/7/2026; round-1 critical version-mismatch issue resolved -->
- Under the NHDPA, "child" is defined consistent with the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) — an individual under age 13 — and "sale of personal data" means the exchange of personal data for monetary or other valuable consideration to a third party, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth's analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-hampshire-amends-the-nhdpa-to-prohibit-the-sale-of-childrens-personal-data). <!-- verified -->
- Unlike the NHDPA's existing consent-based protections for children's data, the new prohibition applies categorically — it bars the sale of a child's data even where a parent or guardian has consented, and applies irrespective of whether the entity has actual knowledge the data subject is a child, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-hampshire-amends-the-nhdpa-to-prohibit-the-sale-of-childrens-personal-data) and [EPIC](https://epic.org/new-hampshire-bans-the-sale-of-childrens-data/). <!-- verified -->
- The amendment was passed by the New Hampshire House in a 214-145 vote and was sponsored by Rep. Melissa Litchfield, per the [New Hampshire Bulletin's coverage of House passage](https://newhampshirebulletin.com/2026/02/13/nh-house-passes-child-data-privacy-bill-despite-criticism-of-hearing-process/) and [EPIC's summary](https://epic.org/new-hampshire-bans-the-sale-of-childrens-data/). <!-- verified: independent WebSearch corroborates sponsor list and House passage -->
- The New Hampshire Department of Justice's Consumer Protection and Antitrust Bureau — which enforces the NHDPA — raised concerns during hearings that the categorical ban could affect its ability to investigate and prosecute matters in coordination with federal COPPA enforcement, though the bill was signed notwithstanding that testimony, per the [New Hampshire Bulletin](https://newhampshirebulletin.com/briefs/ayotte-signs-law-prohibiting-the-sale-of-childrens-data/) and legislative fiscal note referenced in [LegiScan's bill tracking](https://legiscan.com/NH/bill/HB1460/2026). <!-- verified: independent WebSearch corroborates DOJ hearing testimony re: COPPA-coordinated enforcement concern -->
- The amendment takes effect January 1, 2027, per [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-hampshire-amends-the-nhdpa-to-prohibit-the-sale-of-childrens-personal-data), [EPIC](https://epic.org/new-hampshire-bans-the-sale-of-childrens-data/), and [DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/new-hampshire-law-prohibiting-sale-childrens-personal). <!-- verified -->
- The New Hampshire DOJ's fiscal note estimated an indeterminable fiscal impact on enforcement, ranging from $0 to $100,000 annually, depending on whether the law streamlines or increases the volume of investigations, per [LegiScan's bill status page](https://legiscan.com/NH/bill/HB1460/2026). <!-- unverifiable: source unavailable via direct fetch this session; exact dollar figures not independently re-confirmed via WebSearch in round 2, carried forward as minor open item -->

## Action Items

- Organizations operating in New Hampshire that qualify as "controllers" under the NHDPA (currently applicable to entities that control or process personal data of 35,000+ NH consumers, or 10,000+ consumers with 25%+ revenue from data sales) should audit vendor and ad-tech data-sharing arrangements now to identify any flows involving data known or reasonably likely to belong to a child under 13, ahead of the January 1, 2027 effective date.
- Update consent management and age-verification/age-assurance processes: because the ban applies even without actual knowledge that a data subject is a child, entities serving general-audience products used by minors should consider adopting more conservative default treatment of unverified-age users' data for sale purposes.
- Review data broker and monetization contracts (including those covering location data) for children's data carve-outs or termination provisions that need to be updated before the effective date.
- Monitor for the NH DOJ's implementing guidance or FAQ updates, given the agency's own hearing testimony flagging potential tension with COPPA-coordinated enforcement; the NH DOJ maintains a [Data Privacy Act enforcement page](https://www.doj.nh.gov/data-privacy-enforcement) that should be watched for updates.
- Track parallel developments in other states — commentators (EPIC) note this follows a broader 2026 trend of states amending already-enacted comprehensive privacy laws (Delaware, Connecticut, Maryland, Virginia, Utah) to add stronger children's-data protections, and a Vermont advocacy petition has already cited New Hampshire's law as a model.
