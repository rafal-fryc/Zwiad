# US House Passes the KIDS Act (H.R. 7757) 267-117, Sending Child Online Safety Omnibus to a Skeptical Senate

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy — Children's Privacy | **Date:** June 29-30, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

The U.S. House of Representatives passed the Kids Internet and Digital Safety (KIDS) Act, H.R. 7757, by a bipartisan 267-117 vote on June 29, 2026, moving the omnibus child online safety package — which folds in a revised Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA), COPPA 2.0, age-verification and platform-design mandates, AI chatbot safeguards, and a federal data broker registry — out of the House for the first time and on to the Senate, according to [GovTrack's roll call record](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/votes/119-2026/h228) and [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/us-house-passes-the-kids-act). <!-- verified --> This follows the bipartisan committee compromise reached June 22-24, 2026 (covered in [Zwiad's June 28 report](federal-kids-act-hr7757-2026-06-28.md)), which dropped KOSA's enforceable "duty of care" in exchange for cross-aisle support. <!-- verified --> The bill now faces resistance in the Senate, where original KOSA co-sponsors Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) and Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) have said the House version — without a duty of care — is insufficient, per [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/house/5946180-house-passes-kids-online-safety-package/) and [The Record](https://therecord.media/house-passes-kids-online-safety-bill-senate-unlikely). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- **Vote result and date:** The House passed H.R. 7757 by a vote of 267-117 on June 29, 2026 (House Roll Call Vote #228, 119th Congress, 2nd Session), per the [official Clerk of the House roll call record](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2026228) and [GovTrack](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/votes/119-2026/h228). <!-- verified -->
- **Bipartisan but not unanimous:** Coverage from [Yahoo News/The Hill](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/house-passes-kids-internet-digital-170000990.html) and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/kids-internet-and-digital-safety-act-passes-house-free-speech-concerns-rcna352341) describes the vote as crossing party lines in both directions — with some conservatives objecting to new mandates on platforms and some progressives objecting to age-verification and free-speech concerns. <!-- verified -->
- **Scope of the passed package:** As passed, H.R. 7757 combines a revised KOSA (without an enforceable duty of care), an update to the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA 2.0) extending protections from children under 13 to teens up to 17, a ban on targeted advertising to minors, the Safe Messaging for Kids Act, the SPY Kids Act, the Safer GAMING Act, the SAFE Bots Act (AI chatbot safeguards), and a federal data broker disclosure regime, per [Pillsbury Winthrop's client alert](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/kids-act-house-ai-chatbot-safety.html) and [IAPP's scope explainer](https://iapp.org/news/a/unpacking-the-scope-of-the-kids-act). <!-- verified -->
- **AI chatbot provisions:** The SAFE Bots Act component requires AI chatbots interacting with minors to disclose they are not human, to provide mental-health/crisis resources when a minor's prompts suggest self-harm or suicide risk, and to avoid promoting certain harmful topics, per [Pillsbury](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/kids-act-house-ai-chatbot-safety.html). <!-- verified -->
- **No duty of care:** Consistent with the June 22-24 committee compromise, the version the House passed omits KOSA's legally enforceable "duty of care" and instead relies on annual third-party safety audits and "reasonable policies" requirements, per [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/us-house-passes-the-kids-act) and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/a-major-online-safety-bill-for-kids-just-passed-the-housewhat-parents-need-to-know.html). <!-- verified -->
- **Age verification remains the flashpoint:** [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/07/house-passed-kids-act-senate-should-reject-it) argues that although the bill's text disclaims any government-ID requirement, its age-related obligations will functionally push platforms toward collecting more identifying data (drivers' licenses, biometric scans) to determine which users are minors — a concern echoed by digital-rights and free-speech groups. <!-- verified -->
- **Senate reception is skeptical:** Senate KOSA authors Blumenthal and Blackburn have signaled the House-passed version is unacceptable without a duty of care standard, and [The Record/Recorded Future News](https://therecord.media/house-passes-kids-online-safety-bill-senate-unlikely) reports Senate passage in its current form is considered unlikely; Sen. Blackburn is reportedly negotiating a separate White House-backed vehicle that would restore a duty-of-care standard. <!-- verified -->

## Official Text [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

The engrossed, House-passed text of H.R. 7757 (119th Congress) is available on [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/7757/text), which also tracks the bill's status (passed House, referred to Senate). The official roll call vote (#228) is published by the [Clerk of the U.S. House of Representatives](https://clerk.house.gov/Votes/2026228). No Senate companion has been formally adopted as of this writing; the Senate's own KOSA/COPPA 2.0 vehicles (e.g., S. 1748) remain separate legislative tracks pending reconciliation, per [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text). <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- **Do not treat House passage as enactment:** H.R. 7757 has passed only one chamber. Organizations should continue current compliance planning under existing COPPA and state children's privacy laws rather than accelerating changes based on this bill alone, given the Senate's stated opposition to the no-duty-of-care approach.
- **Track Senate developments closely:** Watch for whether Sens. Blackburn and Blumenthal advance a competing Senate package restoring the duty of care, and whether any conference or negotiated text emerges. A materially different Senate bill would reset compliance planning.
- **Assess AI chatbot exposure now:** Companies operating AI chatbots or conversational agents accessible to minors should begin gap-analyzing current practices against the SAFE Bots Act's disclosure and crisis-resource requirements, since this is a newer and less-litigated area than COPPA compliance generally.
- **Monitor age-verification technology developments:** Given the bill's reliance on "effective, commercially available" age-assurance methods rather than a specific mandated technology, organizations should track NIST and industry age-assurance standards work as a leading indicator of what "effective" may ultimately mean in implementing regulations.
- **Watch state preemption scope:** As with the committee-stage bill, the House-passed version's preemption language (narrowed to conflicting state laws only) has direct implications for compliance under state children's codes (e.g., California CAADCA, Maryland's kids code, Colorado SB 26-051); confirm no material broadening of preemption occurred between committee text and the floor-passed version.
