# Texas App Store Accountability Act Takes Effect After Fifth Circuit Stays Preliminary Injunction

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** Privacy / Children's Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-15

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals formally granted Texas a stay pending appeal on June 4, 2026, lifting the preliminary injunction that had blocked enforcement of Texas's App Store Accountability Act (SB 2420) since December 2025. The law is now in effect: app store operators including Apple and Google must verify user ages and obtain verified parental consent before minors can download or purchase apps in Texas. A separate First Amendment challenge has been filed with the U.S. Supreme Court, but the statute remains enforceable while the constitutional merits proceed through the Fifth Circuit.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Texas Governor Greg Abbott signed [Senate Bill 2420](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/Text.aspx?LegSess=89R&Bill=SB2420) — the App Store Accountability Act — on May 27, 2025, with an effective date of January 1, 2026. The 89th Legislature passed the bill 30-1 in the Senate and 120-9 in the House.
- On December 23, 2025, U.S. District Judge Robert Pitman (W.D. Tex.) granted a preliminary injunction in [*CCIA v. Paxton*](https://docs.reclaimthenet.org/ccia-v-paxton-sb2420-preliminary-injunction-2025-12-23.pdf), finding SB 2420 "more likely than not unconstitutional" under strict First Amendment scrutiny and barring Attorney General Ken Paxton from enforcement.
- On May 28, 2026, the Fifth Circuit issued an administrative stay; on June 4, 2026, it formally [granted a stay pending appeal](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-major-victory-protecting-children-online-requiring-age), concluding that "Texas has made a strong showing that it is likely to succeed on the merits" and that the district court committed reversible errors, per [Texas Tribune reporting](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/05/28/texas-apple-google-app-store-age-verification/).
- The Fifth Circuit issued the stay without a published written opinion, which [legal commentators note](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2026/06/fifth-circuit-keeps-doing-fifth-circuit-things-%F0%9F%93%89-seat-v-paxton.htm) made it "virtually impossible to appeal" because the reasoning is undisclosed.
- On June 11, 2026, Students Engaged in Advancing Texas (SEAT), represented by [Davis Wright Tremaine LLP](https://www.dwt.com/about/news/2026/06/texas-youth-scotus-first-amendment-app-law), filed an emergency application to the U.S. Supreme Court asking the justices to vacate the Fifth Circuit's stay. The Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA) filed a [parallel Supreme Court petition](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/ccia-entreats-us-supreme-court-to-intervene-in-texas-app-store-age-check-law).
- New Apple Accounts created in Texas now require age confirmation; parental consent is required before minors can download or purchase apps, per [Apple Insider coverage](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/03/age-verification-now-mandatory-for-app-store-users-in-texas).
- Violations are treated as deceptive trade practices under the Texas Business & Commerce Code, with civil penalties of [up to $10,000 per violation](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318041/20260609/texas-app-store-age-verification-active-sb-2420-ends-anonymous-access-developers-face-10k-penalty.htm) for non-compliant developers.
- Texas AG Ken Paxton is authorized to enforce SB 2420; the [AG's office](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-major-victory-protecting-children-online-requiring-age) characterized the Fifth Circuit's stay as "a major victory protecting children online."

## What the Law Requires [HIGH confidence]

SB 2420 imposes obligations on two categories of regulated entities:

**App Store Operators (Apple, Google, and similar platforms):**
- Implement commercially reasonable methods to verify the age of each user at account creation
- Assign users to one of four age categories: under 13, 13-15, 16-17, and 18 or older
- Require that a minor's account be affiliated with a verified parent or legal guardian's account
- Obtain verified parental consent before a minor can download an app or make an in-app purchase
- Share age category and consent information with app developers

**App Developers:**
- Assign age ratings to their applications consistent with app store ratings frameworks
- Provide notice of significant changes to application content or features
- Use age and consent data received from app stores only for age-appropriate content restrictions, legal compliance, and safety features — not for advertising or other purposes

The [Pillsbury law firm's overview](https://www.pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/app-store-accountability-act-texas.html) and [Reed Smith's analysis](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102kddh/texas-law-requires-age-verification-for-app-stores-and-developers/) provide detailed compliance breakdowns across both entity categories.

## Litigation Status [HIGH confidence]

The operative case at the Fifth Circuit is *CCIA v. Paxton*, No. 1:25-cv-01660. The appeal is on the merits of the constitutional challenge. The Fifth Circuit's stay indicates the panel views Texas as likely to prevail on the argument that the district court erred in applying strict First Amendment scrutiny to an age-verification framework targeting economic transactions rather than content curation.

A Volokh Conspiracy analysis in [Reason](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/06/05/texas-age-verification-parental-consent-requirements-for-app-stores-likely-constitutional-fifth-circuit-holds/) explains that the Fifth Circuit's approach — treating the law as regulating commercial transactions rather than speech — diverges from the district court's framing and from how the Ninth Circuit has treated similar state children's privacy laws.

The Supreme Court emergency applications remain pending. If the Court declines to intervene, the law remains in force through the Fifth Circuit merits decision.

## State Law Landscape [MEDIUM confidence]

Texas is one of several states enacting App Store Accountability Acts. As of June 2026, per the [Future of Privacy Forum's comparison](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/):

- **Utah**: Enacted an ASAA; subsequently amended, with full compliance deadline extended to May 6, 2027
- **Louisiana**: ASAA enacted but effective date delayed by amendment to July 1, 2027
- **Alabama**: Governor signed HB 161 (app store accountability) on February 17, 2026, per [Troutman Privacy reporting](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/02/alabama-enacts-app-store-law/)
- **California**: Enacted a modified version as the Digital Age Assurance Act
- Additional bills pending in at least 10 states including Florida, Georgia, Arizona, and others

The [Wiley law firm's alert](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Key-Developments-With-State-App-Store-Accountability-Acts-as-Texas-Act-Takes-Effect) notes that the Texas law's taking effect creates significant multi-state compliance pressure on Apple and Google, who must now operationalize compliance frameworks simultaneously across multiple jurisdictions with differing requirements.

## Action Items

- **App store operators (Apple, Google):** Age verification and parental consent requirements are operative now in Texas. Existing accounts may be exempt under Apple's implementation, but new accounts require immediate compliance. Verify that consent flows for minors in Texas are live and documented.
- **App developers:** Confirm age ratings have been assigned to all published applications and that the ratings are current. Ensure that any age or consent data received from Apple or Google is used only for permitted purposes (content restrictions, legal compliance, safety) — using this data for advertising is a violation.
- **Developers without Apple/Google distribution integration:** Review [Reed Smith's compliance guide](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102kddh/texas-law-requires-age-verification-for-app-stores-and-developers/) for obligations that exist independently of the app store operator's compliance stack.
- **All affected entities:** Monitor the U.S. Supreme Court emergency docket for *SEAT v. Paxton* and any CCIA application. A SCOTUS stay of the Fifth Circuit's order would reinstate the preliminary injunction and pause enforcement — but cannot be relied upon at this stage.
- **Multi-state compliance teams:** Audit SB 2420 requirements against obligations in Utah, Alabama, and California to identify overlaps and divergences. Louisiana's July 2027 delay provides additional runway for that jurisdiction.
- **Legal teams:** Note that the Fifth Circuit issued its stay without a written opinion; track the merits briefing schedule at the Fifth Circuit for the first opportunity to review the panel's constitutional reasoning.
