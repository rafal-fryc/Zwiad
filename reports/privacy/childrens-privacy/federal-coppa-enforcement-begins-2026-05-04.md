# FTC Begins Enforcing Updated COPPA Rule: Biometric Data, Third-Party Disclosure, and Penalties Now Active

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-05-04

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

As of April 22, 2026, the Federal Trade Commission began enforcing the most significant amendments to the [Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (COPPA Rule, 16 CFR Part 312)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule) in over a decade. The amended Rule — finalized in January 2025 and effective since June 2025 — now covers biometric identifiers and government-issued ID numbers as personal information, requires separate opt-in parental consent for third-party data disclosures, mandates a written information security program, and imposes a data retention cap. FTC Commissioner Mark Meador has publicly stated the Commission is ["willing and eager"](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect) to pursue violations, with civil penalties of up to **$53,088 per violation per day** now available.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Enforcement began April 22, 2026**: The compliance deadline has passed. The FTC's [Associate Director of Advertising Practices Ben Wiseman](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect) forecast "more cases in the coming months and years" as children's privacy is a stated agency priority.
- **Commissioner Meador's statement**: At the [IAPP Global Privacy Summit in late March 2026](https://iapp.org/news/a/iapp-global-summit-2026-ftc-commissioner-meador-stresses-agency-preference-for-case-by-case-enforcement), Commissioner Meador called protecting children online a priority and confirmed the Commission's readiness to enforce new obligations.
- **Civil penalty ceiling**: Each COPPA violation carries a maximum civil penalty of **$53,088 per day per violation**, as adjusted under the [Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). Prior enforcement settlements include: YouTube ($170M), Epic Games ($275M), Disney ($10M), Cognosphere ($20M in January 2025).
- **Biometric identifiers now covered**: The expanded definition of "personal information" under [16 CFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-312) now includes fingerprints, voiceprints, retina and iris patterns, facial templates, gait patterns, and DNA sequences — any identifier that can be used for automated or semi-automated recognition ([Federal Register 90 FR 20498](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).
- **Government-issued identifiers added**: State ID numbers and passport numbers (beyond SSNs) are now covered personal information.
- **Separate parental consent for third-party disclosures**: Operators must obtain a **distinct, unbundled verifiable parental consent (VPC)** before sharing children's data with any third party for purposes not "integral" to the operator's service — including targeted advertising and AI model training ([White & Case analysis](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/unpacking-ftcs-coppa-amendments-what-you-need-know)).
- **Parental notice must name third parties**: Direct parental notices must now identify the specific third parties to whom children's data is disclosed, along with the purposes of those disclosures ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)).
- **AI training explicitly excluded from "integral" use**: The FTC's rulemaking commentary explicitly states that disclosing children's data to train AI models is never "integral" to a website or online service and always requires separate VPC ([Alston & Bird](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/ftc-publishes-amendments-to-coppa-rule/)).
- **Written information security program required**: Operators must now maintain a formal written WISP with designated coordinator, annual risk assessments, and documented safeguards. A general information security program can satisfy this requirement if it addresses children's data ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule)).
- **Data retention policy required and public**: Operators must publish their data retention schedule — including retention periods and deletion processes — in the online notice, and must delete children's data once the stated purpose is fulfilled ([eCFR 16 CFR 312.10](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.10)).
- **Age-verification enforcement relief**: In February 2026, the FTC issued a [Policy Statement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/02/ftc-issues-coppa-policy-statement-incentivize-use-age-verification-technologies-protect-children) exempting operators from VPC requirements when collecting data solely to operate compliant age-verification technology — a narrow carve-out designed to encourage age-gating adoption.

## Action Items

Organizations that have not yet achieved full compliance under the amended Rule should treat the following as urgent:

- **Audit biometric and government-ID data flows immediately**: Confirm whether any child-directed or mixed-audience service collects fingerprints, voiceprints, facial images, iris scans, or government-issued ID numbers from users. Each unconsented collection is a discrete COPPA violation.
- **Disaggregate parental consent flows**: Replace any bundled consent-to-collection checkboxes with separate, purpose-specific consent prompts for each third-party disclosure. Blanket consent no longer satisfies the Rule for advertising or data-sharing disclosures.
- **Update privacy notices to name third-party recipients**: Audit all direct parental notices to confirm they identify specific third parties receiving children's data and the purposes of each disclosure.
- **Gate or cease AI training on children's data**: If children's personal information is used to train or improve any AI model, implement a separate consent gate or suspend such use immediately — the FTC has made clear this use is never "integral."
- **Complete or remediate the WISP**: If a written information security program is not yet in place or does not specifically address children's personal information, designate a coordinator and complete documented safeguards as soon as possible.
- **Publish and enforce the data retention policy**: Verify the online notice includes the specific retention schedule; conduct initial purge of data held beyond stated retention periods.
- **Prepare for enforcement**: Expect the FTC to initiate COPPA investigations in the near term. Maintain documentation of consent flows, third-party disclosure records, VPC mechanisms, WISP, and retention schedules as potential evidence of compliance.
