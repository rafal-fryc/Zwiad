# White House Brokers Deal to Shield Meta and Google CEOs from Senate Child Safety Testimony, Backing James T. Woods Act

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy / Children's Online Safety | **Date:** June 25, 2026

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

The White House intervened in June 2026 to spare Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and Google CEO Sundar Pichai from personally testifying before the Senate Judiciary Committee at a planned child online safety hearing, [according to reporting by Politico](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/technology/4625140/mark-zuckerberg-sundar-pichai-spared-senate-hearing-internet-child-safety/) citing five sources. In exchange for Senate Judiciary Chairman Chuck Grassley (R-IA) accepting lower-level company executives in lieu of the CEOs, the Trump administration publicly endorsed the Grassley-Durbin James T. Woods Act, a bipartisan anti-sextortion and child exploitation legislative package. The substitution allows Instagram CEO Adam Mosseri to appear for Meta and YouTube CEO Neal Mohan to appear for Google at the July 28, 2026 hearing. The arrangement has drawn criticism from child safety advocates and lawmakers who argue the administration shielded powerful technology executives from accountability. This episode unfolds against the backdrop of parallel, contested legislative negotiations: the House Energy and Commerce Committee has advanced a revised bipartisan KIDS Act package consolidating KOSA, COPPA 2.0, and over a dozen other child safety bills, while Senate KOSA authors have declared the House bill "dead in the Senate."

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

Congressional scrutiny of Big Tech's role in children's online safety reached a climax in January 2024, when the Senate Judiciary Committee convened a landmark hearing at which Meta, TikTok, Snap, X Corp, and Discord CEOs testified before senators and a packed public gallery. The hearing — marked by emotional testimony from parents of children harmed by social media — generated widespread public attention and renewed legislative momentum.

Following that 2024 hearing, the Senate passed the Kids Online Safety and Privacy Act (KOSPA) — a combined package of the Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA) and the Children and Teens' Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA 2.0) — with a [bipartisan 91-3 vote in July 2024](https://www.blumenthal.senate.gov/about/issues/kids-online-safety-act). The House did not advance the bill before the end of the 118th Congress, nullifying the legislation's progress. In May 2025, Sens. Blackburn and Blumenthal reintroduced KOSA in the 119th Congress, joined by Sens. Thune and Schumer. (Blackburn is the lead sponsor; the bill is commonly identified as the Blackburn-Blumenthal bill.)

In the House, Chairman Brett Guthrie (R-KY) of the House Energy and Commerce Committee advanced the Kids Internet and Digital Safety (KIDS) Act, H.R. 7757, through a [28-24 party-line markup vote in March 2026](https://www.k12dive.com/news/kids-online-safety-act-moves-to-a-vote-on-the-full-house-floor/814216/), with Democrats objecting to preemption language and the absence of a duty-of-care standard. On June 22, 2026, Chairman Guthrie and Ranking Member Frank Pallone (D-NJ) announced a [bipartisan agreement on a revised KIDS Act text](https://energycommerce.house.gov/posts/chairman-guthrie-and-ranking-member-pallone-announce-agreement-on-bipartisan-kids-safety-package) that added new provisions and secured Democratic support.

In parallel, Senate Judiciary Chairman Grassley called on the CEOs of Meta, Google, TikTok, and Snap to testify at a summer 2026 hearing focused on child safety, predatory exploitation, and digital sextortion — topics that received renewed attention following a wave of criminal cases involving online sextortion of minors. [Meta representatives met with the White House in late May and early June 2026](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/technology/4625140/mark-zuckerberg-sundar-pichai-spared-senate-hearing-internet-child-safety/), expressing concerns that personal CEO testimony would amplify negative attention related to ongoing child online safety litigation.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### The White House Intervention and Legislative Quid Pro Quo

According to Politico's reporting — cited by [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/technology/4625140/mark-zuckerberg-sundar-pichai-spared-senate-hearing-internet-child-safety/), [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/trump-administration-tech-ceos-senate-hearing-child-safety-1805154), and [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/social-media/26/06/60094975/metas-mark-zuckerberg-googles-sundar-pichai-reportedly-spared-from-child-safety-senate-testimony-as-white-house-steps-in-report) — the Trump administration negotiated directly with Chairman Grassley to modify who would testify. The arrangement structured as follows:

- **Tech companies get:** Zuckerberg and Pichai are excused from personal testimony. Instagram CEO Adam Mosseri will represent Meta, YouTube CEO Neal Mohan will represent Google, Snap CEO Evan Spiegel and TikTok CEO Adam Presser are also expected at the July 28, 2026 hearing.
- **Grassley gets:** The White House publicly endorses Grassley's James T. Woods Act.
- **White House gives:** Political credibility and executive branch support for a narrow anti-sextortion legislative package, without endorsing the broader KOSA duty-of-care framework.

The White House formally announced its endorsement of the James T. Woods Act via a [Grassley Senate press release](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/white-house-endorses-grassley-durbin-james-t-woods-act), framing the bill as a critical tool against the growing epidemic of online sextortion. The endorsement came approximately two weeks before the June 25 Politico report linking it explicitly to the hearing substitution arrangement. [Local coverage from Ohio](https://www.cleveland19.com/2026/06/10/white-house-endorses-first-federal-sextortion-bill-after-streetsboro-teens-death/) highlighted the personal backstory: the Act is named after James T. Woods, a minor who died by suicide after being groomed on Instagram, coerced into sending photos, and subjected to threatening messages.

### The James T. Woods Act

The James T. Woods Act ([H.R. 6719, 119th Congress](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/6719)) is a composite of three Grassley-Durbin bills:

1. **SAFE Act (Sentencing Accountability for Exploitation Act):** Updates federal sentencing guidelines related to child sexual abuse material (CSAM), including new enhancements for offenders who use technology to conceal identity.
2. **ECCHO Act (Ending Coercion of Children and Harm Online Act):** Creates a new federal crime prohibiting coercion of children into harming themselves or others. Per the [Senate Judiciary Committee press release](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/press/rep/releases/grassley-durbin-introduce-landmark-legislative-package-to-protect-kids-online) and [S. 3397 bill text on Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3397/text), the penalty structure is tiered: up to life imprisonment when the offense involves the actual or attempted suicide of the victim or the death of another person; up to 30 years' imprisonment for serious harmful conduct not involving death.
3. **Stop Sextortion Act:** Explicitly criminalizes intentional threats to distribute CSAM and establishes new offenses for digital sextortion.

The [Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously advanced the James T. Woods Act](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/press/rep/releases/the-james-t-woods-act-fights-online-child-exploitation-thats-sweeping-the-nation) in February 2026. As of the report date, the Act has since cleared the Senate Judiciary Committee and is now pending a full Senate floor vote, per [Senator Moody's press release](https://www.moody.senate.gov/press-releases/video-release-senator-moodys-james-t-woods-act-protecting-kids-by-targeting-perpetrators-of-sextortion-heads-to-the-senate-floor/) — which announced the bill "Heads to the Senate Floor" — and Senator Moody's public statement that the bill "passed out of @SenJudiciaryGOP and heads to the Senate Floor." The bill is endorsed by over two dozen law enforcement and child safety organizations. Notably, the Act is narrower in scope than KOSA, COPPA 2.0, or the House KIDS Act — it addresses criminal enforcement against individual perpetrators rather than imposing platform-level design or data obligations on social media companies.

### The House KIDS Act: Revised Bipartisan Package

The June 22, 2026 bipartisan KIDS Act agreement reached between Guthrie and Pallone — consolidated in the [official Energy and Commerce press release](https://energycommerce.house.gov/posts/chairman-guthrie-and-ranking-member-pallone-announce-agreement-on-bipartisan-kids-safety-package) — represents a significant restructuring of the March 2026 partisan bill. The revised package includes provisions from:

- Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA) — minus the duty-of-care standard
- Children and Teens' Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA 2.0)
- SCREEN Act, Safe Messaging for Kids Act, SPY KIDS Act, Safer GAMING Act, SAFE Bots Act
- Safe Social Media Act, No Fentanyl on Social Media Act, Data Broker Disclosures
- AWARE Act, Kids Internet and Safety Partnership Act
- Assessing Safety Tools for Parents and Minors Act, Promoting a Safe Internet for Minors Act

Key changes from the March markup include: [explicit removal of KOSA's duty-of-care provision](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318896/20260623/house-kids-act-deal-drops-kosa-duty-care-adds-age-verification-all-users.htm) (replaced with language stating the bill "cannot be construed to impose one"), addition of age verification requirements for all users, and inclusion of a federal data broker registry. The [House Energy and Commerce Committee subsequently advanced the KIDS Act to a full House floor vote](https://iapp.org/news/a/us-energy-and-commerce-committee-advances-kids-act-to-full-house-vote), with House Republican leadership indicating it could move as soon as the following week under suspension.

### Senate Opposition and Bicameral Tensions

Senate authors of KOSA reacted sharply. Blumenthal labeled the House compromise "toothless and tepid capitulation" that is "dead in the Senate and a betrayal of families suffering from Big Tech's greed," [as cited by Ben Brody of Bloomberg Government](https://x.com/BenBrodyDC/status/2069068456981385300). Blackburn joined Blumenthal in opposing the House version, arguing that removing the duty-of-care standard "gives a blank check to Mark Zuckerberg to exploit children." [As The Hill reports](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/5938935-house-breakthrough-on-kids-online-safety-faces-long-odds-in-senate/), three-quarters of the Senate has co-sponsored the version containing the duty-of-care provision, creating a structural obstacle even if the House passes the Guthrie-Pallone bill.

Separately, [Broadband Breakfast reported](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/democratic-senators-oppose-weak-house-kids-online-safety-act/) that Sen. Cantwell, along with Blumenthal, publicly raised the alarm about the House version heading to a floor vote, arguing the weakened KOSA elements and federal preemption language would undermine existing state protections.

### Industry and Advocacy Dimensions

Meta's behind-the-scenes advocacy to avoid Zuckerberg's personal testimony reflects the company's concern that a repeat of the contentious 2024 hearing — particularly in the context of ongoing multidistrict litigation (MDL 3047) involving Meta's alleged harm to children — would heighten litigation risk and reputational exposure. The Politico report, sourced to five individuals, makes clear that Meta initiated outreach to the White House, not the reverse.

Critics including [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/trump-administration-tech-ceos-senate-hearing-child-safety-1805154) and [The Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/06/25/tech-oligarchs-mark-zuckerberg-google-ceo-sundar-pichai-judiciary-committee-testimonies/) noted that the arrangement creates a tiered accountability system: ordinary witnesses must appear when subpoenaed, while powerful technology executives can negotiate around congressional oversight via executive branch intermediaries. According to [The Daily Caller's reporting](https://dailycaller.com/2026/06/25/tech-oligarchs-mark-zuckerberg-google-ceo-sundar-pichai-judiciary-committee-testimonies/), a Grassley spokesperson stated the chairman "isn't interested in simply generating clicks and views online like past hearings." This quote has not been confirmed by a Grassley primary press statement and should be treated as attributed secondary reporting.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

**For Congress and Executive Oversight:** The White House's direct intervention in a congressional hearing format — leveraging legislative endorsements to influence who testifies — represents a notable precedent in executive-legislative relations on technology policy. It may signal to other major technology companies that White House relationships can function as a buffer against congressional scrutiny.

**For Social Media Platforms (Meta, Google, Snap, TikTok):** In the immediate term, Zuckerberg and Pichai avoid high-profile, adversarial testimony that carries litigation risk. However, the arrangement also places meta-level political pressure on the James T. Woods Act — if it fails to pass despite White House endorsement, the trade was arguably unfavorable for the administration. For Snap and TikTok, whose CEOs are still expected to testify personally, no equivalent arrangement appears to have been secured.

**For Children's Online Safety Legislation:** The dual-track legislative environment (House KIDS Act vs. Senate KOSA) creates genuine uncertainty. Even if the House passes the KIDS Act under suspension, the stated Senate opposition from KOSA co-authors — who command three-quarters of the chamber as co-sponsors for their stronger version — suggests the bill faces a difficult conference process or may stall entirely. The James T. Woods Act, by contrast, has bipartisan Senate Judiciary support, White House backing, and is now positioned for a full Senate floor vote, giving it the clearest path to enactment of any child safety measure currently in play.

**For Child Safety Advocates:** The deal has been received negatively by advocacy organizations tracking children's online harms, who argue that substituting division-level executives for CEOs reduces accountability and signals that political relationships can dilute congressional oversight. The arrangement comes at a time when lawsuits against Meta, TikTok, and other platforms for alleged harm to children are proceeding through courts nationwide.

**Compliance and Regulatory Outlook:** No immediate compliance obligations arise from this political development. The outcome of pending legislation — KIDS Act, James T. Woods Act, or a bicameral compromise — will determine actual legal obligations for covered platforms.

## Action Items

- Monitor the July 28, 2026 Senate Judiciary Committee hearing: testimony from Mosseri (Meta/Instagram), Mohan (Google/YouTube), Spiegel (Snap), and Presser (TikTok) may surface new platform policy commitments or admissions relevant to litigation.
- Track the James T. Woods Act (H.R. 6719) Senate floor vote — as of June 2026, the bill has cleared Senate Judiciary Committee and is pending full Senate consideration with White House backing. Its narrower criminal-law scope and bipartisan support give it the strongest near-term passage prospects of any pending child safety bill.
- Track whether the House KIDS Act moves to a full floor vote and whether it passes under suspension, which would require a two-thirds majority.
- Assess whether the Senate will act on a conference or compromise between the House KIDS Act and Senate KOSA versions, particularly around the duty-of-care provision, given the stated opposition from Blackburn and Blumenthal.
- For platforms subject to COPPA, KOSA, or KIDS Act provisions: begin assessing what the KIDS Act's age verification requirements and data broker registry obligations would require operationally, even before any bill passes, as the legislative trajectory suggests some version of comprehensive federal child safety legislation is likely to advance.
- Note in litigation risk assessments: the MDL 3047 multidistrict litigation against Meta involving alleged harms to children is an independent risk vector not directly affected by this legislative outcome.
