# Nebraska Amends Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act: Broader Applicability and Six New Prohibited Design Features

**Jurisdiction:** Nebraska | **Category:** Privacy / Children's Online Safety | **Date:** April 14, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 14, 2026, Nebraska Governor Jim Pillen signed [LB 838](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB838.pdf) into law, significantly amending Nebraska's Age-Appropriate Online Design Code Act (AAODCA), which itself only took effect January 1, 2026. The amendment replaces a narrow, data-sales-revenue applicability trigger with a broader "majority of annual revenue from online services" test, bringing a substantially wider universe of technology companies into scope. It also expands the list of prohibited or restricted design features from five to eleven. Amendments take effect July 17, 2026 — three months after the Nebraska legislature's April 17, 2026 adjournment date — leaving covered companies approximately two months to assess and remediate affected products.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Key dates:** Governor Pillen signed LB 838 on **April 14, 2026** (as shown on the [official slip law PDF](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB838.pdf)). The Nebraska legislature adjourned on **April 17, 2026**. Amendments are **effective July 17, 2026** — three months after the legislature's adjournment date, per the constitutional default effective date in Nebraska.

- **LB 838 is an omnibus bill** amending multiple Nebraska statutes; the AAODCA amendments (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-1301 et seq.) are the focus of this report.

- **Applicability threshold revised:** The original AAODCA (enacted as LB 504, effective January 1, 2026) required, as the revenue-source prong of the coverage test, that a company derive at least **50% of annual revenue from the sale or sharing of consumers' personal data**. LB 838 deletes that revenue-source prong and substitutes a requirement that the company **"generates a majority of its annual revenue from online services"** — a materially broader standard that covers subscription platforms, ad-supported services, and others that do not monetize primarily through data sales. The remaining prongs (conducting business in Nebraska; acting as a data controller; annual gross revenue exceeding $25 million OR processing data of 50,000+ consumers, households, or devices) are preserved. Source: [Troutman Privacy + Cyber + AI](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/nebraska-amends-age-appropriate-online-design-code-act/); [CCIA Opposition Comments](https://ccianet.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CCIA-Comments-on-Nebraska-LB-838-Oppose.pdf).

- **Six new covered design features added:** The definition of "covered design feature" grows from five to eleven items. The six additions are: (1) **auto-playing media** (videos or content that play without affirmative user action); (2) **engagement quantification tools** (visible metrics such as like counts and comment tallies); (3) **gamification elements** including streaks, badges, and rewards that motivate more frequent or extensive use; (4) **virtual currencies** used within the service or used to purchase digital items; (5) **features exploiting ephemerality** to prompt urgent use; and (6) **AI-driven intimacy features** — artificial-intelligence-powered features that seek to elicit feelings of intimacy from the user by creating the illusion of interacting with a human being. Source: [Troutman Privacy + Cyber + AI](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/nebraska-amends-age-appropriate-online-design-code-act/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/19173-bill-amending-age-appropriate-online-design-code-act-lb838).

- **Enforcement and penalties — dual track:** The AAODCA provides for both private civil suits and Attorney General enforcement:
  - **Private right of action:** A minor, or a minor's parent or guardian, may bring a civil action against a covered company to recover actual damages, equitable or declaratory relief, and reasonable attorney's fees and litigation costs for AAODCA violations. Source: [Nebraska LB 504 slip law](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB504.pdf); [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/nebraska-enacts-new-laws-protecting-children-online).
  - **AG enforcement:** The Nebraska Attorney General may enforce the AAODCA, with civil penalties of up to **$2,500 per violation**. AG enforcement authority becomes active July 1, 2026 (a date set in the original AAODCA, not amended by LB 838). Source: [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/nebraska-enacts-new-laws-protecting-children-online); [Koley Jessen](https://www.koleyjessen.com/insights/publications/nebraska-to-enact-childrens-online-privacy-by-design-law).
  - **DTPA overlay:** Any AAODCA violation also constitutes a separate violation of the Nebraska Uniform Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA), carrying an additional civil penalty of up to **$50,000 per violation**, also enforced by the AG. The DTPA penalty is an additional, separate exposure layered on top of the $2,500 AAODCA penalty — not a substitute for it. Source: [Hunton Andrews Kurth](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/nebraska-enacts-new-laws-protecting-children-online).

- **Industry opposition:** The Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA) filed formal opposition comments, arguing the broadened applicability standard is vague and that covered services cannot easily ascertain their responsibilities. See [CCIA Comments](https://ccianet.org/library/ccia-comments-on-nebraska-lb-838/).

- **Context — national landscape:** Nebraska's AAODCA is modeled on the UK Age-Appropriate Design Code and California's Age-Appropriate Design Code Act (CAADCA). Nebraska was the **third state** to enact such legislation, signing LB 504 on May 30, 2025, following California (2022) and Maryland (2024). Vermont subsequently enacted its own Age-Appropriate Design Code Act (S.B. 69), signed on June 12, 2025 — making Vermont the fourth state. Nebraska and Vermont thus both passed design code statutes in 2025, though their approaches differ materially. Loeb & Loeb describes the LB 838 amendments as a broadening of an already-active legal framework. Source: [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/nebraska-and-vermont-pass-age-appropriate-design-codes.html); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/passle/2026/05/nebraska-amends-and-broadens-its-ageappropriate-design-code); [Hunton (Vermont)](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/vermont-enacts-age-appropriate-design-code).

## Action Items

- **Reassess applicability immediately:** Companies that previously concluded they fell outside the AAODCA because they did not derive 50%+ of revenue from data sales must re-run the applicability analysis under the new "majority of annual revenue from online services" standard. The effective date is July 17, 2026.

- **Audit all six new design feature categories:** Map existing product features against the six additions — auto-playing media, engagement quantification, gamification, virtual currencies, ephemerality features, and AI intimacy features — to determine which are subject to the AAODCA's opt-out or default-off requirements.

- **Review minor-detection and age-estimation processes:** The broader applicability test may bring services into scope that have not yet built minor-identification workflows. These processes must be operational before July 17, 2026.

- **Align push notification schedules and default settings:** The original AAODCA requirements (push notifications disabled 10 pm – 6 am and 8 am – 4 pm for minors; highest-protection defaults; chronological feed option) remain in force. New covered companies must implement these alongside the expanded design-feature restrictions.

- **Assess private-litigation exposure:** Unlike many state privacy statutes that confine enforcement to the AG, the AAODCA's private right of action exposes covered companies to individual and potentially class-action suits by minors and their parents. Legal teams should evaluate the scope of this exposure under the amended applicability standard.

- **Monitor AG enforcement posture:** AG enforcement authority against covered companies is active as of July 1, 2026 — meaning the AG could begin investigating conduct under the amended law two weeks after the amendments take effect on July 17. Maximum combined exposure per violation is $52,500 ($2,500 AAODCA + $50,000 DTPA).

- **Watch for litigation:** California's CAADCA is currently before the Ninth Circuit following a lower-court ruling on DPIA requirements. A similar constitutional challenge to Nebraska's AAODCA is possible given CCIA's formal opposition.
