# California DOJ Opens 45-Day Public Comment on Draft Regulations Under SB 976: Protecting Our Kids from Social Media Addiction Act

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** June 1, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The California Department of Justice (DOJ) released its [proposed regulations](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/sb976-proposed-rulemaking.pdf) under Senate Bill 976, the Protecting Our Kids from Social Media Addiction Act, on May 14, 2026, opening a 45-day public comment period that closes June 30, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. PT. The draft rules clarify how operators of "addictive internet-based services or applications" must implement the statute's core obligations — age assurance, parental consent, notification time restrictions, and access controls — before the Attorney General's regulatory deadline of January 1, 2027. With the Ninth Circuit having [upheld the Act's core provisions](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ninth-circuit-upholds-addictive-social-media-feed-ban-and-default-privacy-settings-for-minors-in-californias-protecting-our-kids-from-social-media-addiction-act) in September 2025, compliance is no longer contingent on constitutional litigation — social media platforms and other covered operators must engage with this rulemaking now.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Rulemaking package released May 14, 2026.** The California DOJ released the [Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, proposed regulatory text, and Initial Statement of Reasons](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/california-department-justice-releases-proposed-protecting-our-kids-social-media) simultaneously, adding proposed rules to Title 11 of the California Code of Regulations. All materials are posted at [oag.ca.gov/sb976](https://oag.ca.gov/sb976).

- **45-day written comment period closes June 30, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. PT.** Comments must be submitted by e-mail to sb976@doj.ca.gov, by mail to California DOJ Consumer Protection Section, 1515 Clay Street, Oakland, CA 94612, or by phone at 510-879-3992. ([oag.ca.gov/meetings/613267](https://oag.ca.gov/meetings/613267))

- **Public hearing on June 30, 2026.** A public hearing is scheduled from 1:00–3:00 p.m. PT at the Elihu Harris Auditorium, 1515 Clay Street, Oakland, CA 94612, with remote participation available via video conference and teleconference. ([California DOJ press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/california-department-justice-releases-proposed-protecting-our-kids-social-media))

- **Regulations must be finalized by January 1, 2027.** SB 976 mandates that the Attorney General adopt regulations on age assurance and parental consent by that date. After January 1, 2027, the threshold for compliance shifts: operators must "reasonably determine" a user is not a minor pursuant to the regulations, not merely lack "actual knowledge." ([Bill text, Cal. Health & Safety Code § 27000 et seq.](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB976))

- **Draft rules address four areas.** The DOJ specifically seeks comment on: (1) methods and standards for age assurance on covered platforms; (2) ongoing operator obligations when performing age assurance; (3) parental consent requirements for minors' use of addictive features; and (4) implementation of the Act's "actual knowledge" standard. ([California DOJ preliminary rulemaking press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/california-department-justice-solicit-public-comment-sb-976-protecting-our-kids))

- **Ninth Circuit upheld core provisions September 9, 2025.** In *NetChoice v. Bonta*, the Ninth Circuit [refused to enjoin the addictive feed restrictions and private-mode defaults](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ninth-circuit-upholds-addictive-social-media-feed-ban-and-default-privacy-settings-for-minors-in-californias-protecting-our-kids-from-social-media-addiction-act), finding NetChoice did not meet its burden for a facial First Amendment challenge under *Moody v. NetChoice*. The court did strike down the engagement-metrics display restriction (like counts) as an unconstitutional content-based speech restriction. The case returns to the district court for trial on the merits, but the addictive feed and private-mode provisions are enforceable now. ([EPIC case page](https://epic.org/documents/netchoice-v-bonta-2/))

- **Current operative standard: "actual knowledge."** Until the regulations take effect on January 1, 2027, operators are only required to comply with the Act's feed and notification restrictions when they have "actual knowledge" that a user is a minor. After the regulatory effective date, a "reasonable determination" standard applies. ([Inside Privacy analysis](https://www.insideprivacy.com/uncategorized/california-passes-law-to-protect-minors-from-addictive-feeds/))

## Statutory Background [HIGH confidence]

Governor Newsom signed SB 976 on September 20, 2024. The Act, codified at California Health and Safety Code §§ 27000–27007 (Chapter 24, Division 20), makes it unlawful for the operator of an "addictive internet-based service or application" to:

1. **Provide an addictive feed** to a user when the operator has actual knowledge the user is a minor, unless the operator has obtained verifiable parental consent. (After January 1, 2027: when the operator has "reasonably determined" the user is not a minor.)
2. **Send notifications to minor users** between 12:00 a.m.–6:00 a.m. or between 8:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m. (local time) on school days (Monday–Friday, September through May), absent verifiable parental consent.

Additionally, operators must provide verified parents with mechanisms to:
- Prevent their child's access during specified hours;
- Limit daily addictive-feed use to a configurable time cap (default: one hour per day);
- Disable display of engagement metrics (likes and similar feedback) to their child by default.

Operators are required to make annual disclosures of: total minor users; number of verifiable parental consents received; and the number of minor users for whom access controls are enabled or disabled.

Enforcement is by the California Attorney General in a civil action brought in the name of the People of California. The Act does not provide a private right of action. ([SB 976 official bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB976))

## What the Draft Regulations Address [MEDIUM confidence]

The proposed regulatory text (Title 11, California Code of Regulations) clarifies the following areas where the statute's operative terms require further definition. Note: the official PDF at [oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/sb976-proposed-rulemaking.pdf](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/sb976-proposed-rulemaking.pdf) was not directly retrievable at time of writing; the following summary draws from DOJ press releases, prior preliminary-phase solicitations, and law firm analyses:

**Age Assurance Standards**
The regulations are expected to specify what constitutes a legally sufficient method for "reasonably determining" a user is not a minor. This is the most technically complex area of the rulemaking: the DOJ must balance privacy against verification reliability. The preliminary phase (November 2025 public meeting) surfaced significant stakeholder disagreement about whether self-declaration, third-party identity verification, or device-based inference are appropriate methods. ([Freshfields preliminary rulemaking analysis](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/preliminary-rulemaking-update-on-protecting-our-kids-from-social-media-addiction-102lrmn))

**Ongoing Age-Assurance Obligations**
Operators are expected to face rules on how frequently they must re-verify age, how to handle account transitions (e.g., a user reaching majority), and what records must be kept to demonstrate compliance.

**Parental Consent Mechanisms**
The regulations will specify what constitutes "verifiable parental consent" — a term not fully defined in the statute. The DOJ must reconcile this with the COPPA Rule's consent verification standards and address how consent is documented and revoked.

**"Actual Knowledge" Clarification**
The statute's liability trigger for the period before January 1, 2027 is "actual knowledge" that a user is a minor. The draft regulations are expected to define signals and circumstances that constitute such knowledge — an issue central to platform content systems and age-gating practices. ([Alston & Bird privacy blog](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/california-puts-social-medias-youth-feeds-on-notice/))

## Constitutional Litigation Status [HIGH confidence]

The regulatory roll-out occurs against an ongoing First Amendment challenge. In *NetChoice v. Bonta* (9th Cir.), the court's September 9, 2025 panel decision:

- **Upheld**: The prohibition on providing addictive feeds to minors absent parental consent — applying intermediate scrutiny as a content-neutral regulation of conduct with incidental speech effects.
- **Upheld**: The private-mode default setting requirement — rejected NetChoice's argument that it unconstitutionally restricts communications.
- **Struck down**: The engagement-metrics display restriction (prohibition on showing "like" counts and similar feedback to minors) — held to be a content-based restriction that failed strict scrutiny.

The case returned to the district court for trial on the merits on the upheld provisions. The DOJ's early win at the preliminary injunction stage means the addictive-feed and notification restrictions are presently enforceable. ([Hunton Andrews Kurth analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ninth-circuit-upholds-addictive-social-media-feed-ban-and-default-privacy-settings-for-minors-in-californias-protecting-our-kids-from-social-media-addiction-act)) ([EPIC case page](https://epic.org/ninth-circuit-rejects-netchoices-attack-on-californias-addictive-feed-regulation/))

## Action Items

- **Submit written comments by June 30, 2026, 5:00 p.m. PT.** Covered platforms — and vendors providing age-assurance or consent technology — should submit substantive comments to shape the final regulatory text on age assurance standards, consent mechanics, and the "actual knowledge" definition. Send to sb976@doj.ca.gov.

- **Attend or submit testimony at the June 30 public hearing.** The Elihu Harris Auditorium, 1515 Clay Street, Oakland, CA 94612; remote participation available. This is the last formal public-input opportunity before the regulations are finalized.

- **Audit your age-assurance pipeline now.** With a January 1, 2027 regulatory effective date and final rules expected by fall 2026, operators should map their existing age-signal collection, age-gating, and user-classification logic to identify compliance gaps against the most likely regulatory standards.

- **Verify parental consent architecture.** If you rely on parental consent to enable addictive feeds or notifications for minors, review whether your consent mechanism will satisfy the anticipated "verifiable parental consent" standard, taking into account COPPA Rule precedents.

- **Assess notification systems for current compliance.** The notification time restrictions (12 a.m.–6 a.m. and 8 a.m.–3 p.m. school days) are operative now under the "actual knowledge" standard. Platforms with age-verified or child-designated accounts should confirm their notification engines enforce these restrictions.

- **Monitor the litigation.** The district court trial on the merits of the upheld provisions remains pending. A final adverse ruling could narrow or eliminate some obligations, but compliance planning should proceed on current law.

- **Check interaction with COPPA Rule 2025 amendments.** The COPPA Rule's new parental consent and age-screening provisions (compliance deadline April 22, 2026) overlap with SB 976's consent requirements. Dual compliance analysis is advisable to avoid gaps or redundancies.
