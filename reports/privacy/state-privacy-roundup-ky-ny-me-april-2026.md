## Maine — LD 1822 (Maine Online Data Privacy Act) [HIGH confidence]

The Maine House voted against concurrence on LD 1822 for a second time in one week, ending the bill's consideration in the current session. Disagreement over a political-organizations exemption, combined with a late industry lobbying push, swung a majority of House members against the measure. The Maine legislative session ends April 15, 2026.

This is Maine's second consecutive failed attempt to enact a comprehensive consumer privacy law. The procedural posture (failed concurrence rather than a defeat on merits) leaves the door open for a reintroduction in the next session with revised exemption language.

## Kentucky — HB 692 (ACR Sensitive Data Amendment) [HIGH confidence]

Kentucky HB 692, sponsored by Rep. Josh Branscum (R-Russell Springs), amends the Kentucky Consumer Data Protection Act to:

- Define **"automatic content recognition"** and **"smart monitor"**; and
- Classify ACR data as **sensitive data**, which cannot be collected or processed without consumer consent.

ACR data is information gathered by technology embedded in internet-connected smart televisions or smart monitors that identifies content being displayed by analyzing audio or video fingerprints — a technology widely used for audience measurement, targeted advertising, and content recommendation.

**Legislative status:** The Kentucky House passed HB 692 on **March 13, 2026** by a vote of 92-0, and the Senate passed it on March 31, 2026 by 38-0 ([LegiScan KY HB692](https://legiscan.com/KY/bill/HB692/2026); [Kentucky Legislature record](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26rs/hb692.html); [Kentucky House vote history PDF](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/record/26RS/hb692/vote_history.pdf); [National Today — Frankfort](https://nationaltoday.com/us/ky/frankfort/news/2026/03/17/kentucky-house-passes-bill-to-protect-smart-tv-data-privacy/)). The bill was delivered to the governor and takes effect July 1, 2027 if signed. The official bill text is available at the [Kentucky Legislature PDF](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/recorddocuments/bill/26RS/hb692/bill.pdf).

**Compliance impact:** Kentucky becomes the first U.S. state to expressly elevate ACR data to sensitive-category status under a comprehensive privacy law ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/proposed-state-privacy-law-update-april-6-2026/); [State of Surveillance — Kentucky Smart TV ACR Privacy Bill HB692](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/kentucky-smart-tv-acr-privacy-bill-hb692-2026/)). Smart-TV manufacturers, streaming platforms, ad-tech vendors, and measurement companies operating in Kentucky will need consent flows for ACR-based processing. This may also serve as a template other states adopt.

## New York — Stop Online Predators Act (S4609/A6549) [HIGH confidence]

The Stop Online Predators Act (SOPA), sponsored by Sen. Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Nily Rozic, would require covered online platforms to:

- Implement commercially reasonable age-assurance mechanisms;
- Default children's profiles to private;
- Disable open chat functions by default for minors unless a parent opts in;
- Prevent adult strangers from privately messaging minors;
- Require parental approval for financial transactions connected to a child's account; and
- Allow parents of children under 13 to view the approved-friends list.

Enforcement is vested in the New York Attorney General, with civil penalties of up to $5,000 per violation ([NY State Senate Bill S4609A](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S4609/amendment/A); [LegiScan NY S04609](https://legiscan.com/NY/text/S04609/id/3304245)).

**Recent progress:** Governor Hochul announced inclusion of SOPA's core provisions in her 2026 State Budget, and the Senate One-House budget resolution in March 2026 incorporated the Gounardes proposal ([NY Senate press release — Gounardes/Budget](https://www.nysenate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/andrew-gounardes/sen-gounardes-am-rozic-applaud-gov-hochul-including); [NY Senate One-House resolution](https://www.nysenate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/andrew-gounardes/senate-one-house-resolution-includes-sen-gounardes)). A Common Sense Media fact sheet summarizes advocacy positions ([SOPA fact sheet PDF](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/featured-content/files/ny-stop-online-predators-act-sopa-fact-sheet-modified-jan-9-2026.pdf)).

Budget inclusion materially elevates the probability of enactment over the standalone bill path. Platforms serving New York minors should begin scoping age-assurance integration and default-setting changes.
