# First Conviction Under TAKE IT DOWN Act Sets Landmark Precedent for AI Deepfakes

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 8, 2026, James Strahler II, 37, of Columbus, Ohio, pleaded guilty in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Ohio to a multi-count federal indictment that includes **publication of digital forgeries under the TAKE IT DOWN Act** — the first such conviction in the United States (conviction via guilty plea; sentencing pending). U.S. Attorney Dominick S. Gerace II stated: "We believe Strahler is the first person in the United States to be convicted under the Take It Down Act," according to the [DOJ press release from the Southern District of Ohio](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdoh/pr/columbus-man-pleads-guilty-cyberstalking-exes-creating-ai-generated-obscene-material) and confirmed by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/first-person-convicted-law-criminalizing-intimate-deepfakes-rcna267236).

## Background: The TAKE IT DOWN Act [HIGH confidence]

The **Tools to Address Known Exploitation by Immobilizing Technological Deepfakes on Websites and Networks Act** (TAKE IT DOWN Act) was signed into law by President Trump on **May 19, 2025**. The Act has two principal components ([FTC statute page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/tools-address-known-exploitation-immobilizing-technological-deepfakes-websites-networks-act-take-it); [Congressional Research Service, LSB11314](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11314)):

1. **Criminal provisions.** The Act amends Section 223 of the Communications Act of 1934 by adding a new subsection — **47 U.S.C. § 223(h)** — making it unlawful to knowingly use an interactive computer service to publish an "intimate visual depiction" or a "digital forgery" of an identifiable individual without consent. "Digital forgery" is defined to cover AI-generated or machine-modified imagery that falsely depicts an individual's appearance or conduct in an intimate visual depiction. Penalties include up to two years' imprisonment, with enhanced penalties where minors are depicted. "Intimate visual depiction" is incorporated by reference from [15 U.S.C. § 6851(a)(5)](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11314).
2. **Platform takedown obligation.** Covered online platforms must establish a notice-and-removal process and remove reported nonconsensual intimate imagery (including deepfakes) within **48 hours** of receiving valid notice. The FTC is the designated civil enforcer of the platform obligations ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/tools-address-known-exploitation-immobilizing-technological-deepfakes-websites-networks-act-take-it)).

## The Strahler Case [HIGH confidence]

According to the [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdoh/pr/columbus-man-pleads-guilty-cyberstalking-exes-creating-ai-generated-obscene-material), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/first-person-convicted-law-criminalizing-intimate-deepfakes-rcna267236), and local reporting from [10TV](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/columbus-man-first-convicted-take-it-down-act-pleads-guilty-cybercrimes/530-fff42eac-4089-49ce-847b-0597c8a8fe39) and [ABC6 Columbus](https://abc6onyourside.com/news/local/columbus-ohio-man-james-strahler-first-nation-convicted-under-new-take-it-down-ai-deepfake-law-artificial-intelligence-congress-united-states-legal):

- **Charges of conviction:** (1) cyberstalking, (2) producing obscene visual representations of child sexual abuse, and (3) publication of digital forgeries under the TAKE IT DOWN Act (47 U.S.C. § 223(h)).
- **Conduct:** Between December 2024 and June 2025, Strahler sent harassing messages containing real and AI-generated nude images to at least six adult female victims, including AI-generated pornographic video depicting a victim with her father. He installed more than 24 AI platforms and 100+ web-based AI models on his phone and created more than 700 AI-generated obscene images involving real children from his community and animated figures, which he posted to a dedicated CSAM site.
- **Venue:** U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Ohio (Columbus).
- **Sentencing:** To be scheduled at a later date.

## Legal Significance [HIGH confidence]

1. **First-of-its-kind conviction.** The case is the first reported conviction under the digital-forgeries criminal provision (47 U.S.C. § 223(h)) of the TAKE IT DOWN Act, establishing that federal prosecutors are willing to charge the new statute alongside longstanding cyberstalking (18 U.S.C. § 2261A) and CSAM production offenses. This is consistent with the enforcement posture previewed by the [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/tools-address-known-exploitation-immobilizing-technological-deepfakes-websites-networks-act-take-it) and analyzed by [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-take-it-down-act-a-federal-response-to-non-consensual-intimate-imagery) and [Dynamis LLP](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/navigating-take-it-down-act-in-litigation).
2. **Charge-stacking pattern.** The conviction indicates that DOJ is likely to add TAKE IT DOWN Act counts to existing harassment/CSAM prosecutions rather than bring standalone deepfake cases — a pattern that will increase sentencing exposure without requiring novel investigative theories.
3. **Platform implications.** While the criminal conviction targets an individual, the Act's 48-hour takedown duty applies to interactive computer services. First Amendment challenges to the takedown provision have been flagged by civil-liberties commentators ([NAAG analysis](https://www.naag.org/attorney-general-journal/congresss-attempt-to-criminalize-nonconsensual-intimate-imagery-the-benefits-and-potential-shortcomings-of-the-take-it-down-act/)), but no court has yet enjoined the provision; covered platforms should assume the regime is in force.
4. **AI-tooling ecosystem.** The volume of AI apps installed by the defendant (24 platforms, 100+ models) highlights the proliferation of consumer-accessible image-generation tools and will likely reinforce calls for safety-by-design obligations on model providers.

## Action Items

- **Covered platforms:** Confirm that the 48-hour notice-and-removal workflow required by the TAKE IT DOWN Act is implemented, staffed, and audited; maintain records of takedown requests and response times.
- **AI tool providers:** Review trust-and-safety policies, input/output filters, and red-teaming for nonconsensual intimate imagery and deepfake misuse scenarios.
- **Corporate counsel / HR:** Update workplace harassment and acceptable-use policies to expressly reference AI-generated intimate imagery as prohibited conduct and potential federal crime.
- **Prosecutors and defense counsel:** Monitor the Strahler sentencing for the first judicial guidance on loss calculation, victim-impact, and Guidelines application for TAKE IT DOWN Act counts.
