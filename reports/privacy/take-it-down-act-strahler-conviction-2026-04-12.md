# First Conviction Under TAKE IT DOWN Act Sets Landmark Precedent for AI Deepfakes

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 8, 2026, James Strahler II, 37, of Columbus, Ohio, pleaded guilty in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Ohio to a multi-count federal indictment that includes **publication of digital forgeries under the TAKE IT DOWN Act** — the first such conviction in the United States (conviction via guilty plea; sentencing pending). U.S. Attorney Dominick S. Gerace II stated: "We believe Strahler is the first person in the United States to be convicted under the Take It Down Act," according to the [DOJ press release from the Southern District of Ohio](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdoh/pr/columbus-man-pleads-guilty-cyberstalking-exes-creating-ai-generated-obscene-material) and confirmed by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/first-person-convicted-law-criminalizing-intimate-deepfakes-rcna267236).

## Background: The TAKE IT DOWN Act [HIGH confidence]

The **Tools to Address Known Exploitation by Immobilizing Technological Deepfakes on Websites and Networks Act** (TAKE IT DOWN Act, S. 146, 119th Congress) passed the Senate on February 13, 2025, passed the House on April 28, 2025, and was signed into law by President Trump on **May 19, 2025**. The Act has two principal components ([FTC statute page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/tools-address-known-exploitation-immobilizing-technological-deepfakes-websites-networks-act-take-it); [Congressional Research Service, LSB11314](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11314)):

1. **Criminal provisions (effective immediately).** The Act amends Section 223 of the Communications Act of 1934 by adding a new subsection — **47 U.S.C. § 223(h)** — making it unlawful to knowingly use an interactive computer service to publish an "intimate visual depiction" or a "digital forgery" of an identifiable individual without consent. "Digital forgery" is defined to cover AI-generated or machine-modified imagery that falsely depicts an individual's appearance or conduct in an intimate visual depiction. Penalties include up to two years' imprisonment, with enhanced penalties where minors are depicted. "Intimate visual depiction" is incorporated by reference from [15 U.S.C. § 6851(a)(5)](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11314). The criminal prohibition also extends to threatening to publish such material.
2. **Platform notice-and-removal obligation (47 U.S.C. § 223a).** Covered online platforms must establish a notice-and-removal process and remove reported nonconsensual intimate imagery (including deepfakes) and known identical copies within **48 hours** of receiving valid notice. Platforms have until **May 19, 2026** to comply. The FTC is the designated civil enforcer under Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/statutes/tools-address-known-exploitation-immobilizing-technological-deepfakes-websites-networks-act-take-it)).

## The Strahler Case [HIGH confidence]

According to the [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdoh/pr/columbus-man-pleads-guilty-cyberstalking-exes-creating-ai-generated-obscene-material), [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/first-person-convicted-law-criminalizing-intimate-deepfakes-rcna267236), and local reporting from [10TV](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/columbus-man-first-convicted-take-it-down-act-pleads-guilty-cybercrimes/530-fff42eac-4089-49ce-847b-0597c8a8fe39), [ABC6 Columbus](https://abc6onyourside.com/news/local/columbus-ohio-man-james-strahler-first-nation-convicted-under-new-take-it-down-ai-deepfake-law-artificial-intelligence-congress-united-states-legal), and [Spectrum News](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/04/08/take-it-down-act-conviction), Strahler pleaded guilty to three federal counts:

- **Cyberstalking** (18 U.S.C. § 2261A)
- **Producing obscene visual representations of the sexual abuse of children** (18 U.S.C. § 1466A)
- **Publication of digital forgeries** under the TAKE IT DOWN Act (47 U.S.C. § 223(h))

### Underlying conduct

Between December 2024 and June 2025, Strahler:

- Sent harassing messages containing real and AI-generated nude images to at least six adult female victims (former intimate partners and acquaintances), and in at least one case distributed material to the victim's coworkers and family members, including an AI-generated pornographic video depicting a victim with her father.
- Used AI tools to morph children's faces onto the bodies of adults or other minors to create sexually explicit videos, including depictions of boys with their own family members.
- Created more than 700 AI-generated obscene images involving real victims and animated figures, which he posted to a website dedicated to child sexual abuse material.
- Installed more than 24 AI applications and over 100 web-based AI models on his phone, reflecting a sustained pattern of generative-AI abuse.

### Prosecutorial posture

U.S. Attorney Gerace publicly framed the plea as the first conviction under the TAKE IT DOWN Act. The charging structure — pairing the new TAKE IT DOWN Act count with established cyberstalking and CSAM statutes — suggests DOJ's early enforcement model will use the new statute as an **additive** charge layered on top of existing conduct-based offenses rather than as a standalone prosecution. Because Strahler pleaded guilty, there will be no adjudicated ruling on constitutional challenges to the TAKE IT DOWN Act's criminal provisions in this case.

## Legal Significance [HIGH confidence]

1. **First-of-its-kind conviction.** The case is the first reported conviction under the digital-forgeries criminal provision (47 U.S.C. § 223(h)), establishing that federal prosecutors are willing to charge the new statute alongside longstanding cyberstalking and CSAM production offenses. This is consistent with the enforcement posture analyzed by [McDonald Hopkins](https://www.mcdonaldhopkins.com/insights/news/the-take-it-down-act-a-federal-response-to-non-consensual-intimate-imagery) and [Dynamis LLP](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/navigating-take-it-down-act-in-litigation).

2. **Charge-stacking pattern.** The conviction indicates DOJ is likely to add TAKE IT DOWN Act counts to existing harassment/CSAM prosecutions rather than bring standalone deepfake cases — a pattern that will increase sentencing exposure without requiring novel investigative theories. Title 18 CSAM penalties remain significantly higher than the TAKE IT DOWN Act's criminal penalties, so expect continued charge-stacking where conduct spans adults and minors.

3. **Platform compliance deadline imminent.** The criminal conviction does not change platform obligations, but it underscores FTC and DOJ seriousness as the **May 19, 2026 notice-and-removal compliance deadline** approaches. Covered platforms should have their designated reporting channel, 48-hour takedown workflow, and hash-matching for known identical copies operational by that date. Per [Skadden's analysis](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/06/take-it-down-act) and [Proskauer's client alert](https://www.proskauer.com/blog/take-it-down-act-signed-into-law-offering-tools-to-fight-non-consensual-intimate-images-and-creating-a-new-image-takedown-mechanism), platforms should document good-faith compliance given the Act's FTC Act §5 enforcement backstop.

4. **AI-tooling ecosystem.** The volume of AI apps used by the defendant (24+ platforms, 100+ models) highlights the proliferation of consumer-accessible image-generation tools and will likely reinforce calls for safety-by-design obligations on model providers. Developers of face-swap, undress, and image-morph tools should expect downstream pressure (contractual, reputational, regulatory) even though the Act does not directly impose liability on model providers.

5. **Litigation outlook.** Because this case resolved by plea, the first **contested** constitutional challenge to the TAKE IT DOWN Act's criminal provisions remains pending future prosecutions. First Amendment challenges (vagueness, overbreadth of "digital forgeries") are widely anticipated by commentators including the [National Association of Attorneys General](https://www.naag.org/attorney-general-journal/congresss-attempt-to-criminalize-nonconsensual-intimate-imagery-the-benefits-and-potential-shortcomings-of-the-take-it-down-act/) and [Dynamis LLP](https://www.dynamisllp.com/knowledge/navigating-take-it-down-act-in-litigation); no court has yet enjoined the provision, so covered platforms should assume the regime is in force.

## Action Items

- **Covered platforms:** Confirm that the 48-hour notice-and-removal workflow required by the TAKE IT DOWN Act is implemented, staffed, and audited by May 19, 2026; maintain records of takedown requests, response times, and designated contact publication.
- **AI tool providers:** Review trust-and-safety policies, input/output filters, and red-teaming for nonconsensual intimate imagery and deepfake misuse scenarios; document abuse-prevention measures.
- **Corporate counsel / HR:** Update workplace harassment and acceptable-use policies to expressly reference AI-generated intimate imagery as prohibited conduct and a potential federal crime.
- **Prosecutors and state AGs:** Track DOJ charging patterns from this case as a template for future referrals; preserve coordination channels with USAO cybercrime units.
- **Monitor:** Watch for the Strahler sentencing memo, which may be the first judicial articulation of TAKE IT DOWN Act sentencing considerations, including loss calculation, victim-impact, and Guidelines application.
