# N.D. Cal. Declines to Remand CIPA Pen Register Class Action Against Los Angeles Times

**Jurisdiction:** California, Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-06-10

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

In *Mirmalek v. Los Angeles Times Communications LLC*, No. 3:24-cv-01797-CRB (N.D. Cal. May 23, 2024), Judge Charles R. Breyer denied a plaintiff's motion to remand a putative class action to California state court, holding that the plaintiff failed to demonstrate that the mandatory or discretionary home state exceptions to the Class Action Fairness Act (CAFA) applied. The plaintiff alleged that the LA Times' website deployed three third-party trackers that functioned as unlawful "pen registers" under [California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA) § 638.51](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=638.51.&lawCode=PEN). The court's refusal to remand is part of a broader litigation trend in which defendants successfully maintain federal court jurisdiction over CIPA class actions, undercutting plaintiffs' forum-selection strategies aimed at exploiting California state courts' narrower reading of the statute. The case ultimately settled for [$3.85 million](https://latimescipasettlement.com/), confirming significant exposure even in federal forum.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### California Invasion of Privacy Act — Pen Register Provisions

California enacted CIPA in 1967 to address law enforcement eavesdropping on telephone communications. [Cal. Penal Code § 638.51](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=638.51.&lawCode=PEN) prohibits any person from installing or using a "pen register" without prior court order. The statute defines "pen register" under [§ 638.50(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=638.50.&lawCode=PEN) as "a device or process that records or decodes dialing, routing, addressing, or signaling information transmitted by an instrument or facility from which a wire or electronic communication is transmitted, but not the contents of a communication."

Beginning around 2023, plaintiff's firms began invoking the pen register provisions against commercial websites, arguing that third-party advertising, analytics, and tracking technologies — pixel trackers, JavaScript tags, and data management platforms — qualify as "processes" that record "routing" or "addressing" information (specifically, user IP addresses). This theory spawned over 1,500 class actions against businesses in 2023–2024 alone, according to [K&L Gates analysis](https://www.klgates.com/Pen-Register-and-Trap-and-Trace-Claims-The-Latest-Wave-of-CIPA-Litigation-3-4-2024).

### The State Court vs. Federal Court Interpretive Split

A critical strategic dimension of CIPA pen register litigation is the divergence between state and federal courts on the statute's scope. As documented by [Holland & Knight (February 2026)](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/uncertainty-continues-in-california-on-cipa-section-63851-claims) and the [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-august/californias-invasion-privacy-act/):

- **California state courts** have generally adopted a narrower interpretation, frequently finding that CIPA's pen register provisions apply to telephone technology rather than internet communications. For example, in *Sanchez v. Cars.com Inc.*, 2025 WL 487194 (Cal. Super. Ct. Jan. 27, 2025), a state trial court reviewed CIPA's legislative history and concluded "pen register refers to telephone technology, not internet communications."
- **Federal district courts** have overwhelmingly held that pixels and similar web tracking technologies fall within CIPA's definition of "pen registers." The majority of federal judges rely on the statute's "plain language" and find it "expansive and inclusive."

This asymmetry created a strong plaintiff's forum-selection incentive: if a CIPA class action could stay in California state court, plaintiffs would face more favorable substantive law. Defendants, conversely, sought removal to federal court under CAFA.

### Class Action Fairness Act (CAFA) and Its Exceptions

[CAFA, codified at 28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1332), grants federal courts original jurisdiction over class actions where (1) the proposed class has at least 100 members, (2) minimal diversity of citizenship exists, and (3) the amount in controversy exceeds $5 million in aggregate. Defendants may remove qualifying actions to federal court.

CAFA provides two relevant exceptions that require or permit remand:

1. **Mandatory Home State Exception** (28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(4)(B)): The court *must* decline jurisdiction if two-thirds or more of all proposed class members and the primary defendants are citizens of the state where the action was filed.
2. **Discretionary Home State Exception** (28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(3)): The court *may* decline jurisdiction if more than one-third but fewer than two-thirds of class members are citizens of the forum state and various other factors favor remand.

Both exceptions impose on the plaintiff the burden to demonstrate the citizenship composition of the class.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Facts and Procedural History

On February 13, 2024, plaintiff Taliah Mirmalek filed a putative class action in Alameda County Superior Court against Los Angeles Times Communications LLC, alleging that the LA Times' website deployed three third-party trackers — TripleLift, GumGum, and Audiencerate — that collected website visitors' IP addresses without authorization, constituting an unlawful pen register under [CIPA § 638.51](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=638.51.&lawCode=PEN). The plaintiff proposed a class of "all California residents who accessed the Website in California and had their IP address collected by the Trackers."

On March 22, 2024, the LA Times removed the action to the Northern District of California under CAFA. The plaintiff promptly moved to remand, invoking (1) the mandatory home state exception under [28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(4)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1332) and (2) in the alternative, the discretionary home state exception under [28 U.S.C. § 1332(d)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/1332).

### The Court's Remand Analysis

**Mandatory Home State Exception — Two-Thirds Citizenship Burden**

To satisfy the mandatory exception, the plaintiff needed to establish that two-thirds or more of all proposed class members were California citizens. Judge Breyer identified a threshold methodological problem: to compute whether two-thirds of the class is California-domiciled, the court must first know the overall class size (the "denominator problem"). Because the proposed class definition encompassed website visitors from any jurisdiction who accessed the LA Times' website from within California — not just California residents — the class potentially included out-of-state visitors. The plaintiff offered no evidence establishing how many total persons accessed the website or what proportion were California citizens.

The court denied the remand motion on this basis but granted the plaintiff leave to conduct jurisdictional discovery. Specifically, the court reasoned that the LA Times may possess server logs or third-party analytics data that would allow the plaintiff to determine the denominator — total unique visitors — and ultimately the proportion of California citizens. The door to renew the remand motion remained open, contingent on discovery results, as reported by [Inside Class Actions (June 6, 2024)](https://www.insideclassactions.com/2024/06/06/n-d-cal-court-declines-remand-of-california-focused-wiretap-class-action/).

**Companion Precedent from the Central District**

One month before the Mirmalek ruling, the Central District of California reached a similar result in *Doe v. PHE, Inc.*, 2024 WL 1639149 (C.D. Cal. Apr. 15, 2024), reinforcing defendants' CAFA removal strategies. The *PHE* court rejected the local controversy exception after finding that the plaintiff failed to establish multiple required elements, including the requirement that injury "substantially" occurred in California. As analyzed by [Inside Class Actions (May 1, 2024)](https://www.insideclassactions.com/2024/05/01/a-closer-look-recent-c-d-cal-decision-strengthens-defendants-arguments-for-cafa-removal/), *PHE*'s strict approach benefits defendants seeking to remove CIPA cases involving nationally available websites or applications.

### Substantive CIPA § 638.51 Issues

While the Mirmalek ruling focused on CAFA jurisdiction rather than the merits, the underlying substantive question — whether web trackers qualify as "pen registers" under CIPA — remained contested. Federal courts in the Northern and Central Districts had repeatedly applied the pen register provisions to website technologies:

- In *Moody v. C2 Educational Systems, Inc.* and related cases, federal district courts held that software identifying consumers, gathering data, and correlating results constitutes a "device or process" under CIPA's pen register definition.
- The trackers at issue in Mirmalek — TripleLift, GumGum, and Audiencerate — are ad tech platforms that capture IP addresses for audience segmentation purposes, squarely fitting within the "routing" and "addressing" information language of [§ 638.50(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=638.50.&lawCode=PEN).

As detailed by the [American Bar Association's analysis of CIPA litigation](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/class-actions-derivative-suits/old-law-new-world-california-invasion-of-privacy-act-pen-register-provision/), the overwhelming majority of federal courts applying plain language analysis find website tracking technologies within CIPA's pen register scope.

### Settlement Outcome

The case ultimately settled. On October 2025, Los Angeles Times Communications agreed to pay [$3.85 million](https://latimescipasettlement.com/) to resolve the CIPA pen register claims, with the settlement class comprising California residents who accessed the LA Times website or mobile app between January 31, 2023, and December 19, 2025. The LA Times denied any wrongdoing but agreed to settle to avoid litigation costs and uncertainty.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### For Website Operators with California Users

The Mirmalek ruling, together with *Doe v. PHE*, strengthens defendants' ability to remove CIPA pen register class actions to federal court and keep them there. Federal courts have applied CIPA's pen register provisions more broadly than California state courts; removal to federal court thus changes the playing field in a manner that is generally favorable to defendants on the merits, even though the filing volume and settlement pressure remain high.

Website operators who use third-party ad tech trackers — including retargeting pixels, demand-side platform tags, and audience segmentation tools from providers such as TripleLift, GumGum, The Trade Desk, LiveRamp, and similar companies — face material CIPA § 638.51 exposure if those tools collect IP addresses or other routing information without prior user consent.

Key exposure factors:
- **Ad tech integrations**: Any website tag or SDK that captures IP addresses or user device identifiers and transmits them to a third party for commercial purposes is a primary target.
- **Class size and CAFA thresholds**: The $5 million CAFA threshold is easily satisfied in CIPA pen register cases given statutory damages of $5,000 per violation under [Cal. Penal Code § 638.55](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=638.55.&lawCode=PEN) and the large volume of website visitors.
- **Settlement pressure**: Even in federal court, where substantive law appears to favor plaintiffs on the pen register theory, defendants face significant settlement pressure. The $3.85 million Mirmalek settlement illustrates this dynamic.

### For Defense Counsel

CAFA removal with the CIPA pen register class action pattern is viable and has judicial support in both the Northern and Central Districts. To reinforce removal and resist remand motions:

- Defendants should preserve server logs and third-party analytics data early, as such evidence may be needed to refute plaintiffs' class-size allegations in any jurisdictional discovery.
- The "denominator problem" identified in Mirmalek can be a powerful remand-resistance argument when the proposed class definition is not strictly limited to California residents.
- Early expert analysis of the third-party trackers at issue can support arguments that the trackers do not record "routing, addressing, or signaling information" within the meaning of § 638.50(b), particularly as some courts have begun narrowing the statute's scope.

### Forum Selection Trend

As noted by [Covington & Burling's Inside Privacy blog](https://www.insideprivacy.com/data-privacy/website-wiretapping-litigation-recent-decisions-and-developments/), plaintiff firms have actively experimented with forum-selection tactics in CIPA litigation. Some plaintiffs have filed individual claims specifically alleging less than $75,000 in damages to block diversity jurisdiction, while purporting to reserve class-action rights for later. The Mirmalek ruling contributes to judicial skepticism of unsupported home state exception arguments and reinforces that plaintiffs bear a real evidentiary burden to establish class citizenship when seeking remand.

## Action Items

- **Audit third-party trackers**: Identify all ad tech tags, pixels, and data management platform integrations on California-facing websites that capture or transmit IP addresses or other routing identifiers to third parties. Map each to a consent mechanism.
- **Implement or verify consent mechanisms**: For CIPA § 638.51 compliance, operators should obtain prior consent from users before deploying pen register-type trackers. Consent management platforms (CMPs) should be configured to block these tags absent affirmative user consent.
- **Preserve analytics data**: Retain server-side logs and third-party analytics reports that reflect visitor geography and unique visitor counts. This data can be critical evidence if a CAFA jurisdiction dispute arises.
- **Monitor the state-federal interpretive split**: Track whether California state courts and federal courts converge or diverge further on the pen register theory. The pending [California SB 690](https://www.coblentzlaw.com/news/developments-in-digital-privacy-litigation-in-2024-2025-cipa-vppa-and-californias-sb-690/), which would curtail CIPA pen register claims, is worth monitoring for legislative developments.
- **Evaluate CAFA removal strategy at filing**: If a CIPA pen register class action is filed in California state court, promptly assess whether CAFA removal is available and, if so, preserve documents (especially tracker configuration logs) that could assist in opposing a future remand motion.
