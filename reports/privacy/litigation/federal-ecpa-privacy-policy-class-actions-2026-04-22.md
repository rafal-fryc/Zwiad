# ECPA Federal Court Ruling Opens Door for Privacy Policy Inaccuracy Class Actions

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-22

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

A pivotal August 2025 ruling in [*Smith v. Rack Room Shoes, Inc.*](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2024cv06709/437035/85/), No. 3:24-cv-06709-RFL (N.D. Cal. Aug. 4, 2025), has established a replicable plaintiff-side template for federal Wiretap Act class actions premised on misrepresentations or omissions in a company's privacy policy. The Northern District of California held that inaccuracies in a privacy policy — specifically a failure to disclose that third parties would collect and commercially exploit website visitors' personally identifiable information — can supply the predicate "tortious act" needed to invoke the ECPA's crime-tort exception, bypassing the one-party consent defense that normally shields companies from liability. Because ECPA claims carry nationwide reach and statutory damages of at least **$10,000 per violation** (or $100 per day), the ruling dramatically expands class action exposure for any company whose actual data practices differ from what its privacy policy states. After a [235% increase in ECPA lawsuit filings in 2025](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/the-ecpa-a-federal-private-right-of-action-for-privacy-policy-inaccuracies/), Troutman Pepper Locke projects approximately **460 new ECPA lawsuits in 2026** — a further 60% year-over-year increase.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **The case**: *Smith v. Rack Room Shoes, Inc.*, No. [3:24-cv-06709-RFL](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-cand-3_24-cv-06709), 2025 WL 2210002 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 4, 2025). Judge Rita F. Lin of the Northern District of California denied defendant's motion to dismiss the federal Wiretap Act and California CDAFA claims. ([Justia docket](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/california/candce/3:2024cv06709/437035))

- **The conduct at issue**: Rack Room Shoes had embedded the [Meta Pixel and Attentive Tag](https://natlawreview.com/article/rack-room-shoes-inc-wiretap-act-class-action-survives-motion-dismiss-court-finds) on its website. These tools allegedly transmitted users' search queries, items viewed and carted, and hashed personal identifiers (name, address, phone, email, and Facebook ID) to Meta and Attentive for targeted advertising.

- **The privacy policy problem**: Rack Room's privacy policy failed to disclose that third parties could collect this personally identifiable information and use it for their own commercial purposes. ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/best-foot-forward-rack-rooms-privacy-policy-not-clear-enough-dismissal)) In an earlier ruling (*Smith I*), the court had already found this omission made any consent argument legally deficient.

- **The crime-tort exception theory**: Under [18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511), the ECPA's "party exception" — which normally permits a party to a communication to intercept it — does not apply when the interception is done "for the purpose of committing any criminal or tortious act." The court held that Rack Room's intent to use and disclose customers' PII for targeted advertising was a tortious purpose, and that Rack Room's privacy policy misrepresentations supplied the predicate tort necessary to trigger the crime-tort exception. ([CIPAWorld](https://cipaworld.com/2025/08/05/rack-room-shoes-inc-wiretap-act-class-action-survives-motion-to-dismiss-court-finds-crime-tort-exception-applies/))

- **Monetary motive not a shield**: The court explicitly rejected the argument that a "primary financial motivation" insulates a defendant from liability: "a monetary purpose does not insulate a party from liability under the Wiretap Act, at least at the motion to dismiss stage." ([Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/court_revives_wiretap_cdafa_claims_against_retailer_over_use_embedded_website_tracking_0825.html))

- **Statutory damages**: Under [18 U.S.C. § 2520](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2520), ECPA plaintiffs may recover the greater of (a) actual damages plus profits, or (b) statutory damages of **$100 per day of violation or $10,000** per violation. Unlike state wiretap claims such as CIPA, ECPA claims can be filed in any federal court nationwide — no all-party consent state is required. ([Troutman Pepper Locke alert](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/the-ecpa-a-federal-private-right-of-action-for-privacy-policy-inaccuracies/))

- **Litigation surge**: ECPA class action filings increased 235% in 2025 compared to prior years, based on analysis of over [12,000 complaints filed between 2015 and March 2026](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/the-ecpa-a-federal-private-right-of-action-for-privacy-policy-inaccuracies/). Approximately 70% are filed as class actions. Troutman Pepper Locke projects ~460 new ECPA suits in 2026.

- **Court split on the "purpose" element**: Not all courts apply the crime-tort exception the same way. A March 2026 Massachusetts federal court [dismissed an adtech ECPA class action](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/03/10/massachusetts-federal-court-dismisses-adtech-ecpa-class-action-for-failure-to-allege-defendants-purposefully-committed-a-criminal-act-furthering-split-of-authority/) for failure to allege that the defendant "purposefully" committed a criminal act — deepening a split of authority on whether the crime-tort exception requires proof of an independent tortious motive versus conduct that merely happens to constitute a tort. Illinois federal courts [allowed adtech and edtech ECPA claims to proceed](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2025/08/26/illinois-federal-courts-allow-adtech-and-edtech-ecpa-claims-to-proceed-furthering-split-of-authority/) in August 2025 under a more permissive standard.

- **Broader context**: The IAPP characterizes the crime-tort exception in ECPA class actions as "[dressing old laws in class-action suits](https://iapp.org/news/a/dressing-old-laws-in-class-action-suits-the-ecpa-party-exception)" — plaintiffs leveraging a 1986 statute as the vehicle for modern-day website tracking class actions, driven in part by the advantages ECPA claims carry over state-law alternatives.

## Action Items

- **Conduct an immediate privacy policy audit**: Compare every data-sharing, data-collection, and third-party use disclosure in your privacy policy against your actual data practices. Any gap — even an omission rather than an affirmative misrepresentation — can now serve as the predicate tort supporting an ECPA class action under the *Rack Room* blueprint.

- **Inventory third-party tracking technologies**: Identify every pixel, tag, cookie, SDK, and analytics tool deployed on your websites and apps. For each tool, confirm what data it transmits and to whom. Verify that your privacy policy accurately and specifically describes these transmissions — including that third parties may retain or commercially exploit collected data.

- **Do not rely solely on a generic "we use cookies" disclosure**: The *Rack Room* court found that a general cookie disclosure was insufficient where the policy did not specifically disclose third-party commercial exploitation of PII. Privacy policy language must be specific enough to cover what third parties actually do with the data.

- **Audit for "contradictory statements"**: The [March 2026 Troutman Pepper Locke Privacy Litigation Report](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/privacy-litigation-report-takeaways-from-march-2026-decisions/) identifies "contradictory statements in privacy policies" as independently sufficient to defeat consent even where tracking technology use is technically disclosed. Review for internal inconsistencies.

- **Assess existing third-party vendor agreements**: Ensure that data processing agreements with adtech platforms, advertising networks, and analytics vendors accurately reflect data use limitations and that your privacy policy disclosures are synchronized with contractual scope.

- **Healthcare, financial services, and retail e-commerce operators face heightened risk**: Healthcare websites remain the primary target of ECPA tracking class actions (settlements exceeding $135 million between 2023 and March 2026 per [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20260128-2025-2025-year-in-review-web-tracking-and-the-wiretap-act-crime-tort-exception)). The *Rack Room* ruling expands this playbook squarely into retail e-commerce. Financial services companies face comparable exposure for unauthorized disclosure of financial transaction data to advertising platforms.

- **Monitor the court split**: The question of what "for the purpose of committing" a tortious act requires under § 2511(2)(d) remains unresolved across circuits. Track developments and review defense strategy accordingly for any pending or threatened ECPA claim.
