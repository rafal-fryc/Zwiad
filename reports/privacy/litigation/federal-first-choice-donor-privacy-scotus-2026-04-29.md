# Supreme Court Rules Unanimously for Donor Privacy: First Choice Women's Resource Centers, Inc. v. Davenport

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-29

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 29, 2026, the [US Supreme Court issued a unanimous 9-0 ruling](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-781_pok0.pdf) in *First Choice Women's Resource Centers, Inc. v. Davenport*, No. 24-781, holding that a state attorney general's subpoena demanding nonprofit donor records constitutes an immediate First Amendment injury giving rise to Article III standing. The Court held that nonprofits may seek federal court review of state investigative subpoenas targeting donor information without waiting for state court enforcement. The decision significantly strengthens constitutional protections for organizational donor privacy and limits state government demands for associational records under the First Amendment's freedom of association doctrine.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **The decision:** Justice Gorsuch delivered the [unanimous opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-781_pok0.pdf) on April 29, 2026, reversing a lower court ruling that First Choice lacked standing to challenge the subpoena in federal court before enforcement. ([SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/04/court-unanimously-sides-with-faith-based-pregnancy-centers-in-litigation-dispute-with-new-jersey/))

- **The underlying facts:** First Choice Women's Resource Centers, a religious nonprofit crisis pregnancy center operating in New Jersey since 1985, received a civil investigative subpoena from the New Jersey Attorney General as part of a consumer-fraud investigation. The subpoena demanded 28 categories of documents including names, phone numbers, addresses, and places of employment of all donors. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Choice_Women%27s_Resource_Centers,_Inc._v._Davenport))

- **The key holding on standing:** The Court held that the mere issuance of a government subpoena demanding donor information injures a nonprofit's First Amendment right of association — deterring donors from associating with the group — and that this injury arises at the moment the demand is made, not only upon court enforcement. ([Supreme Court opinion, 608 U.S. ___ (2026)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-781_pok0.pdf))

- **Federal forum access:** Recipients of state attorney general subpoenas demanding donor or member information may now bring Section 1983 constitutional challenges in federal court immediately upon receiving such a demand, without exhausting state proceedings. ([Ropes & Gray LLP](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/05/supreme-court-reinforces-donor-privacy-protections-permitting-immediate-federal-court-challenge))

- **Scope of ruling:** The Court's decision addresses only standing — the right to challenge the subpoena in federal court — and does not resolve the merits question of whether the subpoena itself violates the First Amendment. That merits question is to be decided on remand. ([Faegre Drinker Biddle & Reath LLP](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/4/supreme-court-decides-first-choice-womens-resource-centers-inc-v-davenport))

- **Non-self-executing subpoenas:** The Court expressly held it is immaterial that the attorney general's subpoena was "non-self-executing" — meaning legal duty to produce records arises only when a state court orders enforcement. The constitutional injury from a demand for donor information is ongoing from issuance. ([LII/Cornell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-781))

- **Precedential lineage:** The decision builds directly on *NAACP v. Alabama* (1958), *Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta*, 594 U.S. ___ (2021) (striking down California's mandatory donor disclosure rule as violating the First Amendment), and applied "exacting scrutiny" to government demands for charitable donor information. ([Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta, Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/594/19-251/))

- **Broad applicability:** The ruling applies beyond pregnancy-related organizations. Ropes & Gray identifies affected entities as including "universities, hospitals, health care nonprofits, advocacy organizations, and other entities that maintain sensitive donor, member, or supporter data." ([Ropes & Gray LLP](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/05/supreme-court-reinforces-donor-privacy-protections-permitting-immediate-federal-court-challenge))

- **ACLU response:** The ACLU, which filed an amicus brief supporting First Choice, [celebrated the decision](https://www.aclu.org/press-releases/aclu-celebrates-scotus-decision-protecting-organizations-from-speech-chilling-state-subpoenas) as protecting organizations across the political spectrum from "speech-chilling state subpoenas."

## Action Items

- **Nonprofits, advocacy groups, universities, and healthcare organizations** that receive state investigative subpoenas targeting donor, member, alumni, or supporter information should immediately evaluate whether to challenge the subpoena under Section 1983 in federal court, without waiting for state enforcement proceedings.

- **Review donor data governance protocols** to document the associational interests at stake whenever a government entity demands donor records. Contemporaneous documentation of chilling effects strengthens First Amendment standing arguments.

- **Do not assume confidentiality agreements neutralize constitutional risk.** The Court held that even subpoenas accompanied by confidentiality assurances remain subject to heightened scrutiny — the injury arises from the demand itself. ([Ropes & Gray LLP](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/05/supreme-court-reinforces-donor-privacy-protections-permitting-immediate-federal-court-challenge))

- **State attorneys general and regulatory bodies** should anticipate that investigative subpoenas targeting donor or member records will face immediate federal constitutional challenge under this ruling, and should narrow such demands to minimize First Amendment exposure before issuing.

- **Monitor the remand proceedings** in the New Jersey case, which will address the ultimate merits question: whether the AG's subpoena survives First Amendment exacting scrutiny.
