# EDNY/SDNY Joint Local Rules Amendments Take Effect July 1, 2024: Key E-Discovery Changes and Notable July 2024 Cases

**Jurisdiction:** New York (Federal — S.D.N.Y. and E.D.N.Y.) | **Category:** Privacy / Litigation | **Date:** 2024-07-26

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On June 17, 2024, the United States District Courts for the Southern and Eastern Districts of New York jointly announced comprehensive amendments to their Joint Local Rules, effective July 1, 2024. The amendments represent the most extensive update to these courts' procedural rules in years, covering civil discovery, privilege logging, discovery dispute procedures, summary judgment practice, and motions practice. The most significant e-discovery-related changes are the codification of categorical and metadata privilege logs as presumptively proper alternatives to document-by-document logs, and the unification of discovery dispute procedures across both districts into a single consolidated rule. Against this backdrop, Sidley Austin's July 2024 monthly e-discovery case law review identified three additional notable federal court decisions addressing ESI order scope, spoliation sanctions, and ESI production deficiencies — all of which reflect continuing judicial focus on proportionality, proportionate remedies, and counsel obligations in complex electronic discovery.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The Southern District of New York (S.D.N.Y.) and Eastern District of New York (E.D.N.Y.) are two of the busiest federal district courts in the United States, handling a substantial share of complex commercial litigation, securities enforcement, and privacy class actions. The courts have maintained joint local civil rules for decades, enabling attorneys admitted to both courts to operate under a single procedural framework.

The July 1, 2024 amendments were the product of a multi-year review effort by the [Joint Local Rules Committee](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/rules), which sought to modernize the rules to reflect changes in the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, the widespread adoption of remote depositions and electronic filing, and the realities of large-scale electronic discovery. The drafts were circulated for public comment before adoption, and the [Notice to Bar published by the SDNY on June 17, 2024](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/Notice%20to%20Bar%20-%20Joint%20Local%20Rule%20Amendments%20Adopted%20070124.pdf) announced the formal adoption.

The amendments apply to all civil (and certain criminal) cases pending or filed on or after July 1, 2024, with a narrow carve-out: where a pending deadline falls within 14 days of the effective date, the prior rules continue to govern that specific deadline.

The full text of the amended rules is available on the [SDNY rules page](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/rules) and the [EDNY local rules page](https://www.nyed.uscourts.gov/local-rules-documents-and-administrative-orders).

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### E-Discovery Amendments

#### Local Civil Rule 26.2 — Assertion of Privilege (Privilege Logs)

The most consequential e-discovery amendment is the overhaul of [Local Civil Rule 26.2](https://www.nysd.uscourts.gov/rules), which governs the assertion of claims of privilege in discovery. The amended rule makes three significant changes:

**1. Categorical and Metadata Logs Now Presumptively Proper**

Under the prior rule, parties were expected to provide a document-by-document privilege log. The 2024 amendment explicitly states that it is presumptively proper to provide a categorical privilege log rather than a document-by-document log. New subsection (c) directs that "[w]hen appropriate, parties should consider and discuss the use of a categorical log or a metadata log, instead of a document-by-document log."

The rule also introduces "metadata logs" — logs generated by document review platforms that capture the information necessary to support a privilege claim — as an acceptable format. Crucially, the rule bars a party from objecting to a privilege log solely because it uses categorical grouping or metadata formatting.

This aligns the SDNY/EDNY rules with the broader national trend toward categorical privilege logs, as noted by [Carlton Fields](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/2024/changes-are-coming-to-sdny-and-edny-joint-local-rules) and [Harris Beach Murtha](https://www.harrisbeachmurtha.com/insights/extensive-rule-changes-to-joint-local-civil-rules-in-s-d-n-y-and-e-d-n-y/). The practical effect is particularly significant in cases involving tens or hundreds of thousands of documents, where itemized logging is prohibitively expensive.

**2. Timing of Privilege Assertions**

The amended rule allows parties to agree to assert privilege claims after furnishing their responses to discovery requests. Under the prior rule, privilege could only be asserted after the response if ordered by the court; parties who failed to timely log could face waiver. The amendment codifies what has become common practice through stipulation — parties routinely agree by scheduling order to exchange privilege logs some weeks after the document production deadline. This reduces the frequency of waiver motions arising from logistical constraints.

**3. No Objection Based on Log Format Alone**

A receiving party may no longer object to a privilege log solely on the basis that it uses categorical or metadata grouping. This forecloses a category of tactical challenge and focuses privilege disputes on substantive adequacy.

#### Local Civil Rule 37.2 — Discovery Disputes

Before the 2024 amendments, the SDNY and EDNY maintained separate, divergent procedures for raising discovery disputes with the court. The 2024 amendments created a single [Local Civil Rule 37.2](https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/publications/2024/changes-are-coming-to-sdny-and-edny-joint-local-rules) applicable in both districts. Under the unified rule:

- A party seeking relief regarding a discovery dispute must first request a pre-motion conference by letter motion before filing a formal discovery motion.
- The rule expressly extends to Rule 45 motions for subpoena enforcement, on the theory that such motions are properly understood as discovery motions.

The pre-conference requirement mirrors longstanding informal practice in many individual judges' rules but is now a district-wide default, reducing divergent local practices.

### Other Notable Procedural Amendments [HIGH confidence]

The July 1, 2024 amendments addressed several additional areas of civil practice relevant to litigants in complex cases:

**Local Civil Rule 1.4 — Attorney of Record / Notice of Appearance**

The amended rule requires all attorneys appearing on behalf of a party to file a formal notice of appearance in each case, except attorneys who file case-initiating documents such as a complaint or notice of removal. Previously, practice varied; the new rule standardizes the requirement and clarifies when an attorney becomes an attorney of record.

**Local Civil Rule 6.3 — Motions for Reconsideration**

The clock for serving a reconsideration motion was clarified: the motion must be served within 14 days after entry of the court's order. The rule also establishes page limits: 10 pages for the opening memorandum, 10 pages for opposing memoranda, and 5 pages for reply. Previously no specific page limits applied to reconsideration papers under the joint rules.

**Local Civil Rule 15.1 — Motions to Amend Pleadings**

Motions to amend pleadings must now include both a clean copy of the proposed amended pleading and a redline comparison against the operative pleading. The amendment also clarifies that the proposed amended pleading submitted as an exhibit is not automatically operative; the amending party must file the actual amended pleading within seven days of the court's order granting leave.

**Local Civil Rule 30.1 — Counsel Fees for Remote Deposition Travel (Deleted)**

The prior rule allowed a party to seek counsel fees for an attorney's attendance at a deposition held more than 100 miles from the courthouse. This rule was deleted as obsolete given the widespread adoption of remote depositions since 2020.

**Local Civil Rule 56.1 — Statements of Material Facts on Summary Judgment**

The amended rule requires that in cases where all parties are represented by counsel, the moving party must provide an electronic copy of its Rule 56.1 statement to all other parties. The counterstatement must include each entry from the moving party's statement with the opposing party's response directly beneath it — eliminating parallel documents and making the record more legible.

### Notable July 2024 E-Discovery Cases [MEDIUM confidence]

Sidley Austin's [July 2024 Notable Cases and Events in E-Discovery](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/07/julys-notable-cases-and-events-in-e-discovery) summary identified three additional decisions with practical implications:

**ESI Order Scope — Western District of Washington**

A court in the Western District of Washington held that defendants could not rely on the terms of an existing ESI protocol order to shield them from producing additional ESI where the plaintiff had amended its complaint and served supplemental discovery after the protocol was entered. The ruling underscores that ESI orders are not static: they are tied to the claims and discovery requests operative at the time of entry and may require renegotiation when the case scope changes materially.

**Spoliation Sanctions — Denial Without Prejudice — District of Utah**

In a case where the plaintiff had taken possession of a former employee's phone but lacked the passcode needed to access it, the court declined to impose spoliation sanctions on a without-prejudice basis. Rather than sanction the plaintiff outright, the court ordered the plaintiff to produce the device and take steps to secure the passcode. This reflects the trend toward proportionate, remediation-first responses before harsh sanctions — consistent with the 2015 amendments to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e).

**ESI Production Deficiencies — Eastern District of Pennsylvania**

A special discovery master in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania, whose recommendation was adopted by the district court, declined to order a party to redo its entire ESI collection and production despite numerous production errors. Instead, the court required the party to provide a detailed written account of its collection, search, review, and production processes. The ruling reflects judicial reluctance to impose categorical redo orders — which are costly and rarely proportionate — in favor of disclosure obligations that allow the opposing party and the court to assess the adequacy of what was done.

**Search Term Negotiations — Northern District of California (Humanmade v. SFMade)**

In [Humanmade v. SFMade](https://minerva26.com/2024-ediscovery-case-law-trends-sanctions-strategy-and-the-shifting-legal-landscape/), No. 23-cv-02349-HSG (PHK), 2024 WL 3378326 (N.D. Cal. July 10, 2024), Magistrate Judge Peter H. Kang admonished counsel for failing to engage in adequate meet-and-confer negotiations over disputed ESI search terms. The court imposed specific obligations on the parties to renegotiate. The case reinforces the baseline expectation that counsel negotiate search methodology in good faith before seeking judicial intervention.

**Litigation Holds and Production Obligations — Northern District of California (In re Google RTB)**

In [In re Google RTB Consumer Privacy Litigation](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2024/07/julys-notable-cases-and-events-in-e-discovery), No. 21-cv-02155-YGR (VKD), 2024 WL 3407069 (N.D. Cal. July 11, 2024), Magistrate Judge Virginia K. DeMarchi examined the scope of a party's obligation to produce information about its litigation holds under Rule 26. Plaintiffs sought "extensive information about essentially everything Google did with respect to the retention and preservation of any information responsive to a claim or defense in this action." The ruling clarified the limits of interrogatory practice in extracting details about an opponent's preservation program — a boundary that arises with increasing frequency in large-scale privacy class actions.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Who Is Affected

The July 1, 2024 SDNY/EDNY amendments directly affect:

- Attorneys admitted to or practicing in the Southern and Eastern Districts of New York, in all pending and new civil matters
- In-house counsel at companies that are frequent litigants in these courts — including major financial institutions, technology companies, and media entities that are disproportionately headquartered or incorporated in New York
- Discovery vendors and legal technology providers assisting with privilege logging, ESI processing, and document review for matters in these courts

### Compliance Requirements and Practical Implications

**Privilege Log Practice**

Litigants and outside counsel should update their internal privilege logging workflows to take advantage of categorical and metadata log formats where appropriate. Defensible categorical logs should be organized by privilege type, document author/recipient category, and subject matter, with metadata fields sufficient to allow the court or opposing counsel to assess privilege claims. The rule change does not eliminate the need for substantive adequacy — a poorly organized categorical log will still be challenged, but the challenge must be substantive rather than purely format-based.

Parties should also negotiate the format and timing of privilege log exchange at the Rule 26(f) conference and incorporate agreed terms in the case management or ESI protocol order. The new rule gives parties flexibility to exchange logs after production; locking in those logistics early avoids later disputes.

**Discovery Disputes**

The unified Rule 37.2 pre-conference requirement means that parties can no longer file a formal discovery motion in either district without first requesting a pre-motion conference by letter. Failure to follow this procedure is grounds for the court to reject the motion. Practitioners should build the letter-motion step into their internal litigation calendars and be prepared to outline the dispute concisely in letter form.

The inclusion of Rule 45 subpoena motions within Rule 37.2 is a practical change for third-party subpoena practice — parties must now seek a conference before moving to enforce or quash a subpoena in either court.

**Summary Judgment**

The Rule 56.1 amendments increase the administrative burden on moving parties (who must provide an electronic copy of the statement) but reduce briefing confusion by requiring responses to be integrated directly beneath each numbered paragraph. Counsel should update their standard summary judgment templates accordingly.

### Broader Signals from July 2024 Case Law

The July 2024 case law decisions collectively reflect:

1. **Proportionality remains central**: Courts continue to resist "scorched earth" discovery remedies (full production redos, severe spoliation sanctions) in favor of targeted, proportionate relief.
2. **Counsel obligations in ESI negotiations are non-negotiable**: Courts expect good-faith negotiation of search methodology and will impose obligations — and potentially sanctions — where counsel fail to engage.
3. **Litigation holds are within discovery scope but not unlimited**: Courts are calibrating the extent to which parties can interrogate opposing parties' preservation programs; pure fishing expeditions into every detail of a litigation hold are disfavored.

## Action Items

- Update internal privilege logging guidelines to reflect SDNY/EDNY's codification of categorical and metadata logs as presumptively proper; brief the discovery team and outside counsel on what constitutes a defensible categorical log in these courts.
- Revise standard Rule 26(f) conference checklists and model ESI protocols for SDNY/EDNY matters to address log format, timing, and the new Rule 37.2 pre-conference requirement.
- Amend form summary judgment motion packages for SDNY/EDNY to include the electronic copy of the Rule 56.1 statement and the integrated counter-statement format.
- In cases with existing ESI protocols, evaluate whether the scope of existing orders adequately reflects the current claims and discovery requests; renegotiate where significant amendments have changed the scope.
- For matters in the Northern District of California involving large ESI sets, ensure search term negotiations with opposing counsel are documented — courts will scrutinize whether counsel met their meet-and-confer obligations before seeking judicial intervention.
- Monitor individual judges' rules in SDNY and EDNY, which may layer additional requirements on top of the new joint local rules.
