# Seventh Circuit Vacates Clearview AI's Novel Equity-Based Class Settlement

**Jurisdiction:** Federal (Seventh Circuit; underlying MDL in N.D. Illinois) | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2026-07-14

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On July 13, 2026, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit vacated the district court's approval of a first-of-its-kind class-action settlement in *In re Clearview AI, Inc. Consumer Privacy Litigation*, No. 25-1673, which would have given a nationwide class and four state subclasses a combined 23% equity stake in Clearview AI (worth an estimated $51.75 million) instead of a cash payout, [Justia's summary of the opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/25-1673/25-1673-2026-07-13.html) and [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/clearview-ai-privacy-equity-deal-could-collapse-on-process-issue) confirm. The panel held that the absence of separately counseled representatives for the nationwide class, apart from the state subclasses, violated the adequacy-of-representation requirement of Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(a)(4), reopening the multidistrict biometric-privacy litigation against the facial-recognition company [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2500470/7th-circ-nixes-clearview-ai-privacy-deal-over-class-rift) reports.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

Clearview AI built a facial-recognition database by scraping billions of photographs from social media and other public websites without consent and selling database access primarily to law enforcement, conduct at the center of consolidated multidistrict litigation pending in the Northern District of Illinois since 2021 (case no. 1:21-cv-00135), as summarized by [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/court-rejects-deal-reopens-clearview-ai-lawsuit-over-biometric-data-collection). After mediating in 2023, the parties reached a novel settlement structure under which, upon an initial public offering, merger, or sale of the company, the settlement class would receive a payment equivalent to a 23% equity stake in Clearview as valued on September 6, 2023 -- estimated at $51.75 million but contingent on Clearview eventually reaching a roughly $225 million valuation event, according to [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/clearview-ai-privacy-equity-deal-could-collapse-on-process-issue) and [cryptobriefing's summary of the ruling](https://cryptobriefing.com/appeals-court-overturns-clearview-ai-settlement/). The settlement class was structured as a nationwide class plus four state-specific subclasses (Illinois, California, New York, and Virginia), covering roughly 125,000 members. U.S. District Judge Sharon Johnson Coleman approved the settlement as fair, reasonable, and adequate in March 2025 over the objection of a bipartisan coalition of 22 state attorneys general and the District of Columbia, per [reporting on the underlying settlement approval](https://www.law.com/2025/05/14/judge-approves-rare-privacy-settlement-awarding-class-members-23-stake-in-clearview-ai/?slreturn=20251125162434). Objectors then appealed to the Seventh Circuit.

## The Seventh Circuit's Ruling [HIGH confidence]

The Seventh Circuit vacated the settlement approval and remanded to the district court, holding that the settlement's allocation scheme created an unaddressed structural conflict between the nationwide class and the state subclasses. According to the [Duane Morris Class Action Defense blog's analysis](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/classactiondefense/2026/07/14/seventh-circuit-undoes-novel-privacy-class-settlement-due-to-lack-of-separate-representatives-for-nationwide-class-and-state-sub-classes/) and corroborating coverage from [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/class-actions/1817808/seventh-circuit-undoes-novel-privacy-class-settlement-due-to-lack-of-separate-representatives-for-nationwide-class-and-state-sub-classes), the panel found that none of the named class representatives was positioned to represent solely the interests of the nationwide class in negotiating the allocation ratios between the nationwide class and the more favorably treated state subclasses. The court characterized the "appointment of separately counseled class representatives for identifiable, significantly different groups of claimants with fundamentally conflicting interests" as "Rule 23's primary mechanism" for protecting against exactly this kind of allocation conflict, and found that mechanism absent here.

The court also noted a related process defect: none of the eight original class representatives agreed to the settlement terms, prompting class counsel to substitute four new representatives, each of whom belonged to one of the "favored" state subclasses -- leaving the nationwide class, which received the smallest proportional share of value, with no representative who negotiated exclusively on its behalf, per [Biometric Update's account of the ruling](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202607/court-rejects-deal-reopens-clearview-ai-lawsuit-over-biometric-data-collection) and the [Cook County Record's coverage](https://www.legalnewsline.com/cook-county-record/clearview-biometrics-settlement-tossed-lawyers-didn-t-represent-everyone-involved-judges-say/article_674a89c5-5d98-4e52-a70c-6e57ef2ca8fb.html). The case is remanded for further proceedings, meaning the settlement (and its unusual equity-for-privacy-claims structure) must be renegotiated, reapproved with adequate representation for all class segments, or litigated further.

## Practical Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

- The ruling undercuts, at least temporarily, one of the first equity-for-privacy-claims class settlements and signals that courts will scrutinize novel non-cash settlement structures for conflicts between differently situated class segments.
- Companies and plaintiffs' counsel structuring biometric or other privacy class settlements with multiple state subclasses should ensure each materially distinct claimant group -- including any nationwide "catch-all" class -- has its own negotiating representative and counsel before submitting a settlement for approval.
- The underlying BIPA and multistate privacy claims against Clearview AI remain unresolved and return to the district court for further proceedings, meaning renewed uncertainty for Clearview AI's litigation exposure and for the roughly 125,000 class members awaiting resolution.

## Action Items

- Clients involved in pending or contemplated multi-jurisdiction privacy class settlements should review settlement allocation structures now for potential Rule 23(a)(4) adequacy gaps between a nationwide class and state subclasses, particularly where allocation ratios differ.
- Monitor the N.D. Illinois docket (case no. 1:21-cv-00135) for renewed settlement negotiations, appointment of additional class representatives, or a return to active litigation.
- Track whether the Seventh Circuit's reasoning is cited in other pending biometric privacy MDLs or class settlements involving non-cash consideration.
