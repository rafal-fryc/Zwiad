# Federal Court Finds DOJ Bulk Sensitive Data Regulations Open New Avenue for ECPA Suits

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** July 17, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

On June 16, 2026, Judge Matthew F. Kennelly of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois denied a motion to dismiss in *Baker v. Index Exchange, Inc.*, No. 1:25-cv-10517 (N.D. Ill.), holding that a plaintiff's allegation that a company violated the DOJ's Bulk Sensitive Data ("BSD") Transfer Rule, [28 C.F.R. Part 202](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202), is sufficient to invoke the "crime/tort exception" to the Electronic Communications Privacy Act's ("ECPA," 18 U.S.C. § 2511) one-party consent defense. This is the first federal court decision to allow a private ECPA wiretap claim to survive dismissal on the theory that a BSD Rule violation — a regulation that itself creates no private right of action — can serve as the predicate tortious act defeating consent, opening a new litigation pathway with ECPA's statutory damages and vicarious-liability exposure for companies engaged in cross-border ad-tech and data-sharing arrangements ([Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2026-07/federal-court-finds-doj-bulk-sensitive-data-regulations-open-new-avenue-for-ecpa-suits); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260623-federal-court-greenlights-use-of-the-doj-bulk)). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [DISPUTED]

- Plaintiff John Baker, an Evanston, Illinois resident, filed a putative class action on September 2, 2025 alleging that Index Exchange Inc. (a Canadian supply-side ad-tech platform) and its U.S. subsidiary, Index Exchange USA, LLC, intercepted his communications with BibleGateway.com and transmitted his IP address, cookie identifiers, advertising identifiers, and device/browser data to Temu, a China-linked company, without lawful consent ([Justia docket, No. 1:25-cv-10517](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/illinois/ilndce/1:2025cv10517/485477); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415907/index-exchange-must-face-wiretap-suit-judge-rules.html)). <!-- verified -->
- Baker's ECPA claim depends on the "crime/tort exception," which nullifies a consent defense to unlawful interception where the interception was "for the purpose of committing any criminal or tortious act in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States or of any State" (18 U.S.C. § 2511(2)(d), available via [Cornell LII / U.S. Code](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511)). Baker argued Index Exchange's alleged violation of the BSD Rule's restrictions on bulk sensitive-data transfers to "countries of concern" (including China) supplied the predicate tort. <!-- verified -->
- Index Exchange argued the BSD Rule does not apply to it because it is a Canadian entity, but the court found Baker adequately alleged both vicarious liability for the acts of Index Exchange's U.S.-based officers/employees and direct liability on an alter-ego theory involving Index Exchange USA, a Michigan corporation ([Leagle, Case No. 20260618a83](https://www.leagle.com/decision/infdco20260618a83); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/07/federal-district-court-allows-bulk-data-rule-based-class-claim-to-proceed-a-bellwether)). <!-- verified -->
- Judge Kennelly wrote that "[e]ven if BibleGateway.com consented to the interception of its communications with Baker, he has sufficiently alleged that the crime/tort exception applies based on Index Exchange's alleged violation of the BSD regulations," denying the motion to dismiss the amended complaint ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415907/index-exchange-must-face-wiretap-suit-judge-rules.html); corroborated by [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/index-exchange-must-face-wiretap-claim-over-china-data-transfer)). <!-- verified -->
- The ruling is preliminary and limited to the pleading stage; the court noted unresolved factual questions remain, including whether the BSD Rule's restrictions on transfers to China actually apply to Temu ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415907/index-exchange-must-face-wiretap-suit-judge-rules.html); [ReedSmith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102n4mk/ecpa-and-the-bulk-rule-test-case-survives-major-hurdle/)). The BSD Rule itself does not create a private right of action — the significance of the decision is that it lets plaintiffs use an alleged BSD violation as a predicate tort inside an existing ECPA cause of action, not that it creates a standalone BSD claim ([Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2026-07/federal-court-finds-doj-bulk-sensitive-data-regulations-open-new-avenue-for-ecpa-suits); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/07/federal-district-court-allows-bulk-data-rule-based-class-claim-to-proceed-a-bellwether)). <!-- verified -->
- Legal commentators identify particular exposure for the real-time ad-bidding (RTB) industry, where supply-side platforms routinely transmit user identifiers and device data to ad partners that may include entities connected to countries of concern, and note ECPA's statutory damages (up to $10,000 per violation or actual damages, whichever is greater, plus punitive damages and attorneys' fees under 18 U.S.C. § 2520) substantially raise the stakes compared to the BSD Rule's own IEEPA-based civil/criminal penalty regime, which only DOJ can enforce ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/passle/2026/06/realtime-ad-bidding-faces-new-litigation-vulnerability-key-takeaways-from-baker-v-index-exchange); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260623-federal-court-greenlights-use-of-the-doj-bulk)). <!-- disputed: § 2520(c)(2) authorizes the greater of actual damages/profits or statutory damages equal to the greater of $100/day of violation or $10,000 (a single aggregate figure) -- not a flat "$10,000 per violation" as phrased here -->

## Action Items

- **Ad-tech, SSP/DSP, and data-broker companies:** Map data flows to determine whether any counterparties or downstream recipients qualify as "covered persons" or are located in "countries of concern" under the BSD Rule ([28 C.F.R. § 202.211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-202/subpart-B/section-202.211) et seq.), since a BSD violation can now be leveraged as the predicate tort in a private ECPA suit even though the BSD Rule itself has no private right of action.
- **Companies with foreign parent/subsidiary structures:** Reassess corporate-separateness and vicarious-liability exposure — the *Baker* court allowed both an alter-ego and a vicarious-liability theory to proceed against a foreign parent based on a U.S. subsidiary's employees' conduct.
- **Legal and compliance teams:** Monitor the *Baker v. Index Exchange* docket (N.D. Ill., No. 1:25-cv-10517) for further developments, including class certification and any resolution of the factual dispute over whether Temu is covered by the China restrictions, as the case proceeds past the motion-to-dismiss stage.
- **Privacy and litigation counsel:** Expect a wave of follow-on ECPA suits pleading BSD Rule violations as the predicate tort, particularly in the RTB/ad-tech space; review consent mechanisms and data-sharing agreements with an eye toward both ECPA consent defenses and BSD Rule restricted-transaction obligations.
