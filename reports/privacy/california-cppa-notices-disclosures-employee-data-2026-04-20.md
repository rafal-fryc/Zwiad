# CalPrivacy Launches Pre-Rulemaking on Notices & Disclosures and Employee Data Under CCPA

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** April 20, 2026

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 20, 2026, the California Privacy Protection Agency — now formally known as CalPrivacy — issued an [Invitation for Preliminary Comments](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/notices_disclosures_employee_data.pdf) on two substantial topics under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA): (1) notices and disclosures requirements, and (2) employee and applicant personal data. The pre-rulemaking comment window runs through May 20, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. PT. This engagement phase follows a February 27, 2026 public meeting at which CPPA General Counsel Philip Laird previewed the agency's 2026 rulemaking agenda. Preliminary comments do not constitute a formal rulemaking record and no regulatory text has been proposed; if the agency proceeds, a full Administrative Procedure Act (APA) process — including proposed regulations and another public comment period — will follow, making final rules unlikely before 2027. California employers and businesses with consumer-facing operations should nonetheless engage now, as this is the earliest opportunity to shape the substantive direction of rules that could significantly alter CCPA compliance obligations.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The CCPA's Employee Data History

The [California Consumer Privacy Act](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5), originally enacted in 2018, included a temporary exemption under [Civil Code § 1798.145(m)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.145.) for personal information of job applicants, employees, owners, directors, medical staff members, and contractors collected or used by a business in the course of that employment relationship. The exemption was extended through January 1, 2021, then again to January 1, 2022, and then once more — under the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) — through January 1, 2023.

When the California Legislature ended its 2022 session on August 31 without passing any further extension, the employee data exemption expired on December 31, 2022. As of January 1, 2023, all CCPA rights and protections — including the rights to know, access, delete, correct, and opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information — apply in full to employees, job applicants, independent contractors, and other workers of a covered business. This change, documented by [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/10/california-consumer-privacy-act-employee-and-b2b-exemptions-expire-january-1-2023) and [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.workforcebulletin.com/no-more-exceptions-what-to-do-when-the-california-privacy-exemptions-for-employee-applicant-and-b2b-data-expire-on-january-1-2023), imposed sweeping new notice, access, and rights-response obligations on California employers without purpose-built regulatory guidance.

### Existing Notice and Disclosure Framework

The CCPA and its implementing regulations currently require businesses to provide consumers — now including employees — with a privacy policy, a Notice at Collection at or before the point of data collection, and notices describing consumers' rights. Under [Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.100.), a business controlling the collection of personal information must, at or before the point of collection, inform consumers of the categories of personal information to be collected, the purposes for which those categories are collected or used, and whether such information is sold or shared. These requirements apply to hiring, active employment, and offboarding contexts — yet no CPPA regulations specifically address the practical mechanics of delivering notices in those employment contexts.

### The CPPA's 2026 Rulemaking Agenda

At a January 2026 webinar hosted by the Centre for Information Policy Leadership (CIPL), CPPA General Counsel Philip Laird [identified several regulatory priorities for 2026](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/california-privacy-protection-agency-to-hold-public-meeting-to-discuss-future-regulations), including: employee data, CCPA-mandated notices and disclosures, reducing friction in the exercise of consumer privacy rights, and opt-out signals. On February 27, 2026, the agency held a public meeting to discuss these upcoming rulemaking activities. The April 20, 2026 preliminary comments invitation is the formal launch of the pre-rulemaking engagement phase for two of those identified priorities.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Structure of the Preliminary Comment Invitation

The CPPA's [invitation document](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/notices_disclosures_employee_data.pdf) (also mirrored at [workplaceprivacyreport.com](https://www.workplaceprivacyreport.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/938/2026/05/CPPA-notices_disclosures_employee_data-4-20-2026.pdf)) poses questions on two parallel tracks:

**Track 1 — Notices and Disclosures:** CalPrivacy is evaluating whether existing CCPA regulations on privacy policies, Notices at Collection, and rights notices require updating. The agency is interested in whether the current requirements create confusion or unnecessary compliance burden for businesses or friction for consumers, what format and delivery channel obligations are appropriate, and whether the regulations need to address emerging practices such as layered or just-in-time notices.

**Track 2 — Employee Data:** CalPrivacy is asking what difficulties employers encounter when providing job applicants and employees with the ability to exercise CCPA rights throughout the employment lifecycle — hiring, active employment, and offboarding — and how regulations could better address those concerns. The agency is also soliciting input from employees, applicants, and other workers directly.

The invitation makes clear that the preliminary comment phase is informational only: "The preliminary comments sought are to assist CalPrivacy with its preliminary rulemaking activities and do not reflect any decisions made by CalPrivacy regarding future rulemaking." If the agency decides to propose regulations, it will initiate a formal APA rulemaking with a separate proposed-rule text and another public comment period.

### Why Employee Data Rulemaking Matters

Despite the January 1, 2023 expiration of the employee exemption, employers have been operating under general CCPA obligations without purpose-built guidance. Key practical tensions include:

- **Notice at Collection timing:** Employees interact with employer data systems continuously. Providing a Notice at Collection at each new collection point throughout employment is operationally complex in ways that consumer-facing businesses do not face.
- **Rights requests in the employment context:** Deletion requests from employees, for example, may conflict with legal retention obligations (tax, payroll, workers' compensation, litigation hold). No existing CCPA regulations specifically address how these conflicts should be resolved.
- **Sensitive personal information:** HR data routinely includes sensitive categories under the CPRA — Social Security numbers, racial or ethnic origin, health information, precise geolocation, financial account information — each of which triggers heightened obligations and notice requirements.
- **Service providers in the HR context:** Payroll processors, benefits administrators, and applicant tracking systems all function as CCPA service providers or contractors. Their role in rights-request fulfillment is not clearly mapped in current regulations.

The [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ccpa-employee-data-rulemaking-could-reshape-employer-privacy-compliance-california) has noted that this rulemaking "could reshape employer privacy compliance in California," while [Workplace Privacy Report](https://www.workplaceprivacyreport.com/2026/05/articles/california-consumer-privacy-act/california-privacy-agency-invites-comments-on-ccpa-application-to-employee-and-applicant-data/) observes that the agency is seeking input from workers as well as employers — suggesting CalPrivacy may introduce employee-protective requirements beyond mere notice clarifications.

### Procedural Posture

This invitation is a pre-proposal engagement mechanism, not a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) or California equivalent. Under California's APA (Gov. Code § 11340 et seq.), formal rulemaking requires a 45-day public comment period on published regulatory text, OAL review, and potential additional comment rounds. The current phase precedes all of that. Stakeholders who submit preliminary comments now do so without the procedural protections of formal rulemaking, but the practical influence of early engagement is significant — CalPrivacy's recent rulemaking history (ADMT, risk assessments, cybersecurity audits) shows that the agency substantially reshapes rules based on pre-rulemaking stakeholder input.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

The two tracks affect overlapping but distinct constituencies:

- **Employers subject to the CCPA:** Businesses with annual gross revenues over $25 million, or that buy, sell, or share personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households, or that derive 50% or more of revenues from selling personal information (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(d)) — and that employ California workers — face the greatest exposure from employee data rulemaking.
- **Consumer-facing businesses broadly:** The notices and disclosures track affects every CCPA-covered business that presents privacy policies or Notices at Collection to consumers, regardless of whether they have California employees.
- **HR technology vendors:** Applicant tracking systems, payroll processors, and benefits platforms that handle California employee data as service providers may face new contractual or direct obligations depending on how rulemaking defines responsibilities in the employment data chain.

### Compliance Timeline Projection

| Milestone | Estimated Date |
|---|---|
| Preliminary comment deadline | May 20, 2026 |
| Agency review and possible public meeting | Q3 2026 |
| Formal NPRM (if agency proceeds) | Q4 2026 – Q1 2027 |
| 45-day formal comment period | Q1 2027 |
| OAL review and adoption | Q2–Q3 2027 |
| Effective date (if standard APA timeline) | Late 2027 |

These estimates are speculative. The CPPA's cybersecurity audit and ADMT regulations took approximately 18 months from preliminary engagement to OAL approval. A similar timeline here would put effective rules in late 2027 at the earliest.

### Enforcement Outlook

CalPrivacy has signaled an intent to actively enforce CCPA obligations, including against employers. The agency's February 2026 [enforcement advisory on data minimization](reports/privacy/enforcement-actions/california-cppa-enforcement-advisory-data-minimization-2024-04-02.md) and the [Growbots settlement](reports/privacy/enforcement-actions/california-cppa-data-broker-delete-act-growbots-2025-03-13.md) demonstrate operational enforcement capability. While the agency is unlikely to focus enforcement resources on employer notice deficiencies during the pre-rulemaking period, businesses should not read the absence of specific employee data rules as a safe harbor — general CCPA notice obligations already apply.

## Action Items

- **Submit preliminary comments by May 20, 2026** at 5:00 p.m. PT, electronically to regulations@cppa.ca.gov with subject line "Preliminary Comment – Notices & Disclosures and Employee Data April 2026"; this is the earliest and most influential opportunity to shape the regulatory outcome.
- **Audit current employee Notice at Collection practices**: map when and how Notices at Collection are delivered to job applicants (at application), new hires (at onboarding), and existing employees (when new data collection begins) and identify gaps relative to Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100(a).
- **Review existing HR privacy policies and employee rights-request procedures**: verify that privacy policies disclose HR data categories, retention periods, sensitive personal information categories, and the full scope of employee privacy rights; ensure internal rights-request workflows address deletion conflicts with legal retention obligations.
- **Brief HR and legal leadership** on the rulemaking trajectory and estimated compliance timeline so that HR technology procurement, policy revisions, and vendor contract updates can be phased into 2026–2027 planning cycles.
- **Monitor the CPPA regulations page** ([cppa.ca.gov/regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/)) and specifically the [Notices & Disclosures](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/notices_disclosures.html) and [Employee Data](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/employee_data.html) sub-pages for agency announcements of public meetings, formal NPRM, or additional guidance.
