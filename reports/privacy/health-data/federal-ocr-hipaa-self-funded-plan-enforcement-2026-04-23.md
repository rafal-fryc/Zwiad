# OCR Enforces HIPAA Against Self-Funded Employee Benefit Plan in Landmark Ransomware Settlement

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-23

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 23, 2026, the HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) announced what may be the first HIPAA privacy and security enforcement action taken directly against a self-funded employee benefit plan — the Star Group, L.P. Health Benefits Plan (SG Health Plan), sponsored by a Connecticut-based energy provider. OCR found that a 2021 ransomware attack exfiltrated the electronic protected health information (ePHI) of 9,316 individuals, and that the plan had both failed to conduct an adequate risk analysis and impermissibly disclosed PHI. The plan's employer/sponsor agreed to a $245,000 payment and a two-year Corrective Action Plan (CAP) with OCR monitoring. The action is part of a broader April 23 announcement in which OCR settled four separate ransomware investigations totaling $1,165,000, marking 19 completed investigations under OCR's Risk Analysis Initiative.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Enforcement target:** Star Group, L.P. Health Benefits Plan (SG Health Plan) — the self-funded employee benefit plan of Star Group, L.P., a Connecticut-based energy distributor — reported in October 2021 that a ransomware attack had caused ePHI to be exfiltrated from its information systems. ([HHS OCR press release, April 23, 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html))

- **Individuals affected:** 9,316 plan participants had their ePHI compromised in the incident. ([HHS SG Health Plan Resolution Agreement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/ra-cap-with-sg-health-plan/index.html))

- **Violations found:** OCR determined SG Health Plan (1) failed to conduct an accurate and thorough risk analysis as required under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A), and (2) impermissibly disclosed PHI in violation of the HIPAA Privacy Rule. ([HHS OCR press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html))

- **Settlement:** The employer/plan sponsor agreed to pay **$245,000** in a lump sum within seven days of the agreement's effective date, and to enter into a two-year Corrective Action Plan subject to HHS monitoring. The resolution agreement was signed January 8, 2026. ([SG Health Plan Resolution Agreement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/ra-cap-with-sg-health-plan/index.html))

- **Corrective Action Plan requirements:** The CAP requires SG Health Plan to (a) conduct and document an accurate and thorough ePHI risk assessment, (b) implement security measures to address identified risks, and (c) provide HIPAA training to all workforce members with PHI access within 60 days of HHS approval of policies and then at least annually thereafter, with new workforce members trained within 30 days of hire. ([SG Health Plan Resolution Agreement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/ra-cap-with-sg-health-plan/index.html))

- **Broader April 23 action:** This settlement was announced alongside three other ransomware enforcement actions — Regional Women's Health Group d/b/a Axia Women's Health ($320,000), Assured Imaging Affiliated Covered Entities ($375,000), and Consociate, Inc. d/b/a Consociate Health ($225,000) — with all four cases affecting more than 427,000 individuals in total and yielding $1,165,000 in financial penalties. ([HHS OCR press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html); [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/ocr-fines-four-regulated-entities-hipaa-violations-ansomware-attacks/))

- **Risk Analysis Initiative milestone:** The April 23 settlements mark 19 completed investigations under OCR's Risk Analysis Initiative, which was launched in October 2024 to focus enforcement on the HIPAA Security Rule's risk analysis requirement. The initiative was initially announced in response to a reported 264% increase in reported large breaches involving ransomware since 2018. ([Nixon Peabody analysis, April 30, 2026](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/articles/2026/04/30/ransomware-enforcement-update-19-investigations-completed-by-ocr-four-settlements-added))

- **Legal significance of target:** Under HIPAA, self-funded employer-sponsored group health plans (except self-administered plans with fewer than 50 participants) are "covered entities" and the plan is legally distinct from the employer in its employment capacity. Enforcement against the plan itself — rather than a healthcare provider or commercial insurer — is rare and signals OCR's willingness to extend its enforcement reach into ERISA plan structures. ([Benefits Law Advisor, April 2026](https://www.benefitslawadvisor.com/2026/04/articles/hipaa/ocr-announces-hipaa-enforcement-action-against-self-funded-group-health-plan/); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ocr-announces-hipaa-enforcement-action-against-self-funded-group-health-plan))

## Action Items

- **All employers sponsoring self-funded health plans:** Treat this action as confirmation that HIPAA Security Rule obligations — including the mandatory risk analysis under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) — apply directly to the plan and will be enforced. Conduct or update a documented, thorough risk analysis of all ePHI held or transmitted by the plan without delay.

- **ERISA plan sponsors and benefits administrators:** Review whether the plan (not merely the insurer or TPA) has implemented documented security measures addressing identified risks. Verify that workforce members with plan PHI access have received HIPAA training within the past 12 months and that training records are maintained.

- **Benefits counsel and HR departments:** Assess whether the plan's information security posture would survive an OCR investigation. OCR's CAP in this case required policies, risk assessments, and training programs — confirm these are in place and up to date.

- **Incident response planning:** The underlying 2021 ransomware attack was reported in October 2021 but the resolution agreement was signed in January 2026 — a multi-year investigation window. Employers with any prior security incidents involving plan PHI should assess whether they have adequately remediated identified vulnerabilities and documented that remediation.

- **Monitor SG Health Plan CAP:** OCR will monitor SG Health Plan for two years. If the plan violates the CAP, OCR may impose civil monetary penalties under 45 C.F.R. Part 160. This enforcement mechanism applies to other entities under active CAPs and illustrates the ongoing compliance burden post-settlement.
