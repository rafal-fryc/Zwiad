# Federal Healthcare Regulatory Update: CMS and Senate HELP Committee Developments with Health Data Privacy Implications (April 2024)

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-04-17

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

In April 2024, three significant federal healthcare regulatory developments occurred that carry data privacy implications. First, Senate HELP Committee Chairman Bernie Sanders released a draft Long COVID Moonshot legislative proposal that would create a national de-identified patient health data registry at NIH. Second, CMS announced the proposed Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM), a mandatory episode-based payment model requiring hospitals to receive and manage beneficiary-identifiable Medicare claims data. Third, CMS issued the FY 2025 Inpatient Psychiatric Facilities Prospective Payment System proposed rule (CMS-1806-P), which proposes new quality measures requiring collection of patient-level data on social drivers of health. Each of these developments raises questions about health information privacy under HIPAA and related frameworks, particularly around patient data aggregation, claims data sharing, and expanded quality reporting obligations.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) establishes the baseline federal framework for protecting individually identifiable health information held by covered entities and their business associates. Healthcare payment models and quality reporting programs at CMS routinely involve the exchange of protected health information (PHI), governed by HIPAA's treatment, payment, and health care operations (TPO) exceptions and specific regulatory provisions for research and public health purposes.

The Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions (HELP) has jurisdiction over public health matters, including disease research and health data policy. Its legislative proposals frequently intersect with HIPAA by establishing new data collection or sharing mandates. The April 2024 developments discussed in this memo each create new channels for health data collection, aggregation, or sharing that warrant privacy analysis.

CMS has increasingly conditioned participation in payment models on data exchange, including beneficiary-identifiable claims data under the [CMS Innovation Center's data sharing principles](https://www.crowellhealthsolutionsblog.com/2024/09/cms-innovation-center-outlines-data-sharing-principles/) published later in September 2024, which commit to aligning with HHS Cybersecurity Performance Goals and HIPAA.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### 1. Long COVID Research Moonshot Proposal (Senate HELP Committee)

On April 9, 2024, Senate HELP Committee Chairman Bernie Sanders released a [draft legislative proposal](https://www.help.senate.gov/dem/newsroom/press/news-chairman-bernie-sanders-releases-long-covid-moonshot-legislative-proposal) to address Long COVID through $1 billion per year in mandatory NIH funding over ten years. The proposal sought public comments through April 23, 2024, and was later formally introduced as [S. 4964, the Long COVID Research Moonshot Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/4964/text/is).

**Data Privacy Provisions.** The bill requires the NIH Director to establish a Long COVID database "for the storage and dissemination of de-identified patient data" to make research more accessible. The database would include a registry of clinical trials with patient eligibility criteria and demographic information including sex, age, disability status, ethnicity, race, and trial site locations. The bill also allocates $16.6 million annually to the FDA for an electronic reporting form through which patients would report current and developmental treatments.

**Privacy Implications.** The de-identification requirement addresses HIPAA concerns by mandating that the NIH database strip personally identifiable information before dissemination. However, the breadth of demographic data collected -- combined with geographic trial site information -- raises re-identification risks, particularly for patients in rural areas or with rare conditions. The bill does not specify which HIPAA de-identification standard (Safe Harbor vs. Expert Determination) must be applied, leaving implementation details to NIH rulemaking. The patient-facing FDA electronic reporting form would collect treatment information directly from individuals, potentially outside the HIPAA-covered entity framework depending on implementation. The [AAMC endorsed the proposal](https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/sen-bernie-sanders-floats-long-covid-moonshot-legislative-proposal) but noted the importance of research data infrastructure.

### 2. Transforming Episode Accountability Model (TEAM) -- CMS Proposed Rule

On April 10, 2024, CMS proposed the [TEAM model](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/team-model) as part of the FY 2025 Hospital Inpatient Prospective Payment Systems (IPPS) proposed rule. TEAM is a mandatory episode-based payment model covering five surgical procedures (lower extremity joint replacement, surgical hip fracture treatment, spinal fusion, coronary artery bypass graft, and major bowel procedures) that would run from January 1, 2026, through December 31, 2030.

**Data Sharing Requirements.** CMS proposed to make [beneficiary-identifiable claims data](https://www.mcdermottplus.com/blog/regs-eggs/cmmi-proposes-new-mandatory-episode-based-payment-model/) available to participating hospitals. Hospitals would receive claims data for beneficiaries who initiated episodes at their facility during a three-year baseline period, limited to items and services within the episode. During performance years, hospitals could request monthly beneficiary-identifiable claims data and regional aggregate data for care management and coordination.

**Privacy Implications.** The mandatory nature of TEAM distinguishes it from voluntary alternative payment models where participants opt into data sharing. Hospitals in selected Core-Based Statistical Areas (CBSAs) would be required to participate and would automatically receive beneficiary-identifiable data, creating new obligations under HIPAA's minimum necessary standard and the HIPAA Security Rule. The [AAOS summary](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/advocacy/issues/team-proposed-model-summary.pdf) highlighted that CMS's stratified random sampling methodology means hospitals may receive patient data without prior experience managing episode-level claims information, raising security preparedness concerns. The final rule was issued in [August 2024](https://hallrender.com/2024/09/05/cms-releases-final-rule-transforming-episode-accountability-model/).

### 3. FY 2025 Inpatient Psychiatric Facility Payment Rule (CMS-1806-P)

On April 3, 2024, CMS published the [FY 2025 IPF PPS proposed rule](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/fiscal-year-2025-medicare-inpatient-psychiatric-facilities-prospective-payment-system-ipf-pps-and) in the Federal Register ([89 FR 23440](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/03/2024-06764/medicare-program-fy-2025-inpatient-psychiatric-facilities-prospective-payment-system-rate-update)), proposing a 2.7% payment rate update and new quality reporting requirements.

**New Quality Measure -- Social Drivers of Health.** CMS proposed to adopt a Screening for Social Drivers of Health measure under the IPF Quality Reporting (IPFQR) Program, beginning with voluntary reporting in CY 2025 and required reporting in CY 2026 to inform FY 2027 payment determinations. This measure requires IPFs to collect and submit patient-level data on social determinants including housing instability, food insecurity, transportation barriers, and utility difficulties.

**Privacy Implications.** The collection of social drivers of health data in inpatient psychiatric settings is particularly sensitive. Psychiatric patients have heightened HIPAA protections under 42 CFR Part 2 (if substance use disorder treatment is involved) and state-level mental health confidentiality laws. Requiring IPFs to report patient-level social determinants data -- which by nature reveals information about housing status, food security, and economic circumstances -- expands the scope of PHI that facilities must collect, store, and transmit to CMS. Organizations must ensure that data submission systems meet HIPAA Security Rule administrative, physical, and technical safeguard requirements, and that staff collecting this sensitive information are trained on appropriate privacy protections.

### 4. Medicaid Payment Integrity Initiative

CMS also announced initiatives related to Medicaid payment integrity during this period. The [Comprehensive Medicaid Integrity Plan for FYs 2024-2028](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/comprehensive-medicaid-integrity-plan-fys-2024-2028.pdf) established CMS's strategy for combating fraud, waste, and abuse through modernized data analytics and a new Medicaid and CHIP Program Integrity Reporting Portal. These integrity efforts involve processing claims data containing PHI and personally identifiable information (PII), which CMS states is handled "consistent with all applicable privacy requirements."

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

**Affected Entities:**
- **Acute care hospitals** in CBSAs selected for TEAM participation face mandatory beneficiary-identifiable claims data management obligations starting January 2026.
- **Inpatient psychiatric facilities** nationwide must prepare for expanded patient-level quality data reporting on social determinants of health, with required reporting beginning in CY 2026.
- **NIH-funded research institutions** and clinical trial sites would handle de-identified Long COVID patient data if S. 4964 is enacted.
- **State Medicaid agencies** face enhanced data sharing and reporting requirements under the CMIP 2024-2028.

**Compliance Considerations:**
- TEAM participants must audit their HIPAA security posture to handle monthly beneficiary-identifiable claims data feeds, including encryption, access controls, and business associate agreements with any vendors involved in episode analytics.
- IPFs must implement workflows for collecting, storing, and transmitting social drivers of health data with appropriate privacy protections, particularly given the sensitivity of psychiatric treatment contexts.
- Organizations responding to NIH Long COVID data requests should verify the de-identification methodology used and ensure compliance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule's research provisions (45 CFR 164.512(i)) or de-identification standards (45 CFR 164.514).

**Timeline:**
- TEAM model: Effective January 1, 2026 (finalized August 2024).
- IPF social drivers measure: Voluntary reporting CY 2025; required reporting CY 2026; payment impact FY 2027.
- Long COVID Moonshot Act (S. 4964): Introduced September 2024; did not advance in the 118th Congress.

## Action Items

- Healthcare organizations in TEAM-eligible CBSAs should conduct a HIPAA Security Rule gap assessment focused on claims data ingestion and analytics infrastructure before the January 2026 effective date.
- Inpatient psychiatric facilities should begin developing data collection workflows for social drivers of health screening and ensure transmission pathways to CMS meet HIPAA technical safeguard requirements.
- Privacy officers at research institutions should monitor any reintroduction of Long COVID research legislation in the 119th Congress for new patient data registry obligations.
- Compliance teams should review the CMS Innovation Center's data sharing principles to understand evolving expectations for beneficiary data handling in mandatory payment models.
- Organizations should evaluate whether social determinants data collected under the IPF quality measure is subject to additional state-level privacy protections beyond HIPAA.
