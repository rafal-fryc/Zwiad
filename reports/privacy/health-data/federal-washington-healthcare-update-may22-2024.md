# Federal Healthcare Legislative Update: FY2025 Appropriations Toplines, Senate Finance Medicare White Paper, and House Committee Activity (Week of May 20, 2024)

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-05-22

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

During the week of May 20, 2024, three significant federal healthcare policy developments unfolded with regulatory implications. First, House Appropriations Committee Chairman Tom Cole (R-OK) released interim FY2025 topline subcommittee allocations, with the Labor, Health and Human Services, and Education (LHHS) subcommittee receiving a draft allocation approximately 10–11% below FY2024 enacted levels, reflecting Republican fiscal priorities under the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 (FRA). Second, Senate Finance Committee Chair Ron Wyden (D-OR) and Ranking Member Mike Crapo (R-ID) released a bipartisan [white paper on Medicare physician payment reform](https://www.finance.senate.gov/chairmans-news/wyden-and-crapo-release-white-paper-for-medicare-doctor-pay-reform), calling for overhaul of the Physician Fee Schedule (PFS), reassessment of MIPS quality reporting, and sustained investment in Alternative Payment Models (APMs). Third, the House Energy and Commerce (E&C) Subcommittee on Health advanced 21 healthcare bills to the full committee in a May 16 markup, including major telehealth extensions. Taken together, these developments signal continued federal engagement with Medicare payment infrastructure and healthcare funding — each with downstream implications for health data collection, quality reporting, and HIPAA-regulated data flows.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### FY2025 Appropriations Framework

The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 (P.L. 118-5), enacted June 3, 2023, established statutory caps on discretionary spending: $895.2 billion for defense and $710.7 billion for nondefense programs in FY2025, representing a 1% increase for defense and an effective reduction for nondefense programs. Congress uses a two-stage process for appropriations: first, a 302(a) allocation sets the overall ceiling for each chamber's Appropriations Committee, then 302(b) suballocations divide that ceiling among the 12 subcommittees. The LHHS subcommittee has jurisdiction over HHS, the Department of Labor, and the Department of Education — the primary federal health-program funding vehicle.

By May 2024, the full-year FY2024 LHHS appropriations had been enacted on March 23, 2024, as part of a consolidated appropriations package (P.L. 118-47). The FY2025 process was behind schedule, with the Appropriations Committee needing to adopt subcommittee allocations before beginning bill markups.

### Medicare Physician Payment and Quality Reporting

The Medicare Physician Fee Schedule (PFS) sets payment rates for physician services under Medicare Part B. The conversion factor — the dollar multiplier applied to relative value units (RVUs) — does not incorporate automatic inflation adjustments, unlike the Hospital IPPS payment system. As a result, physician payment rates have declined in real terms over two decades as input costs have risen. Congress has routinely enacted short-term payment patches but has not enacted comprehensive reform since the Medicare Access and CHIP Reauthorization Act (MACRA) of 2015, which created the Quality Payment Program (QPP) encompassing MIPS and Advanced APMs.

The QPP requires eligible clinicians to either participate in MIPS (submitting quality, cost, improvement activity, and promoting interoperability data) or participate in Advanced APMs. MIPS performance data is publicly reported on [CMS's Care Compare website](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/physician-compare), raising patient data transparency questions. The [Merit-based Incentive Payment System (MIPS)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2016/11/04/2016-25240/medicare-program-merit-based-incentive-payment-system-mips-and-alternative-payment-model-apm-incentive) was established by the final rule published at 81 FR 77008 (November 4, 2016).

### House E&C Jurisdiction over Medicare

The House Energy and Commerce Committee shares Medicare jurisdiction with the Ways and Means Committee, with E&C holding jurisdiction over Medicare Parts A and B (alongside Ways and Means) and over public health programs. The Subcommittee on Health handles most healthcare legislation, including telehealth bills that come before the full Committee.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### 1. FY2025 LHHS Appropriations Topline Allocation

On May 23, 2024, the [House Appropriations Committee voted 30-22](https://appropriations.house.gov/news/press-releases/committee-approves-fy25-subcommittee-allocations-prioritizing-defense-homeland) to adopt draft 302(b) suballocations for all 12 subcommittees, formally initiating the markup process. The Republican majority's allocation framework reflected the FRA's nondefense spending cap, distributing cuts unevenly across subcommittees to protect defense, homeland security, and veterans programs.

The LHHS subcommittee's draft allocation was set approximately 10–11% below the FY2024 enacted level, which would place its ceiling below $180 billion in discretionary funding (compared to the FY2024 enacted level of approximately $196.5 billion). Chairman Cole framed the allocations as fulfilling Congress's statutory duty under the FRA: "These allocations uphold the funding level prescribed in law and demonstrate that we can both fund our nation's needs and exercise fiscal discipline."

**Health Program Implications.** A roughly 10% cut to the LHHS discretionary envelope would require HHS to reduce spending across a range of programs, including the NIH, CDC, HRSA, and CMS administrative operations. NIH appropriations, in particular, fund clinical research involving patient-identifiable health data. Reductions to NIH could curtail data infrastructure investments that support de-identified research databases and genomic data repositories. CMS administrative accounts support the audit and oversight infrastructure of HIPAA-regulated programs. Cuts to CMS operational funding could reduce the agency's capacity to investigate HIPAA privacy and security complaints.

**Outcome.** The draft suballocations were not formally reported to the House as a matter of procedure. Subsequent action: the LHHS Subcommittee marked up its FY2025 bill on June 27, 2024, and the full Appropriations Committee reported [H.R. 9029](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/9029) on July 12, 2024, with a final discretionary allocation of approximately $185.8 billion — $8.6 billion (4%) below FY2024 enacted levels. FY2025 was ultimately funded through a continuing resolution rather than a full-year appropriations bill.

### 2. Senate Finance Medicare Physician Payment White Paper

On May 17, 2024, Senate Finance Committee Chairman Ron Wyden and Ranking Member Mike Crapo released a [29-page bipartisan white paper](https://www.finance.senate.gov/chairmans-news/wyden-and-crapo-release-white-paper-for-medicare-doctor-pay-reform) on Medicare physician payment reform, inviting public comments. The white paper addressed the following policy areas:

**Conversion Factor Update.** Senators Wyden and Crapo identified the PFS conversion factor's failure to track inflation as the central structural problem. The paper solicited input on how to provide more predictable and inflation-indexed annual updates to physician payment rates, which have declined roughly 26% in real terms since 2001. The paper's framing mirrors legislation like the [Strengthening Medicare for Patients and Providers Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/senate-bill/4097), which would tie PFS updates to the Medicare Economic Index.

**MIPS Reform and Potential Repeal.** The white paper expressed significant skepticism about MIPS, noting the program had not produced meaningful improvement in patient outcomes and generated excessive administrative burden. The Senators raised the possibility of scaling back or repealing MIPS entirely, directing clinicians instead toward APMs. This has direct implications for quality data reporting: MIPS requires eligible clinicians to submit quality measure data, promoting interoperability data (including electronic health record use metrics tied to the ONC's certification standards), and cost data annually. If MIPS is substantially modified or repealed, the associated data reporting infrastructure — including certified EHR technology (CEHRT) submissions to CMS — would be restructured or eliminated.

**Alternative Payment Model Incentives.** The paper endorsed strengthening incentives for participation in Advanced APMs. In 2023, approximately 227,000 clinicians (17% of QPP participants) received A-APM incentive payments. Advanced APMs typically involve prospective bundled or shared savings arrangements and require participating entities to report patient outcome data and share beneficiary-identifiable claims information with payers. Increasing APM participation would expand the volume of Medicare claims data exchanged between CMS, payers, and provider organizations — with attendant HIPAA data use agreement and business associate agreement implications.

**Telehealth.** The white paper included telehealth access as a key pillar of reform, consistent with the Senate Finance Committee's prior work on telehealth extensions. Sustained telehealth investment in the PFS context means continued transmission of PHI over digital platforms, reinforcing the need for robust HIPAA Security Rule compliance by telehealth providers.

**Public Comment Period.** Comments were accepted through June 26, 2024. The white paper did not advance to formal legislation in the 118th Congress, but the [American Medical Association](https://www.ama-assn.org/health-care-advocacy/advocacy-update/june-28-2024-medicare-payment-reform-advocacy-update), [MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/press-statements/may-17-2024-mgma-statement-on-senate-finance-white-paper-on-medicare-physician-payment-reform), and [AAMC](https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/wyden-and-crapo-release-physician-payment-whitepaper) all submitted responses emphasizing the urgency of conversion factor reform.

### 3. House Energy and Commerce Health Subcommittee Markup (May 16, 2024)

On May 16, 2024, the House E&C Subcommittee on Health advanced [21 healthcare bills](https://energycommerce.house.gov/posts/health-subcommittee-markup-recap-e-and-c-advances-legislation-to-strengthen-america-s-health-care-system) to the full committee. Key legislation with regulatory or data implications included:

**Telehealth Modernization Act of 2024 (H.R. 7623).** Passed by a roll call vote of 21-0. This bill would permanently extend Medicare telehealth flexibilities, including elimination of geographic restrictions on originating sites and authorization of audio-only telehealth for qualifying patients. As discussed in [prior McGuireWoods reporting on May 2024 telehealth developments](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/washington-healthcare-update-may-2024-2-1052626/), expanded telehealth coverage increases the volume of PHI transmitted over consumer-grade internet and mobile networks — environments outside the physical safeguards of clinical settings.

**Telehealth Enhancement for Mental Health Act of 2024 (H.R. 7858).** Passed 22-0. Extended mental health telehealth provisions. Mental health PHI receives heightened state-level protections in many jurisdictions beyond baseline HIPAA and may implicate 42 C.F.R. Part 2 for patients with co-occurring substance use disorder diagnoses.

Additional bills advanced by the subcommittee addressed Medicaid, public health workforce, and rare disease programs. A [subcommittee markup of 23 bills](https://democrats-energycommerce.house.gov/committee-activity/markups/markup-23-bills-subcommittee-health) was announced and conducted during this period.

**Outcome.** Both H.R. 7623 and H.R. 7858 were referred to the full E&C Committee and ultimately subsumed into broader year-end telehealth legislation. Final FY2025 telehealth extensions were enacted as part of the American Relief Act 2025 (P.L. 118-158), enacted December 21, 2024, extending most COVID-era Medicare telehealth flexibilities through March 31, 2025, with further extensions enacted in subsequent legislation.

### 4. Senate HELP Committee Activity (Week of May 20, 2024)

On May 23, 2024, the Senate HELP Committee was scheduled to mark up seven healthcare reauthorization bills, including:

- **S. 3679**, Dr. Lorna Breen Health Care Provider Protection Reauthorization Act (mental health support programs for healthcare providers)
- Alzheimer's disease research and support program reauthorizations
- Emergency Medical Services for Children (EMSC) Reauthorization Act
- Poison Control Centers Reauthorization Act

These bills collectively touch patient data through mental health provider assistance programs, pediatric emergency care data collection, and public health surveillance infrastructure. Provider mental health data generated through programs like the Lorna Breen Act may be processed outside traditional HIPAA covered entity frameworks if provided through employee assistance or peer support structures.

### 5. Obesity Drug Cost Analysis (Congressional Context)

The McGuireWoods update for this week also noted a significant policy analysis circulating in congressional discussions: if 50% of adults with obesity used GLP-1 weight loss drugs, total U.S. spending could reach $411 billion annually, with Medicare and Medicaid costs estimated at $166 billion per year. This figure was referenced in discussions around the Senate Finance Committee's budget outlook and the broader FY2025 appropriations context, where mandatory spending trajectory for prescription drugs was influencing discretionary tradeoffs. Formulary and coverage decisions for GLP-1 drugs involve the exchange of pharmacy claims data and prescription history — data streams regulated under HIPAA and, for Medicare Part D sponsors, under CMS data governance requirements.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

**Physician practices and group practices** participating in MIPS face continued uncertainty about whether current investment in quality data reporting infrastructure (CEHRT upgrades, registry submissions, improvement activities tracking) will carry forward under any reformed framework. Organizations should avoid capital commitments to MIPS-specific technology pending congressional action on the white paper proposals.

**Advanced APM participants** — including ACOs, episode-based payment model participants, and PCMHs — face potential expanded scope if Congress strengthens APM incentives. Additional APM participation obligations typically include new data use agreements, business associate agreements with APM entities and CMS, and expanded beneficiary claims data access.

**Telehealth providers and digital health platforms** should monitor the trajectory of H.R. 7623 (Telehealth Modernization Act). A permanent telehealth authorization would eliminate the cycle of short-term extensions and allow organizations to invest in long-term HIPAA-compliant infrastructure for home-based and audio-only encounters without the uncertainty created by expiring waivers.

**HHS program recipients** (NIH-funded researchers, HRSA grantees, CDC cooperative agreement holders) should monitor FY2025 appropriations for potential reductions to administrative and data infrastructure funding that supports their compliance programs.

**State Medicaid programs** remain affected by the continuing unwinding of COVID-era enrollment flexibilities (see prior report on CMS Medicaid Unwinding Flexibilities Extension). Appropriations cuts to the LHHS budget could constrain CMS's capacity to support state systems modernization under enhanced FMAP provisions.

## Action Items

- Physician practices and health systems participating in MIPS should track Senate Finance Committee's physician payment reform initiative into the 119th Congress; any legislation repealing or substantially modifying MIPS would require redesign of quality data reporting workflows and CEHRT configurations.
- Organizations participating in Advanced APMs should review their existing data use agreements and business associate agreements to ensure they can accommodate expanded data sharing obligations if APM incentive structures are strengthened in future legislation.
- Telehealth providers should maintain HIPAA Security Rule compliance posture consistent with permanent home-based telehealth — rather than relying on temporary flexibility assumptions — given the E&C Committee's bipartisan support for permanent authorization.
- HHS grantees and program recipients should monitor FY2025 LHHS appropriations developments and the continuing resolution process; any significant reductions to NIH, CDC, or HRSA administrative accounts could affect compliance infrastructure support and grant-funded data management obligations.
- Healthcare privacy officers should assess whether GLP-1 prescription data — now subject to increased congressional and payer scrutiny — is handled under appropriate HIPAA privacy rule protections and that any pharmacy data sharing with plan sponsors complies with the minimum necessary standard.
