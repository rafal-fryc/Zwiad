# New Federal Health Data Security Rules Overlay Existing HIPAA Obligations for Health Entities

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-27

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Covered health entities now face simultaneous compliance obligations from at least four distinct regulatory regimes — the existing HIPAA framework, a pending overhaul of the HIPAA Security Rule, the DOJ's Bulk Sensitive Data Rule under Executive Order 14117, and the newly enacted BIOSECURE Act — with state-level genomic and data localization laws adding further layers. Kelley Drye & Warren attorneys [Kate Black and Mason Fitch](https://iapp.org/news/a/new-obstacles-for-health-care-federal-and-state-national-security-regulations-increasingly-target-health-data) have characterized the challenge as no longer a question of whether these obligations apply, but "how many apply simultaneously and where internal resources should be allocated." The result is a fragmented compliance landscape in which data that is fully de-identified under HIPAA may still be regulated under the DOJ's national-security rules.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **DOJ Bulk Sensitive Data Rule (28 CFR Part 202)** — Final rule implementing [Executive Order 14117](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-31486/preventing-access-to-us-sensitive-personal-data-and-government-related-data-by-countries-of-concern) took effect April 8, 2025, with enforcement of key provisions beginning July 8 and October 6, 2025. The rule prohibits covered data transactions involving bulk personal health data (threshold: 10,000+ U.S. persons) with "countries of concern" (China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, Venezuela) or affiliated "covered persons." ([Federal Register, Jan. 8, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-31486/preventing-access-to-us-sensitive-personal-data-and-government-related-data-by-countries-of-concern))

- **DOJ Rule Extends Beyond HIPAA's Scope** — The DOJ rule's definition of "personal health data" is broader than HIPAA's "protected health information (PHI)." It covers any information describing or relating to an individual's past, present, or future health condition regardless of who collects it. Critically, the rule applies even if data is de-identified, pseudonymized, or encrypted in accordance with HIPAA — the national-security threshold is met by data volume and destination, not by health-entity status. ([Foley Hoag, June 2025](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/security-privacy-and-the-law/2025/june/doj-s-bulk-sensitive-data-rule%E2%80%9D-is-in-effect-and-may-require-significant-compliance-obligations-as/))

- **Human 'Omic Data — Lower Threshold** — The DOJ rule separately covers human genomic, epigenomic, proteomic, and transcriptomic data ("human 'omic data"). The bulk threshold for genomic data is just 100 U.S. persons; for epigenomic, proteomic, and transcriptomic data it is 1,000 U.S. persons — far lower than the 10,000-person threshold for general personal health data. ([DOJ Federal Register rule, Jan. 8, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/08/2024-31486/preventing-access-to-us-sensitive-personal-data-and-government-related-data-by-countries-of-concern))

- **HIPAA Security Rule NPRM (HHS/OCR, Jan. 6, 2025)** — HHS Office for Civil Rights published a [proposed rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/06/2024-30983/hipaa-security-rule-to-strengthen-the-cybersecurity-of-electronic-protected-health-information) that would be the first major update to the HIPAA Security Rule since 2013. Key mandates include mandatory MFA for all ePHI access, encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit, mandatory annual compliance audits, technology asset inventory updated at least annually, 72-hour system restoration requirement after a cyberattack, and elimination of the "required vs. addressable" distinction (all specifications become mandatory). The comment period closed March 7, 2025; finalization is listed on HHS OCR's regulatory agenda for May 2026 but remains uncertain under the current administration. ([HHS NPRM fact sheet](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/factsheet/index.html))

- **BIOSECURE Act (Signed Dec. 18, 2025)** — Enacted as part of the [FY 2026 National Defense Authorization Act](https://congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3469/text), the BIOSECURE Act prohibits federal agencies from procuring or contracting with entities that use biotechnology equipment or services from designated "biotechnology companies of concern." Grant and loan recipients face the same restrictions. The Act covers multiomic data including genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics. ([Ropes & Gray, Jan. 2026](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/biosecure-act-enacted); [K&L Gates, Jan. 2026](https://www.klgates.com/BIOSECURE-Act-What-You-Need-to-Know-1-20-2026))

- **BIOSECURE Act — Statutory Implementation Timeline** — The Act imposes a four-step implementation sequence that must not be compressed. First, OMB must publish an initial *list of biotechnology companies of concern (BCC list)* within one year of enactment (deadline: December 18, 2026). Second, OMB must issue *implementing guidance* within 180 days of BCC list publication (deadline: approximately June 2027 — the 180-day clock runs from list publication, not from enactment). Third, the FAR Council must complete FAR revisions within one year of OMB's implementing guidance (deadline: approximately June 2028). Fourth, procurement prohibitions take effect 60–90 days after those FAR revisions (effective approximately late 2028). ([Sidley Austin, Dec. 2025](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/12/biosecure-act-included-in-the-fy2026-national-defense-authorization-act); [K&L Gates, Jan. 2026](https://www.klgates.com/BIOSECURE-Act-What-You-Need-to-Know-1-20-2026); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/); [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/))

- **State-Level Genomic and Data Localization Laws** — Several states have enacted overlapping requirements:
  - *Florida*: Electronic Health Records Exchange Act (eff. July 1, 2023) requires offsite storage of patient information to be physically maintained in the continental U.S., its territories, or Canada. ([IAPP/Kelley Drye analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/new-obstacles-for-health-care-federal-and-state-national-security-regulations-increasingly-target-health-data))
  - *Texas*: HB 130 (eff. Sept. 1, 2025) prohibits genome sequencers or sequencing-related software produced by or on behalf of a foreign adversary; all Texas residents' genomic data must be stored within the U.S. ([IAPP/Kelley Drye analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/new-obstacles-for-health-care-federal-and-state-national-security-regulations-increasingly-target-health-data))
  - *Utah* and similar state laws impose data localization mandates and remote-access bans in various configurations. New bills advanced in West Virginia, Wisconsin, and Virginia in early 2026. ([IAPP/Kelley Drye analysis](https://iapp.org/news/a/new-obstacles-for-health-care-federal-and-state-national-security-regulations-increasingly-target-health-data))

- **Compliance Program Requirement (DOJ Rule)** — Companies conducting restricted (not prohibited) transactions must maintain a written data compliance program including due diligence, risk-based reviews, auditing, and recordkeeping for at least 10 years. ([Crowell & Moring, April 2025](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/ready-to-know-your-data-doj-issues-implementation-and-enforcement-guidance-for-data-security-program-protecting-bulk-sensitive-data))

- **Existing HIPAA Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) May Be Insufficient** — BAAs drafted for HIPAA compliance do not address the DOJ's national-security restrictions. Updated vendor contracts should prevent data recipients from transferring health data to prohibited recipients or countries of concern. ([Foley Hoag analysis](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/blogs/security-privacy-and-the-law/2025/june/doj-s-bulk-sensitive-data-rule%E2%80%9D-is-in-effect-and-may-require-significant-compliance-obligations-as/))

## Regulatory Layer Comparison [MEDIUM confidence]

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Covered entities + BAs | Covered entities + BAs | Any U.S. person/entity conducting covered transactions | Federal contractors/grantees using covered biotech |
| De-identified data exempt? | Yes | Yes | **No** | Partial (publicly available data exempt) |
| Data scope | PHI | ePHI | Personal health data (broader) + 'omic data | Multiomic data |

| Written compliance program required | Yes (policies) | Yes (expanded) | Yes (10-year recordkeeping) | Yes (supply chain audit) |

## Action Items

- **Immediately audit data flows**: Map all health and 'omic data transfers to identify whether any involve third parties with ties to countries of concern (China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, Venezuela). The DOJ rule applies to downstream transfers even if the covered entity does not directly transact with a foreign adversary.

- **Review and update BAAs**: Standard HIPAA BAAs do not cover the DOJ national-security prohibitions. Amend vendor agreements to require downstream transfer restrictions consistent with 28 CFR Part 202.

- **Assess genomic data holdings**: If your organization holds or transfers genomic data of 100+ U.S. persons, epigenomic/proteomic/transcriptomic data of 1,000+ persons, or personal health data of 10,000+ persons in research, clinical, or commercial contexts, review each relationship for country-of-concern exposure.

- **Inventory genome sequencing equipment and software**: The BIOSECURE Act and Texas HB 130 impose supply-chain restrictions. Trace each sequencing instrument and software vendor to confirm no corporate parentage ties to designated foreign-adversary entities. Monitor the OMB biotechnology companies of concern (BCC) list (due December 18, 2026) and OMB implementing guidance (due approximately June 2027 — 180 days after BCC list publication). FAR-based procurement prohibitions are not expected to take effect until late 2028 under the full statutory timeline.

- **Prepare for HIPAA Security Rule finalization**: The NPRM proposes 180 days from a final rule effective date for compliance. Begin gap assessments now — particularly for MFA across all ePHI access points, encryption at rest, annual audits, and technology asset inventory. Do not assume the current "addressable" flexibility will survive.

- **Monitor state genomic privacy legislation**: New bills in West Virginia, Wisconsin, Virginia, and other states are advancing. Data localization requirements vary from state to state and are not aligned with each other or with federal rules.

- **Establish a multi-regime compliance tracking function**: Because the DOJ rule, HIPAA Security Rule update, BIOSECURE Act, and state laws operate independently and have different thresholds, timelines, and exemptions, siloed HIPAA compliance programs are insufficient. A cross-functional team spanning legal, IT/security, procurement, and research operations is needed.
