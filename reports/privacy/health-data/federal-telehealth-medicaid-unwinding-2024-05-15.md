# Federal Healthcare Legislative Update: Telehealth Extension Markup, CMS Medicaid Unwinding Flexibilities, and Senate Activity (May 2024)

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2024-05-15

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

During the week of May 8–15, 2024, three significant federal healthcare policy developments unfolded with implications for health data privacy. First, the House Ways and Means Committee unanimously marked up and advanced the Preserving Telehealth, Hospital, and Ambulance Access Act (H.R. 8261), a bipartisan bill extending Medicare COVID-era telehealth flexibilities through 2026 and hospital-at-home programs through 2029. Second, CMS issued guidance extending state Medicaid unwinding flexibilities through June 30, 2025, preserving temporary verification policies that reduce procedural disenrollment. Third, the Senate HELP Committee advanced seven healthcare reauthorization bills. Each development carries health data privacy dimensions: expanded telehealth increases the volume of protected health information transmitted over digital networks; CMS unwinding flexibilities affect eligibility data flows; and Senate activity includes mental health workforce programs with patient data implications. Compliance officers at healthcare providers, health plans, and digital health vendors should monitor H.R. 8261's Senate path and assess whether existing HIPAA security controls cover expanded telehealth workflows.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence]

### Telehealth and HIPAA

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) governs protected health information (PHI) transmitted and stored in telehealth encounters. The HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) enforced a COVID-era enforcement discretion policy from March 2020 through May 2023, permitting use of non-HIPAA-compliant consumer video platforms. That discretion ended May 11, 2023, after which OCR returned to standard HIPAA enforcement for telehealth. [HHS/OCR](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/telehealth/index.html) and [Telehealth.HHS.gov](https://telehealth.hhs.gov/providers/telehealth-policy/hipaa-for-telehealth-technology) confirm that covered providers must now use HIPAA-compliant platforms, maintain business associate agreements (BAAs) with vendors, encrypt data in transit and at rest, implement access controls and audit logging, and conduct updated security risk analyses reflecting telehealth workflows.

### Medicaid Continuous Enrollment and Unwinding

The Families First Coronavirus Response Act (FFCRA) required states to maintain continuous Medicaid enrollment for enhanced federal matching funds. Following the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023, Congress ended continuous enrollment effective March 31, 2023, requiring states to conduct a 12-month "unwinding" period of eligibility renewals. CMS granted states waiver authority under Section 1902(e)(14)(A) of the Social Security Act to use simplified, temporary renewal procedures to prevent mass procedural disenrollment of eligible individuals. By May 2024, CMS had identified that many states remained out of compliance with federal renewal requirements and needed extended flexibility to address systems, policy, and operational gaps while protecting beneficiary coverage.

### Congressional Jurisdiction

The House Ways and Means Committee has jurisdiction over Medicare and Social Security. The Senate HELP (Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions) Committee has jurisdiction over public health programs and workforce issues. The Senate Finance Committee has jurisdiction over Medicare and Medicaid.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### H.R. 8261 — Preserving Telehealth, Hospital, and Ambulance Access Act

On May 8, 2024, the House Ways and Means Committee marked up [H.R. 8261](https://www.congress.gov/bill/118th-congress/house-bill/8261/text), introduced by Reps. David Schweikert (R-AZ) and Mike Thompson (D-CA), and passed it unanimously by a vote of 41-0. The bill amends Title XVIII of the Social Security Act (the Medicare statute) to:

- **Extend telehealth geographic and originating site flexibility** through December 31, 2026: eliminates the requirement that Medicare beneficiaries be located in a rural or health professional shortage area and that services be received at a clinical facility rather than the patient's home.
- **Extend audio-only telehealth** through 2026 for patients who cannot use video technology, including elderly and low-income beneficiaries.
- **Extend mental health telehealth** provisions: maintains the moratorium on in-person visit requirements within six months of the first telemental health service through 2026.
- **Extend hospital-at-home (Acute Hospital Care at Home) program** through 2029, allowing Medicare patients to receive acute hospital-level care in their homes.
- **Extend Medicare ambulance add-on payments** for urban, rural, and super-rural areas expiring in 2024.

Privacy and data implications of H.R. 8261: by extending the authorization for home-based telehealth and audio-only encounters, the bill increases the volume of PHI transmitted over consumer internet connections and mobile networks. Home environments lack the physical safeguards of clinical settings. The bill does not contain express privacy provisions, meaning HIPAA's existing Security Rule standards apply by default — including the requirement for covered entities to implement reasonable and appropriate technical safeguards for electronic PHI.

The [American Medical Association supported](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/bill-extend-telehealth-flexibilities-clears-house-committee) the bill's passage from committee, noting that without extension, Medicare telehealth flexibilities would expire after September 30, 2024. The [NCPA](https://www.ncpa.org/newsroom/qam/2024/05/09/house-committee-marks-telehealth-legislation) and [American Hospital Association](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2024-05-16-house-subcommittee-passes-legislation-telehealth-hospital-home-during-markup-session) also expressed support.

The Committee simultaneously passed five additional rural healthcare bills, including [H.R. 7931 (PEAKS Act)](https://waysandmeans.house.gov/2024/05/08/ways-and-means-protects-and-expands-telehealth-access-health-care-in-rural-and-underserved-communities/), H.R. 8245 (Rural Hospital Stabilization Act), H.R. 8244 (Ensuring Seniors' Access to Quality Care Act), H.R. 8235 (Rural Physician Workforce Preservation Act), and H.R. 8246 (Second Chances for Rural Hospitals Act).

### CMS Medicaid Unwinding Flexibilities Extension

On May 9, 2024, CMS issued an [Informational Bulletin](https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/cib050924-e14.pdf) extending all unwinding-related Section 1902(e)(14)(A) waivers through June 30, 2025. Key elements:

- States may continue using temporary verification policies reflected in their Unwinding Verification Plan Addendum, which were otherwise set to sunset at the end of the state's unwinding period.
- CMS also extended the "Unwinding Special Enrollment Period" (SEP) for the ACA marketplace from July 31, 2024, to November 30, 2024, allowing individuals losing Medicaid to enroll in marketplace coverage through the next open enrollment period.
- The extension was justified on the grounds that many states needed additional time to implement policy, systems, and operational fixes to address noncompliance with federal renewal requirements identified during the unwinding review process.

The [AHA reported](https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2024-05-10-cms-extends-unwinding-flexibilities-states-through-june-2025) and [Georgetown's Center for Children and Families confirmed](https://ccf.georgetown.edu/2024/05/10/cms-releases-guidance-on-timely-processing-of-applications-and-extension-of-unwinding-flexibilities/) that the extension is intended to protect beneficiary coverage while states fix renewal systems. Privacy implications center on the eligibility data flows between state Medicaid agencies, CMS, and marketplace entities, which are governed by HIPAA and Medicaid data regulations (42 C.F.R. Part 431, Subpart F).

### Senate HELP Committee Activity

On May 23, 2024, the Senate HELP Committee marked up and reported seven healthcare reauthorization bills. These included:

- **S. 3679**, Dr. Lorna Breen Health Care Provider Protection Reauthorization Act: Reauthorizes HHS mental health and substance use disorder support programs for healthcare providers through FY 2029. Provider mental health data is sensitive PHI under HIPAA.
- **S. 3765**, Emergency Medical Services for Children Reauthorization Act of 2024: Reauthorizes the EMSC program through FY 2029.
- **S. 4351**, Poison Control Centers Reauthorization Act: Reauthorizes poison control center programs through FY 2029.

On May 23, the Senate Finance Committee also held a hearing on opioid use disorder (OUD) prevention and treatment, examining school-based OUD services and Medicaid coverage for incarcerated individuals receiving OUD treatment. Patient-identifiable OUD treatment records receive heightened federal privacy protection under 42 C.F.R. Part 2, which is separate from and more restrictive than HIPAA.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

**Healthcare providers** using telehealth platforms must ensure their security risk analyses and BAAs cover expanded home-based and audio-only telehealth workflows consistent with [HIPAA Security Rule requirements](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html). The end of COVID-era OCR enforcement discretion means non-compliant telehealth platforms now carry real enforcement risk.

**Digital health vendors and telehealth platform operators** must maintain HIPAA BAAs with all downstream subcontractors processing PHI, implement end-to-end encryption, and log access to patient data. The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, updated effective July 29, 2024, also applies to consumer-facing digital health tools not covered by HIPAA, treating unauthorized data disclosures as "breaches" requiring notification.

**State Medicaid agencies** with extended unwinding flexibilities must ensure that temporary verification procedures applied through June 2025 comply with federal data security requirements under 42 C.F.R. Part 431, Subpart F, which governs Medicaid information safeguards.

**Health plans** administering Medicare Advantage telehealth benefits should plan for H.R. 8261's eventual enactment (or a successor vehicle) and update benefit designs, provider network agreements, and PHI handling procedures accordingly.

### Legislative Outlook

H.R. 8261 passed the Ways and Means Committee unanimously, reflecting strong bipartisan support for telehealth extension. However, the full House had not yet voted on the bill as of the source date (May 15, 2024). The Senate had its own telehealth extension proposals. Analysts at the time anticipated that some form of telehealth extension would be bundled into must-pass year-end legislation, consistent with prior precedent (the telehealth extensions in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 and the Continuing Appropriations Act, 2024 both followed this pattern).

## Action Items

- **Telehealth providers**: Conduct or update a HIPAA Security Risk Analysis that specifically maps telehealth data flows, including home-based encounters and audio-only services, to appropriate technical safeguards. Verify BAAs are in place with all telehealth platform vendors and subcontractors.
- **Health plans and Medicare Advantage sponsors**: Monitor H.R. 8261's Senate trajectory. If enacted, update benefit design documents and provider agreements to reflect extended telehealth coverage through 2026.
- **Digital health vendors**: Assess applicability of the updated FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (effective July 29, 2024) if the platform processes consumer health data outside HIPAA's scope.
- **State Medicaid agencies**: Review unwinding verification procedures for compliance with 42 C.F.R. Part 431, Subpart F data safeguard requirements through the extended June 30, 2025 flexibility period.
- **Legal and compliance teams**: Track the Senate Finance and HELP Committees' 42 C.F.R. Part 2 reform discussions; any changes to OUD treatment data sharing rules would require system and policy updates for behavioral health providers.
