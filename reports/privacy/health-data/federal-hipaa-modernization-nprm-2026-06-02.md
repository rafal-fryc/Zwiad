# HIPAA Modernization: HHS Proposes Privacy and Security Rule Updates

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Privacy / Health Data | **Date:** 2026-06-02

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) Office for Civil Rights (OCR) published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) on January 6, 2025, to overhaul the [HIPAA Security Rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/06/2024-30983/hipaa-security-rule-to-strengthen-the-cybersecurity-of-electronic-protected-health-information) — the most significant proposed update to the rule in more than two decades — mandating encryption of all electronic protected health information (ePHI), multi-factor authentication, annual penetration testing, and eliminating the longstanding "addressable" vs. "required" distinction. A parallel effort to strengthen reproductive health information privacy under the Privacy Rule was separately vacated by a federal district court in June 2025. As of June 2026, no final Security Rule has been issued; the May 2026 target date passed without a final rule, amid substantial industry opposition and approximately 4,745 unresolved public comments.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- The NPRM, formally titled "[HIPAA Security Rule To Strengthen the Cybersecurity of Electronic Protected Health Information](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/06/2024-30983/hipaa-security-rule-to-strengthen-the-cybersecurity-of-electronic-protected-health-information)" (docket no. 2024-30983), was published in the Federal Register on January 6, 2025, and the public comment period closed March 7, 2025, yielding approximately 4,745 comments according to [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-updates-hipaa-changes/).
- The proposed rule would eliminate the distinction between "required" and "addressable" implementation specifications, making all specifications mandatory with limited exceptions, as described in the [HHS OCR NPRM factsheet](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/factsheet/index.html).
- Mandatory new technical controls include: encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication for all systems accessing ePHI, network segmentation, vulnerability scanning at least every six months, and annual penetration testing, per the [HHS OCR NPRM factsheet](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/factsheet/index.html).
- If finalized, covered entities would have 180 days to comply after the final rule's effective date, with business associates receiving an additional 60 days to update their business associate agreements (240 days total), per analysis by [RubinBrown](https://www.rubinbrown.com/insights-events/insight-articles/hipaa-security-rule-changes-2025-2026-hipaa-updates/).
- OCR's Spring 2025 Unified Agenda targeted a final rule in May 2026, but that deadline passed without issuance; there is no confirmed new timeline as of June 2026, per [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/final-rule-implementing-hipaa-security-rule-updates-edges-closer/).
- A coalition of more than 100 hospital systems and provider associations — including the American Medical Association, American Academy of Pediatrics, Cleveland Clinic, and Yale New Haven Health — formally called for withdrawal of the proposed rule in a December 8, 2025 letter to HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., according to [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hospitals-provider-associations-withdrawl-hipaa-security-rule-update/).
- The College of Healthcare Information Management Executives (CHIME) and eight industry associations also sent a February 2025 letter to President Trump requesting rescission of the NPRM, citing cost and implementation burdens per [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/hipaa-updates-hipaa-changes/).

## Reproductive Health Privacy Rule: Separate Track, Vacated [HIGH confidence]

A distinct rulemaking — the [HIPAA Privacy Rule to Support Reproductive Health Care Privacy](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/hipaas-reproductive-health-rule-is-vacated-nationally) (finalized April 26, 2024 under the Biden administration) — was vacated in its substantive provisions by the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas on June 18, 2025. That rule had prohibited covered entities from disclosing protected health information for investigations or proceedings against individuals who sought, obtained, provided, or facilitated reproductive health care that was lawful in the circumstances in which it was provided.

The court found the rule exceeded HHS's statutory authority and unlawfully restricted state-mandated reporting obligations. The decision took immediate nationwide effect, according to [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/hipaas-reproductive-health-rule-is-vacated-nationally) and [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2025/07/texas-district-court-vacates-2024-hipaa-rule-designed-to-enhance-reproductive-healthcare-privacy-effective-nationwide/). The court preserved only the Notice of Privacy Practices amendments relating to substance use disorder regulations. Appeals of that decision were subsequently dropped, confirming the rule's effective end per [Health Law Diagnosis](https://www.healthlawdiagnosis.com/2025/09/appeals-dropped-of-decision-vacating-hipaa-reproductive-health-privacy-rule-confirming-apparent-end-of-the-rule-and-attestation-requirement/).

The K&L Gates podcast on "[HIPAA Modernization: HHS's Proposed Privacy and Security Rule Updates](https://www.klgates.com/HIPAA-Modernization-HHSs-Proposed-Privacy-and-Security-Rule-Updates-5-26-2026)" (published May 26, 2026) covered both the Security Rule NPRM and the current status of the Privacy Rule landscape, noting that stakeholders face a dual uncertainty: the overdue Security Rule final rule and the post-vacatur Privacy Rule environment.

## Context: Why This Rulemaking Matters [HIGH confidence]

OCR reported that large breach reports increased 102 percent between 2018 and 2023, while the number of individuals affected by large breaches increased 1,002 percent over the same period, primarily due to hacking and ransomware attacks, per the [HHS OCR NPRM factsheet](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/factsheet/index.html). The existing Security Rule was last substantially updated in 2013 and does not reflect current cybersecurity standards or threat environments. OCR's 2026 enforcement initiative has separately targeted risk management deficiencies (see the April 2026 [HHS OCR Risk Management Guidance Video](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html)).

## Regulatory Status and Uncertainty [MEDIUM confidence]

The NPRM remains in limbo. The Trump administration has signaled skepticism toward broad regulatory mandates, and the volume and character of the comment opposition — including from providers, hospital systems, and industry associations aligned with the current administration — make it uncertain whether the rule will be finalized as proposed, substantially revised, or withdrawn. A final rule in "slimmed-down form" was discussed in some analyses, but HHS has not confirmed any revised scope, per [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/final-rule-implementing-hipaa-security-rule-updates-edges-closer/). Organizations should monitor the HHS [Regulatory Initiatives page](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/index.html) for updated rulemaking status.

## Action Items

- **Begin gap assessment now.** Even under regulatory uncertainty, the proposed Security Rule provisions reflect widely accepted cybersecurity best practices. Conduct an internal gap assessment against the proposed requirements — encryption at rest/in transit, MFA, network segmentation, vulnerability scanning cadence, and annual penetration testing — to identify investments needed regardless of final rule timing.
- **Monitor the Federal Register.** Set alerts for docket no. 2024-30983 and the HHS [Regulatory Initiatives page](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/index.html). Once a final rule is published, covered entities have 180 days to comply; business associates have 240 days. Planning must begin before the effective date.
- **Revise business associate agreements.** If a final rule issues, BAAs will require specific amendments. Begin identifying BAA templates and counterparties that will require updates.
- **Adjust reproductive health PHI policies.** With the reproductive health Privacy Rule vacated, covered entities are no longer required to comply with the attestation and use/disclosure restrictions it imposed. Policies and workforce training should be updated to reflect the current legal landscape.
- **Engage counsel on enforcement posture.** OCR's 2026 enforcement initiative is actively targeting risk management and risk analysis noncompliance under the existing Security Rule, independent of whether the NPRM is finalized. Compliance with existing 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1) obligations is not deferred pending the final rule.
- **Watch for a revised NPRM or withdrawal notice.** Given the scale of opposition, HHS may issue a revised NPRM rather than a final rule. Either outcome would restart notice and comment periods.
