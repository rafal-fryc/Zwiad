# Civil Society Coalition Demands Meta Abandon Facial Recognition in Ray-Ban and Oakley Smart Glasses

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** privacy | **Date:** 2026-04-14

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

A coalition of 75+ civil liberties, civil rights, domestic-violence, reproductive-rights, and consumer-protection organizations — led by the ACLU, ACLU of Massachusetts, NYCLU, and the Electronic Frontier Foundation — delivered an open letter to Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg on April 14, 2026, demanding that Meta "immediately halt and publicly disavow" plans to deploy facial recognition technology (FRT) in its Ray-Ban Meta, Oakley Meta HSTN, and Ray-Ban Display AR smart glasses. The coalition letter follows February 2026 New York Times reporting on an internal Meta document (dated May 2025) describing a feature called "Name Tag" and stating Meta intended to launch "during a dynamic political environment" where civil society groups would be distracted — and it arrives after a bipartisan group of three U.S. Senators set an April 6, 2026 response deadline that Meta reportedly missed.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- On April 14, 2026, the [ACLU and 75 signatory organizations](https://www.aclu.org/press-releases/aclu-and-75-organizations-sound-alarm-on-metas-plans-to-add-facial-recognition-technology-to-ray-ban-and-oakley-eyeglasses) published an open letter to Mark Zuckerberg demanding Meta "immediately halt and publicly disavow" plans to embed FRT in its consumer smart glasses.
- Signatories span domestic-violence survivor groups, reproductive-equity advocates, LGBTQ+ organizations, worker-rights groups, and digital-rights organizations, including the [ACLU of Massachusetts, NYCLU, EFF, GLAAD, Mothers Against Media Addiction, Reproductive Equity Now, and the Women's Bar Association of Massachusetts](https://www.aclum.org/publications/coalition-letter-to-meta-regarding-frt-feature/).
- The coalition argues that "no product design safeguards" can adequately mitigate the risks that ambient, always-on FRT poses to stalking victims, domestic-abuse survivors, protesters, patients at reproductive or gender-affirming clinics, immigrants, and religious minorities, per the [ACLU press release](https://www.aclu.org/press-releases/aclu-and-75-organizations-sound-alarm-on-metas-plans-to-add-facial-recognition-technology-to-ray-ban-and-oakley-eyeglasses).
- The New York Times, in a [February 13, 2026 report summarized by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/meta-plans-to-add-facial-recognition-to-its-smart-glasses-report-claims/) and [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/13/meta-facial-recognition-smart-glasses/), quoted an internal Meta document stating: "We will launch during a dynamic political environment where many civil society groups that we would expect to attack us would have their resources focused on other concerns."
- The internal Meta feature is code-named **"Name Tag"** and would let a wearer silently identify strangers in public and retrieve information about them via Meta AI, according to [NYT reporting summarized by MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/13/meta-facial-recognition-smart-glasses/).
- Meta previously considered and abandoned FRT for the original Ray-Ban Stories in 2021 over technical and ethical concerns; the feature was revived amid closer Big Tech-administration ties, per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/meta-plans-to-add-facial-recognition-to-its-smart-glasses-report-claims/).
- On March 17, 2026, [Senators Ed Markey (D-Mass.), Ron Wyden (D-Ore.), and Jeff Merkley (D-Ore.)](https://www.markey.senate.gov/news/press-releases/markey-wyden-merkley-demand-transparency-from-meta-on-facial-recognition-technology-in-smart-glasses) sent a formal inquiry to Zuckerberg demanding answers on consent, biometric-data deletion, bias testing, and misuse prevention, with the [full text of the Senate letter](https://www.markey.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_meta_on_frt_in_smart_glasses1.pdf) publicly posted.
- The senators set an **April 6, 2026** response deadline; [State of Surveillance](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/meta-facial-recognition-april-6-deadline-senate-response-2026/) reported Meta had not publicly responded as the deadline approached.
- State biometric laws create patchwork exposure: Illinois' [BIPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_Information_Privacy_Act) provides a private right of action requiring written consent for face-geometry capture, and Texas CUBI produced Google's $1.375B 2024 settlement; Ring's "Familiar Faces" was blocked in Illinois, Texas, and Portland, OR in December 2025, per [State of Surveillance](https://stateofsurveillance.org/news/ring-facial-recognition-state-exemptions-biometric-laws-2026/).

## Why It Matters [MEDIUM confidence]

The coalition letter is not itself a legal instrument, but it creates three concrete pressure vectors that compliance, policy, and product teams should track:

1. **Regulatory escalation risk.** Combined Senate inquiry + coordinated civil-society campaign is the typical precursor to FTC Section 5 unfairness investigations (see Meta's 2019 $5B FTC order, which already covers FRT practices) and to state AG enforcement under BIPA, CUBI, Washington's My Health My Data Act, and the growing wave of comprehensive state privacy laws with biometric carve-outs.
2. **State-law collision.** Any consumer rollout of "Name Tag" would presumptively capture face geometry of non-consenting bystanders in public — a direct BIPA violation in Illinois with statutory damages of $1,000–$5,000 per scan, per [ACLU of Illinois](https://www.aclu-il.org/en/campaigns/biometric-information-privacy-act-bipa).
3. **Litigation catalyst.** Advocacy campaigns of this scale (75+ orgs, domestic-violence groups included) historically precede class-action filings once a product ships — the [2025 biometric-litigation year-in-review](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/12/2025-year-in-review-biometric-privacy-litigation/) documents continued plaintiff-bar appetite for wearable and ambient-capture products.

## Action Items

- **Privacy/compliance teams at Meta competitors and partners** shipping wearable or ambient-capture devices: reassess launch timing in Illinois, Texas, Portland (OR), and Quebec, and audit whether any on-device face-embedding pipeline constitutes "collection" under BIPA §15(b).
- **In-house counsel at enterprise customers** evaluating Ray-Ban Meta / Oakley Meta for field or retail use: freeze rollouts pending Meta's response to the Senate letter and any SEC/earnings disclosure on "Name Tag."
- **Policy and government-affairs teams**: monitor the Senate Commerce Committee and House Energy & Commerce for follow-up hearings; the March 17 letter is co-signed by a Commerce Committee member (Markey), making a hearing plausible in Q2/Q3 2026.
- **Product counsel**: treat the "dynamic political environment" quote as discoverable — any internal launch-timing analyses referencing regulatory or advocacy attention should be reviewed with litigation-hold counsel.
- **Monitor** for Meta's public response to the coalition letter, any FTC action under the existing 2019/2020 consent order, and follow-on state legislation modeled on Illinois BIPA targeting wearables specifically.
