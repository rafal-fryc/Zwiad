# ShinyHunters Breaches Instructure Canvas LMS, Disrupting 9,000 Schools and Exposing Up to 275 Million Records

**Jurisdiction:** Federal, Multi-State, International | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** 2026-05-08

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Ransomware and extortion group ShinyHunters has breached Instructure, the operator of Canvas LMS — the dominant learning management system used by U.S. higher education — exfiltrating approximately 3.65 TB of data from up to 275 million users across roughly 9,000 institutions in at least six countries. The attack began in late April 2026, escalated on May 7 with a defacement of Canvas login pages (quickly contained by Instructure with no additional data exfiltrated), and Instructure paid an undisclosed ransom on May 11, 2026, one day before the hackers' stated data-release deadline. The incident is one of the largest education sector data breaches on record, triggered FBI and CISA notification, prompted a formal House Homeland Security Committee investigation (also announced May 11), a Department of Education security alert, and a federal class action lawsuit. Affected institutions face concurrent obligations under FERPA, COPPA (compliance deadline April 22, 2026; rule effective June 23, 2025), and dozens of state student-privacy statutes.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- Instructure first detected unauthorized access on approximately April 29, 2026, per [Instructure's disclosure to affected universities](https://canvas.virginia.edu/instructure-cybersecurity-incident-may-2026) and corroborated by [Krebs on Security's investigation](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/05/canvas-breach-disrupts-schools-colleges-nationwide/), with ShinyHunters claiming exfiltration of 3.65 TB of data including names, email addresses, student ID numbers, and private messages from Canvas inboxes. Passwords, financial data, and government identifiers were not confirmed compromised.
- On May 7, 2026, ShinyHunters carried out a second intrusion, defacing Canvas login pages at multiple schools with messages directing institutions to contact the group directly — a tactic corroborated by multiple schools and reported by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/hackers-deface-school-login-pages-after-claiming-another-instructure-hack/). Per [Instructure's official incident update](https://www.instructure.com/incident_update), this second intrusion was detected and terminated approximately 10 minutes after it began due to monitoring implemented after the first attack; Instructure confirmed that no additional data was accessed or exfiltrated. Instructure took Canvas into maintenance mode as a precaution to verify full containment and apply additional safeguards.
- ShinyHunters' May 3 ransom note gave Instructure until May 6 to make contact, and set May 12 as the data-release deadline if demands remained unmet, per [CNN's coverage of the incident](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/07/us/canvas-hack-strands-college-students-finals-week) and [WRAL's reporting on the two-stage ransom structure](https://www.wral.com/news/education/canvas-shinyhunters-ransom-instructure-hack-data-breach-may-2026/). The May 12 deadline was extended from the original May 6 contact deadline after some institutions initiated negotiations.
- **On May 11, 2026, Instructure confirmed it had paid an undisclosed ransom and reached an agreement with ShinyHunters, who returned the stolen data with digital confirmation of data destruction**, per [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/tech-innovation/administrative-tech/2026/05/11/instructure-pays-ransom-canvas-hackers) and [The Record from Recorded Future News](https://therecord.media/instructure-pays-ransom-canvas-incident-congress-investigation). The ransom was paid one day before ShinyHunters' stated May 12 deadline. Cybersecurity experts warned against relying on confirmation of data destruction from extortion groups; ShinyHunters declined to disclose the payment amount to TechCrunch.
- Instructure confirmed it notified law enforcement, including the [FBI and CISA](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/05/millions-of-students-personal-data-stolen-in-major-education-cyberattack), and engaged outside forensics experts. Canvas was taken offline briefly and restored for most users by May 7–8; Canvas Beta and Canvas Test remained in maintenance as of May 7.
- Institutions named in publicly disclosed victim lists include Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Columbia, Princeton, Yale, Penn State, and UC Berkeley among higher education, as well as large K-12 districts including Clark County (Las Vegas), Broward County, Houston ISD, and Charlotte-Mecklenburg, per [Time Magazine](https://time.com/article/2026/05/08/canvas-cyber-attack-shinyhunters-hack-what-to-know/) and [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/top-universities-among-victims-named-in-canvas-data-breach-mit-oxford-and-more-all-hit/).
- The 2026 breach is the second ShinyHunters intrusion into Instructure systems within less than a year. In September 2025, a social-engineering attack compromised Instructure's Salesforce CRM environment. Instructure's official characterization was that the incident exposed only "publicly available business contact information" with no Canvas product data or student data accessed. Third-party security analysts [Rescana](https://www.rescana.com/post/instructure-canvas-cybersecurity-incidents-analysis-of-2025-salesforce-breach-and-2026-canvas-data-2-beta-security-event/) and [Sentra](https://www.sentra.io/blog/the-instructure-breach-was-salesforce-again-heres-the-governance-problem-nobody-is-talking-about) dispute this characterization and assess that the breach exposed student and teacher PII, private messages, and institutional records — not merely business contact information. These third-party assessments are contested by Instructure's official position. Rescana and Sentra further conclude that inadequate remediation after the 2025 breach left data accessible and facilitated ShinyHunters' re-entry in 2026; Instructure has not publicly addressed that remediation claim.
- ShinyHunters is a financially motivated extortion group active since 2019–2020, previously responsible for the 2024 Snowflake supply-chain campaign that exposed 560 million Ticketmaster records and 110 million AT&T customer records (AT&T paid a reported $370,000 ransom), per the [ShinyHunters Wikipedia profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ShinyHunters) and [Newsweek profile](https://www.newsweek.com/who-is-shinyhunters-hacker-group-claiming-canvas-vimeo-pornhub-attacks-11928189).
- In March 2026, ShinyHunters separately breached the European Commission, leaking 350 GB from 42 internal clients, demonstrating the group's continued high-tempo targeting of major institutions immediately before this attack.
- The attack vector was identified by Instructure as exploitation of an issue related to "Free-For-Teacher" accounts, per [university-level disclosures](https://safecomputing.umich.edu/security-alerts/instructure-canvas-cybersecurity-incident), though full forensic details have not been publicly released as of May 8.
- The breach affects institutions internationally, with disruptions or data exposure confirmed in the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, Sweden, and the Netherlands, per [DataBreaches.net](https://databreaches.net/2026/05/07/developing-shinyhunters-hacks-instructure-again-canvas-down/).

## Government and Congressional Response [HIGH confidence]

- **On May 12, 2026, the U.S. Department of Education's Federal Student Aid office issued a Technology Security Alert**, published at [FSA Partners](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/electronic-announcements/2026-05-12/technology-security-alert-ongoing-cybersecurity-incident-involving-canvas-learning-management-system), noting that senior Department of Education leaders were actively engaged with Instructure, and that ED's Student Privacy Policy Office had requested information from Instructure to ensure compliance with FERPA. The alert represents a formal federal agency demand for breach-related information and a signal of anticipated regulatory follow-on.
- **The House Homeland Security Committee formally announced an investigation on May 11, 2026**, per the [official Committee press release](https://homeland.house.gov/2026/05/11/chairman-garbarino-seeks-information-from-canvas-developer-after-cyberattacks-impact-schools-and-universities-nationwide/) and [Garbarino's letter to Instructure Holdings, Inc.](https://homeland.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026.05.11-Homeland-to-Instructure-Holdings-Inc.pdf), both dated May 11. Representative Andrew Garbarino sent a letter inviting Instructure CEO Steve Daly to submit a comprehensive security briefing by May 21, 2026, addressing the circumstances of the incident, the nature and volume of data accessed, and steps taken to contain the threat and notify affected individuals. The congressional letter was issued the same day as the ransom payment — May 11 — drawing attention to whether Instructure's decision to pay was influenced by imminent congressional scrutiny. Coverage in [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/05/12/congress-investigates-canvas-breach-after-instructure-cuts-deal-with-shinyhunters/5238927) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/13/us-lawmakers-demand-answers-from-instructure-after-canvas-data-breaches/) appeared the following day, May 12, but reported on the May 11 event.
- [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/us-congress-calls-instructure-ceo-as-it-investigates-canvas-breach) and [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/05/edtech-firm-instructure-pays-ransom-as-u-s-house-starts-investigation/) confirmed the House investigation as the first congressional inquiry directed at an education technology vendor arising from a student data breach at this scale.

## Litigation [MEDIUM confidence]

- **On May 13, 2026, a proposed class action lawsuit was filed against Instructure in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of California**, per [ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/data-breach-lawsuits/instructure-may-2026) and [ABC4 News](https://www.abc4.com/news/wasatch-front/lawsuits-instructure-data-breach/). The complaint was filed on behalf of a San Diego resident alleging injury from the unauthorized disclosure of personally identifiable information. Three separate lawsuits were filed against Instructure within days of the ransom payment, with plaintiffs' firms including [Chimicles Schwartz Kriner & Donaldson-Smith](https://chimicles.com/instructure-canvas-data-breach-universities-school-districts/) investigating additional class members from universities and K-12 districts. The litigation focuses on whether Instructure had adequate security controls following the 2025 Salesforce breach and whether Instructure provided timely notice to affected individuals.

## Regulatory Obligations Triggered [MEDIUM confidence]

### FERPA
The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (20 U.S.C. § 1232g) governs educational institutions' handling of student education records. FERPA notification obligations flow to the institution, not the vendor: schools, not Instructure, are the "educational agency or institution" under the statute. Schools must assess whether the compromised data (names, student IDs, email addresses, private messages) constitutes "education records" and, where a breach is confirmed, notify affected students and parents (for minors) within a reasonable timeframe. The Department of Education has not issued a specific timeline standard for breach notification, but institutions should act promptly given the public disclosure. The ED Student Privacy Policy Office's information request to Instructure (May 12) signals that the Department views this as a FERPA compliance matter.

### COPPA
The FTC's updated Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule — published in the [Federal Register on April 22, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule), effective June 23, 2025, with a mandatory compliance deadline of April 22, 2026 — tightens consent and data handling requirements for operators collecting information from children under 13. K-12 institutions using Canvas for elementary-age students face potential COPPA exposure depending on how Instructure is characterized as an operator or service provider. As of the breach date, the compliance deadline has passed, meaning all covered operators are now legally required to conform to the amended rule's enhanced requirements. The scale of K-12 districts affected (including Clark County, Broward County, and Houston ISD, collectively serving hundreds of thousands of elementary students) makes COPPA analysis mandatory for affected district counsel. For detailed guidance on the compliance obligations now in effect, see [Davis Polk's client update on the rule](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect).

### State Student Privacy Laws
Approximately 130 state statutes impose vendor-specific security and notification duties on ed-tech providers. Key statutes include:
- **New York Education Law § 2-d**: Requires third-party contractors to implement data security and notify institutions of breaches. Instructure's status as a "third-party contractor" triggers § 2-d obligations.
- **California SOPIPA** (Bus. & Prof. Code § 22584 et seq.): Prohibits covered operators from using student data for non-educational purposes and requires breach notification to schools.
- Similar vendor-facing obligations exist in Texas (Texas Education Code § 32.152), Colorado, and numerous other states with Canvas deployments.

### Breach Notification (State)
All 50 states have breach notification laws. Given the multi-state scale, Instructure will face concurrent state notification obligations. Most state statutes require notification within 30–90 days of discovery (April 29) to affected residents and state attorneys general. Notable states with shorter or recently amended timelines include New York (30 days from discovery, per the [December 2024 SHIELD Act amendment](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/organizations/data-breach-reporting/shield-act) requiring notice to both affected residents and four state agencies), Indiana, Pennsylvania, and Utah.

### CIRCIA (Pending)
The Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act's final rule remains delayed as of early May 2026 (see [CIRCIA delay report](reports/cybersecurity/incident-reporting/federal-circia-final-rule-delay-2026-04-07.md)). Instructure is not a designated critical infrastructure entity, so mandatory CIRCIA reporting does not apply at this time. However, if schools served are within critical infrastructure sectors (e.g., government facilities sector, which includes schools), sector-specific requirements may apply.

## Action Items

- **Institutions (immediate):** Confirm with Instructure in writing the scope of data exposed for your specific institution. Request a breach notification letter citing specific data categories, date of discovery, and date of containment. Document receipt. Note that Instructure's May 11 ransom payment and claimed data destruction do not eliminate breach notification obligations.
- **FERPA counsel review (within 5 days of receipt of this alert):** Determine whether affected data constitutes "education records" and identify all students (and, for minors, parents) requiring individual notification. Establish an internal notification timeline aligned with the April 29 discovery date.
- **COPPA assessment for K-12 (within 5 days):** District counsel serving elementary grades should assess whether any Canvas accounts belong to under-13 users, and evaluate obligations under the COPPA rule now fully in effect as of April 22, 2026.
- **State law notification matrix (within 7 days):** Map the institution's resident student and employee population to applicable state breach notification statutes. Calculate notification deadlines from April 29 (Instructure's identified discovery date). New York's 30-day SHIELD Act deadline makes that state's notification due by May 29, 2026.
- **Ransom payment and OFAC review:** The ransom has been paid by Instructure. If your institution separately made or considered payments, confirm through counsel whether any OFAC obligations attach. Monitor the [Treasury OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sdn-list) for any ShinyHunters members designated post-payment.
- **Congressional briefing preparedness:** If your institution received a letter or inquiry from Representative Garbarino's office or other House Homeland Security Committee members, coordinate with government affairs counsel. The May 21 briefing deadline applies to Instructure directly, but affected institutions may receive related inquiries.
- **Vendor assessment (within 14 days):** Review Instructure's data processing agreement and security addendum. Assess whether Instructure's repeated security failures — including the September 2025 Salesforce breach and questions about the adequacy of post-incident remediation raised by third-party analysts — constitute a material breach of contract or DPA terms. Evaluate whether contract termination or suspension rights are triggered.
- **Phishing alert to campus community (immediate):** The compromised data — names, institutional email addresses, student IDs, private messages — is ideal for highly targeted spear-phishing. Issue an advisory to all students, faculty, and staff warning of elevated phishing risk and advising on recognition and reporting. [Times Higher Education reports](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/personalised-phishing-attacks-likely-after-global-canvas-hack) that personalized phishing attacks are specifically anticipated by security researchers.
- **Monitor litigation:** Class action plaintiffs are actively seeking class members from institutions across all affected states. In-house and outside counsel should coordinate to document the institution's own notification and remediation steps to support any defense of claims that institutions failed to act on breach information.
