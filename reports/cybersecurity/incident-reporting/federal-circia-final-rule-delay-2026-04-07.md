# CIRCIA Final Cyber Incident Reporting Rule Delayed Again -- Now Expected May 2026 at Earliest

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** 2026-04-07

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

CISA's final rule implementing the Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act of 2022 (CIRCIA) has been delayed beyond the original October 2025 statutory deadline and is now expected no earlier than May 2026, with the March 2026 DHS appropriations lapse likely pushing the timeline further. The rule will require critical infrastructure operators to report covered cyber incidents to CISA within 72 hours and ransomware payments within 24 hours. CISA has indicated it will use the additional time to streamline the rule, reduce industry burden, and harmonize reporting requirements with other federal agencies -- but the cancellation of planned stakeholder town halls in March-April 2026 due to the government shutdown creates further uncertainty about when the final rule will be published.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act of 2022 (CIRCIA) was signed into law in March 2022 as part of the Consolidated Appropriations Act. The statute, codified at [6 U.S.C. 681-681g](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/6/681b), directed CISA to conduct a rulemaking to establish mandatory cyber incident and ransomware payment reporting requirements for critical infrastructure entities. Congress gave CISA 24 months to publish a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM), followed by 18 months to finalize the rule.

CISA published the [NPRM in the Federal Register on April 4, 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/04/2024-06526/cyber-incident-reporting-for-critical-infrastructure-act-circia-reporting-requirements), opening a public comment period that was [extended through July 3, 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/06/2024-09505/cyber-incident-reporting-for-critical-infrastructure-act-circia-reporting-requirements-extension-of) in response to public requests for additional time. The proposed rule established two core reporting obligations: covered entities must report covered cyber incidents within 72 hours of reasonably believing an incident has occurred, and must report ransom payments within 24 hours of payment. The NPRM defined broad categories of "covered entities" spanning all 16 critical infrastructure sectors identified under [Presidential Policy Directive 21](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cyber-threats-and-advisories/information-sharing/cyber-incident-reporting-critical-infrastructure-act-2022-circia).

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### Initial Delay to May 2026

Under the statutory timeline, CISA was required to issue a final rule by approximately October 2025. However, in September 2025, the Office of Management and Budget's Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) published an update moving the expected final rule date to [May 2026](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2025/09/cisa-delays-cyber-incident-reporting-rules-2026). A CISA official told [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/cisa-pushes-final-cyber-incident-reporting-rule-to-may-2026/) that the delay would give the agency time to consider streamlining the rule and reducing the compliance burden on industry, citing the significant volume of public comments received during the NPRM comment period. Many commenters emphasized the need to narrow the rule's scope, improve harmonization with other federal cyber incident reporting requirements, and ensure regulatory clarity.

### Town Hall Meetings and DHS Shutdown

In February 2026, CISA announced a series of sector-specific [town hall meetings](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/13/2026-02948/cyber-incident-reporting-for-critical-infrastructure-act-circia-rulemaking-town-hall-meetings) scheduled for March 9 through April 2, 2026, intended to give stakeholders additional input on refining the rule's scope and burden. The sessions were organized by critical infrastructure sector, covering chemical, water, energy, nuclear, healthcare, financial services, information technology, communications, and other sectors as reported by [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/cisa-announces-town-halls-seeking-input-on-circia-implementation).

However, a lapse in DHS appropriations in March 2026 forced CISA to cancel all scheduled town halls. According to [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/cybersecurity/2026/03/cisa-delays-cyber-incident-reporting-town-halls-due-to-shutdown/), CISA stated it would issue an updated notice with a revised town hall schedule once DHS operations resumed. [MeriTalk reported](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/cisa-says-dhs-shutdown-will-likely-further-delay-circia-rule/) that CISA acknowledged the appropriations lapse would likely further delay issuance of the final rule beyond the already-extended May 2026 target.

### Streamlining and Harmonization Efforts

CISA has signaled it intends to use the extended timeline to address key industry concerns, including reducing the number of entities subject to reporting obligations, narrowing the definition of "covered cyber incident," and harmonizing CIRCIA requirements with overlapping federal cyber reporting mandates from the SEC, banking regulators, and other agencies, as noted by [Industrial Cyber](https://industrialcyber.co/cisa/cisa-moves-to-finalize-circia-rules-by-2026-eyes-streamlined-cyber-reporting/). The proposed rule received criticism for its broad scope, with some estimates suggesting it would cover over 300,000 entities.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

Critical infrastructure operators across all 16 sectors should anticipate mandatory cyber incident reporting obligations, though the final rule's scope may be narrower than what was proposed in the April 2024 NPRM. The core requirements -- 72-hour incident reporting and 24-hour ransomware payment reporting -- remain unchanged from the statute and will be part of any final rule.

The repeated delays create both opportunity and uncertainty for affected entities. Organizations have additional time to develop internal reporting procedures and incident response capabilities, but the lack of a firm timeline makes it difficult to budget and plan for compliance. Organizations that are already subject to other federal cyber incident reporting requirements (e.g., SEC registrants, financial institutions, pipeline operators) should monitor CISA's harmonization efforts, as the final rule may align with or cross-reference those existing obligations.

Once the final rule is published, covered entities will have a defined compliance window (the NPRM proposed various effective dates ranging from 30 to 270 days depending on the provision). Noncompliance may result in CISA issuing subpoenas for information and referring matters to the Department of Justice, as authorized by [6 U.S.C. 681b](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/6/681b).

## Action Items

- **Monitor CISA's website** at [cisa.gov/circia](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cyber-threats-and-advisories/information-sharing/cyber-incident-reporting-critical-infrastructure-act-2022-circia) for rescheduled town hall dates and final rule publication
- **Assess whether your organization qualifies as a "covered entity"** under the proposed rule's definitions, particularly if operating in one of the 16 critical infrastructure sectors
- **Review existing incident response plans** to evaluate readiness for 72-hour incident reporting and 24-hour ransomware payment reporting timelines
- **Map overlapping federal reporting obligations** (SEC, banking regulators, TSA pipeline directives) to identify opportunities for streamlined compliance once the final rule clarifies harmonization provisions
- **Participate in rescheduled town halls** when announced to provide input that may shape the final rule's scope and burden
