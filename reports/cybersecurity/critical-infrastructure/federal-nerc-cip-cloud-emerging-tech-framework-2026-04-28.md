# NERC Explores CIP 100-Series Framework for Cloud and Emerging Technologies in Critical Infrastructure

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** 2026-04-28

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

The North American Electric Reliability Corporation (NERC) is actively exploring a fundamental restructuring of its Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) reliability standards to address the growing adoption of cloud computing and emerging technologies across the bulk power system. The proposed "100-series" CIP standards would operate in parallel with the existing framework, allowing bulk electric system operators to choose a more flexible, outcome-oriented compliance model better suited to cloud and third-party environments. This development arrives alongside two recently finalized FERC orders — Order No. 918 (CIP-003-11) and Order No. 919 (Virtualization Standards) — that have already begun modernizing the CIP regime, with mandatory compliance deadlines extending to 2028–2029. Energy sector operators should assess their current cloud and virtualization exposure now, as the regulatory trajectory is clearly toward expanded and restructured cybersecurity obligations for cloud-dependent critical infrastructure.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The NERC CIP Framework

NERC's CIP standards are a suite of mandatory reliability standards governing cybersecurity for the bulk power system (BPS) in North America. The standards are developed by NERC and must be approved by the [Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC)](https://www.ferc.gov/) before taking effect. Registered entities — including transmission operators, generation owners, distribution providers, and others — are required to comply with the applicable CIP standards based on the impact classification of their Bulk Electric System (BES) Cyber Systems: High, Medium, or Low Impact.

The CIP standards have historically been designed around hardware-based infrastructure — on-premises systems with defined electronic security perimeters and physical security controls. As cloud adoption, virtualization, and software-defined networking have accelerated across the energy sector, the standards have struggled to map cleanly onto third-party hosted environments, shared cloud tenancy models, and distributed architectures.

### NERC's January 2026 CIP Roadmap

In January 2026, NERC released its [Critical Infrastructure Protection Roadmap](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/reports/special-reports/nerc_cip_roadmap_01122026.pdf) — a strategic document produced by NERC's Security Integration team in collaboration with Regional Entities and industry subject matter experts. The Roadmap applied a structured, risk-based methodology to assess the most consequential security risks to bulk power system reliability and prioritized them through a comprehensive security risk registry and industry-wide survey.

The Roadmap's central finding is that the sector's operating environment has changed faster than the standards' scope and cadence of revisions. NERC identified expanding operational dependencies in low-impact systems, third-party operated infrastructure, and technology categories — including cloud platforms — that historically sat outside the most stringent CIP requirements. The Roadmap called for targeted, risk-driven evolution of the CIP standards to strengthen coverage where threats have outpaced current scope.

### Concurrent FERC Rulemaking Actions (March 2026)

In March 2026, FERC issued two final rules that represent the most significant near-term CIP modernization:

- **[Order No. 918](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/24/2026-05711/order-no-918-critical-infrastructure-protection-reliability-standard-cip-003-11-cyber)** approved CIP-003-11 (Cyber Security – Security Management Controls), adding new baseline cybersecurity controls for low-impact BES Cyber Systems: remote user authentication requirements, protection of authentication credentials in transit, and expanded detection of malicious communications. Effective May 26, 2026, with a 36-month implementation window (mandatory compliance by approximately May 2029).

- **[Order No. 919](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/24/2026-05716/order-no-919-virtualization-reliability-standards)** approved 11 revised CIP Reliability Standards, four new glossary definitions, and 18 modified definitions to NERC's Glossary of Terms to enable compliant virtualization. These standards formally address how existing CIP protections apply to virtual machines, cloud platforms, and software-defined networks used in EMS, SCADA, and other critical functions. Effective May 26, 2026, with a 24-month implementation window (mandatory compliance by April 1, 2028).

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### The Proposed CIP 100-Series: A Parallel Compliance Framework

NERC's exploration of the "100-series" standards represents the most forward-looking component of current CIP modernization activity. As described in [Morgan Lewis's April 2026 analysis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/rethinking-cip-compliance-for-cloud-and-emerging-technologies) of NERC's white paper on the subject, the concept would establish a parallel set of reliability standards — designated in the 100 series — that would operate alongside the existing numbered CIP standards rather than replacing them.

The key design principle is optionality: a registered entity could choose to comply with either the existing standard or the corresponding 100-series standard for a given asset, but not both simultaneously for the same asset. This "parallel track" architecture is intended to create a more objective-based, outcome-oriented compliance model that accommodates cloud-based architectures and third-party environments without forcing the round peg of cloud infrastructure into the square hole of hardware-focused perimeter requirements.

### Cloud-Specific Standard Under Consideration

Beyond the general 100-series framework, NERC has signaled intent to develop a dedicated cloud-specific CIP standard. According to [Morgan Lewis's reporting](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/rethinking-cip-compliance-for-cloud-and-emerging-technologies) on the NERC white paper, NERC stated that such a standard should address:

- **Data sovereignty considerations** across jurisdictions (relevant where cloud infrastructure spans national or state borders);
- **Allocation of responsibility** between cloud service providers and registered entities for compliance obligations;
- **Third-party environment-specific requirements** that differ structurally from on-premises compliance.

No draft of this cloud-specific standard currently exists. It remains at the exploratory/concept stage as of April 2026. Morgan Lewis hosted a webinar on the [NERC Proposed CIP 100-Series Reliability Standards White Paper](https://www.morganlewis.com/events/2026/04/nerc-proposed-cip-100-series-reliability-standards-white-paper-webinar) in April 2026.

### What Order No. 919 Already Accomplished (Virtualization Standards)

While the 100-series framework is still conceptual, Order No. 919 has already taken a concrete step by revising 11 existing CIP standards to accommodate virtualization. Per the [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/24/2026-05716/order-no-919-virtualization-reliability-standards), the revised standards clarify how protections apply when critical functions run on virtual machines, cloud platforms, and software-defined networks. The FERC NOPR that preceded Order No. 919 (Docket No. RM24-8-000) was issued in September 2025 based on NERC's July 2024 filing, demonstrating the standards development cycle can span 18–24+ months from filing to final rule.

### CIP-003-9 and the Low-Impact Supply Chain Gap

Before the 100-series framework becomes a live compliance obligation, energy sector operators have a more immediate compliance requirement: CIP-003-9, which became enforceable on April 1, 2026. This standard requires Low Impact BES Cyber System operators to implement documented cybersecurity management controls for supply chain and vendor remote access — for the first time subjecting low-impact assets to evidence-based vendor management requirements. CIP-003-11 (from Order No. 918) builds on this by adding authentication and detection controls. Both standards together reflect NERC's recognition that low-impact systems, often operated by smaller generators and distributed resources with significant third-party connectivity, represent a growing systemic risk vector.

### Scope and Scale

The CIP standards apply to a broad range of registered entities across the North American bulk power system. The [NERC CIP Roadmap](https://www.nerc.com/globalassets/our-work/reports/special-reports/nerc_cip_roadmap_01122026.pdf) notes that newly registered "Category 2" inverter-based resource (IBR) registrants — representing the growing renewable energy fleet — and third-party-operated pathways now account for an expanding share of operational dependency. As supply chain risk assessments have shown, adversaries can leverage shared vendor pathways to create high-impact events from aggregations of nominally low-impact assets.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

The 100-series framework and concurrent rulemaking affect all NERC-registered entities that operate or plan to operate cloud-hosted or virtualized BES Cyber Systems, including:

- **Transmission Operators and Owners** using cloud-based energy management systems (EMS) or SCADA platforms;
- **Generation Owners and Operators** (including renewable energy operators) with third-party remote monitoring and control;
- **Distribution Providers** subject to CIP requirements;
- **Cloud Service Providers** (CSPs) who host BES Cyber Systems or Bulk Electric System Cyber System Information (BCSI) — they face potential new direct obligations or contractual requirements under a cloud-specific standard;
- **Reliability Coordinators and Balancing Authorities** with distributed control architectures.

### Compliance Timeline

| Development | Status | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| CIP-003-9 (low-impact supply chain) | In effect | April 1, 2026 (already effective) |
| Order No. 918 / CIP-003-11 (low-impact authentication) | Final rule issued | ~May 2029 (36 months from May 26, 2026 effective date) |
| Order No. 919 / Virtualization Standards (11 CIP standards revised) | Final rule issued | April 1, 2028 (24 months from May 26, 2026 effective date) |

### Compliance Implications

The 100-series framework, if finalized, would create significant strategic choices for compliance programs. Entities currently investing in hardware-based compliance infrastructure to meet existing CIP standards will need to assess whether re-architecting toward an objective-based 100-series approach makes long-term economic sense. The parallel-track design reduces the cost of transition (no forced migration), but introduces the complexity of operating dual compliance frameworks.

Vendor contracting practices will need to evolve regardless of which framework entities choose. Order No. 919's allocation of responsibility between CSPs and registered entities, and the anticipated cloud-specific standard's focus on data sovereignty and shared responsibility models, will drive new contractual clauses in cloud service agreements.

### Enforcement Outlook

NERC and FERC have demonstrated consistent willingness to enforce CIP standards vigorously, including through substantial penalties for non-compliance. The [FERC/NERC joint white paper on CIP enforcement](https://ferc.gov/news-events/news/ferc-nerc-outline-cip-procedures-new-white-paper) has outlined how Notices of Penalty are processed. As CIP-003-9 is now in effect and Order No. 918/919 implementation periods begin, entities that lag on cloud and virtualization compliance readiness risk being caught underprepared during the 2027–2028 audit cycle.

## Action Items

- Assess current cloud and virtualization inventory: identify all BES Cyber Systems or BCSI hosted in cloud or virtual environments and determine how they map to the revised Order No. 919 virtualization standards (mandatory by April 1, 2028).
- Verify CIP-003-9 compliance: confirm that documented vendor electronic remote access security controls are implemented and evidence-ready for all Low Impact BES Cyber Systems, as enforcement began April 1, 2026.
- Begin CIP-003-11 readiness planning: the 36-month implementation window for Order No. 918 runs to approximately May 2029 — initiate gap analysis for remote user authentication and malicious communications detection requirements now.
- Monitor NERC standards development activity for the CIP 100-series and cloud-specific standard: subscribe to NERC's Standards Development Plan updates and track proceedings in FERC dockets related to CIP cloud.
- Review and update cloud service provider contracts: anticipate new allocation-of-responsibility requirements for data sovereignty and shared compliance obligations that a finalized cloud-specific CIP standard would impose.
- Engage in NERC stakeholder comment processes: the 100-series concept is pre-draft and industry comment will shape its final form; entities with significant cloud exposure should participate.
