# HHS OCR Settles Ransomware Investigation with Spencer Gifts Health Plan for $450,000

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity / HIPAA Enforcement | **Date:** June 18, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On June 18, 2026, the HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) announced a [$450,000 settlement](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-ransomware-investigation-health-plan.html) with Spencer Gifts LLC Flexible Benefits and Welfare Benefit Plans — the employer-sponsored group health plan of national retailer Spencer Gifts LLC — resolving potential HIPAA Privacy and Security Rule violations stemming from a November 2021 ransomware attack that exposed the protected health information (PHI) of 10,023 individuals. OCR found the health plan had failed to conduct an accurate security risk analysis and had not implemented required HIPAA policies and procedures before the breach occurred. This is OCR's 20th completed ransomware enforcement action and its 14th enforcement action under the Risk Analysis Initiative.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- On November 24, 2021, an unauthorized actor accessed Spencer Gifts' network, deployed ransomware encrypting company servers including those storing the Plan's PHI, and demanded a ransom, per the [HHS OCR press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-ransomware-investigation-health-plan.html).
- The breach notification was filed with OCR on January 24, 2022. The compromised PHI of 10,023 health plan members included names, addresses, zip codes, phone numbers, email addresses, and Social Security numbers, per [HHS.gov](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-ransomware-investigation-health-plan.html).
- OCR found two categories of potential HIPAA violations: (1) failure to conduct an accurate and thorough risk analysis under [45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-C/section-164.308); and (2) failure to implement reasonable and appropriate HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rule policies and procedures, per the [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-ransomware-investigation-health-plan.html).
- The resolution agreement requires the Plan to: (1) conduct a comprehensive enterprise-wide risk analysis; (2) review and revise HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rule policies and procedures; and (3) provide role-specific HIPAA training to all workforce members. The full [resolution agreement and corrective action plan](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/ocr-ra-cap-spencer.pdf) is available on HHS.gov.
- The Plan is subject to a two-year OCR compliance monitoring period under the corrective action plan, per [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/spencer-gifts-hipaa-settlement/).
- This settlement is OCR's 20th enforcement action for ransomware breaches and 14th enforcement action under OCR's Risk Analysis Initiative, making it the 7th HIPAA financial penalty announced in 2026. OCR has now collected $1,728,000 in total penalties from seven entities in 2026 alone, per [Haynes Boone's analysis](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/blogs/hipaa-enforcement-action-against-employer-sponsored-health-plan-ransomware-attack).
- This action targets an employer-sponsored group health plan — not a healthcare provider or traditional health plan insurer — a category that has been a growing OCR focus following the April 2026 Star Group Health Benefits Plan settlement (the first such action of this type), per [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/hhs-restructuring-and-new-enforcement-signal-increased-focus-on-privacy-security-and-health-plans/).

## Significance for Employer-Sponsored Health Plans [HIGH confidence]

OCR's two settlements in 2026 involving employer-sponsored group health plans — Star Group Health Benefits Plan in April ([$245,000](https://compliancehub.wiki/ocr-employer-health-plan-hipaa-ransomware-245k-settlement-2026/)) and now Spencer Gifts — represent a meaningful enforcement expansion. Many employers administering self-funded health plans have historically underinvested in HIPAA compliance relative to their healthcare provider peers.

Both plans shared the same violation profile: no documented risk analysis and inadequate HIPAA policies before a ransomware event. OCR's pattern of enforcement strongly suggests that employer-plan HIPAA compliance — particularly security risk analysis documentation — has become a standing enforcement priority.

The proposed amendments to the HIPAA Security Rule (NPRM published [January 6, 2025](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/hipaa-security-rule-nprm/factsheet/index.html)) would codify and expand risk analysis requirements with new specificity, including mandating asset inventories, ePHI data maps, and annual audits. Although no final rule has issued as of this report, OCR's enforcement posture already applies these standards operationally through corrective action plans, per [Sidley Austin's analysis](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/01/risk-analysis-in-the-crosshairs-four-recent-ransomware-resolutions-preview-the-hipaa-security-rule-amendments/).

## Action Items

- **Employer-sponsored group health plans:** Immediately assess whether a documented, comprehensive risk analysis exists under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A). OCR has now penalized two employer health plans in 2026 specifically for the absence of this analysis.
- **Plan sponsors:** Verify that plan-level HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rule policies and procedures are in place, current, and distributed to relevant workforce members. Do not rely on vendor or TPA compliance without documented verification.
- **HIPAA Security Officer:** Confirm all workforce members with access to plan ePHI have completed role-specific training. Generic HIPAA training is insufficient — OCR's corrective action plans specify training tailored to job duties.
- **Incident response planning:** Given that the Spencer Gifts breach was discovered in November 2021 but reported to OCR in January 2022, ensure breach identification and notification timelines comply with HIPAA's requirement to notify affected individuals without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery ([45 C.F.R. § 164.404(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-D/section-164.404)), and to contemporaneously notify HHS for breaches affecting 500 or more individuals ([45 C.F.R. § 164.408(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-D/section-164.408)).
- **Watch the Security Rule NPRM:** The pending HIPAA Security Rule final rule (proposed January 2025) would require more rigorous risk analysis documentation, ePHI asset mapping, and annual audits. Begin gap assessments against proposed requirements now.
