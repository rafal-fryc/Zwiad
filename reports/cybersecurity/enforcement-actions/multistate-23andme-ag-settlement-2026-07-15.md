# 42-State Coalition Secures $150 Million Bankruptcy Settlement Against 23andMe Over 2023 Genetic Data Breach

**Jurisdiction:** Multi-state | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** July 15, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

A coalition of 42 state attorneys general announced on July 14-15, 2026 a settlement of bankruptcy claims against 23andMe (now operating through the TTAM Research Institute/23andMe Research Institute) resolving allegations that the company's unreasonable data security practices enabled the 2023 credential-stuffing breach that exposed genetic and ancestry data of roughly 6.9 million consumers worldwide. The settlement establishes **$150 million in allowed claims** against the bankruptcy estate, but because 23andMe's estate has finite funds and numerous competing creditor claims, the states' actual immediate recovery is capped at **$18 million**, to be paid out promptly from available bankruptcy funds. Beyond the monetary recovery, the settlement imposes binding data-security and governance obligations on the successor entity, including mandatory risk assessments, an oversight advisory board, and continued consumer rights to delete genetic data and destroy biological samples. This is distinct from both the earlier California AG civil enforcement lawsuit against Chrome Holding Co. (filed May 27, 2026) and the separate $46.75 million consumer class-action bankruptcy distribution ([recommended by the plan administrator on June 11, 2026 and approved by the bankruptcy judge on July 7, 2026](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/07/08/338661.htm)); it represents the first coordinated multistate AG settlement arising from the breach. <!-- verified: independent search confirms June 11, 2026 administrator recommendation (Insurance Journal, WSAU) and July 7, 2026 judge approval (Insurance Journal, Claims Journal, Yahoo Finance/Reuters wire; one outlier source cites July 6) -->


## Key Facts [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- On July 14-15, 2026, a coalition of **42 state attorneys general** announced a settlement of bankruptcy claims against 23andMe over the 2023 data breach, per parallel official releases from [Delaware AG Kathy Jennings](https://news.delaware.gov/2026/07/14/ag-jennings-announces-multistate-settlement-of-bankruptcy-claims-against-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach/), [Texas AG Ken Paxton](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-150-million-settlement-against-23andme-over-data-breach-exposed), [Massachusetts AG Andrea Joy Campbell](https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-campbell-announces-multistate-settlement-with-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach), [Georgia AG Chris Carr](https://law.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-07-14/carr-announces-multistate-settlement-23andme-over-genetic-data-breach), and [New York AG Letitia James](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2026/attorney-general-james-secures-18-million-23andme-failing-protect-customers).
- The settlement establishes **$150 million in allowed claims** for the state coalition against the bankruptcy estate, but recovery is limited to **$18 million** due to the finite funds remaining in the estate and competing creditor claims, per [The Record (Recorded Future News)](https://therecord.media/genetic-testing-settlement-data-breach) and the Texas AG release.
- The $18 million will be **paid immediately** from available bankruptcy funds and distributed among the participating states; reported individual state shares include Texas ($1,266,860), Massachusetts ($387,218), Georgia ($452,232), New York ($705,000, per [Yahoo Finance/AP coverage](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/genetic-testing-firm-23andme-settles-140133191.html)), Alabama (more than $260,000, per [ABC 3340](https://abc3340.com/news/local/alabama-to-receive-more-than-260000-from-settlement-over-23andme-data-breach-attorney-general-steve-marshall-july-2026)), and Washington (over half a million dollars, per [KIRO 7](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/wa-receive-over-half-million-dollars-multistate-settlement-over-23andme-genetic-data-breach/HKVU7XE3ORBBFEPBU6PJQO2PB4/?outputType=amp)).
- The multistate investigation found 23andMe engaged in **unreasonable data security practices**, including: failing to employ safeguards against credential-stuffing attacks (such as blocklisting known-breached passwords or requiring multi-factor authentication); failing to implement appropriate rate limiting or intrusion prevention; failing to implement logging and monitoring tools likely to detect a breach; and failing to investigate or address unusual login patterns, including a documented spike in login attempts, per the Georgia AG release and the Massachusetts AG release.
- The breach, disclosed by 23andMe in **October 2023**, compromised data belonging to **6.9 million consumers worldwide**, in some cases including genetic ancestry information, subsets of which were subsequently offered for sale on the dark web, per the Delaware AG release and Massachusetts AG release.
- As non-monetary relief, the settlement requires the successor entity — **23andMe Research Institute / TTAM Research Institute** — to conduct ongoing risk assessments, establish a **special advisory board** to oversee data security, agree to be bound by comprehensive privacy-law obligations without carve-outs, and continue to honor consumers' rights to **delete personal data and destroy genetic samples indefinitely**, per [SC Media](https://www.scworld.com/brief/23andme-agrees-to-18-million-settlement-over-data-breach) and the Massachusetts AG release.
- This multistate settlement is **separate from** two prior 23andMe-related actions already tracked in this knowledge base: (1) the California AG's civil enforcement lawsuit (*People v. Chrome Holding Co., fka 23andMe*), filed May 27, 2026 in San Francisco Superior Court under GIPA, CCPA, and the Reasonable Data Security Law (venue confirmed against the cross-referenced [California AG report](../../privacy/enforcement-actions/california-23andme-chrome-holding-breach-2026-05-29.md) and its cited [official complaint](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/People%20v%20Chrome%20Holding%20fka%2023andMe%20et%20al.%20-%20Stamped%20Complaint.pdf)), which remains pending independently; and (2) the $46.75 million private class-action bankruptcy distribution to individual breach victims: the bankruptcy plan administrator recommended the distribution on June 11, 2026, per [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/06/12/873539.htm), and U.S. Bankruptcy Judge Brian Walsh (E.D. Mo.) granted final approval on July 7, 2026, per [Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/07/08/338661.htm) (see the linked related report, federal-23andme-data-breach-settlement-2026-06-12.md, for full details of that separate proceeding). <!-- verified: dates and roles independently confirmed against Insurance Journal (06-12), Claims Journal (07-08), and cross-checked against the related California AG report, which confirms the May 27, 2026 filing date and San Francisco Superior Court venue -->

- 23andMe filed for **Chapter 11 bankruptcy in March 2025**; its assets, including its consumer genetic database, were acquired by the **TTAM Research Institute**, a nonprofit founded by 23andMe co-founder Anne Wojcicki, in a sale completed July 2025.

## Regulatory and Legal Context [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

The settlement was reached through the bankruptcy claims process in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Eastern District of Missouri, where 23andMe's Chapter 11 case is pending, rather than through a standalone civil complaint. Attempts to locate the underlying multistate settlement agreement, consent order, or bankruptcy court filing on PACER or the bankruptcy docket were not completed as part of this research; the report relies on the coordinated official press releases issued by 42 individual state attorneys general offices, which corroborate the same core facts (coalition size, $150 million allowed claims, $18 million immediate recovery, and the specific security failures identified). Because no single official settlement document or court filing has been directly retrieved and reviewed, and because the underlying agreement text was not located, this section — and the overall legal-mechanics analysis — is tagged MEDIUM confidence notwithstanding the multiple corroborating official state sources on the headline facts. Readers requiring the settlement's precise contractual terms should consult the bankruptcy docket in *In re 23andMe Holding Co.*, Case No. 25-40976 (Bankr. E.D. Mo.), or the individual state AG settlement/consent documents linked from the press releases above.

The security failures identified — absence of MFA, no credential-stuffing defenses, inadequate anomaly detection — mirror the allegations in California's parallel GIPA/CCPA lawsuit, reinforcing a consistent multistate AG theory that DTC genetic testing companies must meet a heightened security standard given the sensitivity and immutability of genetic data, consistent with the analysis in the earlier California AG report in this knowledge base.

## Action Items

- **Track the bankruptcy docket** in *In re 23andMe Holding Co.* (E.D. Mo.) for the filed settlement agreement or consent order to confirm the precise legal terms, effective date, and any conditions precedent to the advisory board and risk-assessment obligations becoming binding.
- **DTC genetic and biometric data companies** should treat the specific security failures cited (no MFA, no credential-stuffing defenses, no anomaly detection for login spikes) as an express multistate AG enforcement checklist; remediate any gaps proactively.
- **Monitor the 23andMe Research Institute/TTAM's compliance** with the advisory board and risk-assessment requirements, since this creates a template for ongoing consent-decree-style oversight of successor entities in bankruptcy-driven data asset transfers.
- **Compliance and privacy counsel** advising clients in bankruptcy-adjacent M&A involving consumer data assets should note that state AGs are willing to pursue claims against the bankruptcy estate itself (not just successor entities post-sale, as in the California action), creating an additional recovery avenue independent of civil litigation.
- **Track parallel proceedings**: the California AG's *People v. Chrome Holding Co.* lawsuit remains pending and is not resolved by this multistate settlement; monitor for potential overlap or preclusion issues as that case proceeds.
