# OCR Settles Four HIPAA Ransomware Investigations Totaling $1.165 Million

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity / HIPAA Enforcement | **Date:** April 24, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 23, 2026, the HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR) announced simultaneous resolution agreements with four HIPAA-regulated entities — Assured Imaging, Consociate Health, Regional Women's Health Group, and Star Group Health Benefits Plan — following ransomware breach investigations. The four entities collectively paid $1,165,000 and agreed to two-year corrective action plans monitored by OCR. All four cases center on a single recurring violation: failure to conduct an accurate and thorough risk analysis under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) of the HIPAA Security Rule, a provision OCR has now weaponized in 13 completed Risk Analysis Initiative investigations and 19 completed ransomware-triggered investigations.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- OCR's [April 23, 2026 press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html) confirms total penalties of $1,165,000 across the four entities, affecting over 427,000 individuals whose ePHI was exposed in separate ransomware incidents.
- **Assured Imaging** (medical imaging, AZ/CA) paid [$375,000](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/ra-cap-with-assured-imaging/index.html) after OCR determined it had impermissibly disclosed PHI, failed to conduct a risk analysis, and failed to timely notify individuals following a May 2020 ransomware attack affecting 244,813 individuals.
- **Regional Women's Health Group** (d/b/a Axia Women's Health, NJ/PA/OH/IN/KY) paid [$320,000](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/ra-cap-with-rwhg/index.html) for failure to conduct an adequate risk analysis following a December 2020 ransomware attack affecting 37,989 individuals.
- **Consociate Health** (business associate, health plan administration) paid $225,000 after a November–December 2021 ransomware attack exposed ePHI of approximately 136,539 individuals; OCR found failure to conduct an accurate and thorough risk analysis under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A), per the [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html).
- **Star Group Health Benefits Plan** (self-funded employee benefits plan of a CT-based energy provider) paid [$245,000](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/agreements/ra-cap-with-sg-health-plan/index.html) following an October 2021 ransomware attack affecting 9,316 individuals — notably what multiple law firm analyses describe as one of the first, if not the first, OCR enforcement actions directly targeting a self-funded employer-sponsored group health plan, per [Benefits Law Advisor](https://www.benefitslawadvisor.com/2026/04/articles/hipaa/ocr-announces-hipaa-enforcement-action-against-self-funded-group-health-plan/).
- Each resolution agreement requires the entity to: (1) conduct a thorough enterprise-wide risk analysis; (2) develop and implement a risk management plan; (3) revise and maintain security policies and procedures; and (4) provide workforce security training, per the [HIPAA Journal](https://www.hipaajournal.com/ocr-fines-four-regulated-entities-hipaa-violations-ansomware-attacks/).
- All four entities are subject to two-year OCR compliance monitoring.
- These settlements mark OCR's 19th completed ransomware investigation and 13th completed Risk Analysis Initiative investigation, per [HHS's press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/ocr-settles-four-ransomware-investigations.html). OCR has reported a 264% increase in large breaches involving ransomware since 2018.
- The applicable statutory provision, 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A) (Security Management Process — Risk Analysis), is available on [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-C/section-164.308).

## Action Items

- **Covered entities and business associates:** Conduct or update a comprehensive, documented enterprise-wide risk analysis that identifies all systems containing ePHI, assesses threat-vulnerability pairs, and assigns likelihood and impact ratings. Failure to maintain a current risk analysis has appeared in every major OCR enforcement action in the Risk Analysis Initiative.
- **Employer-sponsored group health plan sponsors:** The Star Group settlement signals OCR willingness to pursue enforcement directly against self-funded ERISA plans. Plan sponsors should verify that the plan (not merely the third-party administrator) has an up-to-date risk analysis and security policies on file.
- **Business associates:** Consociate Health's settlement confirms that BAs face the same risk analysis obligations as covered entities under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A). BAs should not rely on covered entity risk analyses to satisfy their own HIPAA obligations.
- **Incident response:** Assured Imaging was cited separately for failure to timely notify individuals of the breach. Covered entities should confirm breach notification procedures satisfy the 60-day notification window under 45 C.F.R. § 164.404.
- **Corrective action plan preparation:** Organizations that have experienced ransomware incidents should proactively conduct risk analyses and document remediation steps before OCR initiates an investigation — OCR's resolution agreements consistently require the same corrective steps that reasonable pre-incident compliance would have provided.
