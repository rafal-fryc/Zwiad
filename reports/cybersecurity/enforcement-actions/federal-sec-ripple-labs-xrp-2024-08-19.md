# SEC v. Ripple Labs: $125 Million Civil Penalty and Permanent Injunction After Four-Year XRP Securities Battle

**Jurisdiction:** Federal (S.D.N.Y.) | **Category:** Financial Regulation / Securities Enforcement | **Date:** August 7, 2024

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On August 7, 2024, U.S. District Judge Analisa Torres of the Southern District of New York issued a [final judgment](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2020cv10832/551082/973/) in *Securities and Exchange Commission v. Ripple Labs Inc. et al.*, No. 1:2020cv10832, imposing a civil monetary penalty of $125,035,150 on Ripple Labs and permanently enjoining the company from future violations of Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933. The judgment concludes the remedies phase of a four-year enforcement action originating from the SEC's December 2020 complaint alleging unregistered XRP token sales. Judge Torres rejected the SEC's request for disgorgement — which had sought approximately $876 million — finding no evidence of investor harm from Ripple's institutional sales. The ruling is a landmark precedent for digital asset securities enforcement, establishing a transaction-by-transaction analytical framework under the [*Howey* test](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/sec-v-ripple-labs-securities-law-analysis-under-howey-applied-on-a-transaction-by-transaction-basis) that distinguishes institutional sales from open-market retail trades.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The SEC's Original Complaint (December 2020)

The SEC filed its complaint against Ripple Labs, Inc., CEO Bradley Garlinghouse, and co-founder Christian A. Larsen in the [S.D.N.Y. on December 22, 2020](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26306). The complaint alleged that Ripple had been conducting an unregistered offering of securities — denominated XRP — dating back to 2013. The SEC alleged Ripple raised more than $1.3 billion from XRP sales without registering the transactions with the Commission as required under Sections 5(a) and 5(c) of the Securities Act of 1933. Garlinghouse and Larsen were alleged to have aided and abetted these violations through their own personal XRP sales totaling approximately $600 million.

A central element of the complaint was that Ripple had received two legal memoranda in 2012 from outside counsel warning that XRP bore some risk of being classified as an investment contract. The SEC used these memos to argue that Ripple proceeded with XRP sales despite awareness of the potential regulatory exposure.

### The *Howey* Test and Digital Assets

The SEC's theory rested on the [*SEC v. W.J. Howey Co.*, 328 U.S. 293 (1946)](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2023/07/sec-v-ripple-when-a-security-is-not-a-security) framework, under which an investment contract exists when there is (1) an investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with an expectation of profits, (4) derived from the efforts of others. The case required the court to determine whether XRP token sales fit this framework — and whether the answer varied depending on the type of transaction.

This was one of the first major federal court confrontations with the question of whether a native blockchain token constitutes a security, making the proceeding closely watched across the digital assets industry, compliance community, and financial regulators.

### July 2023 Summary Judgment: Partial Ripple Victory

On July 13, 2023, Judge Torres issued a [landmark partial summary judgment ruling](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2023/07/ripple-labs), finding for each party in part:

- **Institutional Sales — Unregistered Securities:** Ripple's direct sales of XRP to sophisticated institutional investors (raising $728 million) constituted unregistered offers and sales of investment contracts. Institutional buyers had a reasonable expectation of profit derived from Ripple's managerial efforts, and Ripple's communications and marketing campaigns reinforced that expectation.
- **Programmatic Sales — Not Securities:** Ripple's sales on digital asset exchanges to retail buyers did not constitute unregistered securities offerings. Because exchange transactions occurred through anonymous matching on secondary markets, retail buyers could not reasonably know whether they were purchasing from Ripple or another seller. As a result, the "expectation of profit from the efforts of others" prong of *Howey* was not satisfied.
- **Other Distributions — Not Securities:** Token distributions to employees and ecosystem partners also did not meet the *Howey* standard.

The July 2023 ruling was widely analyzed by law firms including [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2023/07/ripple-labs), [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/230717-ripple-ruling-adds-to-digital-asset), and [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2023/07/defining-digital-asset-securities-us-district-court-issues-summary-judgment-rulings-in-sec-v-ripple-labs) as a potential turning point in digital asset regulation, with some commentators noting the partial nature of the win left significant issues unresolved.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The August 2024 Remedies Order

Following the July 2023 summary judgment, the parties proceeded to remedies briefing. On August 7, 2024, Judge Torres issued the [final judgment](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2020cv10832/551082/973/) addressing three potential remedies: disgorgement, prejudgment interest, and a civil monetary penalty.

**Disgorgement and Prejudgment Interest — Denied**

The court denied the SEC's request for disgorgement of Ripple's institutional sales proceeds — which the SEC had quantified at approximately $876 million. Judge Torres found that the SEC failed to demonstrate that institutional investors who purchased XRP from Ripple suffered any monetary harm. Disgorgement in SEC enforcement actions is available only where there are victims to be made whole; without a showing of investor loss, the equitable remedy was unavailable. This was a significant limitation on the SEC's theory.

**Civil Monetary Penalty — $125,035,150**

The court imposed a [civil monetary penalty of $125,035,150](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2024/08/07/judge-fines-ripple-125m-bans-future-securities-law-violations-in-long-running-sec-case). Judge Torres used a transaction-by-transaction methodology — assessing each of Ripple's 1,278 institutional sale transactions — rather than applying a single aggregate multiplier. The penalty was calibrated to reflect several factors:

- The violations were "recurrent" over a period of years;
- Ripple had prior legal advice that XRP could be classified as a security;
- However, the SEC brought no fraud or misappropriation allegations;
- Ripple's institutional buyers were sophisticated parties who were not shown to have been harmed.

The resulting $125 million figure was significantly below the SEC's requested $876 million but well above the $10 million Ripple had proposed. The [Manatt client alert](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/ripple-labs-ordered-to-pay-$125-million-civil-fine) described the penalty as reflecting "the recurrent nature of the violations against the absence of fraud and investor harm."

**Permanent Injunction**

The court also issued a permanent injunction barring Ripple from future violations of Section 5 of the Securities Act. This injunction does not prohibit secondary market trading in XRP by third parties; it prohibits Ripple from conducting unregistered securities offerings. The injunction was notable for what it did not say: it did not constitute a finding that all XRP sales by all parties are securities transactions.

### Post-Judgment Developments

In October 2024, the SEC [filed a notice of appeal](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2024/10/02/sec-appeals-125-million-judgment-in-ripple-labs-xrp-lawsuit/) to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, challenging aspects of the August 2024 judgment and the July 2023 summary judgment.

In early 2025, under new leadership following President Trump's reelection, the SEC and Ripple began settlement negotiations. A proposed settlement would have reduced the civil penalty to $50 million and lifted the injunction, but District Judge Torres [declined to approve the proposed settlement modification](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/ripple-locked-in-standoff-with-judge-blocking-sec-crypto-accord), holding that it would improperly modify a final judgment and that the SEC's changed litigation posture did not warrant vacating the injunction.

Ultimately, the parties filed a joint stipulation dismissing both the SEC's appeal and Ripple's cross-appeal. The [Second Circuit approved the dismissal](https://cryptobriefing.com/ripple-sec-settlement-xrp-enforcement/), leaving the $125 million final judgment and the permanent injunction in place. The [SEC issued a statement](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26369) confirming the enforcement action was resolved.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### On the Crypto Industry

The *Ripple* judgment established foundational precedent for digital asset securities analysis in U.S. federal courts. The transaction-by-transaction *Howey* analysis — distinguishing institutional sales (unregistered securities) from open-market retail trades (not securities) — has been applied in subsequent digital asset enforcement matters and cited extensively by legal practitioners navigating the SEC's enforcement posture. [Fenwick & West's analysis](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/sec-v-ripple-decision-makes-waves-in-digital-assets-enforcement) noted the case created a framework courts were likely to consult when assessing remedies in digital asset enforcement cases, alongside *SEC v. LBRY, Inc.*

Following the litigation's resolution, multiple U.S. asset managers applied for and received approval for [spot XRP exchange-traded funds (ETFs)](https://www.ccn.com/ripple-vs-sec-lawsuit-decision-xrp-court-ruling-timing-details-in-full/), marking a significant milestone for institutional XRP adoption. The broader regulatory environment for digital assets also shifted under the Trump administration's 2025 SEC leadership, with new guidance and simplified approval pathways for blockchain projects.

### On SEC Enforcement Strategy

The court's refusal to award disgorgement absent proof of investor harm may constrain the SEC's remedies toolkit in future digital asset enforcement cases where no specific investor losses are identifiable. The case underscores that civil penalties remain available for recurrent unregistered offering violations, but equitable disgorgement requires demonstrated harm. Compliance professionals and in-house counsel at digital asset companies should note that the existence of prior legal advice flagging potential securities law risk — as Ripple had in its 2012 memos — can aggravate penalty calculations even in the absence of fraud.

### Compliance Implications for Token Issuers

The case's framework creates a clear distinction that digital asset companies must navigate:

- **Direct institutional sales** of tokens, accompanied by promotional material suggesting profit from the issuer's efforts, carry high risk of characterization as unregistered securities offerings under Section 5.
- **Anonymous secondary market sales** through exchanges are less likely to satisfy the *Howey* expectation-of-profits element when buyers cannot identify the seller.
- The nature of the offering — not the token itself — determines whether securities laws apply.

## Action Items

- Token issuers and digital asset companies conducting direct institutional placements should obtain current securities law counsel to evaluate whether offerings constitute investment contracts under *Howey*.
- Compliance teams should document and evaluate prior legal advice concerning token classification, as such advice cuts in both directions and may be presented by the SEC to establish knowledge of potential violations.
- Monitor the SEC's evolving digital asset regulatory guidance under post-2025 leadership for any further shifts in enforcement priorities that may affect how the *Ripple* framework applies to future token offerings.
- Counsel advising on digital asset fund launches should note that the *Ripple* outcome — and subsequent XRP ETF approvals — may inform how the SEC approaches novel fund structures referencing non-security tokens.
- Companies holding XRP or relying on secondary XRP markets should confirm that the injunction in *Ripple* applies only to Ripple Labs itself and does not restrict third-party trading.
