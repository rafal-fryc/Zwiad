# Texas AG Paxton Opens CID Investigation Into Carnival Cruise Line Over ShinyHunters Data Breach Affecting 800,000+ Texans

**Jurisdiction:** Texas | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** 2026-06-29

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has announced an ongoing investigation into Carnival Corporation & plc, the parent company of Carnival Cruise Line, following an April 2026 data breach that Carnival's own notification to the Texas Office of the Attorney General (OAG) reported as affecting 800,060 Texas consumers, out of an estimated 6 million (and, per the extortion group's own claims, up to 8.7 million) affected individuals company-wide [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach). <!-- verified: independently corroborated via WebSearch (texasattorneygeneral.gov press release headline, breach notice filings reporting ~5,995,277/~6M affected, ShinyHunters' own claim of 8.7M records) --> Paxton has issued a Civil Investigative Demand (CID) to Carnival to determine whether the company maintained "reasonable procedures" to protect sensitive personal information as required under Texas law, and whether its 44-day gap between breach discovery and consumer notification complied with the Texas Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act's notification timeline [Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 521](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/bc/pdf/bc.521.pdf). <!-- verified: 44-day gap and CID confirmed by independent WebSearch corroboration --> The breach itself was attributed by the extortion group ShinyHunters, which claimed responsibility via a pay-or-leak extortion portal [The State AG Report](https://www.stateagreport.com/news/ag-paxton-full-steam-ahead-on-into-carnival-cruise-line-data-breach/). <!-- verified: ShinyHunters attribution and April 18, 2026 portal listing corroborated by independent WebSearch (The Register, Malwarebytes, BleepingComputer coverage) --> This is the latest in a wave of 2026 state-AG and private enforcement responses to ShinyHunters-linked breaches at large consumer-facing companies.

## Background [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Carnival's IT security team identified unauthorized activity involving an employee account on April 14, 2026, and determined that an unauthorized actor had used social engineering to deceive an employee into granting access to a limited portion of the company's IT systems [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach); [WFAA](https://www.wfaa.com/article/money/business/a-serious-matter-texas-ag-ken-paxton-launches-investigation-into-carnival-cruise-line-data-breach/287-984670b7-4c51-4bde-bb84-0442b1f72aef). <!-- verified: April 14, 2026 discovery date and social-engineering vector confirmed via independent WebSearch corroboration --> Carnival determined on April 22, 2026, that personal information had been illegally copied, and the company posted a formal notice of the breach on May 27, 2026 [The State AG Report](https://www.stateagreport.com/news/ag-paxton-full-steam-ahead-on-into-carnival-cruise-line-data-breach/). <!-- verified: dates match independent search corroboration --> The extortion group ShinyHunters claimed responsibility for the intrusion via its pay-or-leak data extortion portal on April 18, 2026, asserting it had stolen more than 8.7 million customer records and several terabytes of corporate data [The State AG Report](https://www.stateagreport.com/news/ag-paxton-full-steam-ahead-on-into-carnival-cruise-line-data-breach/). <!-- verified: April 18 listing and 8.7M claim corroborated independently (The Register, "ShinyHunters claim they have cruise giant Carnival's booty") --> ShinyHunters has been linked to a series of large-scale 2026 breaches at other major consumer brands, including Charter Communications/Spectrum and Madison Square Garden, both of which are separately tracked in this knowledge base (see Related Reports below).

Under Texas's Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act (Business & Commerce Code Chapter 521), entities holding "sensitive personal information" of Texas residents must implement and maintain reasonable procedures to protect that data, and must notify affected Texas residents of a breach without unreasonable delay and, in any event, no later than 60 days after discovery [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.053](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=BC&Value=521.053). <!-- verified: independent WebSearch of statutory text confirms "without unreasonable delay" and "not later than the 60th day after the date on which the person determines that the breach occurred" language --> The Texas OAG's CID authority under the Act allows the Attorney General to compel documents and information from a company to determine compliance before any enforcement suit is filed.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence] [NEEDS REVIEW]

Carnival's data breach notification to the Texas OAG reported that 800,060 Texas consumers were affected, and that the notification was submitted 44 days after the company's determination that data had been improperly accessed/copied — inside the Chapter 521 60-day outer limit, but a gap that the Attorney General's office has flagged as relevant to its inquiry into whether Carnival acted "without unreasonable delay" as the statute separately requires [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach); [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/texas/800000-texans-personal-info-compromised-in-cruise-company-data-breach/). <!-- verified: 800,060 figure and 44-day gap independently corroborated via WebSearch -->

The categories of information implicated include names, contact information, dates of birth, payment information, passport information, driver's license information, health information, and other identifying information collected through Carnival's account creation, booking, customer communication, and rewards-program processes [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach); [KHOU](https://www.khou.com/article/news/nation-world/ken-paxton-carnival-data-cruise/285-e4501298-72de-4488-a390-ccef3484c5e4). <!-- verified: full data category list, including payment and health information, corroborated by independent WebSearch aggregation of news coverage --> The presence of passport numbers, driver's license numbers, and health information alongside payment data raises the potential severity of downstream identity-theft and fraud risk beyond a typical financial-account breach, which is likely a factor in the Attorney General's decision to escalate to a formal CID rather than a routine compliance review [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/data-privacy-security/articles/2492293/texas-ag-investigates-carnival-cruise-line-following-data-breach). <!-- needs-human-review: analytical inference about the AG's motivation for escalating to CID rather than routine review; reasonable but not independently confirmable from public sources -->

The CID compels Carnival to produce information and documents so the OAG can assess (1) whether Carnival maintained reasonable security procedures as required by Chapter 521, and (2) whether the company's notification timeline and process complied with the statute [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach). As of this writing, no enforcement suit, consent order, or civil penalty has been announced; the matter remains at the investigative (CID) stage. Chapter 521 authorizes civil penalties of up to $50,000 per violation for a company's failure to implement reasonable procedures, and separate per-consumer penalties for failure to timely notify, though no specific penalty exposure figure has been publicized by the OAG for this matter [Tex. Bus. & Com. Code Ch. 521](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/bc/pdf/bc.521.pdf). <!-- disputed: independent verification of Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.151 shows the penalty range is $2,000-$50,000 per violation (not simply "up to $50,000"), plus a separate penalty of up to $100/day per individual for untimely notification, capped at $250,000 per breach -- the report's characterization is directionally correct but omits the statutory floor and the specific untimely-notification cap figures -->

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence] [VERIFIED]

Companies in the travel and hospitality sector that collect passport, driver's license, and health data alongside payment information should treat this investigation as a signal that Texas will scrutinize both the adequacy of security controls and the promptness of breach notification, even where a company's notification falls within Chapter 521's 60-day statutory ceiling [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach). Texas has been among the more active state AG offices in cybersecurity enforcement in 2026, and this action follows a pattern of aggressive CID issuance against companies suffering large-scale, socially-engineered breaches. Carnival is reported to be offering affected U.S. residents two years of complimentary credit monitoring through TransUnion, a standard mitigation step that may factor into any eventual settlement negotiations but does not resolve the underlying compliance questions the CID is designed to test [Texas Attorney General press release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-announces-ongoing-investigation-carnival-cruise-line-over-data-breach). <!-- verified: two-year TransUnion credit monitoring offer independently corroborated via WebSearch -->

Because the underlying intrusion has been attributed to ShinyHunters — the same group linked to the Charter Communications/Spectrum and Madison Square Garden breaches tracked elsewhere in this knowledge base — companies facing ShinyHunters-style social-engineering attacks (particularly help-desk/employee-impersonation vectors) should expect continued multistate AG attention to vendor and employee-authentication controls as a recurring enforcement theme in 2026.

## Action Items

- Monitor the Texas OAG's public enforcement docket and press releases for any escalation from CID to formal enforcement action or settlement against Carnival.
- Companies holding passport, driver's license, or health data alongside standard PII/payment data should review whether breach-notification timelines and internal escalation procedures could withstand a "reasonable procedures" / "without unreasonable delay" challenge under Chapter 521 or comparable state identity-theft statutes.
- Reassess employee-authentication and social-engineering/help-desk-impersonation defenses in light of the recurring ShinyHunters attack pattern across multiple 2026 breaches.
- Track whether other state AGs (multistate coordination is common in large breaches) open parallel investigations into the same Carnival incident.
