# Madison Square Garden Faces Multiple Class Actions After ShinyHunters Data Breach Exposing 26 Million Records

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** 2026-06-28

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

Madison Square Garden Entertainment Corp. (MSGE) faces at least five federal class action lawsuits after the cybercrime group ShinyHunters exfiltrated and published approximately 45 gigabytes of internal data in June 2026, following MSGE's refusal to pay a ransom. The breach — initiated via a voice phishing (vishing) attack on June 5, 2026 — exposed facial recognition surveillance logs, internal threat assessments profiling civil rights activists who criticized MSG's biometric programs, background check records, and customer personal information allegedly covering up to 26 million individuals. The lead case is captioned *Avalos v. Madison Square Garden Entertainment Corporation* on the Justia docket (No. 1:26-cv-05095, S.D.N.Y., filed June 16, 2026), though some press accounts and the complaint itself use "Avalo" — the correct spelling should be verified against the PACER filing. The suit seeks a minimum of $5 million in damages. This breach compounds an earlier February 2026 incident in which the Cl0p ransomware group exposed data on approximately 131,070 individuals, raising acute questions about the adequacy of MSGE's security posture and its legal obligations under the New York SHIELD Act.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Attack vector — vishing and credential hijacking:** On June 5, 2026, ShinyHunters executed a voice phishing call targeting a low-level MSGE employee, successfully impersonating a trusted caller and obtaining Microsoft Entra (Azure AD) credentials. The compromised credentials gave the attackers access to MSG's identity platform and internal corporate resources including SharePoint. No software vulnerability was exploited. ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/chloe-aiello/the-massive-madison-square-garden-hack-exposed-celebrities-and-customers-and-started-with-1-old-school-trick/91365306); [ComplianceHub.Wiki](https://compliancehub.wiki/msg-sports-shinyhunters-ransomware-breach-2026/))

- **Data published after missed ransom deadline:** ShinyHunters listed MSGE on its dark-web leak site on approximately June 12, 2026. When MSGE did not meet the June 15, 2026 ransom deadline, ShinyHunters published the entire 45-gigabyte dataset on June 16, 2026. ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/shinyhunters-madison-square-garden-45gb-data-leak-facial-recognition); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-madison-square-garden-hack-26m-records/))

- **Scope of exposed data:** The published dataset reportedly includes: approximately 9.8 million email addresses; close to 5 million street addresses; full names, phone numbers, and dates of birth; facial recognition surveillance entry logs and biometric tracking records for millions of venue visitors; internal threat assessment reports assigning "low risk" or "high risk" ratings to celebrities without documented criteria; background check information; and customer records totaling up to 26 million people. ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/shinyhunters-madison-square-garden-45gb-data-leak-facial-recognition); [CPO Magazine](https://www.cpomagazine.com/cyber-security/data-breach-impacts-madison-square-garden-sports-and-the-new-york-knicks/))

- **"Facial Recognition Activists.docx" — dossiers on civil liberties critics:** Among the leaked documents was a Word file titled "Facial Recognition Activists.docx," stored in an MSG SharePoint folder labeled "Activists," containing detailed profiles on three individuals who publicly criticized MSG's facial recognition program: Evan Greer (Fight for the Future), Albert Fox Cahn (Surveillance Technology Oversight Project), and Adam Schwartz (Electronic Frontier Foundation). Each profile included background information, social media follower counts, screenshots of tweets, and quotes from media coverage. The document also misgendered Greer, a trans woman. ([SOFX](https://www.sofx.com/phone-scam-exposed-msgs-internal-dossiers-on-facial-recognition-critics/); [ClassActionU](https://classactionu.org/our-news/madison-square-garden-hit-with-massive-class-action-after-surveillance-and-fan-data-leaks-online/))

- **Five class actions filed within three days:** The lead suit, No. 1:26-cv-05095 (S.D.N.Y.), was filed June 16, 2026 — the same day ShinyHunters published the data. **Note on plaintiff surname:** The Justia docket caption spells the plaintiff's name "Avalos," while multiple press accounts and sources citing the complaint text use "Avalo." This report uses "Avalos" consistent with the Justia docket, but the correct spelling should be verified against the PACER filing for No. 1:26-cv-05095. Four additional proposed class actions followed: two filed June 17 and two filed June 18, 2026, all in the Southern District of New York on behalf of different lead plaintiffs. The lead suit seeks at least $5 million in damages. ([Justia Dockets](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/new-york/nysdce/1:2026cv05095/666463); [Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/msg-facial-recognition-data-breach-class-action/))

- **Legal theory — negligence, not biometric statute:** The suits plead common-law negligence. New York does not have a statewide biometric privacy statute comparable to Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) that grants individuals a private right of action. Plaintiffs allege MSGE negligently collected and inadequately secured sensitive biometric and personal data. ([Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/news/msg-facial-recognition-data-breach-class-action/); [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202606/new-york-knicks-owner-sued-following-biometric-data-breach))

- **Prior breach context — February 2026 Cl0p incident:** MSGE suffered a separate breach discovered in December 2025 (with intrusion beginning in August 2025) in which the Cl0p ransomware group exploited a vulnerability in an Oracle eBusiness Suite application, exposing the full names, home addresses, and Social Security numbers of approximately 131,070 individuals. That breach was publicly disclosed in February 2026. The two incidents together indicate systemic security program deficiencies. ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/madison-square-garden-hit-with-class-action-after-apparent-data-breach/); [ComplianceHub.Wiki](https://compliancehub.wiki/msg-sports-shinyhunters-ransomware-breach-2026/))

## Applicable Legal Framework [MEDIUM confidence]

**New York SHIELD Act (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §§ 899-aa, 899-bb):** The Stop Hacks and Improve Electronic Data Security Act was signed into law July 25, 2019, with a split effective date: the breach notification amendments to § 899-aa took effect October 23, 2019; the security safeguards requirements of § 899-bb took effect March 21, 2020. The Act imposes two distinct obligations on businesses holding private information of New York residents. Section 899-aa requires breach notification to affected individuals and to the New York Attorney General, Department of State, and State Police. For breaches affecting more than 500 New York residents, notification to the AG must occur within 10 days of the breach determination. The statute explicitly covers biometric information — defined to include fingerprints, voice prints, retina or iris images, and "other unique physical representation or digital representation of biometric data" used to authenticate identity — meaning MSGE's facial recognition records are squarely within scope. Section 899-bb requires businesses to "develop, implement, and maintain reasonable safeguards to protect the security, confidentiality, and integrity of the private information."

Civil penalty structure under the SHIELD Act has three tiers: (1) for general (negligent) failure to provide timely breach notification, the court may award the actual costs or losses incurred by affected New York residents, including consequential financial losses — there is no per-notification cap at this tier; (2) for knowing or reckless violations of the notification requirement, the greater of $5,000 or $20 per instance of failed notification, capped at $250,000; (3) failure to maintain reasonable safeguards under § 899-bb carries a civil penalty of up to $5,000 per violation. The $20-per-notification formula applies exclusively to tier (2) knowing or reckless conduct. ([NY Senate — GBL § 899-AA](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/899-AA); [NY AG SHIELD Act page](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/organizations/data-breach-reporting/shield-act); [Jackson Lewis — NY SHIELD Act FAQs](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/new-york-shield-act-faqs); [Proskauer — SHIELD Act Alert](https://www.proskauer.com/alert/the-new-shield-act-changes-breach-notification-rules-and-data-security-standards-for-new-yorkers-personal-information))

**FTC Act Section 5:** The Federal Trade Commission's authority over unfair or deceptive trade practices applies to MSGE's data security practices and any representations made to consumers about how their biometric and personal data would be protected. The FTC has increasingly scrutinized facial recognition deployments and sensitive data security. ([ComplianceHub.Wiki](https://compliancehub.wiki/msg-sports-shinyhunters-ransomware-breach-2026/))

**Absence of New York Biometric Privacy Statute:** New York has no equivalent to Illinois BIPA that allows private litigants to sue over biometric data collection itself — as distinct from security failures. This gap is why the class actions are framed in negligence rather than biometric-specific statutory claims. The New York City Biometric Identifier Information Law (NYC Admin. Code § 22-1201 et seq.) applies to commercial establishments in NYC but does not directly create the liability framework these plaintiffs are pursuing. ([Recording Law — NY Biometric Privacy Laws](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/data-privacy-laws/new-york-data-privacy-laws/biometric-privacy/))

## Action Items

- **Assess biometric data inventory immediately:** Any organization operating facial recognition entry systems at public venues should audit what biometric data is collected, how long it is retained, where it is stored (including cloud-based SharePoint and identity platforms), and who has access. Facial recognition surveillance logs represent a new category of sensitive exposure risk.

- **Review Microsoft Entra / identity platform access controls:** The MSG breach and the Charter Communications breach (also a ShinyHunters vishing attack against Microsoft Entra credentials) confirm a pattern. Implement phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/hardware keys) for all privileged identity accounts. Conduct vishing simulation training, particularly for help desk and IT staff with credential reset authority.

- **Verify SHIELD Act notification compliance:** New York entities that have suffered a breach involving biometric data of New York residents must notify the AG within 10 days of the breach determination and notify affected individuals within 30 days. Note: the 30-day individual notification deadline was added by a December 2024 amendment to § 899-aa, replacing the prior standard of "the most expedient time possible." Review whether your incident response plan addresses the SHIELD Act's expanded definition of "private information" including biometric identifiers.

- **Audit internal threat assessment documents for legal exposure:** The "Facial Recognition Activists.docx" document illustrates that internal dossiers on civil liberties critics create independent legal and reputational risk. Organizations maintaining surveillance programs should work with counsel to assess whether internal documents profiling critics could constitute tortious conduct, particularly where such records co-mingle with biometric surveillance data.

- **Prepare for regulatory scrutiny of prior incidents:** MSGE's two separate major breaches within six months (Cl0p in February 2026, ShinyHunters in June 2026) invite heightened regulatory attention. The New York AG's office, which enforces the SHIELD Act, is likely to examine the adequacy of safeguards implemented between the two events.

- **Monitor consolidation of class actions:** With five class actions already filed in S.D.N.Y., litigation will likely proceed to a motion for consolidation or lead plaintiff designation. Companies in similar situations should track any motion practice or scheduling orders that could accelerate discovery timelines.
