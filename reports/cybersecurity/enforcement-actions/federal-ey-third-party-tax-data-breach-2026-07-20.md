# EY Discloses Third-Party Platform Breach Exposing Client Tax Data; Faces SDNY Class Action

**Jurisdiction:** Federal, California, Massachusetts, Texas, Vermont | **Category:** cybersecurity | **Date:** 2026-07-20

## Summary [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

Ernst & Young LLP ("EY") disclosed that an unauthorized third party accessed a third-party IT service management (support-ticket) platform used by EY IT staff to support tax-related client work, and downloaded documents containing clients' personal and financial information between March 28, 2026 and April 12, 2026. EY detected the anomalous activity on April 23, 2026, but did not begin notifying affected individuals until July 13, 2026 — nearly three months later — filing breach notifications with the California, Massachusetts, Texas, and Vermont regulators in mid-July 2026. The firm has been sued in a proposed class action in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, and is offering affected individuals 24 months of Experian IdentityWorks identity-monitoring services, per [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ernst-and-young-discloses-data-breach-after-support-system-hack/), [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/ernst-young-data-breach-affects-personal-financial-information/), and the [Massachusetts data breach notification archive](https://www.mass.gov/lists/data-breach-notification-letters-july-2026). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts: The Breach [HIGH confidence] [VERIFIED]

- EY uses a third-party IT service management (support-ticket) platform to help its IT personnel support internal teams performing tax-related work for clients; support tickets submitted through the platform routinely included attachments containing sensitive client tax documents, per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320969/20260719/ey-tax-data-stolen-through-third-party-help-desk-platform-four-states-notified.htm) and [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/security/ey-data-breach-tax-documents/). <!-- verified -->
- An unauthorized third party accessed the platform and downloaded documents belonging to a number of EY clients between **March 28, 2026 and April 12, 2026**; EY detected the anomalous activity on **April 23, 2026** and launched an investigation with outside cybersecurity experts, per [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ernst-and-young-discloses-data-breach-after-support-system-hack/) and [SecurityAffairs](https://securityaffairs.com/195550/data-breach/ernst-young-ey-investigates-data-breach-involving-third-party-support-tickets.html). <!-- verified -->
- Compromised data categories include names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, email addresses, phone numbers, financial account codes, credit/debit account information, investment holdings, and information contained in or used to prepare client tax filings, per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320969/20260719/ey-tax-data-stolen-through-third-party-help-desk-platform-four-states-notified.htm) and [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/ernst-young-data-breach-affects-personal-financial-information/). <!-- verified -->
- EY did not send notification letters to affected individuals until **July 13, 2026** (nearly three months after detection), and filed breach notifications with the **California Attorney General on July 15, 2026** and the **Vermont Attorney General on July 16, 2026**; the letters are also listed on the [Massachusetts Data Breach Notification Letters archive for July 2026](https://www.mass.gov/lists/data-breach-notification-letters-july-2026) and the [California AG's public data breach list](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/databreach/list), per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320969/20260719/ey-tax-data-stolen-through-third-party-help-desk-platform-four-states-notified.htm). <!-- verified -->
- State filings disclose **873 Texas residents**, **480 Massachusetts residents**, and **13 Vermont residents** affected; EY has not publicly disclosed a total or global figure, the identity of the compromised third-party platform vendor, or whether ransomware/data-extortion actors were involved — no group has claimed responsibility, per [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320969/20260719/ey-tax-data-stolen-through-third-party-help-desk-platform-four-states-notified.htm) and [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ernst-and-young-discloses-data-breach-after-support-system-hack/). <!-- verified -->
- EY states it has secured its systems, removed unauthorized access, notified federal law enforcement, and is offering affected individuals 24 months of complimentary Experian IdentityWorks credit and identity-monitoring services (enrollment deadline **11:59 p.m. UTC on October 31, 2026**), with a dedicated inquiry line at 833-391-7884, per [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/ernst-young-data-breach-affects-personal-financial-information/). <!-- verified -->

## Key Facts: Litigation and Regulatory Exposure [MEDIUM confidence] [NEEDS REVIEW]

- A proposed class action, *Wyatt v. Ernst & Young LLP*, was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York on behalf of Illinois resident Markishi Wyatt, alleging EY — as a "sophisticated organization with the resources to deploy robust cybersecurity protocols" — knew or should have known such protections were necessary and failed to safeguard sensitive client tax and financial data, per [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2503497/ey-sued-over-breach-targeting-client-tax-financial-info), [CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/ey-faces-proposed-class-action-data-breach-cybersecurity/825742/), and [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/ey-hit-proposed-class-action-053943732.html). The complaint seeks class certification and monetary relief, and alleges heightened risk of identity theft and tax-refund fraud for the (unspecified, but potentially "tens or hundreds of thousands" per plaintiffs' counsel estimates) affected class. <!-- verified -->
- Separately, plaintiffs' firm Edelson Lechtzin LLP announced it is investigating additional data-privacy claims arising from the same incident, per its own [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ernst--young-data-breach-exposes-client-tax-records-edelson-lechtzin-llp-investigates-class-action-claims-302829205.html) — a solicitation announcement rather than an independent confirmation of new facts. <!-- verified -->

## Action Items

- **EY tax and advisory clients (individuals and financial institutions):** Confirm whether you received a breach notification letter dated July 13, 2026; if so, enroll in the complimentary Experian IdentityWorks monitoring before the October 31, 2026 UTC deadline, and place a credit freeze/fraud alert given the exposure of Social Security numbers and tax-filing data (elevated tax-refund fraud risk ahead of the next filing season).
- **In-house privacy and vendor-risk teams:** Treat this as a reminder to inventory third-party IT service management / help-desk platforms that routinely receive sensitive attachments (tax documents, SSNs, financial account data) via support tickets, and confirm those platforms are in scope for vendor security assessments and data-minimization controls (e.g., attachment scanning/redaction on support tickets).
- **Compliance and legal teams at accounting/professional-services firms:** Note the near-three-month gap between EY's April 23 detection and July 13 individual notification; monitor for any state AG scrutiny of notification timeliness (several state breach statutes, including Texas's Identity Theft Enforcement and Protection Act and Vermont's Security Breach Notice Act, impose "as soon as practicable" or fixed-day notification triggers that could be implicated by this timeline).
- **Monitor for developments:** Watch for (a) additional state AG notifications/enforcement beyond CA, MA, TX, and VT; (b) consolidation of the Wyatt suit with any subsequent Edelson Lechtzin-led filings; and (c) EY's disclosure (if any) of the compromised platform vendor's identity, which would be relevant to any secondary vendor-liability claims.
