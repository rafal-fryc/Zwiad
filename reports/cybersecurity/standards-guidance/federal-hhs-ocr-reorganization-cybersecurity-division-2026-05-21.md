# HHS Announces Reorganization Creating Separate Cybersecurity Division Within OCR

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity / Healthcare Privacy | **Date:** May 18, 2026

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 18, 2026, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) [announced](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-announces-restructuring-of-its-office-for-civil-rights.html) a program-based reorganization of its Office for Civil Rights (OCR), splitting the agency into three distinct subject-matter divisions: the Conscience and Religious Freedom Division, the Civil Rights Division, and the Health Information Privacy, Data, and Cybersecurity Division. OCR Director Paula Stannard described the change as reinstating a structure that "rightly prioritizes civil rights and conscience and religious freedom alongside health information privacy and security." The reorganization formalizes separate cybersecurity oversight within OCR for the first time since the Biden administration consolidated OCR's programmatic functions in 2023. For covered entities and business associates subject to HIPAA, the reorganization signals organizational continuity for privacy and cybersecurity enforcement — though resource constraints created by broader HHS workforce reductions mean effective enforcement capacity remains significantly reduced from prior years.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

OCR is HHS's law enforcement agency responsible for enforcing three distinct bodies of federal law: civil rights statutes prohibiting discrimination in health programs, conscience and religious freedom protections, and the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA), including the Privacy Rule, Security Rule, and Breach Notification Rule.

The Conscience and Religious Freedom Division (CRFD) was originally established during the first Trump administration in 2018. The Biden administration dissolved it in March 2023, consolidating the CRFD's functions with the Civil Rights Division into a unified Policy Division. Critics of that consolidation argued it de-emphasized conscience protections; supporters contended it streamlined operations. OCR retained legal jurisdiction over conscience and religious freedom matters throughout this period, but the dedicated divisional structure was eliminated.

In parallel with the divisional merger, the Biden administration also reorganized OCR's health information privacy and cybersecurity work. The net effect was a flatter, less program-differentiated structure that combined what had previously been distinct operational teams.

The broader HHS context matters: on March 27, 2025, HHS [announced](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-restructuring-doge.html) a departmental restructuring reducing agency workforce by approximately 20,000 positions — bringing HHS headcount back to pre-2002 levels of around 62,000 employees. OCR was among the units affected. As of the May 2026 reorganization, OCR employs 116 full-time staff and operates at a budget deficit of $39.7 million, well below staffing levels typical in the early 2020s.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

The May 18, 2026 reorganization returns OCR to a program-based structure with three subject-matter divisions:

**1. Conscience and Religious Freedom Division** — Restores the discrete operational unit eliminated in 2023. Per the [HHS press release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-announces-restructuring-of-its-office-for-civil-rights.html), this division will address conscience rights, anti-discrimination on religious grounds, and what HHS characterizes as eradicating antisemitism and anti-Christian bias in federal health programs.

**2. Civil Rights Division** — Continues enforcement of civil rights statutes prohibiting discrimination on the basis of race, sex, national origin, disability, and age in health programs receiving federal financial assistance. HHS noted the division will "address race-based discrimination in a color-blind manner."

**3. Health Information Privacy, Data, and Cybersecurity Division** — The division directly relevant to HIPAA compliance. This unit inherits OCR's full portfolio of privacy, security, and breach notification enforcement under 45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164. Per an HHS official quoted by [GovInfoSecurity](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/hhs-revamps-hipaa-enforcement-agency-a-31723), the reorganization "will not impact the agency's health information privacy, data and cybersecurity division or the staffing for rulemaking within that division." The division will continue providing "subject-matter expertise and analytic support and leadership to high impact investigations stemming from complaints, compliance reviews and breaches," along with rulemaking and policy development functions.

**OCR's Position Within HHS Hierarchy**

The reorganization also situates OCR within a new HHS structural unit — the [Office of the Assistant Secretary for Enforcement](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/hhs-revamps-hipaa-enforcement-agency-a-31723) — which consolidates under one umbrella OCR, the Departmental Appeals Board (DAB), the Office of Medicare Hearings and Appeals (OMHA), and the Office for Human Research Protections (OHRP). The FY 2026 budget request for this combined ASE unit is $230.7 million and 893 FTE. The placement of OCR within ASE reflects the Trump administration's framing of HHS enforcement as fundamentally about combating waste, fraud, and abuse.

**Enforcement Operations**

Complaint intake, breach reporting review, and field office execution will continue through an Enforcement Division that functions as a shared operational backbone for all three program divisions. This means that the Health Information Privacy, Data, and Cybersecurity Division sets substantive policy and leads complex investigations, while the Enforcement Division handles volume processing.

**HIPAA Security Rule NPRM — Pending Action**

The reorganization occurs against the backdrop of the December 2024 HIPAA Security Rule NPRM, published in the Federal Register on January 6, 2025 ([90 Fed. Reg. 898](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/06/2024-30983/hipaa-security-rule-to-strengthen-the-cybersecurity-of-electronic-protected-health-information)). The NPRM proposes sweeping changes including mandatory encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication, 72-hour incident reporting timelines, and annual penetration testing — with HHS projecting first-year compliance costs of approximately $9 billion industry-wide. OCR received over 4,700 public comments on the NPRM. As of the HIMSS 2026 conference, Director Stannard confirmed OCR is still parsing those comments. No final rule publication date has been announced. The reorganization does not delay or affect the NPRM timeline, which is being handled within the newly named Health Information Privacy, Data, and Cybersecurity Division.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

**Covered Entities and Business Associates**

For hospitals, health systems, physician practices, health plans, and their business associates, the organizational change is largely structural rather than operational. The same legal authority — HIPAA as codified at 42 U.S.C. § 1320d et seq. and its implementing regulations at 45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164 — remains unchanged. OCR continues to accept HIPAA complaints, conduct compliance reviews, investigate reported breaches, and negotiate resolution agreements.

However, the resource picture is materially constrained. OCR's current 116-person workforce enforces HIPAA obligations on behalf of hundreds of millions of Americans across a regulated population of hundreds of thousands of covered entities. [BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/hhs-revamps-hipaa-enforcement-agency-a-31723) notes that the HHS-wide workforce reductions and regional office closures significantly reduced the civil servants available for HIPAA enforcement, creating a "resource-starved" environment. The FY 2027 budget request would raise OCR funding to $42.7 million and 144 FTE — an improvement, but still below historical allocations.

The practical effect is that OCR will continue enforcement activity — as evidenced by the April 2026 ransomware settlements totaling $1.165 million — but may prioritize high-impact cases and systemic violations over routine compliance inquiries. HIPAA rulemaking timelines, including the final HIPAA Security Rule, may also slow.

**Healthcare Cybersecurity Oversight**

The creation of a dedicated Health Information Privacy, Data, and Cybersecurity Division with its own divisional identity provides organizational clarity. Previous analyses by security consultants noted confusion about whether cybersecurity expertise was adequately resourced within OCR's prior merged structure. A dedicated division with named cybersecurity responsibilities may improve OCR's technical engagement with emerging healthcare cyber threats — though this improvement depends on staffing that is currently below historical norms.

[Clearwater Security's analysis](https://clearwatersecurity.com/blog/hipaa-security-rule-enforcement-2026/) of 2026 HIPAA enforcement trends notes that OCR's investigation posture has shifted toward evaluating not just whether a risk analysis exists, but whether the organization acted on it — a trend the new division structure is unlikely to reverse.

**Conscience and Religious Freedom Implications for Healthcare Privacy**

The restoration of the Conscience and Religious Freedom Division and the emphasis placed on it by Director Stannard has raised concerns among civil rights organizations about potential deemphasis of traditional health discrimination enforcement. The [African Elements analysis](https://www.africanelements.org/news/why-the-hhs-ocr-reorganization-threatens-black-civil-rights/) argues the reorganization structurally deprioritizes enforcement of race and disability non-discrimination in health programs. This is a contested political assessment, but compliance officers at health systems receiving federal funding should monitor whether OCR's civil rights enforcement posture shifts materially under the new structure.

## Action Items

- **HIPAA Security compliance readiness**: Continue preparing for the anticipated HIPAA Security Rule final rule. Core requirements flagged in the January 2025 NPRM — MFA, encryption, annual penetration testing, 72-hour incident reporting — are likely to survive in some form. Gap assessments against these requirements should be completed before a final rule is issued.
- **OCR complaint and breach reporting**: The reorganization does not change breach reporting obligations under 45 C.F.R. § 164.400 et seq. Continue timely breach notification to OCR (within 60 days of discovery for breaches affecting 500+ individuals) and to affected individuals (without unreasonable delay).
- **Monitor NPRM finalization**: Track the HIPAA Security Rule NPRM docket for a final rule publication date. The compliance period after a final rule is expected to be 180 days from effective date, with a transition period for business associate agreement modifications.
- **Risk analysis documentation**: OCR's Risk Analysis Initiative has now resulted in 13 completed investigations. Ensure risk analyses are current, accurate, documented, and that remediation actions are tracked and implemented — OCR investigations now evaluate follow-through, not just existence of a risk analysis.
- **Conscience protections for healthcare providers**: Organizations employing clinicians with conscience objections to certain procedures should review compliance with applicable conscience statutes (e.g., the Church, Coats-Snowe, and Weldon Amendments), as the restored Conscience and Religious Freedom Division is expected to actively investigate violations.
