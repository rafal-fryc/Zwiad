# NIST Publishes SP 800-238: FY2025 Cybersecurity and Privacy Program Annual Report

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** May 21, 2026

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) published [Special Publication 800-238](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/238/final), the Fiscal Year 2025 Cybersecurity and Privacy Program Annual Report, on May 21, 2026. The report documents the Information Technology Laboratory (ITL) Cybersecurity and Privacy Program's activities from October 1, 2024 through September 30, 2025, spanning seven major priority areas: cryptography, cybersecurity and AI, education and workforce development, hardware and software security, infrastructure security, privacy, and risk management. Key achievements include expanded post-quantum cryptography (PQC) validation activity, progress on the Cybersecurity Framework Profile for AI (NISTIR 8596), advancement of the CSF 2.0 adoption ecosystem, and continued international standards engagement. For compliance professionals and regulated entities, the report provides a consolidated reference for NIST's current guidance landscape, reveals the near-term publication roadmap, and signals where federal cybersecurity expectations are heading.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

NIST's ITL Cybersecurity and Privacy Program has produced annual reports for well over a decade, cataloguing standards development, research milestones, and cross-sector collaboration. The reports are published as Special Publications in the 800 series — the same series that houses foundational frameworks such as [SP 800-53](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final) (security and privacy controls) and [SP 800-37](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/37/r2/final) (Risk Management Framework). The immediately preceding edition was SP 800-236 (FY2024).

Fiscal Year 2025 was a particularly active period for NIST cybersecurity output. The year opened with NIST holding the first validations under the newly finalized post-quantum cryptography FIPS standards — [FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final), [FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final), and [FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) — which had been formally issued in August 2024. The Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (CSF 2.0), released in February 2024, was entering its first full year of adoption-support activities. Simultaneously, the agency was scaling up its AI risk and security work in response to growing federal and executive attention to AI governance.

The CSRC annual report is the authoritative source for understanding what NIST's cybersecurity program accomplished, what publications entered the public record, and which multi-stakeholder projects moved forward — information directly relevant to organizations whose compliance programs are built around NIST frameworks.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### Post-Quantum Cryptography

PQC migration was one of the highest-profile workstreams of FY2025. NIST completed [170 validations](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/238/final) for ML-DSA (from FIPS 204) and ML-KEM (from FIPS 203), the two primary lattice-based algorithms. Overall, the Cryptographic Module Validation Program (CMVP) tested 262 products during the fiscal year, with NIST working to improve workflow and reduce validation processing times — an acknowledged bottleneck as vendors race to ship PQC-compliant products.

The [PQC Migration Project](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography) expanded to more than 50 collaborating organizations, focused on demonstrating discovery and inventory tooling to help enterprises understand their cryptographic asset exposure before migration. This is a prerequisite step organizations must complete before they can prioritize which systems to upgrade first. NIST also delivered nearly 1 million automated algorithm test vectors during FY2025, enabling broad, low-friction validation across the industry.

### Cybersecurity and AI

The report's AI section reflects the depth of NIST's commitment to integrating cybersecurity and AI risk management. Key FY2025 accomplishments include:

- Publishing a concept paper and gathering input through workshops involving thousands of participants for the [Cybersecurity Framework Profile for AI (NISTIR 8596)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2025/NIST.IR.8596.iprd.pdf), the "Cyber AI Profile," which layers AI-specific cybersecurity priorities onto CSF 2.0.
- Launching the NCCoE's [Control Overlays for Securing AI Systems (COSAiS)](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/projects/all) project, which develops SP 800-53 control overlays for AI systems using SP 800-218A, Draft AI 800-1, and AI 100-2e2025.
- Releasing "Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations" (AI 100-2e2025), addressing the threat landscape for deployed AI systems.

The Cyber AI Profile development — which involved a community of interest exceeding 6,500 participants — is expected to produce an initial public draft in summer 2026, with finalization targeted for 2027.

### Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 Adoption

NIST advanced CSF 2.0 adoption across multiple fronts during FY2025:

- Publishing seven sector-specific community profiles and related resources through the NCCoE, including the [Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 Manufacturing Profile](https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/projects/all) providing manufacturers with a voluntary, risk-based framework.
- Releasing quick-start guides aligned with the CSF 2.0 toolkit, including [NIST SP 1308](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/1308/final), which addresses cybersecurity, enterprise risk management, and workforce management.
- Adding informative references to the CSF 2.0 online tool via the Online Informative References (OLIR) platform.
- Publishing translated CSF 2.0 resources to support international uptake.
- Launching a webinar series and updating the [NIST IR 8286 series](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8286/final) on cybersecurity and enterprise risk management integration.

### 5G Security

NIST's 5G Cybersecurity project released three white papers during FY2025 — [CSWP 36C, CSWP 36D, and CSWP 36E](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/238/final) — addressing potential security concerns and mitigation strategies for 5G deployments. These publications are relevant to telecommunications operators, enterprises deploying private 5G networks, and organizations dependent on 5G-connected critical infrastructure.

### IoT and Hardware/Software Security

NIST advanced IoT cybersecurity guidelines under the program initiated by the IoT Cybersecurity Improvement Act. Through the NCCoE, NIST also published APIs and cloud security guidance, including:

- "Guidelines for API Protection for Cloud-Native Systems" — recommending practical security controls and risk-based approaches to API protection.
- "Service Mesh Proxy Models for Cloud-Native Applications" — addressing security architecture for containerized environments.

### Software and Supply Chain Security

The report documents continued activity on software supply chain security, building on work initiated under [Executive Order 14028](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/05/17/2021-10460/improving-the-nations-cybersecurity) (Improving the Nation's Cybersecurity, May 2021). NIST maintained its [Software Security in Supply Chains initiative](https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-order-14028-improving-nations-cybersecurity/software-security-supply-chains-software-1), including SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) resources and the secure software development framework. The FY2025 report covers ongoing participation in international standards processes related to supply chain integrity, reflecting coordination with ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27 and related bodies.

### Privacy Program

NIST's privacy activities included work on Privacy-Enhancing Technologies (PETs) through the NCCoE's Privacy-Enhancing Technologies Testbed, which provides evaluation frameworks with real-world use cases, model solutions, and utility/privacy trade-off metrics. The program also continued advancing the NIST Privacy Framework and the Phish Scale tool (a research-based method for evaluating phishing exercise difficulty).

### Risk Management and Identity

NIST launched a new public comment site for the Risk Management Framework (RMF), improving stakeholder access to the ongoing development process for RMF-related publications. Progress was also documented in the Identity and Access Management (IAM) program, which underpins zero trust architecture adoption across the federal enterprise.

### Education and Workforce Development

NIST's education and workforce focus area coordinated programs to grow and sustain a skilled cybersecurity workforce, including increasing public awareness of cybersecurity, cybersafety, and cyberethics, and disseminating technical standards and best practices for individuals and enterprises. This work advances the National Initiative for Cybersecurity Education (NICE) framework.

### International Standards Participation

The annual report specifically highlights NIST's participation in developing international standards — including contributions to ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27 (information security, cybersecurity, and privacy protection). This international engagement means that NIST frameworks increasingly align with and inform global standards, relevant for multinational organizations managing cross-border compliance obligations.

## Impact Assessment [HIGH confidence]

### Who Is Affected

SP 800-238 is primarily informational but has practical significance for:

- **Federal agencies and contractors** — The report serves as a roadmap for which NIST publications have been finalized, are in draft, or are expected in the coming year. Federal agencies must align their security programs with NIST standards under FISMA; knowing the publication pipeline is operationally important for compliance planning.
- **Critical infrastructure operators** — Sector-specific profiles (manufacturing, energy, healthcare) are being developed or updated; operators should assess applicability to their security programs.
- **Cryptography teams and vendors** — The PQC validation acceleration and 50+ organization migration project signal that PQC readiness is becoming a procurement and compliance expectation, not merely a theoretical concern.
- **AI system developers and deployers** — The COSAiS overlays and Cyber AI Profile development have direct implications for organizations building or operating AI systems subject to federal procurement or risk management requirements.
- **Compliance professionals and counsel** — The report provides a current-state summary of the NIST guidance ecosystem, useful for gap assessments, program updates, and board-level reporting.

### Compliance Timelines and Implications

The report itself carries no mandatory compliance requirements — NIST Special Publications are voluntary guidance except where incorporated by reference into binding rules (as FISMA and sector-specific regulations do). However, several developments flagged in the report have near-term compliance implications:

- **PQC:** The National Security Memorandum on PQC (NSM-10, May 2022) set federal agency timelines for cryptographic inventory and migration. The FY2025 validation activity confirms that compliant implementations are now available for procurement. CMVP validation is increasingly a requirement in federal contracting.
- **CSF 2.0:** While not mandatory for non-federal entities, CSF 2.0 is being incorporated into sector regulations and cyber insurance underwriting standards. FY2025 sector profiles make sector-specific alignment easier.
- **AI Security:** The COSAiS project and Cyber AI Profile are likely precursors to regulatory expectations. Federal procurement and FISMA-related guidance will likely reference these frameworks once finalized.

### Enforcement Outlook

NIST itself does not enforce; enforcement flows through agencies that reference NIST frameworks (CISA for critical infrastructure, FTC for commercial entities, HHS OCR for healthcare). The importance of this report for enforcement purposes is indirect: it establishes the current authoritative state of NIST guidance, which regulators and courts use as a benchmark for reasonable security practice.

## Action Items

- **Review PQC migration readiness:** Conduct or update a cryptographic inventory to assess which systems depend on algorithms targeted for replacement. With 170+ validated ML-DSA and ML-KEM implementations now available, procurement timelines for replacement components are becoming actionable.
- **Map CSF 2.0 sector profile applicability:** If operating in manufacturing, healthcare, energy, or financial services, identify whether a relevant NCCoE CSF 2.0 sector community profile exists and assess its applicability to your security program.
- **Track NISTIR 8596 (Cyber AI Profile) development:** The initial public draft is expected summer 2026. Organizations developing or deploying AI systems subject to federal frameworks should engage in the comment process when it opens.
- **Assess supply chain SBOM posture:** The ongoing NIST SBOM and secure software development framework work should be reviewed against current vendor contracts and procurement standards.
- **Monitor the new RMF comment site:** Organizations participating in federal programs should track RMF updates via the new NIST public comment portal, as updates may affect FISMA compliance programs.
- **Evaluate NCCoE PETs Testbed applicability:** Privacy teams at data-intensive organizations should review the Privacy-Enhancing Technologies Testbed outputs for applicable evaluation methodologies.
