# White House ONCD Queries Tech Industry on AI-Enhanced Cyber Defenses

**Jurisdiction:** Federal | **Category:** Cybersecurity | **Date:** May 1, 2026

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

On April 23, 2026, the White House Office of the National Cyber Director (ONCD) sent an email from Jennifer Belair, the assistant national cyber director for external affairs, to a broad but undisclosed set of technology and cybersecurity companies, requesting written responses to 11 questions covering AI-powered defensive tools, critical infrastructure vulnerability management, and network isolation practices. Responses were due by May 1, 2026. The outreach follows the administration's March 2026 National Cyber Strategy — which explicitly calls on federal agencies to adopt AI-powered cybersecurity solutions — and occurs in the immediate wake of Anthropic's limited release of the Claude Mythos frontier model, which has demonstrated autonomous zero-day vulnerability discovery at scale. The ONCD's questions span both technical capability assessments (current AI tool deployment, AI model integration into software stacks) and sensitive operational information (lists of critical networks and hardware, isolation strategies, and worst-case compromise scenarios). The initiative reflects the administration's effort to develop an evidence-based AI security policy framework and assess how the private sector can serve as a force multiplier for national cyber defenses.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### National Cyber Strategy (March 2026)

On March 6, 2026, the White House released [President Trump's Cyber Strategy for America](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/president-trumps-cyber-strategy-for-america.pdf), alongside an Executive Order targeting transnational cybercrime. The strategy outlines six pillars: deterring cyber adversaries, improving cybersecurity regulation, modernizing federal networks, strengthening critical infrastructure, expanding the cyber workforce, and securing emerging technologies. A central element of the strategy is a directive for federal agencies to procure AI-powered cybersecurity solutions to protect public sector systems and fight cyber attacks at scale. The strategy explicitly positions AI as a tool for defending federal networks, calling for adoption of "AI-powered cybersecurity solutions to protect federal systems and mitigate intrusions at scale."

Law firm analyses from [Goodwin Procter](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/alerts-otherindustries-dpc-white-house-makes-cyber-ai-policy-push), [Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/white-houses-new-cyber-strategy-and-executive-order-seek-to-deter-adversaries-and-strengthen-resilience), and [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/white-house-releases-national-cyber-strategy-and-accompanying-eo-targeting-transnational-cybercrime) characterized the strategy as emphasizing offensive deterrence, deregulation, and accelerated AI adoption — a posture that sets the stage for the ONCD's subsequent industry outreach.

### ONCD AI Security Policy Framework Development

In February 2026, [Nextgov/FCW reported](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/02/white-house-cyber-shop-crafting-ai-security-policy-framework-top-official-says/411154/) that the ONCD was developing an AI security policy framework intended to embed security into AI systems without slowing innovation. The Department of Homeland Security announced plans to establish an AI Information Sharing and Analysis Center (AI-ISAC) to coordinate AI-related threat intelligence across critical infrastructure sectors. These actions established the institutional infrastructure within which the later industry questionnaire would be deployed.

### Anthropic Claude Mythos and the Broader AI-Cyber Nexus

The ONCD outreach occurs against the backdrop of Anthropic's April 7, 2026 announcement of Claude Mythos Preview — a frontier model capable of autonomously identifying and exploiting software vulnerabilities. As detailed in the [ONCD's April 23 outreach email](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/white-house-oncd-ai-tech-industry-questions/819133/), Jennifer Belair noted the administration was "proactively engaging across government and industry to address several AI/cybersecurity priorities," and cited collaboration with frontier AI labs on scaling challenges.

Prior to the Mythos announcement, Vice President JD Vance and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had [questioned leading tech CEOs](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/10/trump-white-house-ai-cyber-threat-anthropic-mythos.html) — including the heads of Anthropic, xAI, Google, OpenAI, and Microsoft — about AI model security and cyberattack response strategies. The call occurred the week before Anthropic's formal Mythos release. Simultaneously, [Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell and Secretary Bessent](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/10/powell-bessent-us-bank-ceos-anthropic-mythos-ai-cyber.html) convened major U.S. bank CEOs to assess the systemic financial risk posed by Mythos-class AI cyber capabilities.

Separately, a bipartisan [Senate letter from Senators Hassan and Ernst](https://www.hassan.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/hassan-ernst_anthropic_ai_cyberattack_letter.pdf) asked the ONCD about its communications with Anthropic regarding the first documented large-scale AI-executed cyberattack (attributed to Chinese government-linked actors using Anthropic's Claude platform) and what plans existed to work with AI companies to limit AI's potential use in future cyberattacks.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The 11 Questions and Their Scope

[Cybersecurity Dive's reporting](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/white-house-oncd-ai-tech-industry-questions/819133/) provides the most detailed public account of the ONCD questionnaire. ONCD asked companies to respond to 11 questions by May 1, 2026, spanning a spectrum from operational snapshots to sensitive critical infrastructure disclosures:

**Straightforward AI adoption questions:**
- "Are you currently using AI detection and response tools and services?"
- "How are AI models or platforms integrated into your software stack?"

**Vulnerability management questions:**
- How quickly companies can identify and fix known vulnerabilities
- What barriers companies face to improving their cyber posture

**Critical infrastructure disclosure questions:**
- A request for a list of networks, hardware, and software that are critical to companies' continuity of operations
- A description of how companies isolate those systems from traditional business networks
- A description of what would happen if the company's most critical systems were compromised

The breadth of the questionnaire — from current AI tool deployment to worst-case compromise scenarios — reflects the dual purpose of the ONCD's outreach: mapping the AI-cybersecurity capabilities landscape across major private sector actors and simultaneously identifying systemic vulnerabilities that federal policy or assistance might address.

The April 23 email did not include a visible list of recipients, leaving it unclear which companies received it. The White House appeared to have sent different question sets to different stakeholder categories, with some questions reportedly tailored to specific sectors.

### Policy and Regulatory Context

The questionnaire's issuance constitutes a form of informal regulatory engagement — a pre-rulemaking information-gathering exercise — rather than a mandatory directive. The ONCD has no independent enforcement authority over the technology sector; its questions are requests for voluntary information. However, the timing alongside the March 2026 cyber strategy and the Mythos fallout suggests the responses will inform at least two downstream policy actions:

1. **AI security policy framework finalization**: The ONCD has stated it is developing a framework intended to embed security into AI systems; industry responses to questions about AI tool adoption and integration are likely to shape that framework's requirements and scope.

2. **Federal procurement and supply chain guidance**: The White House is [separately drafting guidance](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/04/white-house-drafting-plans-permit-federal-anthropic-use/413202/) to allow federal agencies to use Anthropic's tools despite an existing supply chain risk designation. Industry responses about AI model integration and critical infrastructure protection may accelerate or complicate that process.

The [Goodwin Procter client alert](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/alerts-otherindustries-dpc-white-house-makes-cyber-ai-policy-push) — also published on JDSupra as [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/new-technology/1777090/client-alert-the-white-house-makes-a-cyber-and-ai-policy-push) — characterized the administration's broader AI and cybersecurity policy push as emphasizing evolving risks, "light touch" regulation, and greater responsibility for companies to manage their own governance. This framing is consistent with the information-gathering nature of the questionnaire: rather than proposing new mandates, the ONCD is positioning government as a facilitator and convener, seeking to understand what industry needs and what risks it faces before asserting authority.

### Project Glasswing and the Controlled Access Model

The ONCD's questionnaire also fits within the broader governance model pioneered by [Anthropic's Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing) — a controlled consortium through which Mythos Preview access is distributed to vetted organizations including AWS, Apple, Cisco, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, CrowdStrike, and JPMorganChase, backed by up to $100 million in Mythos usage credits ([ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/project-glasswing-anthropic-announces-big-tech-consortium-to-test-claude-mythos-ai-model-that-could-reshape-cybersecurity); [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/anthropic-launch-project-glasswing/)) plus $4 million in direct donations to open-source security organizations. The Glasswing model exemplifies the kind of public-private partnership the ONCD appears to be trying to replicate: government involvement in prioritizing vulnerability remediation while leaving execution to private sector partners.

However, the [White House has reportedly opposed Anthropic's plan](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/04/white-house-pushes-back-against-anthropics-mythos-expansion/) to expand Glasswing from roughly 40 organizations to approximately 110, citing concerns about Mythos's misuse potential and the infrastructure required to support a wider rollout. This tension — between maximizing defensive AI deployment and controlling offensive risk — is precisely the subject of the ONCD's industry questions.

### Congressional Oversight Dimensions

The [Congressional Research Service published an analysis](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12667) of the Trump administration's cyber strategy in March 2026, signaling congressional interest in the strategy's implementation. The Hassan-Ernst Senate letter requesting ONCD disclosures about the AI-executed cyberattack and inter-agency coordination suggests that congressional oversight of AI-cyber policy will intensify regardless of the administration's preferred "light touch" regulatory posture.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

The ONCD questionnaire most directly affects large technology and cybersecurity companies that received the April 23 email. Given the Vance-Bessent call that preceded it, likely recipients include major cloud providers (AWS, Google, Microsoft, Oracle), cybersecurity vendors (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks), and frontier AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI). However, the email's undisclosed recipient list means smaller vendors and sector-specific security companies may also have been targeted.

Companies in critical infrastructure sectors — financial services, healthcare, energy, telecommunications — face secondary exposure if the ONCD's information gathering leads to sector-specific guidance or regulatory requirements. The DHS AI-ISAC initiative, once operational, is likely to expand mandatory information-sharing obligations across critical infrastructure sectors.

### Compliance Considerations

Because the ONCD questionnaire is voluntary, there are no immediate legal compliance requirements triggered by its issuance. However, companies considering whether to respond should weigh several factors:

- **Response privilege and classification**: Questions about critical network inventories and isolation strategies touch on information that could constitute sensitive security information if disclosed to unauthorized parties. Companies should assess whether responses warrant marking under applicable information protection frameworks before submitting.
- **Competitive sensitivity**: Disclosures about AI tool adoption, vulnerability identification timelines, and worst-case compromise scenarios could constitute material non-public information for publicly traded companies. Legal and compliance review of response content is advisable.
- **Policy influence**: Voluntary engagement with the ONCD's framework-development process provides an opportunity to shape AI security policy before it crystallizes into binding requirements. Companies with established AI security capabilities may benefit from participating to demonstrate leadership.

### Enforcement Outlook

The ONCD questionnaire itself does not create enforcement exposure. The downstream risk emerges from the policy instruments likely to follow: the AI security policy framework under development, potential sector-specific CISA guidance, and any future executive orders or rulemakings informed by industry responses. Companies that respond to the questionnaire should retain copies of their submissions as a baseline for future compliance gap analyses.

## Action Items

- Determine whether your organization received the April 23 ONCD email; if so, assess whether a response was submitted and what commitments were made.
- Review your organization's current AI detection and response tool inventory and integration posture against the ONCD's question framework — this maps directly to potential future regulatory disclosure requirements.
- Evaluate whether disclosures of critical network inventories and isolation strategies in government questionnaire responses trigger any existing information-sharing agreements, classification obligations, or SEC material information considerations.
- Monitor the ONCD's AI security policy framework publication, expected in the near term; engage via public comment if a notice-and-comment process is initiated.
- Track the Project Glasswing access expansion dispute between the White House and Anthropic — the outcome will signal how the administration will balance defensive AI deployment against offensive misuse risk across the broader vendor community.
- Assess eligibility and interest in Project Glasswing participation if your organization builds or maintains critical software infrastructure; contact Anthropic's Glasswing team via [anthropic.com/glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing).
