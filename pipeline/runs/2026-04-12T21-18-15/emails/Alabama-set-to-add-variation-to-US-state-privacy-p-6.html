Alabama set to add variation to US state privacy patchwork

Alabama is poised to add to the U.S. state comprehensive privacy law network after House Bill 351 cleared the state legislature 7 April. Recent additions to the patchwork have not strayed much in the way of nuanced provisions, but Alabama will change that trend if enacted by the governor. Coverage thresholds and some notable exemptions are among the areas that could add new compliance wrinkles. IAPP News Editor Joe Duball unpacks the contents of the bill with reaction from the bill's sponsor, state Rep. Mike Shaw, R-Ala.

Maine's comprehensive privacy bill fails on final House vote

The Maine Legislature will not finalize comprehensive privacy legislation before the end of its current legislative session 15 April, the Maine Morning Star reports. The House voted against concurrence on Legislative Document 1822, the Maine Online Data Privacy Act, for the second time in a week, ultimately ending the bill's consideration during the current session. The House and Senate had been debating a controversial political organizations exemption before local industry groups made a final lobbying push to sway a majority of House lawmakers against the bill.

US appeals court holds BIPA amendment can be applied to pending cases

According to Hunton Andrews Kurth, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 7th Circuit upheld a lower court's ruling affirming the 2024 amendment to the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act can be applied retroactively to pending cases. BENCHMARKING & RESEARCH
Stanford's HAI releases paper examining privacy risks created by foundational AI models

The Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence published a research paper exploring the privacy risks foundational AI models pose and the necessary governance mechanisms needed to address them. According to the paper's brief, foundation models present "unprecedented and largely unaddressed privacy risks" beyond what traditional AI systems see throughout a model's life cycle, including mass scraping of personally identifiable information during training, to producing sensitive personal information in its outputs and what users disclose to a foundation model's chatbots.

Survey finds 82% of US AI users are concerned about potential misuse of the technology

Deloitte's 2025 Connected Consumer Survey found 82% of U.S. consumers who use AI tools are concerned about how the technology could be misused, with 74% noting the technology could impact individuals' critical thinking skills, The Wall Street Journal reports. The survey also found an increase in consumer concerns surrounding data protection, with 47% of survey respondents experiencing a data security incident in the last year, with 58% experiencing "phishing, fake profiles, chatbots impersonating real people, deepfake videos, ransomware or malware attacks, and voice cloning."

'Transparency Hub' aims to make social media policy documents more accessible

Harvard's Berkman Klein Center for Internet & Society created the open-access "Transparency Hub" that aims to help the public and researchers easily find and review social media companies' privacy notices, terms of service, community standards and other platform policy documents. The Transparency Hub also has an embedded tool that allows users to review how companies' policy documents have changed over time. SURVEILLANCE
PCLOB issues 2026 FISA Section 702 report

The U.S. Privacy and Civil Liberties Oversight Board released the 2026 "Report on the Surveillance Program Operated Pursuant to Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act." The board said Section 702 "remains one of the country's most valuable tools for countering an array of critical foreign threats" and 2023 reforms to the program "are having positive privacy and civil liberties effects." The report also indicates intelligence agencies are operating at 99.8% compliance rates under law and court-approved procedures. Section 702 expires 20 April if Congress does not reauthorize the program.
