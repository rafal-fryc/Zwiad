Alabama set to add variation to US state privacy patchwork

Alabama is poised to add to the U.S. state comprehensive privacy law network after House Bill 351 cleared the state legislature 7 April. Recent additions to the patchwork have not strayed much in the way of nuanced provisions, but Alabama will change that trend if enacted by the governor. Coverage thresholds and some notable exemptions are among the areas that could add new compliance wrinkles. IAPP News Editor Joe Duball unpacks the contents of the bill with reaction from the bill's sponsor, state Rep. Mike Shaw, R-Ala.

Global push for 'digital sovereignty' risks complicating global data flows, innovation

Conversations around countries' pursuit of some form of a "digital sovereignty" agenda reverberated throughout the IAPP Global Summit 2026 in Washington, D.C. On one side of the debate are efforts to break clean of U.S. technology reliance in pursuit of domestic tech production through significant data localization measures. The other side still envisions an interconnected world of free-flowing data, albeit in a modified environment that maintains respect for data subjects' privacy. IAPP Staff Writer Alex LaCasse has the details.

CYBERSECURITY—AUSTRALIA

Australian Signals Directorate publishes frontier AI model cybersecurity guidance

The Australian Signals Directorate issued guidance on how frontier AI models impact cybersecurity and how they can be used to remediate vulnerabilities in IT systems. The ASD recommended organizations establish a cybersecurity baseline that aligns with its Information Security Manual and pursue mitigation strategies that include reducing "attack paths and attack surfaces," patching vulnerabilities daily, and developing "layered security."

BENCHMARKING & RESEARCH

Stanford's HAI releases paper examining privacy risks created by foundational AI models

The Stanford Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence published a research paper exploring the privacy risks foundational AI models pose and the necessary governance mechanisms needed to address them. According to the paper's brief, foundation models present "unprecedented and largely unaddressed privacy risks" beyond what traditional AI systems see throughout a model's life cycle, including mass scraping of personally identifiable information during training, to producing sensitive personal information in its outputs and what users disclose to a foundation model's chatbots.

PROGRAM MANAGEMENT—U.S.

Survey finds US state CPOs are often inadequately resourced

The National Association of State Chief Information Officers issued its fourth report examining the work of U.S. state chief privacy officers, GovTech reports. The survey found 64% of state CPOs lacked funding for privacy initiatives, 54% of CPOs are faced with a lack of understanding and/or confusion among their employees and 50% of CPOs lack proper authority to execute state government privacy initiatives.

ENFORCEMENT—U.S.

Man pleads guilty to violating TAKE IT DOWN Act 

A man pleaded guilty to violating the TAKE IT DOWN Act by allegedly creating AI-generated nonconsensual sexual images to threaten individuals. U.S. Attorney Dominick Gerace II said the defendant, James Strahler II, would be the first individual convicted of violating the act, noting law enforcement "will not tolerate the abhorrent practice of posting and publicizing AI-generated intimate images of real individuals without consent. And we are committed to using every tool at our disposal to hold accountable offenders ... who seek to intimidate and harass others by creating and circulating this disturbing content."

ENFORCEMENT—EU

EDPB highlights EU's digital regulatory growth in annual report

The European Data Protection Board released its 2025 annual report detailing changes in the regulatory landscape and highlighting the EDPB's efforts to strengthen cross-border cooperation, provide organizations with resources to aid compliance and support data protection authorities' enforcement efforts. EDPB Chair Anu Talus noted, the EDPB "prioritised meaningful dialogue with stakeholders to ensure our work reflected real-world needs. Our achievements support economic growth while continuing to protect individuals' fundamental rights to privacy and data protection."

REGULATORY GUIDANCE—U.S.

US HHS releases guidance on HIPAA security rule

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services released guidance detailing the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act Security Rule's Risk Management obligations to protect patient privacy when using digital records. The HHS said organizations and health care providers must ensure they assess overall data security and mitigate potential risks and data protection challenges due to systems' processing of sensitive health information.

DIGITAL GOVERNANCE—NIGERIA

Op-ed: Nigeria's digital growth highlights data protection challenges

In an op-ed for Tech Policy Press, The OpEd Project Public Voices Fellow Timi Olagunju noted Nigeria's digital growth, particularly the Independent National Electoral Commission's efforts to digitize voting information has raised concerns over the vast collection of sensitive personal data. Olagunju said Nigeria's "major parties are digitizing politics without fully accepting the duties that come with digital data collection. And if they are failing at this visible and elementary first step, what else might they be failing to do with the data of millions of Nigerians at home and abroad?"
