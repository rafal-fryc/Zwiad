LAW & REGULATION—JAPAN

Japan's executive body approves easing data protection law for AI innovation

The Cabinet of Japan approved a bill amending the Act on the Protection of Personal Information to support AI innovation, The Mainichi reports. Approved changes to the law would allow data to be collected and used without consent for AI development where data is pseudonymized. The bill also empowers the Personal Information Protection Commission to issue fines for violations where data of more than 1,000 people is processed. The reforms seek to complement Japan's AI Basic Law.

LAW & REGULATION—SINGAPORE

Singapore's IMDA launches informational hub for Code of Practice for Online Safety

Singapore's Infocomm Media Development Authority created a dashboard containing information on the country's new Code of Practice for Online Safety, which entered into force 1 April. The code requires designated app stores to implement age-assurance measures that can determine if a user is younger than age 18, and restrict those users from downloading age-inappropriate apps.

LAW & REGULATION—U.S.

US appeals court holds BIPA amendment can be applied to pending cases

According to Hunton Andrews Kurth, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 7th Circuit upheld a lower court's ruling affirming the 2024 amendment to the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act can be applied retroactively to pending cases. The law was modified to reflect that damages for BIPA violations can no longer be assessed on a per-scan basis. Editor's note: IAPP News Editor Joe Duball wrote about the impacts of BIPA and the potential for similar frameworks to be raised in other states.

CHILDREN'S ONLINE SAFETY—EU

Big Tech coalition critical of EU institutions allowing ePrivacy CSAM derogation to expire

Google, Meta, Microsoft and Snapchat criticized EU lawmakers for not renewing the ePrivacy Directive derogation allowing technology to automatically scan for child sexual abuse material on platforms. The companies claimed the lack of an extension to interim measures "clouds the legal certainty that has helped responsible platforms try to protect our communities, safeguard child victims, and preserve the integrity of our services." They called on EU institutions to wrap up negotiations for creating a new framework, while committing to continue to voluntary action to detect CSAM.
Full story

IDENTITY & VERIFICATION—SPAIN

AEPD explores considerations for establishing an individual's digital identity

Lorenzo Cotino, the president of Spain’s data protection authority, the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos, discussed how an individual's digital ID is "understood no longer as a mere authentication tool, but as the individual's projection into digital environments defined by data, profiles, reputations, inferences and other forms of representation." Cotino provided an overview of problems individuals face when attempting to secure their digital identity, as well as several recommendations for establishing a digital ID framework.

IDENTITY & VERIFICATION—EU

ENISA to hold public consultation on EU Digital Wallet proposal

The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity launched a public consultation on the draft EU Digital Wallet. The consultation aims to solicit feedback from specific components of the European Digital Identity Wallet proposal, including its annexes. ENISA framed digital wallets as a "pivotal step towards a seamless and secure identification in both the physical and digital world" that enhance personal data protection. The deadline to submit comments to ENISA is 30 April.

OPINION

Op-ed: Understanding the White House's approach to tech regulation

In an op-ed for the Financial Times, Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence at Stanford University International Policy Fellow Marietje Schaake argued the digital policy stances held by U.S. President Donald Trump's administration "are more radical than anything (she has) ever witnessed in the EU," when comparing it to the EU's regulatory approach for Big Tech. She referenced examples including the executive order banning TikTok in the U.S. and the administration taking a 10% stake in Intel to compel the company to ramp up domestic production of advanced AI chips.

CHILDREN'S ONLINE SAFETY—U.K.

ICO establishes campaign to support children's privacy education

The U.K. Information Commissioner's Office launched its "Switched on to privacy" campaign, which aims to support parents with children ages 4-11 and help them educate their children about online privacy. According to ICO research, 24% of children in that age range have shared their full name or address online, 22% have shared personal information with AI chatbots and 35% of parents believe their child would share personal information in exchange for game tokens or rewards.
