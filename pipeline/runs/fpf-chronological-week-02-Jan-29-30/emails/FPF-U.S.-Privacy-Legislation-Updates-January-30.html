Stuff from the States 

 

Alabama - ASAA Bill Passes Committee (HB 219): This fast-moving bill was reported out of the Senate Committee on Children and Youth Health on January 28, advancing the bill to the Senate floor for further consideration.

 

California: 

  • AG “Surveillance Pricing” Probe: Attorney General Bonta announced an investigative sweep focused on companies’ use of personal information to set individualized prices for consumers, termed “surveillance pricing.” The announcement states that such practices may not be consistent with consumers’ reasonable expectations and therefore in violation of the purpose limitation requirements in the CCPA regulations. Industries singled out include the retail, grocery, and hotel sectors. The AG is sending letters requesting information on how businesses may be using shopping and browsing history, location, demographics, inferences, and other data to set prices. 
  • CCPA Deletion Right Amendment Introduced (SB 923): This bill would expand the CCPA's deletion right to cover personal information the business did not collect directly from a consumer, consistent with other state privacy laws. This bill would also require a business that operates exclusively online to offer an online method (e.g., web form) for consumers to submit rights requests, in addition to an email address.
  • Prohibition on Selling Public Servants’ PI Passes Appropriations (AB 883): Asm. Lowenthal’s data broker bill—which would prohibit data brokers from selling the protected information of elected officials, judges, and their immediate family members—was reported out of the Appropriations committee. The bill allows for claims by elected officials and judges but it provides only for declaratory relief, injunctive relief, reasonable attorney’s fees, and actual damages—no statutory damages.

 

Hawaii - Companion Chatbot Bills Introduced (SB 2788) (HB 1782): Two recently introduced bills focusing on companion chatbots and youth safety would impose substantive data minimization requirements on providers of a conversational AI service or AI companion system, including prohibiting: collecting, using, or retaining a minor's personal data beyond what is "reasonably necessary" to operate the AI companion system and to ensure safety and security; using minors' personal data for targeted advertising or profiling minors for engagement optimization or behavioral manipulation; or collecting or processing minors' sensitive data unless "necessary for system safety or accessibility and subject to heightened data minimization and security safeguards." 

 

Kansas - ASAA Bill Introduced (SB 372): Introduced by the Senate Judiciary Committee on January 22, this bill would establish an App Store Accountability Act-style age assurance framework with a few divergences, including requirements for age verifying existing accounts, parental consent obligations for pre-installed apps, and the absence of application exemptions (a factor in the preliminary injunction analysis against Texas’ law).

 

New Jersey - AADC Introduced (A. 1358): As New Jersey’s new two-year legislative session begins, Asm. Park (D) introduced an AADC framework that mirrors other states’ approaches while incorporating distinct features. Most notably, this AADC would only impose requirements on social media platforms, as defined, rather than the broader range of online services, products, and features scoped within other frameworks. Additionally, this bill would employ a “likely to be accessed by children” standard, require DPIAs assessing risk of “material detriment” to children (under 18), require platforms to conduct age estimation or apply child protections to all users on the platform by default, and obligate platforms to implement high default privacy settings for children. 

 

Oregon - Data Broker & Law Enforcement Bill Introduced (SB 1587): Sen. Campos (D) prefiled a bill that would prohibit data brokers from providing brokered personal data to any person or entity if the data broker has reason to believe that the person will use the data (directly or indirectly) for purpose related to enforcement of civil law.

 

Rhode Island - Consumer Health Bill Introduced (HB 7357): Similar to a bill that was held for study last year, Rep. Knight (D) has introduced a broad, My Health My Data–style consumer health bill that would require both consent and a permissible purpose for collecting and processing gender-affirming care information and reproductive or sexual health information.

 

Tennessee - Mass-Deletion Amendment Introduced (SB 2100): This bill would require a controller to register annually with the state. The state would be required to establish a webpage with an "accessible deletion mechanism" that allows a consumer or the consumer's authorized agent to submit an authenticated consumer data deletion request to every registered controller, subject to the law's existing requirements for handling deletion requests. Although clearly inspired by the California Delete Act, this bill is much broader in scope in that it applies to controllers rather than data brokers and therefore implicates first-party data.

 

Utah - Vehicle Privacy Amendment Introduced (HB 357): Similar to an amendment enacted in Oregon last year, this bill would amend the Utah Consumer Privacy Act to apply to any motor vehicle manufacturer who manufactures motor vehicles that are sold or leased in the state and collects, transmits, or stores personal data through a vehicle data collection system. This bill would go further by adding requirements (effective 2029) for manufacturers to add privacy controls in the vehicle allowing a consumer to view the categories of personal data the vehicle data collection system collects, view the categories of third parties with whom the motor vehicle manufacturer shares personal data, opt out of the sale of personal data or processing for targeted advertising, and delete readily accessible data. The bill includes exceptions for product improvement and safety. 

 

Vermont - “Duty of Data Loyalty” Bill Introduced (H.812): Rep. Priestley (D) introduced a unique comprehensive privacy bill largely, largely based on the ADPPA, that would prohibit a covered entity (i.e., controller) from collecting, processing, or transferring covered data in a way that conflicts with the best interests of trusting parties. The bill would prohibit cross-context behavioral advertising.

 

Washington - Data-Driven Pricing Bill Passes Committee (HB 2481): The House Committee on Technology, Economic Development, & Veterans voted “do pass” on a that would prohibit both “surveillance-based price discrimination” as well as “surge pricing,” subject to exceptions (e.g., uniformly applicable discounts).  

 

West Virginia - Financial Privacy Bill Introduced (HB 4868): Similar to a bill from last session, Del. Hite (R) introduced a bill that would give consumers who apply for credit (from financial institutions, creditors utilizing a credit reporting agency, or private businesses) the right to opt-in to each disclosure of their personal financial information.

 

Wisconsin - Comprehensive Privacy Bills Progressing (AB 172) (SB 166); AADC Forthcoming (LRB-5458/1): After making little progress in 2025, Wisconsin is now progressing two Connecticut-style comprehensive privacy bills. The Assembly bill had an executive session this week after a hearing last week. The Senate bill had an initial hearing this week. Unfortunately, there is no livestreaming of hearings or votes at the moment due to lapsed funding for WisEye, so we cannot report on the content of the hearings. Additionally, lawmakers have introduced an Age-Appropriate Design Code (text not yet available). 

 

Federal Happenings 

 

FTC Age Assurance Workshop: On Data Privacy Day, January 28, the FTC hosted a workshop on age assurance technologies. Chair Ferguson made notable opening remarks that emphasized the importance of companies adopting age assurance technologies, particularly to make COPPA compliance easier and more cost-effective, rather than finding "innovative ways of breaking the law." He also appeared to indicate that he may aim to address potential conflict between the use of age assurance and COPPA compliance through a non-enforcement policy or updated COPPA rulemaking. The workshop also featured a variety of speakers, including FPF Senior Fellow Jim Siegl. During the workshop FPF debuted its updated “Unpacking Age Assurance” infographic, which reflects significant technical and policy developments, such as including a fourth category of assurance, inference, and new risk management approaches. 

 

SCOTUS Takes up VPPA Circuit-Split: On January 26, the Supreme Court granted cert in Salazar v. Paramount Global for next term, setting the Court up to potentially resolve a circuit split on the scope of who is a “consumer” under the Video Privacy Protection Act (VPPA). The VPPA prohibits a “video tape service provider” from “knowingly disclos[ing], to any person, personally identifiable information concerning any consumer of such provider,” subject to exceptions (including written, informed consent). The plaintiff alleged that a website disclosed his PII to Facebook via a tracking pixel without his consent in violation of the law. The Sixth Circuit ultimately held that the company operating the website is a “video tape service provider” but that the plaintiff is not a consumer under the law because he was subscribed to the website’s newsletter and not a subscriber to “‘goods or services’ in the nature of ‘video cassette tapes or similar audio visual materials’”—creating a split with the Second Circuit.

 

What We’re Reading 

 

New Location Data Model Bill from Consumer Reports: Location data privacy remains an area of focus for lawmakers. Last year, Oregon amended its comprehensive privacy law to ban controllers from selling precise geolocation data. Similar bills continue to appear this year, alongside more robust location privacy frameworks. Adding to the conversation, advocacy group Consumer Reports this week released a model geolocation data privacy bill. Prior model bills from Consumer Reports have been introduced in various states, so it is likely that this model will be introduced by some lawmakers.
