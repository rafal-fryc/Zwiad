Welcome back to your weekly roundup of AI legislation!

 

Housekeeping 

 

💻 February Community Call: Our next U.S. Legislation Community Call will be Thursday, February 19 from 3 pm to 4 pm ET. For suggestions for future call topics, please reach out to jfrancis@fpf.org. 

 

Highlights 

  • A very busy week: 45 new state AI bills were introduced, with 7 additional bills advancing out of committee (Iowa’s HSB 611, Utah’s HB 286, Virginia’s SB 269, and Washington’s HB 1170 and HB 2225) or their first chamber (California’s SB 300 and SB 813).
  • Chatbots remain the dominant theme, with new or advancing bills across 11 states, California, Hawaii, Iowa, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Utah, Vermont,  Virginia, Washington, and in Congress.
  • Frontier model regulation expands further, with Tennessee (HB 1898) joining Nebraska and Utah in introducing SB 53-style catastrophic risk and child safety frameworks. Utah’s HB 286 also advanced out of committee this week.
  • Six Illinois HB 1806-style bills introduced or advanced in California, Kentucky, Mississippi, Rhode Island, Virginia, and West Virginia.

 

Stuff from the States

 

Alabama -

  • Chatbots - Two new chatbot bills were introduced (HB 324 and HB 325).

 

California - 

  • Chatbots - SB 300 (a clean up bill for SB 243) passed the Senate (38-Y 0-N) and heads to the Assembly.
  • AI Safety - SB 813 (two-year bill, establishing independent verification orgs for AI safety standards) passed committee and subsequently passed the Senate (31-Y 7-N).
  • Health - SB 903: Introduced by Sen. Padilla (D), this bill is modeled on Illinois’ HB 1806. 

 

Hawaii - 

  • Chatbots - Two new chatbot bills introduced (SB 2788 on companion chatbots and SB 3001 on mental health chatbots).
  • ADMT/High-Risk AI - SB 2967: Introduced by Sen. McKelvey (D), this bill would establish consumer protections for the use of AI in consequential decisions such as housing, credit, and employment. Deployers would be required to maintain documentation on data used to make decisions, and make that available to consumers. Deployers would also implement a risk management program. 
  • AI Safety - SB 2923: Introduced by Sen. Gabbard (D), this bill would create the “Office of Artificial Intelligence Safety and Regulation” and would mandate that any AI product must provide a proof of safety to the office and implement a risk-based approach to protect public welfare. 
  • Data-driven pricing- HB 2136: Introduced by Sen. Lee (D), this bill would prohibit businesses from using surveillance data to set consumer-specific prices, discounts, or fees, or to steer consumers toward higher-priced offers, with limited exceptions for objective cost differences and opt-in loyalty programs. 
  • Transparency - HB 2357: Introduced by Rep. Ilagan (D), this bill would require that all “tangible products” created with the use of artificial intelligence must disclose that AI was used to make that product. Additionally, the bill would ban all streaming of music that is “performed or attributed to an artificial intelligence music artist.”

 

Illinois - Data Driven Pricing - HB 4544: Introduced by Rep. Delgado (D), this bill would require that consumers be notified anytime they are paying a price that was algorithmically determined based on their personal data. Use of data related to protected classes would be prohibited if it leads to denying or withholding goods/services or price differences. 

 

Iowa - Chatbots - HSB 611: Introduced by the House Committee on Health and Human Services and passed out of its committee, this bill would prohibit chatbots from encouraging/aiding suicide or harm, mandate disclosures of their non-human status every 30 minutes, and prohibit chatbots from representing themselves as licensed professionals. 

 

Kentucky - Health - HB 455: Introduced by Rep. Banta (R), this bill copies Illinois’ HB 1806.

 

Louisiana - Health - SB 5: Introduced by Sen. Hensgens (R), this bill copies Utah’s HB 452. (mental health chatbots & advertising).

 

Maryland - Data Driven Pricing - SB 0387: Introduced by a group of 11 state Senators (D), this bill would prohibit food retailers from utilizing dynamic pricing or consumer surveillance data to set prices for goods and services. Food retailers would also be prohibited from using a protected class to offer, advertise, or sell goods or services. 

 

Mississippi - 

  • Health - HB 1720: Introduced by Rep. Yancey (R), this bill copies Illinois HB 1806. 
  • Miscellaneous - HB 1723: Introduced by Rep. Ford (R), this bill would set a statutory definition for AI based on the OECD definition.

 

New Mexico - 

  • Gen AI - HB 141: Introduced by Rep. Serrato (D), this bill would establish comprehensive requirements for disclosure and accountability for the use of genAI systems. “Covered providers” are required to embed latent provenance data that can be used to identify synthetic content. Online platforms would also be mandated to respond to takedown requests of deepfakes within 72 hours. 
  • Chatbots - HB 174: Introduced by Rep. Romero (D), this bill would establish safety, transparency, and liability standards for companion AI products. Additionally, operators would be prohibited from using variable reinforcement to maximize user engagement, generate unsolicited emotionally manipulative messages, or misrepresent the product’s identity or capabilities, unless these features are specifically enabled by an adult user.

 

New York -

  • Employment - Four new employment-related bills were introduced by both Republicans and Democrats (S 9028, A 9641, A 9638, and A 9601).
  • ADMT/High-Risk AI - A 9654: Introduced by Asm. Solages (D), this bill would establish protections from the use of algorithms in consequential decisions including employment, banking, healthcare, and government services and would prohibit developers and deployers from discriminating based on protected characteristics. Third party evaluations would be mandatory. It would offer individuals the right to appeal algorithmic based decisions to a human. 
  • Chatbots - S 9051: Introduced by Sen. Gonzalez (D), this bill would prohibit providing unsafe chatbot features to minors including: simulating companionship, facilitating self-harm, encouraging secrecy/isolation, and generating sexually explicit conduct. 

 

Rhode Island -

  • Chatbots - SB 2195: Introduced by Sen. Urso (D), this bill would mandate safety protocols addressing suicidal ideation and other potential harms as well as notifications that the chatbot does not have human emotions. 
  • Health - SB 2197: Introduced by Sen. Urso (D), this is an Illinois’ HB 1806 style bill.

 

Tennessee -

  • Data Driven Pricing - HB 2052/ SB 1998: Introduced by Rep. Camper (D), this bill would require certain food establishments to use non-digital price tags and prohibits food retailers from using personalized algorithmic pricing. 
  • Liability/ Health - HB 1951: Introduced by Rep. Williams (R), this bill would create a new offense called “coercive suicide.” The owner of an AI system that encourages or advises on committing suicide would be guilty of this offense. 
  • Frontier Models/ Chatbots - HB 1898: Introduced by Rep. Zachary (R),  this bill would combine California SB 53-style definitions of frontier models and catastrophic risk with child safety and chatbot-related requirements. Comparable legislation was recently introduced in Nebraska (LB 1083) and Utah (HB 286).
  • Chatbots - HB 1946: Introduced by Rep. Love (D), this bill would require safety protocols, regular disclosures of chatbot’s non-human status, prohibits use of minor’s data without parental consent, and mandates additional disclosures when the chatbot is giving legal, medical, or financial advice. 

 

South Dakota - Health - HB 1144: Introduced by Rep. Rehfeldt (R), this is an Illinois’ HB 1806-style bill.

 

Utah - 

  • Frontier Models/Chatbots - HB 286 (combines California SB 53-style definitions with child safety plan requirements) passed out of committee with amendments to create a fund for penalties and settlements obtained in civil actions under the law.
  • Sandbox - HB 320: Introduced by Rep. Cutler (R), among other things, this bill would create “regulatory mitigation agreements,” where providers of AI systems in Utah can provide those systems even if existing law would impede doing so as long as certain conditions and safeguards are met. 

 

Vermont - 

  • Chatbots - Three new chatbot bills introduced (H 804, H 784, and H 783).
  • Liability - H 792: Introduced by Rep. Priestley (D), this bill would establish liability standards and safe harbor provisions for developers and deployers of AI systems. 

 

Virginia - 

  • Health - SB 269 (Illinois’ HB 1806-style bill) passed out of committee with amendments that expand the bill’s scope by allowing a mental health service provider to “use an artificial intelligence system to assist in providing therapy or counseling services,” replacing the introduced version’s narrower allowance for only “administrative support.” The amendments also narrow the definition of “therapeutic communication,” replace a ban on direct client interaction with a requirement for “provider oversight,” and remove the prohibition on detecting emotions or mental states.
  • Chatbots - SB 796: Introduced by Sen. Durant (R), this bill would create a duty of loyalty for chatbot providers, which would require systems to detect and mitigate mental health issues. Explicit content also requires age verification. If imminent death or serious injury is detected, the platform has 24 hours to notify emergency services. 
  • Employment - Two new employment-related bills introduced (HB 1514 and HB 1451).

 

Washington - 

  • Data Driven Pricing - SB 6312: Introduced by Sen. Saldaña (D), this bill prohibits “surveillance based price discrimination and surge pricing” for grocery items. It would also impose a four year moratorium on electronic label systems in grocery stores (digital price tags). 
  • ADMT/High-Risk AI / Transparency - HB 2667: Introduced by Rep. Shavers (D), this bill creates a framework to protect consumers from algorithmic discrimination, mandates disclosures and risk management by deployers, and expands the state AI task force. 
  • GenAI / Transparency - HB 1170 (second year bill, provenance disclosure) passed through committee with a substitute that incorporates an SB 53 style definition for “covered provider.”
  • Chatbots - HB 2225 passed out of committee with a substitute that clarifies when an operator “knows” a user is a minor and adds more detailed compliance requirements.

 

West Virginia - Health - HB 4770: Introduced by Del. Worrell (R), this is an Illinois’ HB 1806-style bill.

 

Federal

 

Chatbots - HR 7218: Introduced by Rep. Lawler (R) (NY), this bill would require age verification for chatbot providers as well as specific protections for minors (no text available yet).
