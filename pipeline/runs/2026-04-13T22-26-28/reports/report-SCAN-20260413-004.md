# FISA Section 702 Renewal Deadline Looms as Congress Debates Reauthorization

## Overview [HIGH confidence]

Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) is scheduled to sunset on **April 20, 2026**, absent congressional reauthorization. The authority, last renewed via the [Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act (RISAA) in April 2024](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48592), permits the FBI, NSA, CIA, and NCTC to compel U.S. electronic communications service providers to assist in the warrantless collection of communications of non-U.S. persons reasonably believed to be located outside the United States. With days remaining before the deadline, the Trump administration is lobbying for a "clean" 18-month reauthorization, while a cross-ideological coalition of lawmakers demands reforms — most prominently a warrant requirement for U.S.-person queries. Communications carriers have privately warned the Intelligence Community they will stop collecting under Section 702 directives if the statute lapses.

## Background on Section 702 [HIGH confidence]

Section 702, codified at 50 U.S.C. § 1881a, was enacted as part of the FISA Amendments Act of 2008 and has been reauthorized in 2012, 2018, and most recently in April 2024. Unlike traditional FISA, which requires individualized Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court (FISC) orders based on probable cause, Section 702 permits the Attorney General and Director of National Intelligence to jointly authorize annual "certifications" targeting categories of foreign intelligence information. Under these certifications, the government issues directives to U.S. providers to acquire communications of specific non-U.S. person "selectors" (e.g., email addresses, phone numbers) belonging to targets abroad.

Because targets communicate with U.S. persons, Americans' communications are routinely swept in as "incidental collection." U.S.-person identifiers are minimized under court-approved procedures but remain queryable by FBI and NSA analysts — the so-called "backdoor search" that drives most of the civil-liberties controversy. The 2024 RISAA imposed new limits on FBI U.S.-person queries, expanded FISC oversight, and included a controversial expansion of the "electronic communication service provider" definition.

On April 11, 2026, the FISC renewed the annual certifications and targeting/minimization procedures, meaning collection under existing directives can legally continue through the certification period even as the statutory authority approaches sunset — but new directives cannot be issued once Section 702 lapses.

## Current Debate [HIGH confidence]

### The Trump administration's position
White House senior adviser Stephen Miller and CIA Director John Ratcliffe are leading an eleventh-hour push for a **clean 18-month extension** without substantive reforms. President Trump — who in 2024 posted "KILL FISA" on Truth Social during the RISAA debate — publicly reversed course in March 2026, endorsing reauthorization. Administration officials argue that ongoing tensions with Iran and the threat environment make any lapse unacceptable.

### Republican fractures
House Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan (R-OH) and House Intelligence Chair Rick Crawford (R-AR) have been shuttling between GOP factions. Rep. Andy Biggs (R-AZ) and a bloc of Freedom Caucus members insist on a **warrant requirement for U.S.-person queries** as the price of their votes. During the 2024 cycle, a warrant-requirement amendment failed on a 212–212 tie; proponents believe they are closer this time.

### Democratic concerns
Democratic support has eroded. Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), who voted for RISAA in 2024, has circulated a Dear Colleague letter opposing clean reauthorization, arguing that "the safeguards put in place in 2024 have been badly eroded by the Trump Administration." In the Senate, Judiciary Ranking Member Dick Durbin (D-IL) has re-introduced the bipartisan **Security and Freedom Enhancement (SAFE) Act** with Sen. Mike Lee (R-UT), which would reauthorize Section 702 with a warrant requirement for queries of Americans' communications absent narrow exceptions.

### National security establishment
Former national security officials have sent a public letter urging immediate reauthorization, warning that "we cannot afford to let our Intelligence Community lose this tool that helps keep our nation safe, even for a day." Brookings analysis describes the reauthorization path as "unclear" and flags operational degradation if providers halt compliance.

### Civil liberties coalition
EPIC's Reform or Sunset campaign, the ACLU, Demand Progress, FreedomWorks, and the Brennan Center argue that post-RISAA compliance problems — including documented U.S.-person query abuses — justify, at minimum, a warrant requirement, narrowing of the provider definition, and stronger FISC amicus authority.

## Compliance and Business Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

**Electronic communication service providers (ECSPs):** U.S. telecoms, cloud providers, and (post-RISAA) a broader category of service providers subject to Section 702 directives face near-term operational uncertainty. Carriers have signaled to the IC that they will **cease collection** if the statute lapses. Providers should:
- Confirm standing legal positions on directive compliance in the event of a lapse;
- Coordinate with counsel on whether existing FISC-renewed certifications provide coverage for ongoing directives during a statutory gap;
- Prepare communications plans for government and customer inquiries.

**Enterprises relying on threat intelligence:** Section 702 feeds significant cyber-threat intelligence shared with private sector critical-infrastructure partners via CISA and sector ISACs. A lapse — even a brief one — would likely reduce indicator sharing on foreign cyber actors.

**Privacy and compliance teams:** If a reform-laden reauthorization passes, expect new query logging and reporting obligations. If a clean reauthorization passes, expect continued litigation over the expanded ECSP definition and renewed focus on minimization procedures.

**Litigation risk:** Any lapse-and-revive scenario would generate significant Fourth Amendment and APA litigation over collection during and immediately after the gap.

## Timeline [HIGH confidence]

- **2008** — FISA Amendments Act enacts Section 702
- **2012, 2018** — Prior reauthorizations
- **April 20, 2024** — RISAA reauthorizes Section 702 for two years with reforms
- **February 2026** — Classified House hearing on Section 702 renewal
- **March 27, 2026** — President Trump publicly backs reauthorization
- **April 11, 2026** — FISC renews Section 702 certifications/procedures
- **Week of April 13, 2026** — House floor vote expected on clean 18-month extension; outcome uncertain
- **April 20, 2026** — Statutory sunset; absent reauthorization, no new directives may issue
