Highlights 

It’s another busy legislative week, with 30 new state AI bills introduced across the country.

  • Chatbots still lead the pack: 11 new chatbot bills were introduced this week in Arizona (HB 2311) California, (SB 867, SB 300), Iowa (SSB 3011), Nebraska (LB 939), New York (S 8874), Oklahoma (SB 1521, HB 3544), and Virginia (HB 758, HB 669, HB 635). The Kentucky AG also filed a lawsuit against Character.AI for child-related safety and privacy harms under the state’s consumer protection and data privacy law.
  • Mental health + AI: Legislators continued to focus attention on AI in mental health therapy, introducing 6 bills on the topic in New Jersey (S 735), South Carolina (SB 0788), Tennessee (SB 1580), Vermont (H 644/ S 241), and Virginia (HB 669, HB 668/ SB 269).
  • Virginia kicks off: Virginia opened its legislative session this week, paired with its bill introduction deadline, resulting in 10 new AI bills: HB 758 (chatbots), HB 669 (health/ chatbots), HB 635 (chatbots), HB 668/ SB 269 (health), HB 580 (government use), HB 713 (transparency), HB 797 (transparency), HB 999 (ADMT/ High-Risk AI), HB 1250 (government use), and SB 245 (ADMT/ High-Risk AI / Transparency).
  • Federal activity continues: At the federal level, a new memo was released by the AG’s office regarding the AI Litigation Task Force and Congress held several hearings on AI.

 

Stuff from the States

 

Arizona - Chatbots - HB 2311: Introduced by Rep. Rivero (R), this bill would require operators of “conversational AI services,” to provide  disclosures for minor users, and prohibit them from generating statements to minor account holders that simulate emotional dependence and romantic innuendos, among other requirements.

 

California-

  • Chatbots - SB 867: Introduced by Sen. Padilla (D), this bill would prohibit the  manufacture, sale, exchange, or possession with intent to sell any toy that contains a companion chatbot.
  • Chatbots - SB 300: Introduced by Sen. Padilla (D), this bill would expand last year’s companion chatbot law, SB 243. It would revise the standard triggering operator obligations from when an operator “knows a user is a minor” to an “actual knowledge” standard. The bill would also remove language requiring only “reasonable measures” to prevent the exchange of sexually explicit content, instead requiring operators to simply “prevent” such exchanges. The bill passed in the Senate Judiciary Committee (13-Y 0-N) on 1/13.
  • California: Chatbot Ballot Initiative Merged: OpenAI and Common Sense Media have negotiated to merge two competing ballot initiatives on companion chatbots, set to appear on California’s November 2026 ballot. With negotiations, the entities agreed to introduce the “Parents & Kids Safe AI Act,” which would, among other requirements:
    • Require chatbot developers to estimate users’ age ranges and apply enhanced protections for users under 18;
    • Mandate independent child-safety audits, with reporting to the AG;
    • Prohibit child-targeted advertising and the sale or sharing of minors’ data without parental consent;
    • Restrict emotionally manipulative practices, including encouraging isolation, simulating romantic relationships with minors, or claiming sentience.

Check out FPF’s recent blog on CA’s SB 243 for more information on companion chatbot legislation in California

 

Florida-

  • Data Centers - HB 1517: Introduced by Rep. Joseph (D), the “Data Center Transparency Act” would require data centers to disclose specified information, like energy management plans and greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Governor DeSantis Advocates for AI Bill of Rights in State of the State Address: Governor DeSantis (R) used his final State of the State address to urge Florida lawmakers to enact new consumer protections for AI. He pointed to his recently introduced AI Bill of Rights, which would require notice for chatbots and prohibit entities from providing “licensed” therapy or mental health counseling through AI, amongst other requirements. 

 

Iowa- Chatbots - SSB 3011: Introduced by the Committee on Technology, this bill would set disclosure requirements for chatbots (not minor specific). Among the requirements, the bill would prohibit chatbots from encouraging violence/self harm, setting disclosure requirements every 30 minutes, and prohibiting representation as a medical, legal, financial, or psychological services professional.

 

Kentucky - AG Sues AI Chatbot Company Over Child Safety: Kentucky Attorney General Russell Coleman announced the filing of a lawsuit against Character.AI, and its founders, marking the first state enforcement action against an AI chatbot company over alleged harms to children. The complaint alleges that Character.AI (1) violated the Kentucky Consumer Protection Act by falsely marketing the product as safe and age-appropriate, and deceptively repurposing user data for LLM fine-tuning; (2) violated the Kentucky Consumer Data Protection Act by failing to provide adequate child safeguards, age verification, and parental consent mechanisms that resulted in the unlawful collection of children’s data; and (3) was unjustly enriched as a result of the unlawful conduct (AG acting as parens patriae). 

 

Nebraska-

  • Chatbots - LB 939: Introduced by Rep. Merman (R), the “Saving Human Connection Act” would prohibit chatbots with “human-like features” from being made available to minors and require reasonable age verification systems. Among other requirements, the bill would prohibit covered platforms from processing data in ways that conflict with a user’s best interests.
  • Data Driven Pricing - LB 1006: Introduced by Sen. Buereca (D), the "Protecting Consumers and Jobs from Predatory Pricing Act" would require disclosures for any business that engages in personalized algorithmic pricing using individualized consumer data. Additionally, the bill would prohibit businesses from using electronic shelving labels to display personalized algorithmic pricing. Would include a private right of action.

 

New Jersey

  • Health - S 735: Introduced by Sen. Burzichelli (D), this bill would prohibit advertising AI as a mental health professional.
  • Data Centers - S 680: Introduced by Sen. Smith (D) and Sen. McKeon (D), this bill requires energy usage plans for data centers that mandate all electricity be sourced from new clean energy sources. 
  • AI Safety/ Transparency - S 1802: Introduced by Sen. Singleton (D) and Sen. McKnight (D), this bill requires AI companies to conduct safety tests and to send the results to the state Office of Information Technology. 
  • Liability - SR 52: Introduced by Sen. Mukherji (D), this bill urges Gen AI companies to make voluntary commitments regarding employee whistleblower protections.

 

New York-

  • Chatbots / Transparency - S 8874: Introduced by Sen. Rhoads (D), this bill would require any person to disclose the use of AI to influence customer interaction, which would include automated customer support and personalized ad targeting. The disclosure would be required at the “point of interaction” with the consumer, i.e. where the customer first encounters AI.
  • Employment/ Government Use - S 8831: Introduced by Asm. Gonzalez (D), this bill would require disclosure of any automated employment decision making tools (AEDMTs) used by cities, counties, schools, etc. The bill would also state that the use of AI systems shall not result in the discharge, displacement, or reduction in wages of employees.

 

Oklahoma - 

  • Chatbots - SB 1521: Introduced by Sen. Hamilton (R), this bill is modeled on the federal CHAT Act and would require age verification for individuals seeking access to a companion chatbot. The bill would also require disclosures every 30 minutes of use and prohibit representations as a licensed professional.
  • Chatbots - HB 3544: Introduced by Rep. Maynard (R), this bill would prohibit chatbots from being made available to minors if they have human-like features and would require the implementation of reasonable age-verification systems.

 

South Carolina - Health - SB 0788: Introduced by Sen. Blackmon (R), this is a Illinois’ HB 1806-style bill that limits the use of AI by mental health practitioners. 

 

Tennessee - Health - SB 1580: Introduced by Sen. Walley (R), this bill would prohibit the development or deployment of an AI system that advertises that it is able to ask as a qualified mental health professional. 

 

Vermont - Health - H 644/ S 241: Introduced by Rep. Rachelson (D) and Sen. Gulick (D), these bills are modeled on Illinois’ HB 1806 and would prohibit persons from providing AI therapy unless provided by a licensed professional. It would also prohibit licensed professionals from using AI in administrative or supplementary support functions unless they maintain “full responsibility” for all interactions, including outputs and data use.

 

Virginia - January 14 marked both the first day of session and the bill introduction deadline, leading to the introduction of 10 new AI bills.

  • Chatbots - HB 758: Introduced by Del. Runion (R), the bill would prohibit chatbots from being made available to minors if they have human-like features and would require the implementation of reasonable age-verification systems.
  • Health/ Chatbots - HB 669: Introduced by Del. Maldonado (D), this bill would prohibit chatbots from providing any substantive information or advice that could constitute the practice of a variety of licensed professions (including medicine, social work, engineering, dentistry, and more).
  • Chatbots - HB 635: Introduced by Del. Maldonado (D), the billis modeled in part on California’s AB 1018, with some differences. The bill would prohibit making companion chatbots available to minors unless they are incapable of certain harmful behaviors, such as encouraging disordered eating or self-isolation. It would also require disclosures every 30 minutes and impose data privacy restrictions, including prohibiting the training of companion chatbots on minors’ inputs without written parental consent.
  • Health - HB 668/ SB 269: Introduced by Del. Maldonado (D) and Sen. Favola (D), this is a Illinois’ HB 1806-style bill that limits the use of AI by mental health practitioners. 
  • Government Use - HB 580: Introduced by Del. Glass (D), this bill would establish a program under the Department of Law to address AI fraud and abuse, including establishing a statewide AI fraud and abuse alert system.
  • Transparency - HB 713: Introduced by Del. Maldonado (D), this bill would require AI model developers of "base AI models” (defined as a large-scale artificial intelligence system model trained on broad and diverse data to learn general patterns and relationships) to disclose the terms of service governing their model, such as the model and developer’s name.
  • Transparency - HB 797: Introduced by Del. Hayes (D), this bill would create a  framework for any person or entity seeking to act as an independent verification organization (IVO) to audit AI systems for prevention of personal injury and property damage.
  • ADMT/ High-Risk AI - HB 999: Introduced by Del. Cole (D), this bill would require disclosures when ADS is used to make a decision related to employment, education, or access to public accommodations and would require annual assessments by deployers for bias and discriminatory impact (e.g. evaluation of training data and outputs).
  • Government Use - HB 1250: Introduced by Del. Anthony (D), this bill would establish a centralized program within the AG’s office to coordinate Virginia’s response to AI, algorithmic systems, biometric systems, and ADMTs. Among other obligations, the AG would be required to maintain a public process to receive complaints regarding these technologies.
  • ADMT/ High-Risk AI / Transparency - SB 245: Introduced by Sen. Head (R), this bill would require controllers and processors of AI systems to exercise “reasonable care” to protect consumers from “known or foreseeable” risks of algorithmic discrimination and would set other obligations for controllers, like obtaining consent from consumers before using their data. The bill would also require that controllers register with the Commonwealth, which would include submitting the privacy policy for the AI system.

 

Washington -

  • Data Driven Pricing - HB 2481: Introduced by Rep. Fosse (D), the “Fair Pricing and Transparency Act” would prohibit the use of surveillance-based price discrimination to modify goods prices for consumers, including  the use of surge pricing to modify the price of goods (regardless of frequency or duration). 
  • High-Risk/ADMT - SB 6120: Introduced by Sen. Wellman (D), this bill is a VA HB 2094 (vetoed) style bill, which would set disclosure requirements for developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems and set protections against reasonably foreseeable risks of algorithmic discrimination.

 

Federal

 

DOJ Releases Memo Announcing AI Litigation Task Force: Attorney General Pam Bondi released a memo announcing the creation of an AI Litigation Task Force within the Justice Department, pursuant to the deadline established in President Trump’s recent AI executive order. The next agency deadline is March 11 with directives to the Department of Commerce, Federal Communications Commission, Federal Trade Commission, and all agencies with discretionary grant funding. 

 

Congress Holds AI Hearings: The House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology held a hearing, “Advancing America’s AI Action Plan,” featuring OSTP Director Michael Kratios. In the hearing, Kratsios spoke on the White House’s recent AI EO, arguing that a patchwork of AI regulations favored major tech companies, at the expense of smaller tech start ups. Additionally, the House Committee on Education and Workforce also hosted a hearing titled “Building an AI Ready America,” focusing on how AI could be better used in education and workforce systems. Testimony included discussions on the risks and challenges of AI chatbots.
