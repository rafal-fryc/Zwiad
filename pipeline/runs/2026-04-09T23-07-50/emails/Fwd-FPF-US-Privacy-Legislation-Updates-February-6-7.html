Good morning everyone,  

 

February may be the shortest month of the year, but it’s one of the busiest for privacy legislation. The South Carolina Age-Appropriate Design Code was signed into law yesterday and went into effect immediately upon signature. This is an expansive law that includes requirements for users who are not minors, and it imposes personal liability on employees of covered services. That is the big news of the week, but there is much else to cover. Bills are being introduced left-and-right, reported out of committee, and passing chamber votes. We also have an update for you on FPF’s new U.S. privacy enforcement report.  As always, you can find our legislation and state enforcement trackers in the FPF Portal.

💬New Resource on U.S. Privacy Enforcement Trends: This week FPF released a new report on U.S. Privacy Enforcement in 2025. Looking back at activity by state attorneys general and the FTC in the last year, FPF’s U.S. Policy team identified four key trends: California and Texas are lead growing public enforcement of comprehensive privacy laws; states are demonstrating increasing concern for kids’ and teens’ online privacy and safety; regulators are going “full speed ahead” on location and driving data enforcement; and the FTC is prioritizing enforcement on harms to kids and teens, and deceptive AI marketing, under the new administration.

 

Stuff from the States 

 

Alabama

  • Comprehensive Privacy Bill Introduced (HB 351): Alabama came close to enacting a privacy law last year. Rep. Shaw (R) is back at it again this year, introducing another Connecticut-style bill. This bill includes a few unique aspects, including expanded authority for authorized agents to exercise consumer rights and a unique exemption for AI models (“provided that no personally identifiable data is present in the model or can be extracted from the model”).
  • ASAA Bill Passes Senate (HB 161): This fast-moving bill was passed by the Senate with amendments on February 5. The bill now goes back to the House to consider the Senate’s amendments.

 

Arizona

  • Comprehensive Privacy Bill Introduced (SB 1815): Sen. Kuby (D) introduced a Virginia-style bill that includes the broader definition of sale (including exchanges for “other valuable consideration”). This bill defines “child” as someone under the age of 16, extending many child-specific protections to teenagers. 
  • Data Broker Registry Proposed (SB 1790): Sen. Kuby (D) also introduced a bill that would require data brokers to post a public privacy notice, register annually with the state, and maintain a comprehensive information security plan. 

 

California - New Privacy Committee in Legislature (SR 74): The California Senate has responded to the ever-growing tide of privacy and AI legislation by launching a new committee—the Senate Committee on Privacy, Digital Technologies, and Consumer Protection, which will have jurisdiction over bills concerning data privacy, artificial intelligence, digital and information technologies and systems, the consumer protection and security of personal information, the Department of Technology, and the California Privacy Protection Agency. Sen. Cabaldon (D) confirmed on LinkedIn that he will chair the new committee, which is currently “staffing up.”

 

Colorado - Homeowner’s Insurance Privacy Bill Introduced (HB 26-1091): Lawmakers in Colorado have introduced a financial privacy bill that would regulate the processing of personal data by “licensees” (a person licensed to do business in Colorado in connection with the transaction of homeowner's insurance) in insurance transactions. This bill also includes consumer rights of access, correction, deletion, and opt-in with respect to practices such as targeted advertising and the sale of personal data. The bill includes many business obligations normally seen in comprehensive privacy laws, such as data minimization, opt-in consent requirements, and data protection assessments. This bill includes a private right of action.

 

Connecticut - Proposed Funding for Enforcement (SB 4): A proposal has been introduced to appropriate additional money to the Connecticut office of the Attorney General for “enforcing the state’s laws concerning consumer privacy.”

 

Hawaii - Location and Browsing Information Bill Passes Committee (SB 1163): Scrutiny on the sale of location data is a growing trend early this legislative session. Hawaii has now reported a bill out of committee that would require consent for the sale of geolocation information and internet browser information. “Precise location” is defined broadly as locating a user within a geographic area equal to or less than the area of a circle having a radius of one mile. It would also ban the sale of data collected through “eavesdropping” or “through an application operating in the background of a device that uses the device's microphone.”

 

Illinois

  • AADC Introduced (SB 3241): Sen. Rezin has introduced another AADC this year. This bill is a Maryland AADC copycat, but notably, the Act would have an immediate effective date if enacted. 
  • Comprehensive Privacy Bill Introduced (SB 3220): Sen. Rezin (R) has introduced yet another comprehensive privacy bill this year. This is a largely Virginia-style bill that includes the broader definition of “biometric data” (including data generated from photos, video, and audio if generated to identify an individual), a narrow definition of “sale” (applying only to exchange for monetary consideration), and exempts pseudonymous data from consumer rights. 
  • BIPA ‘Security Purpose’ Amendment Introduced (SB 3122): Sen. Balkema (R) introduced a proposed amendment to BIPA that would: exempt information converted to a mathematical representation that cannot be used to recreate the biometric identifier; exempt information that cannot reasonably be used to identify an individual; exempt biometric time clocks; allow the collection and use of biometric identifiers or information for security purposes; add a cure requirement to the private right of action; and add an exemption for entities whose employees are covered by a collective bargaining agreement establishing policies for biometric information. 
  • Delete Act Proposed (HB 4809): Rep. du Buclet (D) has introduced a bill that would require data brokers to register with the state annually and would require the state to establish an accessible deletion mechanism allowing consumers to submit one deletion request to all registered data brokers. 

 

Iowa - ASAA INtroduced (S.F. 2197): Introduced by Sen. Alons (R) on February 4, this bill would establish an App Store Accountability Act framework aligning with the models enacted in Utah, Louisiana, and Texas in 2025.

 

Maryland

  • Enforcement Division and Privacy Working Group Proposed (SB 564): Sen. Love (D) introduced a bill that would create a “Division of Data Protection” within the Maryland AG's office to enforce MODPA. This bill would also establish a “Maryland Data Privacy Implementation and Innovation Workgroup,” consisting of a variety of stakeholders (including a representative of a Maryland-based small business, a representative of the digital advertising industry, and a representative of industry with experience in data governance, cybersecurity, or compliance). The group would study Maryland's consumer data privacy framework and evaluate implementation on five key areas, including data minimization, business compliance challenges, other states' privacy law updates, enforcement capacity, and artificial intelligence and privacy. Findings would need to be reported to the governor by January 1, 2027.
  • Geolocation Data Amendment Introduced (SB 569): Currently, MODPA bans the sale of sensitive data, including precise geolocation data. A recently introduced bill from Sen. Ellis (D) would amend MODPA to (1) redefine “process” to exclude deleting personal data or efforts to identify personal data solely for the purpose of deletion or data minimization, and (2) provide that a controller may not collect, process, share, or sell precise geolocation data for the purpose of targeted advertising without the consumer's consent. 

 

New Hampshire - Amendment on Selling Location and Children’s Data Passes Committee (HB 1460): Similar to an amendment enacted in Oregon last year, New Hampshire is considering amending its comprehensive privacy law to prohibit a controller from selling “the location and other sensitive data regarding children.” That bill passed committee on February 2. 

 

Pennsylvania - Comprehensive Privacy Bill Re-referred (HB 78): Last October, the Pennsylvania House passed a comprehensive privacy bill. That bill is progressing again and was re-referred to a new committee this week. This is a Connecticut-style bill with a few unique aspects, including a narrow definition of biometric data (excluding data that has been converted to a “mathematical representation”), no right to access, and a definition of sensitive data that includes certain government identifiers and financial data.

 

Rhode Island - AADC Introduced (SB 2406): Introduced by Sen. Lawson (D), this AADC variant proposes a blend of Maryland AADC-style applicability, DPIAs, data processing prohibitions and enforcement, and a Colorado/Connecticut-style duty of care to minors and “actual knowledge or willful disregards” knowledge standard.

 

South Carolina - AADC Signed into Law on February 5 & NOW IN EFFECT (HB 3431): While this AADC draws upon prior state privacy laws, it also takes a novel shift in a few key ways, including a unique approach to a duty of care requiring covered services to prevent certain harms to minors, provision of robust tools for all users in addition to specific requirements for minors, third party audit requirements (rather than DPIAs) with mandatory public reporting, and personal liability for employees. FPF will be releasing detailed, comparative analysis of this law early next week, and it will be the topic of our February community call. 

 

South Dakota - ASAA Bill Introduced (HB 1275): Introduced by Rep. Soye (R), this bill proposes an App Store Accountability Act-style age assurance framework with a few divergences, including requirements for age verifying existing accounts, parental consent obligations for pre-installed apps, and the absence of application exemptions (a factor in the preliminary injunction analysis against Texas’ law).

 

Utah

  • ASAA Challenged: The Computer & Communications Industry Association (CCIA) filed a federal lawsuit challenging the constitutionality of Utah’s App Store Accountability Act (SB 142) on February 5. Similar to CCIA’s challenge against Texas’ App Store Act last year, the organization argues that the broad age verification and parental consent requirements in Utah’s Act unconstitutionally burden protected speech because they are not narrowly tailored to serve the state’s purported interest in protecting minors online and ignore less restrictive alternatives to address alleged harms. Moreover, the CCIA argues that the Act’s age rating requirements infringe on app stores’ and developers’ First Amendment rights, and that the Act as a whole excessively burdens interstate commerce. 
  • Bill to Amend ASAA Introduced (HB 498): Introduced by Rep. Dunnigan (R), this bill would amend Utah’s App Store Accountability Act to provide certain exceptions for developers of “family account applications” (defined as apps that allow accounts with multiple user profiles and verify that primary account holders are over the age of 18), including not having to verify through the app store’s data sharing methods whether VPC has been obtained for minor accounts.

 

Virginia 

  • Ban on Selling Precise Geolocation Data Passes Senate (SB 338): On February 3, the Virginia Senate passed SB 338 by a vote of 39-0. This bill would amend the VCDPA to prohibit a controller from selling precise geolocation data.
  • Reproductive Privacy Amendment Passes Senate (SB 177): The Senate voted 39-0 to pass a proposed amendment to Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act that would add a GLBA data-level exception to the consent requirement for obtaining, disclosing, selling, or disseminating personally identifiable reproductive or sexual health information.
  • First Wave of Bills to Fail: Several bills ended their legislative journey for the year as they either failed to advance or were held for 2027, including SB 201 (VPC for registering users under the age of 18), SB 232 (proposed VCDPA amendment concerning minors), SB 615 (a data-driven pricing bill), HB 638 (data broker registry), HB 654 (VCDPA amendment concerning biometrics and neural data), and HB 1368 (social media PRA). 

 

West Virginia - Comprehensive Privacy Bill Introduced (HB 5123): Del. Young (D) has introduced a CCPA-style bill that includes a right to correct inaccurate personal information. 

 

Wisconsin:

  • ASAA Bill Heard in Committee (AB 962): Introduced by Rep. Goeben (R) on January 29, this bill proposes an App Store Accountability Act-style age assurance framework with a few divergences, including requirements for age verifying existing accounts, parental consent obligations for pre-installed apps, and the absence of application exemptions (a factor in the preliminary injunction analysis against Texas’ law). This bill is already moving quickly, as it was heard in the Committee on Children and Families on February 5.
  • Comprehensive Privacy Bill Reported and Re-referred (AB 172): A Connecticut-style comprehensive privacy bill continues to progress through the Wisconsin Legislature and has now been re-referred to the Committee on Rules. 

 

Federal Happenings 

 

Agenda Released for Upcoming FTC Workshop on “Data-Driven Economy”: The Federal Trade Commission will host a workshop on Thursday, February 26 concerning “Consumer Injuries and Benefits in the Data-Driven Economy.” The recently released agenda includes panels on: 

  • Quantifying Injuries and Benefits to Consumers (two panels on this);
  • Data Breaches, Impacts on Consumers, and Efforts to Minimize Injuries;
  • The Costs and Benefits of Behavioral & Contextual Advertising; and
  • Measuring Consumer Preferences, Beliefs, and Decisions.
