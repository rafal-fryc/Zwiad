Welcome to your weekly roundup of AI legislation!

 

Housekeeping 

  • IYMI - Community Call Slides: Last Thursday’s Legislation Community Call slides are now available in the Portal, covering recent legislative activity and directives of the White House AI Executive Order. If you have requests or suggestions for future call topics, please reach out to jfrancis@fpf.org. 

 

Highlights 

 

  • 28 new state AI bills were introduced across the country, alongside committee movement in several states.
  • Florida’s AI Bill of Rights (SB 482) was reported favorably out of its first committee.
  • Chatbots continue to dominate introductions, with new or advancing bills still largely focused on youth protections: California (AB 1609), Florida (SB 482, SB 7030), Indiana (SB 108), Maine (LD 2162), Nebraska (LB 1083, LB 1185), Oklahoma (SB 2085, HB 4083), Tennessee (SB 1700), and Utah (HB 286).
  • Frontier model regulation expands to new states: Legislation with SB 53-style definitions and provisions was introduced in Nebraska (LB 1083) and Utah (HB 286), combining catastrophic risk frameworks with child safety and companion chatbot requirements.
  • Health AI remains a key theme, with Illinois HB 1806-style bills introduced in Mississippi (HB 1048), Washington (HB 2599), and Oklahoma (SB 2037), alongside new healthcare disclosure and oversight proposals in Hawaii (SB 2281).
  • At the federal level, Sen. Markey (D-MA) and Rep. Lee (D-PA) introduced S 3680 / H.R. 7110, which would require agencies to establish civil rights offices on AI bias and discrimination.

 

Stuff from the States

 

Arizona - 

  • Liability - HB 2410: Introduced by Rep. Kolodin (R), this novel bill would establish that user communications with AI are privileged if such communication would be privileged if sought from a human professional. 
  • Data Driven Pricing - HB 2489: Introduced by Rep. De Los Santos (D), this bill would prohibit surveillance pricing unless certain criteria are met. 

 

California - Chatbots - AB 1609: Introduced by Asm. Zbur (D) this bill would prohibit chatbots from misleading a person about their artificial identity to incentivize the purchase or sale of goods or services.

 

Florida - Two bills were reported favorably out of their respective committees yesterday January 21: 

  • Chatbots/ Liability/ Privacy - SB 482 (AI Bill of Rights): Providing citizens certain AI rights, such as the right to limit control of minors’ access to AI or know if AI companies are collecting personal information, as well as requiring notice for chatbots and prohibiting entities from providing “licensed” therapy or mental health counseling through AI. 
  • Liability / Chatbots - SB 7030: Introduced by the Commerce Committee, this bill would prevent certain information pertaining to chatbot litigation from being made public until the completion of an investigation. 

 

Hawaii - Health - SB 2281: Introduced by Sen. Buenaventura (D), this bill would require health care providers using AI to disclose their use to patients. Providers would also be required to hire “AI oversight personnel” to oversee the use of AI in the healthcare context. 

 

Indiana - Chatbots - SB 108 (chatbots) passed in policy committee on Jan. 15 and is set to be heard in Appropriations on Jan. 22.

 

Iowa - Transparency / ADMT - HF 2048: Introduced by Rep. Gearhart (R), this bill would require disclosures for data used in automated decision making or AI training.

 

Maine - Chatbots - LD 2162: Introduced by Rep. Gramlich (D), this bill would prohibit chatbots from being made available to minors if they have human-like features and would require the implementation of reasonable age-verification systems. This bill is similar to legislation recently introduced in VA, HB 758.

 

Mississippi -

  • Health - HB 1048: Introduced by Rep. Ford (R), this is an Illinois HB 1806-style bill that would set restrictions on the use of AI in mental or behavioral health care. 
  • Misc - SB 2437: Introduced by Sen. Williams (R), this bill would define “artificial intelligence” in Mississippi code.
  • Liability - SB 2354: Introduced by Sen. Blackmon (D), this bill would allow any entity damaged by AI fraud to seek compensatory or statutory damages for violations. The bill would state that any entity that “knowingly” develops, deploys, or facilitates AI systems for the purpose of fraudulent behavior shall be held liable.

 

Nebraska -

  • Frontier Models / Chatbots - LB 1083: Introduced by Sen. Storer (R), this bill combines California SB 53-style definitions of frontier models and catastrophic risk with child safety and chatbot-related requirements. It would require large frontier model developers and large chatbot providers to develop and publish a public safety and child protection plan, including processes to assess and manage catastrophic risk. An identical bill has been introduced in Utah this week, HB 286.
  • Chatbots/Transparency - LB 1185: Introduced by Sen. Bostar (D), this bill mandates disclosures to minors when they are conversing with chatbots, requires implementation of safety protocols, and requires privacy/account management functions for the parents of minor users.

 

New York - A 9487 (employment) and A 9449 (Governors’ amendments for the RAISE Act) were reported favorably out of the Assembly Science and Technology Committee on Jan. 21.

 

Oklahoma -

  • Chatbots/ Transparency/ Government Use - SB 2085: Introduced by Sen. Hamilton (R), this bill is a Florida AI Bill of Rights-style bill, with similar requirements for government use of AI, NIL protections, parental controls for minors, and deidentification requirements. However, this bill notably does not include restrictions on AI providing advice usually reserved for licensed professionals, including therapists. 
  • Data Driven Pricing - HB 3959: Introduced by Rep. Munson (D), this bill would prohibit all food retail establishments from using personalized algorithmic pricing or surveillance pricing and includes a private right of action.
  • Other introduced bills include: SB 1785 (miscellaneous; offering prohibitions on the use of AI in life and death situations or to replace workers), HB 4083 (chatbots; protections for minors and requirements for therapeutic chatbot use); and SB 2037 (health; Illinois 1806-style bill).

 

Tennessee -

  • Chatbots - SB 1700: Introduced by Sen. Akbari (D), this bill would replicate the federal CHAT Act (introduced), which would impose specific safety requirements, mandate disclaimers, and require explicit consent for training AI models with minors’ data.

 

Utah -

  • Frontier Models/Chatbots - HB 286: Introduced by Rep. Fiefia (R), this bill combines California SB 53-style definitions of frontier models and catastrophic risk with child safety and chatbot-related requirements. See, Nebraska’s LB 1083, an identical bill introduced this week. 
  • Other introduced bills include:  HB 276 (GenAI; provenance data requirements), SB 150 (health; expanding healthcare practitioner’s ability to use AI); and SB 177 (data driven pricing; disclosure requirements for algorithmic pricing)

 

Washington - 

  • GenAI/ Transparency - 
    • HB 1170: This California AI Transparency Act-style bill passed Committee on Jan 14. 
    • HB 2503: Introduced by Rep. Shavers (D), this bill is based on California AB 2013, requiring generative AI providers to publish certain information regarding data used to train the system. 
  • High risk / ADMT - SB 6284: Introduced by a bipartisan group of Senators, this CAIA-style bill would impose requirements on deployers of high-risk AI systems (unlike CAIA, no developer requirements).   
  • Health - HB 2599: Introduced by Rep. Kloba (D), this bill is identical to Illinois HB 1806. 

 

West Virginia - GenAI - HB 4496: Introduced by Del. Flannigan (R), this bill would require disclosures when media is generated or altered by AI.

 

Federal

 

High Risk AI/ Government Use - S 3680 / H.R. 7110: Introduced by Sen. Markey (D-MA) and Rep. Lee (D-PA), the bill would require agencies that use, fund, or oversee algorithms to have an office of Civil Rights focused on bias, discrimination, and other harms (no text yet).
