Housekeeping 

 

IYMI: FPF released a new blog and chart, “Enacted State AI Laws 2023-2025,” identifying the 27 enacted AI laws across 14 states from 2023-2025, highlighting key compliance requirements and consumer rights, major legislative trends, and effective dates. Notably, most enacted AI laws are already effective as of 2026, requiring entities to begin navigating compliance obligations. 

 

Highlights 

 

Five Significant Chatbot Bills Passed First Chamber: Arizona HB 2311 and Iowa SF 2417 use a similar minor-focused framework, with the Arizona bill additionally requiring parental controls. Utah HB 438 takes a comprehensive approach, requiring operators to comply with the state’s privacy law and have minor-specific protections. Oregon SB 1546 and Washington SB 5984 follow the California SB 243 model.

 

Four Other AI Bills Pass First Chamber: Including Arizona HB 2410 (liability), Utah HB 276 (genAI) and SB 150 (health), and Wyoming HB 102 (health/genAI/ liability).

 

Federal Tensions on State AI: Two congressional letters were sent late last week regarding state AI regulation, one requesting a GAO review of the full federal and state AI regulatory landscape, and another urging the Department of Commerce to examine the impact of state AI laws, including Colorado’s AI Act and New York’s RAISE Act.

 

Stuff from the States

 

Arizona - 

  • Chatbot Bill Passed the House: HB 2311 would require operators of “conversational AI services” to provide nonhuman disclosures to minors (every three hours) and prohibit minors from accessing sexually explicit content, similar to CA’s SB 243. The bill would also restrict engagement optimization for minors, like preventing offering rewards at unpredictable intervals. Among other requirements, the bill would require the development of suicide and self-harm protocols and prohibit representation as a mental health professional. Operators would be required to provide privacy and account-management tools for minor users, including parental controls and oversight features. There would be no private right of action. 
  • Liability Bill Passed the House: HB 2410 would provide that a person’s communication with an artificial intelligence system is legally privileged if the communication would have been privileged had the advice been sought from a human professional.
  • GenAI Bill Passed Committee: SB 1786 would require GenAI providers to embed provenance data.

 

California - Four notable bills were introduced at last Friday’s deadline, including AB 2575 (health disclosures and prohibiting replacing employees with AI), SB 1146 (disclosures for AI-generated advertisements re consumer health products), SB 1142 (individual rights to revoke access to their digital replicas and remove unauthorized genAI content), and AB 2169 (requiring AI model operators to provide users with access to their personal data and interoperability). 

 

Colorado - Chatbots - HB 1263: Introduced by Rep. Camacho (D), this bill would mandate nonhuman disclosures for minors, restrict engagement optimization, and prohibit minors from accessing sexually explicit content.

 

Florida - Liability / Chatbot Bill Passed Committee: SB 7030 would prevent certain information pertaining to chatbot litigation from being made public until the completion of an investigation. 

 

Georgia - Chatbot Bill Passed Committee: SB 540 went quickly from introduction to passage this week. The bill would require operators of “conversational AI services” to provide nonhuman disclosures to minors, restrict engagement optimization, and prohibit minors from accessing sexually explicit content. 

  • HB 1439 was also introduced, regarding data-driven pricing for food establishments

 

Idaho - Chatbot Bill Passed Committee: S 1297 would establish disclosure, safety, and content requirements for public-facing conversational AI services, with enhanced protections for minors. 

 

Iowa - 

  • Chatbot Bill Passed the Senate: SF 2417 was introduced by the Senate Committee on Technology and passed by the Senate this week. The bill is largely identical to Arizona’s HB 2311, which also passed its first chamber this week. Unlike the Arizona bill, however, this bill does not require operators to provide privacy or account-management tools for minor users. Additionally, H 647 (safety, privacy, and age verification requirements for chatbot deployers) passed committee.
  • Other bills introduced: SF 2415 (mental health chatbots), SF 2414 (ADMT/employment), HF 2691 (genAI output ownership). 

 

Kentucky - Health AI Bill passed the House: HB 455 is largely identical to Illinois’ HB 1806 and would prohibit licensed professionals (i.e. therapy providers) from using AI for anything beyond defined “supplementary support” (e.g., preparing client records, analyzing anonymized data, or identifying external resources) unless the client is informed in writing of the AI’s use and purpose and provides consent. Notably, the law also bans entities from offering or advertising AI-delivered therapy to “the public of the State” unless conducted by a licensed professional, with exemptions for religious counseling, peer support, and educational resources.

 

Louisiana - Three notable bills were introduced: HB 230 (genAI disclosures), HB 197 (health), and HB 295 (chatbots). 

 

Michigan - ADMT/ Employment - HB 5579: Introduced by Rep. Tsernoglou (D), this bill would set comprehensive requirements for employers' use of automated decision-making and electronic monitoring tools in the workplace. 

 

Nebraska - Chatbots/Transparency Passed Committee: LB 1185 would require disclosures to minors, implementation of safety protocols, and privacy/account management functions for the parents of minor users.

 

New Jersey - Two notable bills were introduced: S 3668 (transparency) and S 3612/A4085 (data-driven pricing). 

 

New York - Chatbot Bill Passed Committee: S 9051 would prohibit providing unsafe chatbot features to minors including simulating companionship, facilitating self-harm, encouraging secrecy/isolation, and generating sexually explicit conduct. 

 

Oklahoma Chatbot Bill Passed Committee: SB 1521 is modeled on the federal CHAT Act, requiring age verification for individuals seeking access to a companion chatbot.

 

Oregon Chatbot Bill Passed the Senate: SB 1546 would establish a companion chatbot framework similar to California’s SB 243, with some additional requirements. It would require clear and conspicuous disclosures to users and mandates safety protocols, including referrals to crisis resources when users express self-harm ideation. The bill also includes additional protections for minors, prohibiting chatbots from making statements that could lead a reasonable user to believe they are interacting with a human or that simulate emotional dependence, while requiring recurring three-hour disclosures and banning sexually explicit visual content. Going beyond SB 243, the bill also would prohibit engagement-maximization or reward mechanisms directed at minors. It would create a private right of action.

 

Utah - 

  • GenAI Bill Passed the House: HB 276 would require large online platforms to detect, disclose, and preserve provenance data and provide user tools to inspect that information. Device manufacturers would be required to embed latent disclosures, generative AI providers (with >1 million monthly users) would be required to include latent disclosures in AI-generated or substantially modified content, and there would be new protections and takedown procedures for non-consensual AI-generated intimate images.
  • Chatbots Bill Passed the House: HB 438 would establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for AI companion chatbots, requiring operators to comply with the Utah Consumer Privacy Act, including prohibiting undisclosed advertising within conversations. For minors, the bill mandates hourly disclosures and referrals to crisis resources when suicidal ideation is detected, and prohibits encouraging illegal substance use, sexual conduct, or other harmful behavior. The bill also limits collection of minors’ data to what is necessary for core functionality and prohibits the sale of users’ personal data. The Division of Consumer Protection would be authorized to establish age assurance standards through rulemaking, with compliant operators eligible for a safe harbor.
  • Privacy/ADMT Bill Passed Committee: HB 450 would update Utah's data privacy laws by introducing new definitions for automated decision making and profiling. 
  • Sandbox - SB 322: Introduced by Sen. Johnson (R), this bill would establish an Artificial Intelligence Regulatory Sandbox for public education, allowing pilot programs for testing AI tools under defined safety, privacy, and oversight requirements. Approved pilots would require educator supervision, parental notice and opt-out rights, student data protections, safety testing, and evaluation of educational outcomes. This bill follows SB 149 (2024) which created a broader AI sandbox program in Utah.

 

Washington Chatbot Bill Passed House: SB 5984, as amended, would regulate companion chatbots, including disclosure mandates, minor-specific requirements, and creating a safety protocol.  

 

Wyoming Health/GenAI/ Liability Bill Passed the House: HB 102 would prohibit the development or distribution of AI systems intended to promote self-harm. Among other requirements, the bill would also create new criminal offenses for the development or distribution of CSAM. The bill also provides immunity to AI developers from liability for damages arising from the misuse of their systems by third parties, except when the developer should have known the system would be used for illegal purposes.

Federal

 

Lawmakers Request GAO Review of AI Regulatory Landscape: Representatives Obernolte (R-CA) and Babin (R-TX) sent a letter to the Government Accountability Office (GAO) requesting a comprehensive review of existing federal and state laws governing artificial intelligence. The request asks GAO to evaluate both AI-specific rules and technology-neutral laws that may apply to AI, with the goal of assessing regulatory overlap, barriers to innovation, and gaps in oversight. The effort reflects growing bipartisan interest in mapping the current AI regulatory landscape as Congress considers future federal legislation and amid President Trump’s executive order.

 

CO and NY Lawmakers Raise Concerns About State AI Laws: Representatives Evans (R-CO) and Langworthy (R-NY) sent a letter to the Secretary of Commerce urging review of state AI regulations, warning that a growing patchwork of laws could undermine U.S. innovation and competitiveness. The letter specifically cites Colorado’s AI Act (SB 205) and New York’s RAISE Act, arguing that their broad compliance requirements and potential interstate effects highlight the need for a more uniform federal AI policy framework. The lawmakers called on the Department of Commerce to evaluate the impact of state-level AI regulation as part of ongoing federal efforts to develop a national AI strategy.
