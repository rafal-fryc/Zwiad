Highlights 

 

  • Another surge in state activity: Legislatures introduced 47 new AI bills this week, with 13 additional bills advancing through committees.
  • Chatbot legislation continues to move, with bills advancing through policy committees in Hawaii (HB 1782), Pennsylvania (SB 1090), and Utah (HB 286).
  • Bills also advanced through committees regarding Health (Hawaii’s SB 2281, Utah’s SB 150, and West Virginia’s HB 4770), Sandboxes (Utah’s HB 320), GenAI (Washington’s HB 1170, HB 2503), ADMT/ High-Risk AI (Washington’s SB 6284 and HB 2157), Employment (Washington’s HB 2144), and Data-Driven Pricing (Washington’s HB 2481).
  • Illinois approaches its bill introduction deadline tomorrow: February 6 marks the final day to introduce bills, with 11 new AI proposals already introduced this week spanning data-driven pricing, chatbots, health, generative AI, and transparency.
  • Federal developments to watch: HUD proposed rescinding its discriminatory effects regulations under the Fair Housing Act, a move that could carry implications for the future of algorithmic discrimination enforcement.

Stuff from the States

 

Arizona - Gen AI - SB 1786: Introduced by Sen. Petersen (R), this bill would require providers of generative AI systems in Arizona to embed tamper-resistant provenance data in AI-generated content. 

 

California - 

  • New Senate Privacy Committee Announced: The California Senate established a new standing committee this session, the Privacy, Digital Technologies and Consumer Protection Committee, which will hear legislation related to privacy and emerging technologies, including AI. The committee will be chaired by Sen. Cabaldon (D) and represents a shift from prior sessions, when most AI bills were routed through the Judiciary Committee. The committee’s creation was formalized in a recent Senate resolution announcing the Senate’s standing committees for the 2026 session.
  • ADMT/Employment - SB 947: Introduced by Sen. McNerney (D), this bill would establish comprehensive regulations for the use of automated decision systems in employment, requiring human oversight, transparency, worker data access, and anti-retaliation protections. 
  • Employment - SB 951: Introduced by Sen. Reyes (D), this bill would require employers to provide advance notice and protections for workers affected by technological displacement due to AI or automation.

 

Hawaii - 

  • Passed two bills through committee: SB 2281 (health) and HB 1782 (chatbots).
  • Chatbots - HB 2502: Introduced by Rep. Matayoshi (D), this bill would require chatbot providers to disclose to the user that they are not conversing with a human, with increased frequency for minors. Providers would be prohibited from using reward systems designed to increase engagement for minors.
  • ADMT/Transparency - HB 2500: Introduced by Rep. Hartsfield (D), this bill would require developers of ADMT systems to give deployers disclosures on risks and intended uses. Deployers would then be required to give disclosures to consumers about the use of such systems and of their right to access and correct the data used. 
  • Data-Driven Pricing - HB 2458: Introduced by Rep. Grandinetti (D), this bill would prohibit retailers from using surveillance pricing in the sale of food that is sold, or qualifies to be sold, as part of the federal Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). The bill would set several exemptions, such as for disclosed discounts and loyalty programs. This bill is set to be heard today, February 5.

 

Illinois - 

  • Health/Data Driven Pricing - SB 3027: Introduced by Sen. Simmons (D), this bill would prohibit all hospitals from using artificial intelligence to set or influence health care pricing or billing.
  • Data Driven Pricing - HB 4717: Introduced by Rep. Grant (R), this bill would require disclosures by entities that use personalized algorithmic pricing stating that “this price was set by an algorithm using your personal data.”
  • Health - SB 2993: Introduced by Sen. Ventura (D), this bill would prohibit licensed medical professionals from allowing AI to prescribe medication unless the professional maintains full control and responsibility over the prescription. The bill would also grant rulemaking responsibilities to the development of regulations for AI use in this context.
  • Frontier Models/ Chatbots - HB 4705/ SB 3261: Introduced by Rep. Didech (D) and Sen. Edly-Allen (D), this bill would combine California SB 53-style definitions of frontier models and catastrophic risk with child safety and chatbot-related requirements. Comparable legislation was recently introduced in Nebraska (LB 1083), Utah (HB 286), and Tennessee (HB 1898).
  • Transparency - SB 2995: Introduced by Sen. Ventura (D), this bill would prohibit the use of AI to interact with consumers without disclosure that the AI system is not human. It would also mandate that consumers must be offered the ability to communicate with humans during business hours when AI is used in consumer communications.
  • GenAI - HB 4711: Introduced by Rep. Scherer (D), this bill would require GenAI tool providers to apply provenance data to wholly-generated synthetic content. It would set other requirements for large online platforms, such as retaining provenance data in content posted on the platform. 
  • Chatbots - SB 3262: Introduced by Sen. Edly-Allen (D), this bill would establish comprehensive safeguards, transparency, and liability measures for companion AI products and includes special protections for minors. 
  • Gen AI - SB 3263: Introduced by Sen. Edly-Allen (D), this bill would require AI providers, large online platforms, and device manufacturers to embed and preserve machine-readable provenance labels in digital content and provide free verification tools.
  • Frontier Models/AI Safety - SB 3312: Introduced by Sen. Edly-Allen (D), this bill would establish comprehensive safety, transparency, and reporting requirements for frontier model developers. It also would mandate risk management and public accountability measures.
  • Health - HB 4804: Introduced by Rep. Morgan (D), this bill would establish strict regulations for the use of AI in the nursing practice, ensuring human oversight, patient consent, transparency, and accountability.
  • Frontier Models/Transparency - HB 4799: Introduced by Rep. Buclet (D), this bill would establish robust transparency, risk management, and reporting requirements for frontier model developers and would amend state law to protect sensitive AI safety information. 
  • Transparency - SB 3180: Introduced by Sen. Ventura (D), this bill would prohibit developers from training AI on a user's data and retaining that data indefinitely unless they provide written notice to the user and obtain the user's express written consent.

 

Iowa - 

  • Chatbots / Health - H 2204: Introduced by Rep. Wichtendahl (D), this bill would set requirements for deployers of both companion and therapeutic chatbots, including limiting user data collection, requiring age verification for minors, and requiring disclosures.
  • Chatbots - H 647: Introduced by the House Committee on Economic Growth and Technology, this bill would set safety, privacy, and age verification requirements for chatbot deployers, including limits on minors’ access to chatbots designed for mental health counseling.

 

Kansas - 

  • AI Executive Order - HR 6023: Introduced by the Committee on Legislative Modernization, this nonbinding resolution would express opposition to any future efforts to impose a moratorium on state AI policymaking or preempt state AI laws. A similar nonbinding resolution was introduced in Ohio (SCR 14).
  • AI Safety/Health - SB 405: Introduced by the Committee on Federal and State Affairs, this bill would prohibit the training of AI that encourages suicide, simulates human relationships, or acts as a healthcare professional. 
  • Chatbots - HB 2671: Introduced by the House Committee on K-12 Education Budget, this bill would require age verification, parental controls, and user notifications for companion AI chatbots.

 

Maryland - 

  • Health/Chatbots - HB 883: Introduced by Del. Qi (D), this bill would prohibit AI developers from claiming that AI is a behavioral health provider and sets general disclosure requirements for AI systems. 
  • Liability - HB 712: Introduced by Del. Grammer (R), this bill would introduce a new liability standard that holds developers and deployers accountable for harm caused by defective or dangerous AI products, and includes special provisions for risk mitigation and protection of minors.

 

Missouri - Health/Liability - SB 1598: Introduced by Sen. Beck (D), this bill would amend the law to include negligent use or reliance on artificial intelligence by health care providers as grounds for malpractice liability. 

 

New Jersey -

  • Transparency - A 3307: Introduced by Asm. Sampson (D), this bill would require news platforms to clearly disclose when gen AI is used to produce news content. 
  • GenAI - A 2767: Introduced by Asm. Katz (D), this bill would make it a crime to offer, sell, or distribute AI or automated interactive computer technology capable of performing or simulating sexual conduct directed at children. 
  • Chatbots - A 2710: Introduced by Asm. Kanitra (R), this bill would prohibit providers from developing, marketing, or making available any AI relationship simulation unless it is exclusively for legitimate medical purposes, such as clinical or therapeutic treatment under the supervision of a licensed healthcare professional.
  • Health - A 799: Introduced by Asm. Bailey (D), this bill would prohibit advertising AI systems as licensed mental health professionals, similar to IL HB 1806.

 

New York -

  • GenAI - A 10047: Introduced by Asm. Lavine (D), this bill would require disclosure of AI-generated or materially altered media depicting major human disasters when publicly distributed in New York. It would mandate online platforms support such labeling, including providing spoken disclosures for auditory content. 
  • Employment - S 9089: Introduced by Sen. Comrie (D), this bill would prohibit platforms from using algorithms that require or incentivize delivery workers to meet rigid delivery times that cannot be achieved while complying with traffic and safety laws. It would also ban algorithmic practices that penalize workers for delays caused by external factors, such as traffic or weather, and prohibit reductions in pay or work opportunities for refusing to engage in unsafe or unlawful conduct. The bill would require platforms to disclose how algorithms affect work and would grant workers the right to request written explanations for suspensions or limitations on work access.

 

Ohio - Data Driven Pricing - HB 665: Introduced by Rep. Cockley (D), this bill would prohibit the use or distribution of pricing algorithms that are trained with nonpublic competitor data. It would also set disclosure requirements for large entities (annual revenues > 5 mil) using a pricing algorithm. 

 

Oregon - Chatbots - SB 1546: Introduced by the Senate Committee on Early Childhood and Behavioral Health , this bill would require AI companion and platform operators to clearly disclose AI interactions and implement protocols to detect and prevent harm related to suicidal ideation or self-harm, with heightened protections for minors. 

 

Pennsylvania - 

  • Chatbots - HB 2175: Introduced by Rep. Shusterman (D), this bill would prohibit chatbot suppliers from selling or sharing consumer information with a third party, as well as advertising products to users without disclosure. The bill would also require chatbot suppliers to maintain a written policy, which includes disclosure requirements.
  • Chatbots: SB 1090 (regulating the use of companion chatbots by users, including minors) passed through committee.

 

Rhode Island -

  • Chatbots - HB 7350: Introduced by Rep. Spears (D), this bill would require operators of AI companion systems to implement safety protocols and notify users of the system’s non-human status every 3 hours. 
  • Health - HB 7349: Introduced by Rep. Spears (D), this bill is modeled on Illinois HB 1806. 

 

South Dakota -

  • Chatbots - SB 170: Introduced by Sen. Larson (D), this bill is modeled on Alabama HB 325 and would require that consumers be notified when they are interacting with a chatbot during commercial transactions.
  • Chatbots - SB 168: Introduced by Sen. Larson (D), this bill would require chatbot providers to implement age verification, restrict minor access to chatbots with human-like features, and limit minor use of therapy chatbots. 

 

Utah - 

  • Passed three bills out of committee: HB 320 (sandbox), SB 150 (health), HB 286 (frontier models/chatbots)
  • Chatbots - HB 438: Introduced by Rep. Fiefia (R), this bill would establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for AI companion chatbots, with a particular focus on protecting minors and addressing risks related to emotional dependency, self-harm, and harmful content. The bill would require providers to implement safety protocols, conduct ongoing assessments, enable independent evaluations, and publish regular public reports on safety measures. It also would set restrictions on harmful content and data sharing, mandatory disclosures that chatbots are not human, and clear account and data deletion options. 
  • ADMT - HB 450: Introduced by Rep. Schallenberger (R), this bill would update Utah's data privacy laws by introducing new definitions for automated decision making and profiling, including requiring the development of a data privacy framework that incorporates best practices for automated decision making. 

 

Vermont - 

  • Gen AI/Transparency - H 822: Introduced by Rep. Cina (D), this bill requires providers of generative AI systems to offer tools detecting AI generated content and to give users the option to watermark AI generated content. 
  • Transparency - H 821: Introduced by Rep. Cina (D), this bill mandates that any person or entity using generative AI to interact with consumers must clearly disclose, upon request, that the consumer is interacting with AI and not a human. If the interaction is high-risk, involving sensitive data or significant advice, the disclosure must be made prominently at the start of the interaction. 
  • Health - H 816: Introduced by Rep. Berbeco (D), this bill is an Illinois HB 1806-style bill. 
  • Health - H 814: Introduced by Rep. Cina (D), bill mandates that patient communication generated by AI must include a disclaimer and instructions for contacting a human provider, restricts advertising and data collection for mental health chatbots, and enumerates a list of “neurological rights.”
  • Liability - H 855: Introduced by Rep. Harvey (R), this bill prohibits claiming in court that harm was autonomously caused by artificial intelligence as a defense in civil lawsuits, placing accountability on those who develop, modify, or use AI systems.

 

Virginia - Transparency - SB 384 and HB 797 (companion bills, creating frameworks for IVOs to audit AI systems) both passed through committee.

 

Washington - Passed six bills passed out of committee: HB 1170 (GenAI / Transparency), SB 6284 (High risk / ADMT), HB 2503 (GenAI / Transparency), HB 2144 (Employment), HB 2481 (Data-Driven Pricing), HB 2157 (ADMT / High-Risk AI)



West Virginia - Health - HB 4770 (Illinois’ HB 1806-style bill) passed out of committee.



Wisconsin - 

  • Chatbots - AB 965: Introduced by Rep. Franklin (R), this is a California AB 1064-style bill (vetoed) restricting minor access to companion chatbots across a variety of use cases (e.g. not prioritizing “factual accuracy”).
  • Liability - AB 959: Introduced by Rep. Brill (R), this bill would clarify that AI is not a person and would provide statutory definitions for terms like developer, entity, and owner, in Wisconsin.

 

Federal

 

Federal - HUD Proposes Rollback of Disparate Impact Standard: The Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) has proposed removing its discriminatory effects regulations under the Fair Housing Act, which since 2013 have provided a regulatory framework for assessing disparate impact claims, even absent discriminatory intent. Under the proposal, HUD would remove these regulations and leave questions of disparate impact liability to the courts. If finalized, the change could have significant implications for algorithmic discrimination enforcement in housing, including precedent used in cases involving targeted advertising and automated decision-making systems.
