Welcome back to your weekly roundup of AI legislation!

 

Housekeeping 

 

📣 NEW BLOG POST: FPF released a new blog and chart, “Enacted State AI Laws 2023-2025,” identifying the 27 enacted AI laws across 14 states from 2023-2025, highlighting key compliance requirements and consumer rights, major legislative trends, and effective dates. Notably, most enacted AI laws are already effective as of 2026, requiring entities to begin navigating compliance obligations. 

 

Highlights 

 

  • Six bills passed first chamber this week in 3 states: Utah (HB 320, sandbox), Virginia (HB 797, AI safety) and (SB 796, chatbots), and Washington (HB 2225 and SB 5984, chatbots) and (HB 1170, genAI / transparency)
  • Nine bills passed committee this week in 6 states: Arizona (HB 2410, liability) and (HB 2311, chatbots), Iowa (SSB 3011, chatbots), Kentucky (HB 227, chatbots) and (HB 455, health), Oregon (SB 1546, chatbots), Tennessee (HB 1951, liability/ health) and (HB 1470, health), Utah (HB 276, genAI).
  • Utah AI bill draws federal attention: The White House reportedly urged Utah lawmakers to reconsider HB 286 (frontier models/ chatbots) signaling increased federal engagement in state AI legislation debates, particularly around frontier model safety proposals.
  • Novel liability proposal advances: Tennessee’s HB 1951, which would create a new “coercive suicide” felony offense for owners of AI systems that encourage or advise users to commit suicide, passed out of committee this week.
  • Federal: NIST launched a new AI Agent Standards Initiative to advance technical standards, interoperability, and security research for emerging autonomous AI agents.

 

Stuff from the States

 

Arizona - Two bills passed committee: HB 2410 (liability- AI communications treated as legally privileged professional communications) and HB 2311 (chatbots - disclosures for minor users of “conversational AI services”).

 

California - With the bill introduction deadline tomorrow, February 20, a surge of new proposals is expected on the final day of the filing window. Two newly introduced measures from this week include:

  • Chatbots - AB 1988: Introduced by Asm. Pellerin (D), this bill would require companion chatbots to implement structured interventions, including a 20-minute pause and referral to human crisis support, when detecting credible expressions of self-harm or harm to others, and mandates annual reporting.
  • ADMT/Employment - AB 1898: Introduced by Asm. Schultz (D), this bill would require employers to provide detailed written notice and annual updates to workers about the use of AI tools in employment decisions and workplace surveillance.

Colorado - 

  • Data-Driven Pricing/Employment - HB 1210: Introduced by Rep. Bacon (D), would prohibit the use of surveillance data and automated decision systems to set individualized prices or wages. 
  • Health - HB 1195: Introduced by Rep. Mabrey (D), this bill is modeled on Illinois’ HB 1806.

 

Hawaii - Transparency - HB 2357 (ban music streaming that is performed or attributed to an AI music artist) was deferred in committee and is unlikely to advance this session absent further action.

 

Iowa - 

  • Chatbots - SSB 3011 (disclosure requirements for chatbots (not minor specific)) passed through the Senate Technology Committee.
  • Bills were also introduced on chatbots (HF 2507) and data-driven pricing (HF 2469 and SF 2278). 

 

Indiana - Chatbots - S 1297: Introduced by the Senate Committee on State Affairs, this bill would establish disclosure, safety, and content requirements for public-facing conversational AI services, with enhanced protections for minors.

 

Kentucky - 

  • Two bills passed committee: HB 227 (chatbots - obligations on AI companion platforms, including verifiable parental consent and PRA) and HB 455 (health - modeled on Illinois’ HB 1806).
  • Chatbots/Health - HB 641: Introduced by Rep. Grossl (R), this bill would create strict privacy, transparency, and safety requirements for mental health chatbots in Kentucky, mandating clear disclosures and limiting data sharing.

 

Louisiana - AI Safety/ Transparency - HB 134: Introduced by Rep. Schlegel (R), this bill would prohibit interactive computer services from delivering or algorithmically promoting material harmful to minors to minor users. 

 

Maryland -

  • ADMT - HB 1399: Introduced by Del. Hill (D), this bill would impose strict transparency, accuracy, and anti-bias requirements on consumer reporting agencies that use algorithmic systems, mandating human oversight, regular audits, and consumer opt-out options.
  • Transparency/ AI Safety - HB 1261: Introduced by Del. Coley (D), this bill would establish stringent child safety, data privacy, and oversight requirements for AI-enabled toys, mandating parental controls, transparency, and would prohibit inappropriate, manipulative, or harmful content.
  • Bills were also introduced on chatbots (HB 1250) and data-driven pricing (SB 889).

 

Mississippi: Miscellaneous - HB 1723 passed the House. This bill would set a definition for artificial intelligence in Mississippi code, largely modeled on the OECD’s AI definition.

 

Oregon - Chatbots - SB 1546: (require AI companions to disclose AI interactions and implement safety protocols) passed through committee.

 

Pennsylvania - Chatbots - HB 2215: Introduced by Rep. McNeill (D), this bill would establish strict age verification, content restrictions, and data security requirements for AI chatbots to protect minors from harmful content and ensure responsible use.

 

Rhode Island -

  • Health - SB 2570: Introduced by Sen. Lauria (D), this bill would require healthcare providers and facilities to notify patients whenever artificial intelligence is used to document their in-person or telehealth visits.
  • ADMT/Employment - HB 7767: Introduced by Rep. Noret (D), this bill would establish strict regulations on the use of AI, automated decision systems, and electronic monitoring in employment, prioritizing worker privacy, transparency, anti-discrimination, and meaningful human oversight. 

 

Tennessee - Two bills passed committee: HB 1951 (liability/ health - creating a new “coercive suicide” felony offense for owners of an AI system that encourage/advise on committing suicide) and HB 1470 (health - prohibit the advertising of an AI system as a mental health professional).

 

Utah - 

  • Sandbox - HB 320 passed out of the House and heads to the Senate. This bill amends Utah’s existing regulatory sandbox (SB 149, 2024) by expanding the program by allowing applicants to apply for a regulatory mitigation agreement or “a joint interpretation agreement” (an agreement that clarifies the application of a provision of state law/ rule to AI). Among other changes to the sandpox program, this bill would direct the Office of AI Policy to perform regular audits of a participant’s application of AI while the regulatory mitigation/ joint interpretation agreement is in effect. Additionally, the bill would allow up to two extensions to be granted on these agreements. The bill would also make amendments to Utah’s Artificial Intelligence Learning Laboratory Program.
  • GenAI - HB 276 (provenance data requirements) passed committee.
  • White House Raises Concerns About Proposed Utah AI Bill: Reporting this week indicates that the White House has urged Utah lawmakers to reconsider HB 286, a bill that would require frontier AI developers to publish safety and child protection plans and establish whistleblower protections. The Administration stated it opposes the bill as inconsistent with its broader AI policy agenda. The outreach follows the federal executive order emphasizing opposition to diverging state-level AI regulation and signals increasing federal engagement in ongoing state legislative debates. Utah lawmakers have indicated discussions with federal officials are ongoing. Comparable bills have been introduced in Nebraska (LB 1083), Tennessee (HB 1898), and Illinois (HB 4705/ SB 3261).

 

Virginia - Two bills passed the first chamber:

  • Transparency - HB 797: This bill would establish a state licensing framework for independent verification organizations (IVOs) that assess whether AI systems meet defined safety and risk-mitigation standards. The Virginia CIO would oversee licensing, set independence and transparency requirements, and coordinate with an Artificial Intelligence Safety Advisory Council. Licensed IVOs would evaluate developers’ governance practices, risk controls, and mitigation measures, with authority to revoke verification if systems fail to meet acceptable risk thresholds. The bill also requires annual public reporting and creates an oversight structure intended to support third-party AI safety auditing.
  • Chatbots - SB 796: This bill would require covered entities (defined as chatbot operators with >500,000 monthly active users worldwide, excluding internal tools and limited customer-service systems) to detect and mitigate user “emotional dependence," while also prohibiting human misrepresentation and mandating crisis interventions when users show signs of self-harm. Among other requirements, if a covered entity becomes aware that a user faces an imminent risk of death or serious physical injury, the operator must make reasonable efforts within 24 hours to notify appropriate emergency services or law enforcement, to the extent practicable. The bill includes a private right of action.

 

Washington - Three bills passed the first chamber:

  • Chatbots - HB 2225 and SB 5984: These companion bills follow a California SB 243–style companion chatbot framework, while adding several new requirements. Similar to SB 243, operators must provide recurring disclosures (every three hours) that a chatbot is not human and must implement heightened protections when the operator knows a user is a minor (or, unlike SB 243, when the chatbot is “directed to” minors). The bills expand beyond SB 243 by prohibiting manipulative engagement practices for minors, including excessive praise, simulated romantic relationships, or encouraging minors to conceal interactions from parents or trusted adults. Operators would also be required to implement protocols to detect and respond to suicidal ideation.
  • GenAI / Transparency - HB 1170: This bill would require covered providers to make a provenance detection tool available to users. The tool must publicly return detected provenance data and be accessible via uploads, links, or API access without requiring users to visit the provider’s website. A covered provider may not collect or retain personal information, except under designated circumstances. Covered providers must give users the option to include a manifest disclosure indicating when image, audio, or video content was created or altered by the provider’s generative AI system.

Federal

 

NIST Launches AI Agent Standards Initiative: NIST’s Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) announced a new AI Agent Standards Initiative aimed at supporting the secure and interoperable deployment of autonomous AI agents. The initiative will focus on advancing industry-led technical standards, open protocols, and research on agent security and identity, in coordination with federal partners including NSF. NIST plans to release additional guidance and seek public input through RFIs, listening sessions, and future research outputs as AI agents become more widely deployed.
