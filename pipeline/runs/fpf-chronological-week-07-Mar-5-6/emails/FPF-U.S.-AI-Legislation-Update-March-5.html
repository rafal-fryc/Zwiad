Highlights 

 

  • First Two AI Bills of 2026 Sent to Governors’ Desks: Utah’s HB 320 (amending Utah’s existing regulatory sandbox) and Mississippi’s HB 1723 (adding a definition of “artificial intelligence” to Mississippi code) are both awaiting signature.
  • Utah Bills Pass Both Chambers and Await Amendment Concurrence: HB 450 (updating Utah's data privacy laws by introducing new definitions for automated decision making and profiling) and HB 276 (requiring generative AI providers to detect and disclose digital provenance data).
  • Three Other AI Bills Pass First Chamber: Arizona’s SB 1786 (GenAI), Utah’s SB 322 (Sandbox), and Florida's SB 482 (AI Bill of Rights).
  • Two Bills Passed Committee: Georgia’s SB 488 (Liability) and Oklahoma’s HB 3544 (Chatbots).
  • House Considers SAFE Bots Act: The House Energy & Commerce Committee is marking up a youth online safety package that includes the Kids Act, which incorporates the SAFE Bots Act establishing requirements for AI chatbots interacting with minors, including disclosures, crisis resources, and safety policies. The bill also would preempt conflicting state chatbot laws.

 

Stuff from the States

 

Arizona - GenAI Bill Passed First Chamber: SB 1786 would require genAI providers to embed provenance data in any video, image, or audio content that is created or “materially altered” (defined as significant changes to the perceived content or the content’s meaning) by their genAI system. Additionally, the genAI provider is required to include in the provenance data any information that relates to a reasonably identifiable individual. 

 

Connecticut - 

  • ADMT/Employment/Chatbots/Gen AI - SB 5: Introduced by the General Law Committee, this bill would implement transparency and anti-bias measures in automated employment-related decision processes. The bill would also require providers of AI companions to include protocols for detecting harmful content, and restricts minors’ interactions with those AI companions. The bill also requires AI generated content to be clearly marked and detectable. A public hearing on the bill was held earlier this week.
  • AG Issues Guidance on AI and Existing Laws: Attorney General Tong (D) released a memo outlining how existing state laws apply to AI systems. The guidance highlights several legal frameworks, including Connecticut's civil rights laws, privacy and data security laws (e.g. Connecticut Data Privacy Act), and consumer protection laws (antitrust law and the Connecticut Unfair Trade Practices Act) that may apply to AI systems used in multiple contexts such as employment, housing, lending, insurance, and targeted advertising. The memo signals that the Attorney General’s office intends to pursue enforcement actions in certain cases (e.g. algorithmic collusion coordinating rental pricing under antitrust law). The memo also encourages consumers and businesses to report potential violations and provides guidance to companies deploying AI on their legal obligations under existing law.

 

Florida - AI Bill of Rights Passes Senate But Unlikely to Move This Session - SB 482 (AI Bill of Rights) which would provide citizens certain AI rights, such as the right to limit control of minors’ access to AI or know if AI companies are collecting personal information, as well as requiring notice for chatbots, passed the Senate on Wednesday. However, the House Speaker told reporters that he will not be bringing the bill to the House Floor, citing the White House’s position on state AI regulation.

 

Georgia - Liability Bill Passed Committee - SB 488 would classify generative AI systems as personal property for product liability cases involving minors, establishes liability for product sellers, and imposes a duty to warn of risks.

 

Louisiana - The Louisiana Legislature convenes on Monday, March 9, following a wave of AI bill introductions this week ahead of the start of session.

  • Chatbots - HB 791: Introduced by Rep. Boyd (D), this bill would implement strict data minimization requirements for chatbot providers, prohibit using chat logs for advertising purposes, and prohibits chatbots from providing medical, legal, or financial advice.
  • Health - HB 475: Introduced by Rep. Berault (R), this bill would require healthcare professionals to obtain a patient’s verbal consent before utilizing AI transcription services during medical visits. 
  • ADMT/Employment - HB 421: Introduced by Rep. Cox (R), this bill would require employers to provide clear notice to job applicants when AI is used and prohibit using solely ADMT for discipline, termination, or deactivation decisions. 
  • Other introduced bills include: HB 686 (transparency), HB 425 (transparency), SB 362/HB 471 (data-driven pricing), and HB 734 (chatbots/AI safety).

 

Mississippi - AI Definition Bill Sent to Governor - HB 1723 passed both chambers and was sent to the Governor, who has until March 9 to sign the bill. The measure would add a definition of “artificial intelligence” to Mississippi code, largely modeled on the OECD’s AI definition.

 

New York - Asm. Bores Introduces New AI Chatbot Bill - A 10379: Introduced by Asm. Bores (D), the author of last year’s RAISE Act on frontier models, this bill would prohibit AI chatbots from offering features deemed unsafe for minors and would require age verification.

 

Oklahoma - Chatbot Bill Passed Committee - HB 3544 would prohibit chatbots from being made available to minors if they have human-like features and would require the implementation of reasonable age-verification systems, with additional requirements for therapeutic chatbots.

 

Pennsylvania -  Governor Announces AI Safety Actions: Governor Shapiro (D) announced several steps aimed at addressing risks from AI systems, particularly AI companion bots. The administration is establishing a new AI Enforcement Task Force and launching a formal complaint process to investigate whether AI-powered systems may be engaging in unlicensed professional practice, such as impersonating licensed healthcare providers. The effort will also involve coordination with the Attorney General to pursue potential consumer protection violations related to AI chatbots. The administration also released a multi-agency AI Literacy Toolkit designed to help residents understand the risks and benefits of AI tools.

 

Rhode Island - Liability - HB 8052: Introduced by Rep. Carson (D), this bill would establish a civil cause of action for injuries caused by AI, holding developers strictly liable for those harms. 

 

South Carolina - ADMT/High Risk AI - SB 963: Introduced by Sen. Leber (R), this bill would require developers and deployers of high risk AI systems to implement reasonable care to protect against foreseeable risks, including by adhering to recognized frameworks like NIST’s AI RMF or ISO. 

 

Utah - 

  • Sandbox Bill Heads to the Governor - HB 320 passed out of both chambers and was sent to the Governor. This bill amends Utah’s existing regulatory sandbox (SB 149, 2024) by expanding the program by allowing applicants to apply for a regulatory mitigation agreement or “a joint interpretation agreement” (an agreement that clarifies the application of a provision of state law/ rule to AI). Among other changes to the sandpox program, this bill would direct the Office of AI Policy to perform regular audits of a participant’s application of AI while the regulatory mitigation/ joint interpretation agreement is in effect. Additionally, the bill would allow up to two extensions to be granted on these agreements. The bill would also make amendments to Utah’s Artificial Intelligence Learning Laboratory Program.
  • Privacy/ADMT Bill Passed Both Chambers and Awaits Amendment Concurrence- HB 450 would update Utah's data privacy laws by introducing new definitions for automated decision making and profiling, including requiring the development of a data privacy framework that incorporates best practices for automated decision making. The bill would define automated decision making as “using personal data to make a decision about an individual through automated processing, without human review or intervention.” This definition differs from other states governing automated decision-making technology (ADMT), like California’s CCPA regulations. The CCPA, for example, defines ADMT more broadly as technology that replaces or substantially replaces human decision-making, including cases where humans rely on system outputs to make decisions. By contrast, Utah’s definition appears narrower, focusing specifically on decisions made entirely without human involvement.
  • GenAI Bill Passed Both Chambers and Awaits Amendment Concurrence - HB 276 would require large online platforms (>2 million monthly users) to detect, disclose, and preserve digital provenance data in distributed content and provide users with tools to inspect that information. The bill would also require generative AI providers (with >1 million monthly users) to embed latent disclosures in AI-generated or substantially modified content, and require device manufacturers to embed similar disclosures (e.g., manufacturer and creation date) in content captured by devices sold after January 1, 2028. The Division of Consumer Protection would enforce the provisions and could impose administrative fines. The bill also includes new protections and takedown procedures addressing non-consensual AI-generated intimate images.
  • Sandbox Bill Passed First Chamber - SB 322 would establish an Education Technology Regulatory Sandbox for public education, allowing three-year pilot programs for testing AI tools under defined safety, privacy, and oversight requirements. Approved pilots would require human-in-the-loop educator supervision, parental notice and opt-out rights, student data protections, safety testing, and evaluation of educational outcomes. The bill would set a series of requirements for the capabilities of the AI tools being piloted, such as prohibiting the tools from stimulating emotional dependency. Among the requirements, vendors must conduct annual red teaming exercises that test system responses to harmful requests that minors might reasonably make, like requests for assistance for self-harm or suicide. This bill follows SB 149 (2024) which created a broader AI sandbox program in Utah.

Federal

 

House Markup Includes SAFE Bots Act on AI Chatbots: Today the House Energy & Commerce Committee holds a full committee markup hearing on the youth privacy and online safety package introduced last November. The Committee will consider the KIDS Act, which combines 12 proposals introduced in the original package, including the Safeguarding Adolescents From Exploitative (SAFE) Bots Act. 

The SAFE Bots Act would regulate consumer-facing AI chatbots interacting with minors. While the bill broadly defines “AI chatbot” as any consumer-facing chatbot, it excludes websites or apps whose chat functions are only incidental to the primary purpose of the application or service, a sizeable exemption that may likely scope out customer service chatbots and possibly platform-based chat features and chatbots in gaming. Specifically, the bill would prohibit providers from offering a chatbot to a “covered user” (the provider knows is a minor) if the chatbot falsely claims to be a licensed professional; and require: 

  • Disclosures at the start of an interaction (and when asked by the user) confirming the chatbot is not human;
  • Crisis resources disclosure when a user expresses suicidal ideation;
  • Policies addressing harmful sexual material, gambling, and substance use; and
  • Policies providing for a “take a break” disclosure after three hours of continuous interaction.

Notably, the bill would preempt state laws that conflict with the title would be preempted (e.g., may impact laws like California’s SB 243 and New York’s S-3008C).  The SAFE Bots Act follows other federal proposals targeting minors’ chatbot use, including Sen. Hawley’s (R-MO) GUARD Act (prohibit AI companion chatbots for minors) and Sen. Husted’s (R-OH) CHAT Act (requiring age verification and other protections).

 

AI Innovation Bill Reintroduced - S 3952: Senators Young (R-IN) and Cantwell (D-WA) reintroduced the Future of AI Innovation Act, legislation aimed at strengthening U.S. leadership in AI research and standards development. The bill would formally authorize the NIST’s Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) to develop voluntary AI standards, benchmarks, and evaluation frameworks in collaboration with industry and federal agencies. It would also establish public-private AI testbeds with national laboratories to evaluate AI systems and support research in areas such as advanced manufacturing, robotics, and quantum technologies. Additional provisions include creating AI “grand challenge” prize competitions, expanding access to federal datasets for AI research, and promoting international collaboration on AI standards and R&D.
