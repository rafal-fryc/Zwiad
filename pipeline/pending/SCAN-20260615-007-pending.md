# California Superior Court Limits CIPA Pen Register Provisions to Telephone Communications in Blaker v. NetScout

**Jurisdiction:** California | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** May 27, 2026

## Summary [HIGH confidence]

On May 27, 2026, Judge Gary Roberts of the Los Angeles County Superior Court issued a significant ruling in [*Blaker v. NetScout Systems, Inc.*](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2026-05-29-mintz-privacy-team-secures-landmark-ruling-california), Case No. 25STCV31283, holding that California's pen register and trap-and-trace device provisions under the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA), [Cal. Penal Code §§ 638.50–638.53](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=638.51.), apply exclusively to telephone communications and do not extend to software development kits (SDKs) or other web analytics tools deployed on commercial websites. The court sustained the defendant's demurrer without leave to amend, dismissing the case with prejudice. The ruling provides defendants in the ongoing wave of CIPA class-action litigation with a compelling, text-based argument that pen register claims grounded in website tracking technologies are legally defective.

## Key Facts [HIGH confidence]

- **Case and court:** *Blaker v. NetScout Systems, Inc.*, Case No. 25STCV31283, Los Angeles County Superior Court, decided May 27, 2026, by Judge Gary Roberts — reported by [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2026-05-29-mintz-privacy-team-secures-landmark-ruling-california) and [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/cipa-win-california-superior-court-sustains-dismissal-website-tracking-claims).

- **Claim at issue:** The plaintiff alleged that NetScout violated [Cal. Penal Code § 638.51](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=638.51.) by deploying a data-collection SDK on its website that captured visitor communications without notice or consent, arguing the SDK constituted a prohibited "pen register" or "trap and trace device" — per [Robinson+Cole analysis](https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2026/06/big-win-for-companies-facing-cipa-website-tracking-lawsuits/).

- **Holding:** The court held that CIPA's pen register and trap-and-trace provisions apply to telephone communications only, not to software operating on commercial websites. The court adopted defendant's position after analyzing statutory construction and legislative intent — per [Loeb & Loeb commentary](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/passle/2026/06/decoding-the-code-how-simple-rigorously-accurate-advocacy-may-have-broken-the-cipa-litigation-impass).

- **Outcome:** Demurrer sustained without leave to amend. The plaintiff cannot replead, and the case is dismissed with prejudice — per [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/cipa-win-california-superior-court-sustains-dismissal-website-tracking-claims).

- **Statutory context:** The pen register and trap-and-trace provisions (§§ 638.50–638.53) were added to CIPA in 2015 via [AB 929](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=201520160AB929) — at a time when internet tracking technologies were already ubiquitous. Since 2023, plaintiffs' attorneys have filed hundreds of claims under § 638.51 against companies with third-party SDKs, pixels, and analytics tools on their websites — per [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/the-cipa-seesaw-netscout-snags-a-win-but-the-web-tracking-litigation-nightmare-isnt-over/).

- **No binding precedent:** As a trial-level California Superior Court decision, this ruling carries persuasive authority but does not bind other courts in the state. Courts across California have reached divergent conclusions on the same question, with [Fisher Phillips reporting](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/courts-still-divided-on-whether-california-privacy-law-applies-to-website-tracking) four conflicting rulings issued within a 10-day span.

- **Separate § 631 wiretapping claims not addressed:** This ruling addresses only CIPA's pen register provisions (§§ 638.50–638.53). CIPA § 631 (the separate wiretapping provision frequently invoked against session replay tools and chat widgets) was not at issue in *Blaker* and remains contested — per [National Law Review coverage of Ninth Circuit developments](https://natlawreview.com/article/ninth-circuit-scrutinizes-california-wiretap-law-will-website-chat-and-analytics).

## Action Items

- **Pending § 638.51 defendants:** If your company faces a pending CIPA pen register claim, cite *Blaker v. NetScout Systems, Inc.* (Case No. 25STCV31283) in any forthcoming demurrer or motion to dismiss. While persuasive only, the ruling's statutory-construction rationale — grounded in the statute's telephone-centric text and 2015 legislative history — provides a structured framework courts can adopt.

- **Distinguish § 631 from § 638.51:** Confirm which CIPA provisions are actually pleaded in any complaint you receive. This ruling does not affect § 631 wiretapping claims, which remain more actively litigated and carry a different legal framework. Do not conflate the two.

- **Do not over-rely on this ruling:** Other California courts have allowed functionally identical § 638.51 claims to proceed. Plaintiff firms are expected to continue filing, steering toward judges with a more expansive view of the statute. Legal strategy should account for the possibility that a given judge will reach a different outcome.

- **Audit web-tracking technologies:** Even if § 638.51 claims are now more vulnerable to dismissal, other CIPA provisions, CCPA, and state AG enforcement under the Delete Act remain live risks. Conduct an inventory of all third-party SDKs, pixels, analytics tools, and chat widgets on California-facing websites and evaluate consent mechanisms.

- **Monitor appellate developments:** A California Court of Appeal decision resolving the circuit split on § 638.51 applicability could significantly shift the landscape in either direction. Flag this issue for ongoing monitoring.
