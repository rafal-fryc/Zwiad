# Wyssta Services Settles Pixel Tracking Class Action for $12.67 Million Over Delta Dental Member Portal

**Jurisdiction:** Illinois | **Category:** Privacy | **Date:** 2026-06-25

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

Wyssta Services, Inc., a dental and vision insurance claims administrator and subsidiary of Delta Dental of Wisconsin, has agreed to pay $12,670,284 to settle a class action lawsuit alleging it secretly embedded advertising and analytics tracking technologies — including cookies and pixels — on the Delta Dental member portal (my.deltadentalcoversme.com) without user knowledge or consent. The case, *Michael Feeler v. Wyssta Services, Inc.*, Case No. 2026LA000050, was filed in the Circuit Court of Sangamon County, Illinois, and received preliminary approval on May 7, 2026. The settlement class covers approximately 767,896 U.S. individuals who held accounts on the portal between January 23, 2021 and January 23, 2025. Alleged violations include the federal Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) and the Illinois Eavesdropping Statute. Claims must be submitted by August 20, 2026, with a final approval hearing set for September 9, 2026.

The settlement is the latest in a sustained wave of pixel tracking class actions targeting healthcare-adjacent organizations, which have generated aggregate settlements exceeding $135 million since 2023. It is particularly notable because it reaches a dental insurance claims administrator operating a member-facing web portal — a category of healthcare service provider not previously prominent in this litigation wave.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### The Pixel Tracking Litigation Wave

Beginning in earnest around 2022 and accelerating through 2025 and 2026, a generation of class action plaintiffs has deployed the Federal Wiretap Act (Title I of the ECPA, [18 U.S.C. §§ 2510–2523](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/part-I/chapter-119)) and its state counterparts against website operators that embedded third-party advertising and analytics pixels — code snippets from companies such as Meta (Facebook Pixel), Google (Google Tag Manager), TikTok, and others — on web pages where users transmitted sensitive information. The legal theory, broadly stated, is that these pixels "intercept" users' electronic communications and transmit them to third parties in real time, without the user's knowledge or consent, constituting electronic eavesdropping under federal and state wiretapping statutes.

After a [235% increase in ECPA lawsuit filings in 2025](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/cipa-ecpa-website-tracking-privacy-litigation-in-2026), the United States is on pace for approximately 460 new such lawsuits in 2026 — another 60% year-over-year increase, according to [Barnes & Thornburg's 2026 CIPA/ECPA litigation survey](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1788754/cipaecpa-website-tracking-privacy-litigation-in-2026). Healthcare and healthcare-adjacent organizations have been disproportionately targeted because (1) the data transmitted through pixels on health portals is inherently sensitive; (2) HIPAA violations can serve as the predicate "crime or tort" for overcoming ECPA's party-consent defense (discussed below); and (3) statutory damages under the ECPA run to the greater of $100 per day of violation or $10,000, creating massive aggregate exposure across large class populations.

### Illinois Statutory Framework

**Federal ECPA / Wiretap Act.** The core federal provision at issue, [18 U.S.C. § 2511](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2511), prohibits intentional interception of electronic communications. One-party consent is a general defense — a party to a communication can consent to its interception — but this defense is eliminated by the "crime-tort exception" under § 2511(2)(d): one-party consent does not apply where the interception is "for the purpose of committing any criminal or tortious act in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States or of any State." Courts have split on whether a purely commercial tracking motive satisfies this exception, though by early 2026 multiple federal circuits have allowed ECPA claims predicated on HIPAA or state-law privacy violations to survive motions to dismiss. A pending First Circuit decision on the crime-tort exception is expected to be among the most significant online wiretap liability rulings in years, per [Barnes & Thornburg's analysis](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/cipa-ecpa-website-tracking-privacy-litigation-in-2026).

**Illinois Eavesdropping Statute.** The Illinois Eavesdropping Statute, [720 ILCS 5/14-1 et seq.](https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/072000050K14-1.htm), makes Illinois an all-party consent jurisdiction for electronic communications. Under this statute, a "private electronic communication" encompasses "any transfer of signs, signals, writing, images, sounds, data or intelligence . . . transmitted in whole or part by a wire, radio, pager, computer . . . when the sending or receiving party intends the electronic communication to be private under circumstances reasonably justifying that expectation." The statute prohibits any individual from intercepting, recording, or transcribing such communications without the consent of all parties. The statute was narrowed in 2014 (PA 98-1142, signed December 30, 2014) to focus on *surreptitious* recording of private communications, but that narrowing did not eliminate its application to nonconsensual interception of sensitive user inputs transmitted through a website. Under the [Illinois Eavesdropping Statute's prohibition on eavesdropping devices](https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/072000050K14-2.htm), violations are a Class 4 felony, and the statute provides a civil right of action. The combination of federal ECPA and state eavesdropping claims is now a standard template in Illinois pixel tracking complaints, as detailed by [legal analysis from the Chicago Business Litigation Lawyer Blog](https://www.chicagobusinesslitigationlawyerblog.com/when-your-own-website-becomes-the-wiretap-defending-illinois-businesses-against-pixel-tracking-class-actions-under-the-federal-wiretap-act/).

### Prior Illinois Healthcare Pixel Settlements

The *Feeler v. Wyssta Services* matter is not an isolated event in Illinois. A closely analogous case, *Doe v. Southern Illinois Healthcare Enterprises*, was filed in Williamson County Circuit Court and alleged that Southern Illinois Healthcare Enterprises, Southern Illinois Hospital Services, and Southern Illinois Medical Services deployed tracking pixels (including the Meta pixel) on patient-facing websites without consent, transmitting identifiable health information to Meta and other third parties. That case received preliminary settlement approval on March 16, 2026, with class members eligible for up to $17.50 cash and one year of privacy monitoring services, per the [HIPAA Journal's coverage of the settlement](https://www.hipaajournal.com/southern-illinois-healthcare-enterprises-pixel-settlement/) and the [settlement website](https://www.southernillinoishealthpixelsettlement.com/).

At the national level, high-profile healthcare pixel settlements between 2023 and March 2026 have exceeded $135 million in aggregate, according to [Duane Morris LLP's June 2025 litigation analysis](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/healthcare_tracking_pixel_litigation_signals_continued_challenges_defendants_0625.html). Named defendants have included Kaiser Permanente ($47.5 million), Mass General Brigham ($18.4 million), Aspen Dental Management ($18.5 million), Advocate Aurora ($12.2 million), and dozens of other health systems and insurance operators.

## Detailed Analysis [HIGH confidence]

### The Defendant and the Portal at Issue

Wyssta Services, Inc. is a subsidiary of Delta Dental of Wisconsin. It functions as a Health Care Benefit Manager, performing enrollment, claims processing, and customer service on behalf of Delta Dental plan members in Wisconsin and at least 12 additional states. The portal at the center of this litigation, my.deltadentalcoversme.com, served as the online member account system through which Delta Dental plan members could access their coverage information, dental claims history, and benefit details. As an insurance claims administrator handling healthcare benefit data, Wyssta occupied a position of structural trust with respect to sensitive member data — a factor likely to have influenced both the litigation strategy and the settlement value.

### Allegations and Legal Claims

The complaint in *Feeler v. Wyssta Services* alleged that Wyssta embedded advertising and analytics tracking technologies — described generically in settlement materials as "cookies and pixels" — on the member portal without users' knowledge or consent. The specific third-party platforms to which data was allegedly transmitted are not identified in publicly available settlement notices (official settlement notice PDF available at the [settlement website](https://wysstaservicesclassaction.com/)), though the pattern in similar cases strongly suggests Meta Pixel, Google Analytics/Tag Manager, or comparable advertising infrastructure.

The operative legal claims were:

1. **Violation of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, 18 U.S.C. §§ 2510–2523** — specifically the Wiretap Act's prohibition on intentional interception of electronic communications. Plaintiffs likely invoked the crime-tort exception to ECPA's one-party consent defense, arguing that the interception was in furtherance of tortious or criminal conduct (potentially HIPAA violations or state privacy torts), as courts have allowed in similar healthcare settings.

2. **Violation of the Illinois Eavesdropping Statute, 720 ILCS 5/14-2** — the state analog requiring all-party consent for interception of private electronic communications. Illinois courts have allowed eavesdropping claims based on pixel tracking to proceed, with plaintiffs arguing that member portal logins and benefit inquiries constitute "private electronic communications" the users intended to be private.

Wyssta denied all allegations. The defendant's position in settling was to avoid the expense, risk, and disruption of protracted litigation and a potential trial — a standard litigation posture in this category of class action, where the volume of affected class members creates disproportionate statutory damages exposure even if per-member actual harm is modest.

### Settlement Structure and Distribution

The total settlement fund is $12,670,284. Distribution terms, per the [official class action settlement notice](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/642f23e5f54b794300ac12a6/6a399fa0237c619e0ed3ce3a_EHJC5iwbAa9J7k9qI9iLr79dj1ckgKA7qyTtPT1KaNo.pdf) and press release:

- **Class definition:** All U.S. individuals who held an account on my.deltadentalcoversme.com between January 23, 2021 and January 23, 2025.
- **Estimated class size:** Approximately 767,896 individuals, per court documents.
- **Per-member payment:** Up to $16.50 per class member who submits a timely, valid claim form.
- **Claims deadline:** August 20, 2026.
- **Final approval hearing:** September 9, 2026 at 1:00 p.m., Circuit Court of Sangamon County, Illinois.
- **Preliminary approval:** May 7, 2026.

The settlement notice and claim form are administered through [wysstaservicesclassaction.com](https://wysstaservicesclassaction.com/). Settlement class members can also access the [PR Newswire announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/did-you-hold-an-account-at-mydeltadentalcoversmecom-between-january-23-2021-and-january-23-2025-you-may-be-eligible-for-cash-payment-from-a-class-action-settlement-302803604.html) for basic eligibility information.

The $12.67 million fund represents a per-class-member exposure of approximately $16.50 — a figure consistent with the broader healthcare pixel settlement landscape (the Southern Illinois Healthcare settlement also paid up to $17.50 per member). Attorney fees and administrative costs will be paid from the fund before distribution, meaning actual per-member payments may be lower than $16.50 depending on the claim rate and fee award.

### Legal Significance: The Crime-Tort Exception in the Healthcare Context

The pivotal legal mechanism enabling this category of litigation is the crime-tort exception to ECPA's one-party consent rule. [Federal courts in Illinois and New York](https://www.bytebacklaw.com/2025/09/healthcare-website-tracking-lessons-from-four-recent-ecpa-rulings/) have allowed ECPA claims predicated on HIPAA violations to survive motions to dismiss, reasoning that a HIPAA violation constitutes the requisite predicate "crime or tort." This has been a significant threshold litigation question: in the absence of a HIPAA or other statutory violation as the predicate, some courts have ruled that purely commercial tracking purposes are insufficient to satisfy the crime-tort exception.

However, courts remain divided. Some have dismissed ECPA pixel tracking claims where plaintiffs could not adequately allege that the defendant's primary purpose was tortious rather than commercial. The split creates meaningful geographic variation in litigation risk, and the outcome of pending appellate decisions — particularly the First Circuit case noted by [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/cipa-ecpa-website-tracking-privacy-litigation-in-2026) — could significantly alter the litigation landscape. As [Troutman Pepper's privacy group noted in April 2026](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/the-ecpa-a-federal-private-right-of-action-for-privacy-policy-inaccuracies/), ECPA is now functioning as a de facto federal private right of action for privacy policy inaccuracies — a role far beyond what Congress originally contemplated for the statute.

The *Feeler v. Wyssta Services* case settled at the state circuit court level in Illinois rather than in federal court, which is notable. State court proceedings under the Illinois Eavesdropping Statute may face somewhat different procedural and jurisdictional dynamics than federal ECPA claims litigated in the Northern or Central Districts of Illinois. The [Chicago Business Litigation Lawyer Blog's April 2026 analysis](https://www.chicagobusinesslitigationlawyerblog.com/the-tracking-pixel-lawsuit-wave-hits-illinois-why-the-crime-or-tort-exception-argument-is-splitting-the-federal-courts/) notes that this court split on the crime-tort exception argument is materially affecting strategy in Illinois cases.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Entities

The *Feeler v. Wyssta Services* settlement has implications beyond the immediate parties for the following categories of organizations:

**Dental insurance administrators and managed care organizations.** The case demonstrates that ECPA and state eavesdropping exposure is not limited to hospital systems or large health networks. Claims administrators operating member-facing web portals — even where those portals are branded under a parent insurer's name — face equivalent exposure. Wyssta's position as a third-party administrator operating a portal on behalf of Delta Dental entities underscores that intermediary organizations can be independently liable for tracking choices on portals they control technically, regardless of whose brand the portal carries.

**Multi-state dental benefit plan operators.** Wyssta administered individual and family dental plans to Wisconsin residents and Delta Dental member companies in 12 additional states. The class was defined nationally (all U.S. individuals with portal accounts), reinforcing that a single tracking deployment on a nationally accessible portal creates nationwide class exposure. The settling class of approximately 767,896 individuals illustrates the scale of exposure available to plaintiffs.

**Any organization with authenticated user portals in health, dental, or vision insurance.** Authenticated portals — where users log in to access claims, coverage, or benefits information — are higher-risk than public marketing websites because users have a heightened reasonable expectation of privacy in the data they transmit after authentication. Courts are more likely to find that authenticated session inputs constitute "private electronic communications" under the Illinois statute and its analogs in other states.

### Compliance and Remediation Outlook

Driven by this litigation wave, [Fisher Phillips LLP's multi-court analysis](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/multi-court-split-on-website-tracking-federal-wiretapping-claims-creates-compliance-confusion) identifies six compliance strategies for organizations with web tracking exposure. [Barnes & Thornburg's 2026 assessment](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/cipa-ecpa-website-tracking-privacy-litigation-in-2026) is sharper: organizations that still treat website tracking as a pure marketing issue are materially underestimating class action exposure. The recommended posture in 2026 is to treat tracking technologies as regulated data infrastructure — inventoried, classified, constrained, and consent-documented — with particular attention to ensuring that legal disclosures match technical reality.

[McDermott Will & Emery's healthcare-specific analysis](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/managing-ecpa-exposure-in-healthcare-web-tracking/) recommends that healthcare and healthcare-adjacent organizations specifically: (1) audit all third-party scripts and pixels deployed on authenticated or sensitive pages; (2) review whether any such scripts are present on pages that handle health, claims, or benefits data; (3) update privacy policies and consent mechanisms to accurately disclose tracking; and (4) evaluate whether existing cookie consent infrastructure captures the type of consent required under state wiretapping statutes (which generally demand affirmative, informed consent rather than opt-out).

### Pending Proceedings

The settlement is not yet final. The court will hold a final approval hearing on September 9, 2026. Prior to that date:
- Class members who wish to object must do so before the hearing.
- Class members who wish to exclude themselves from the settlement must opt out.
- All valid claims must be submitted by August 20, 2026.

If the court denies final approval — an uncommon but not unprecedented outcome in large class settlements — the case would likely return to active litigation.

## Action Items

- **Before August 20, 2026:** If your organization has current or former Delta Dental plan member accounts that may have been active at my.deltadentalcoversme.com between January 23, 2021 and January 23, 2025, evaluate whether class counsel's notice was received and whether participation in the settlement process is appropriate.
- **Immediately:** Conduct a technical audit of all third-party scripts, pixels, and tracking tags deployed on any authenticated member or customer portals, particularly those handling insurance, claims, benefits, or health-related data. Disable or explicitly consent-gate any advertising or analytics pixels on these pages.
- **Within 30 days:** Review privacy policy and cookie disclosure language against actual tracking deployments. Where disclosures understate or omit third-party tracking, update disclosures promptly. Retroactive disclosure gaps are a core element of ECPA and Illinois Eavesdropping Statute class action complaints.
- **Ongoing:** Monitor the pending First Circuit ruling on the ECPA crime-tort exception, which is expected to clarify when commercial tracking purposes are sufficient to satisfy that exception. A plaintiff-favorable ruling would expand ECPA exposure for organizations that do not handle HIPAA-covered data.
- **Governance:** If your organization is a third-party administrator or claims processor operating web portals on behalf of an insurer, confirm in contract and practice which party is responsible for tracking technology compliance and privacy disclosures on jointly operated portals — the *Feeler v. Wyssta* fact pattern (administrator operates portal, brand is insurer's) shows both can face independent exposure.
- **Monitor September 9, 2026:** Final approval hearing in *Feeler v. Wyssta Services*. If denied, the matter returns to litigation and plaintiffs may pursue more aggressive legal theories.
