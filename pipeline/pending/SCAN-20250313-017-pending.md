# The Evolving Framework of Data Governance: A Global Perspective

**Jurisdiction:** Multi-jurisdiction (California, Japan, Canada, Brazil, EU, Quebec) | **Category:** Privacy | **Development Type:** Guidance | **Date:** March 13, 2025

---

## Executive Summary [HIGH confidence]

A March 2025 [Morgan Lewis & Bockius LLP client alert published via Lexology](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/the-evolving-framework-of-data-governance-a-global-perspective) surveys the rapid global proliferation of GDPR-inspired data privacy frameworks across four major non-EU jurisdictions: Japan, Canada, Brazil, and the United States. The alert documents how each jurisdiction is at a distinct stage of regulatory maturity — Japan is amending its Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) with new enforcement teeth and AI-friendly carve-outs; Canada's ambitious Bill C-27 died on the Order Paper when Parliament was prorogued in January 2025; Brazil's Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados (ANPD) has escalated enforcement significantly, including high-profile actions against Meta and X Corp; and eight US states activated comprehensive privacy laws in 2025. Businesses operating across multiple jurisdictions face compounding compliance obligations with no unified federal standard in sight.

---

## Background [HIGH confidence]

The EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which became enforceable in May 2018, set the global benchmark for comprehensive data protection law. Its core principles — lawful basis, data minimization, purpose limitation, individual rights, mandatory breach notification, and administrative fines — have become the template that non-EU governments reference when drafting their own frameworks.

By 2025, this diffusion has produced a fragmented global landscape. The [IAPP US State Privacy Laws overview](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-laws-overview) documents 19 comprehensive state-level privacy laws enacted in the United States as of the end of 2024 (Rhode Island, enacted June 2024 as the 19th state, takes effect January 1, 2026). At the international level, the EU's adequacy framework has expanded to 15-16 jurisdictions as of late 2025, including Japan, South Korea, and the UK, according to [GDPR cross-border transfer tracking by Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/cross-border-data-transfers-2025-guide).

For multinational organizations, this proliferation means layered obligations across domestic and foreign data protection regimes that share GDPR DNA but diverge significantly in scope, enforcement, and sector-specific requirements.

## Key Findings [HIGH confidence]

### Japan: APPI Amendment Bill Approved by Cabinet

Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC) finalized a major revision to the Act on the Protection of Personal Information. The Japanese Cabinet approved the amendment bill on [April 7, 2026](https://dig.watch/updates/japan-appi-personal-data-ai-fines), with the amended law estimated to take effect in 2027.

Key provisions of the APPI amendment include:

- **Administrative monetary penalties**: Introduction of a formal fine system. For violations involving more than 1,000 data subjects where the business derived financial benefit, failed to exercise reasonable care, and data subjects were actually or likely harmed, fines may equal the profits derived from the violation. This marks a significant departure from Japan's historically guidance-heavy enforcement model. ([Anderson Mori & Tomotsune analysis](https://www.amt-law.com/en/insights/others/publication_0029311_en_001/))
- **Children's data protections**: New explicit obligations for handling minors' personal information, including age-defined consent requirements. ([IAPP coverage of PPC interim summary](https://iapp.org/news/a/japan-s-dpa-publishes-interim-summary-of-amendments-to-data-protection-regulations))
- **Biometric data regulation**: New consent requirements and specific obligations for facial recognition and other biometric data processing. ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202604/japan-introduces-new-rules-on-biometric-data-in-appi-amendment-bill))
- **AI training carve-out**: Expanded consent exemptions for statistical processing and AI development, including use of sensitive personal data without consent for these purposes. ([The Register, April 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/04/08/japan_privacy_law_changes_ai/))
- **Reduced breach notification requirements**: Some relaxation of breach notification obligations in limited contexts.

### Canada: Bill C-27 Dead; PIPEDA Remains in Force [HIGH confidence]

Canada's ambitious three-in-one privacy and AI bill, [Bill C-27](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/44-1/c-27), died when Parliament was prorogued on January 6, 2025. The bill had been under detailed clause-by-clause review by the Standing Committee on Industry and Technology (INDU) when prorogation halted all legislative work.

Bill C-27 contained three distinct acts:
1. **Consumer Privacy Protection Act (CPPA)** — intended to replace Part I of PIPEDA
2. **Personal Information and Data Protection Tribunal Act (PIDPTA)** — establishing an independent enforcement tribunal
3. **Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)** — Canada's first proposed federal AI law

A snap federal election in April 2025 further delayed the prospect of re-tabling. [Gowling WLG analysis](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2025/federal-privacy-reform) notes that there is now broad consensus that the next iteration of privacy legislation will decouple AI regulation from privacy reform.

Canada currently operates under PIPEDA (enacted in 2000). Quebec's Law 25 (Bill 64), which reached full implementation in September 2023, provides substantially stronger protections for Quebec residents and de facto serves as the benchmark for Canadian businesses awaiting federal reform.

### Brazil: ANPD Enforcement Escalation [HIGH confidence]

Brazil's LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados), in force since 2020, is modeled closely on the GDPR. The ANPD has escalated from a cautious early enforcement posture to active sanctioning across sectors, with fines totaling approximately BRL 98 million (~USD 20 million) between 2023 and 2025.

Notable 2024 enforcement actions:

- **Meta (July 2024)**: The ANPD issued a preventive measure requiring immediate suspension of Meta's use of Brazilian users' personal data to train its AI systems. The order was enforceable by a daily fine of BRL 50,000 (~USD 9,000). The action rested on findings that Meta relied on "legitimate interest" as an inadequate legal basis for AI training. On approximately August 30, 2024, the ANPD suspended the preventive measure after Meta made adjustments and submitted a compliance plan — including updated user notices and opt-out mechanisms — that the ANPD accepted as resolving the identified deficiencies. ([FPF analysis](https://fpf.org/blog/processing-of-personal-data-for-ai-training-in-brazil-takeaways-from-anpds-preliminary-decisions-in-the-meta-case/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/22305-announced-suspension-of-preventive-measures-and-approval-of-compliance-plan-in-anpd-investigation-into-metas-privacy-policy-allowing-the-use-of-personal-data-for-ai-training))
- **X Corp (December 2024)**: The ANPD ordered X to suspend data processing from children's and adolescents' accounts to train its Grok AI system.
- **ByteDance/TikTok**: The ANPD in December 2024 ordered ByteDance to strengthen age verification mechanisms on TikTok.
- **DPO failures**: A monitoring proceeding was opened against twenty large companies that failed to appoint a Data Protection Officer or provided inadequate communication channels.

The EU Commission published a draft adequacy decision for Brazil on September 5, 2025, and the EDPB adopted a positive opinion on November 4, 2025 (Opinion 28/2025).

### United States: Eight State Laws Active in 2025 [HIGH confidence]

**State-level proliferation.** Eight comprehensive state privacy laws became effective during 2025: Delaware (DPDPA), Iowa (ICDPA), Nebraska (NDPA), New Hampshire (NHPA), New Jersey, Maryland, Minnesota, and Tennessee. These joined 11 previously effective state laws, bringing the total number of enacted comprehensive state privacy laws to 19. (Rhode Island enacted its Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act in June 2024 as the 19th state; it takes effect January 1, 2026 and is counted in the enacted total.)

Notable features across the 2025 cohort:
- **Delaware**: Covers most nonprofits and educational institutions, with only narrow exemptions — nonprofits dedicated exclusively to preventing insurance crime are exempt, and FERPA-regulated data processed by educational institutions is exempt at the data level. This remains unusually broad applicability compared to most state frameworks.
- **Minnesota**: Consumers can request a list of specific third parties to whom their data was disclosed; rights regarding profiling decisions including the right to know the reasons and to appeal.

Note on Oregon: Oregon's Consumer Privacy Act (OCPA) was enacted in 2023 and took effect for most entities on July 1, 2024. Oregon is not among the eight 2025-effective laws. Its nonprofit-coverage provisions took effect July 1, 2025, extending OCPA obligations to most nonprofits. Businesses with Oregon nonprofit clients should assess compliance with this extended scope.

**California CPPA rulemaking.** The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) Board on July 24, 2025 adopted final regulations on automated decision-making technology (ADMT), cybersecurity audits, and risk assessments. Key compliance dates:
- Risk assessment requirements: compliance by January 1, 2026
- ADMT opt-out / appeal rights: compliance by January 1, 2027
- Risk assessment summary submission to CPPA: by April 1, 2028

**Federal stasis.** No federal comprehensive privacy law has been enacted. The American Privacy Rights Act (APRA) did not reach a floor vote before the end of the 118th Congress.

## Legal Analysis and Implications [MEDIUM confidence]

### Convergence Around GDPR Principles, Divergence in Mechanics

The global trend confirms convergence around core GDPR principles — lawful basis for processing, data minimization, transparency, individual rights, mandatory breach notification, and regulatory enforcement — but significant divergence persists in:

1. **Enforcement architecture**: The EU model uses a single lead supervisory authority for cross-border processing. Japan uses the PPC as a single national authority with historically weak enforcement. Brazil's ANPD is ascending toward aggressive enforcement. US enforcement is fragmented across state attorneys general, sector-specific regulators, and the CPPA.

2. **AI training carve-outs**: Japan's pending APPI amendment explicitly allows sensitive data use for AI without consent. The EU AI Act and GDPR interact to constrain AI training on sensitive data. Brazil treated AI training as a standard data processing question governed by lawful basis requirements. The US has no unified federal approach.

3. **Cross-border transfer mechanisms**: EU adequacy decisions cover Japan, South Korea, the UK, and — pending — Brazil. US organizations rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

### The "GDPR Plus" Trend at State Level

Several US state laws enacted in 2024-2025 incorporate features not found in the original GDPR, including specific AI profiling rights (Minnesota), explicit minors' protections tied to age verification (Oregon, Connecticut), and nonprofit coverage (Delaware, Oregon). This "GDPR Plus" trend at the state level exceeds EU baseline requirements in some dimensions.

### Canada's Regulatory Gap

Canada's continued reliance on PIPEDA (effective 2000) creates a recognized adequacy gap relative to GDPR standards. The EU's adequacy decision for Canada was last reviewed in 2001. Quebec's Law 25 provides stronger protections for Quebec-resident data. This creates a practical risk for EU-Canada data transfers if the EC conducts a formal re-adequacy review before federal reform is enacted.

## What This Means for Businesses

1. **Japan operations**: Begin reviewing data processing inventories against the anticipated 2027 APPI amendment requirements, particularly for biometric data, children's data, and AI training datasets. Track the official bill text as it moves through the National Diet.

2. **Canada operations**: Do not assume federal privacy reform will arrive quickly. Align Canadian practices with Quebec's Law 25 as the de facto federal benchmark. Monitor the 45th Parliament's legislative agenda for re-tabling of privacy reform legislation.

3. **Brazil operations**: Treat the ANPD as an active, escalating enforcer. AI training programs using Brazilian user data require careful legal basis analysis — "legitimate interest" has been explicitly found inadequate in this context. DPO appointments and communication channels must be operationally functional. Note that the Meta enforcement action was resolved in August 2024, but the ANPD's willingness to act swiftly — and the compliance plan framework it imposed — sets binding expectations for all AI training programs in Brazil.

4. **US multi-state operations**: Reassess applicability thresholds for the eight laws that took effect in 2025. Begin gap analysis for CPPA's ADMT and risk assessment requirements given January 1, 2026 and January 1, 2027 compliance deadlines. Organizations serving Oregon nonprofits should confirm OCPA compliance as nonprofit provisions took effect July 1, 2025.

5. **Cross-border transfers**: Document transfer mechanisms for each corridor (EU-US, EU-Japan, EU-Canada, EU-Brazil). The EU-US Data Privacy Framework requires annual re-certification. Monitor the EU-Brazil adequacy process.

6. **Data governance infrastructure**: The Morgan Lewis alert's core thesis is that robust data governance infrastructure — including data mapping, interoperability documentation, and processing registers — is increasingly a regulatory prerequisite rather than a best practice.
