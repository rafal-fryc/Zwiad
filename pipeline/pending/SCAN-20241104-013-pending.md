# Illinois IDOL Guidance Clarifies E-Verify Is Not Prohibited for Private Employers

**Jurisdiction:** Illinois | **Category:** Privacy / Employment | **Date:** November 4, 2024

## Executive Summary [MEDIUM confidence]

On October 29, 2024, the Illinois Department of Labor (IDOL) issued [FAQ guidance](https://labor.illinois.gov/faqs/right-to-privacy-in-the-workplace-e-verify.html) clarifying that the [Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act, 820 ILCS 55](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=2398&ChapterID=68) does not prohibit private employers from voluntarily enrolling in and using E-Verify. This guidance responded to widespread employer confusion following the August 2024 amendments to the Act (SB0508 / Public Act 103-879, signed by Governor Pritzker on August 9, 2024), which expanded employee protections when discrepancies arise during employment eligibility verification. The IDOL confirmed that voluntary E-Verify use remains permissible, but any employer that participates in E-Verify must comply with the Act's notification, anti-retaliation, and procedural requirements, effective January 1, 2025. Noncompliance carries civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation. A subsequent federal preemption challenge by the DOJ was dismissed on August 19, 2025, confirming the law's validity.

## Background [HIGH confidence]

### Illinois Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act

The [Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act, 820 ILCS 55](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=2398&ChapterID=68), has long governed four categories of employee privacy rights in Illinois:

1. **Lawful product use** — prohibiting adverse employment action based on lawful off-premises, off-duty product use;
2. **Workers' compensation inquiries** — restricting employer inquiries about prior workers' compensation claims;
3. **Social media access** — prohibiting employer demands for employees' social media usernames and passwords;
4. **Employment eligibility verification discrepancies** — prohibiting adverse action based solely on receipt of a federal or state "no match" or discrepancy notification regarding work authorization documents.

The fourth category, governing employment eligibility verification, became the focus of significant statutory expansion in 2024 and subsequent agency guidance.

### 2024 Amendments: SB0508 / Public Act 103-879

The Illinois General Assembly passed [SB0508](https://ilga.gov/legislation/fulltext.asp?DocName=10300SB0508enr&DocNum=508&DocTypeId=SB&GA=103&GAID=17&LegID=&Session=&SessionId=112&SpecSess=&amp=&amp=&amp=&amp=&amp=&amp=&amp=&amp=) in July 2024, and Governor Pritzker signed it into law as Public Act 103-879 on August 9, 2024, with an effective date of January 1, 2025. The amendments substantially expanded the Act's employment eligibility verification provisions. Among the key new requirements:

- **I-9 inspection notifications**: Employers must notify all current employees — in English and in any language commonly used in the workplace — within 72 hours of receiving notice of any government inspection of I-9 Employment Eligibility Verification forms or other employment records.
- **Discrepancy notification obligations**: Upon receiving a tentative nonconfirmation (TNC) or other discrepancy notification from E-Verify or any federal or state agency, employers must provide written notice to the affected employee within five business days, in the employee's preferred language.
- **Anti-retaliation**: Employers may not take adverse action — including rescinding a job offer, altering terms, or terminating employment — based solely on a discrepancy notification.
- **Right to representation**: Employees have the right to have a representative of their choosing present during any meetings, discussions, or proceedings related to a discrepancy.
- **Pre-screening prohibition**: E-Verify may not be used to pre-screen applicants before a job offer is extended.
- **Poster requirements**: E-Verify participating employers must display both the Right to Privacy in the Workplace/E-Verify poster and the E-Verify Participation poster (provided by the federal government) in English and Spanish.

The passage of SB0508 prompted widespread employer concern that the new law might constitute an outright prohibition on private-sector E-Verify use, particularly as the statute imposed substantial new procedural obligations on voluntarily participating employers.

## Detailed Analysis [MEDIUM confidence]

### The October 29, 2024 IDOL FAQ Guidance

To address employer confusion, the [Illinois Department of Labor published FAQ guidance](https://labor.illinois.gov/faqs/right-to-privacy-in-the-workplace-e-verify.html) on October 29, 2024. The FAQs addressed the central question directly: "Illinois law does not prohibit any employer from using E-Verify. However, employers who use E-Verify must follow the requirements of the Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act."

Several additional FAQ answers bear on employer compliance posture:

- **No local government mandate permitted**: The Act expressly prohibits the State of Illinois and any unit of local government — including home rule units — from requiring any employer to use E-Verify. This provision pre-dates SB0508 and remains unchanged.
- **Federal contractor exception**: Federal contractors required to use E-Verify under the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) E-Verify clause must continue to do so regardless of the Act's voluntary participation framework.
- **Voluntary enrollment remains permissible**: Private employers that choose to enroll in E-Verify may do so, but must thereafter comply with the Act's entire suite of notification and procedural requirements triggered by participation.

[Law firm analyses](https://www.littler.com/publication-press/publication/illinois-guidance-finds-law-does-not-prohibit-private-employers-using) published contemporaneously with the guidance, including Littler Mendelson and [Seyfarth Shaw](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/e-verify-in-illinois-sb0508-myths-dispelled-rights-protected.html), characterized the IDOL's clarification as dispelling a "myth" that had circulated after SB0508's passage — namely, that Illinois had effectively banned E-Verify for private employers. Both firms confirmed the IDOL's interpretation aligned with the statutory text.

### Key Compliance Obligations for E-Verify Participants

Under the Act as amended and the IDOL's guidance, employers voluntarily using E-Verify face a layered compliance framework:

**1. No pre-screening.** E-Verify may only be run after a conditional job offer has been made and accepted. Running E-Verify on an applicant who has not yet received a job offer violates the Act.

**2. Discrepancy notification within five business days.** When a TNC or other discrepancy is generated, the employer must promptly notify the affected employee in writing, in the employee's preferred language. The notice must:
   - Identify the specific deficiency;
   - Provide instructions on how the employee can contest or correct the discrepancy;
   - Inform the employee of the right to have a representative present.

**3. No adverse action during the contest period.** Employers may not terminate, reassign, reduce pay, or otherwise take adverse action against an employee while the employee is contesting a discrepancy with E-Verify or any federal or state agency.

**4. I-9 inspection notifications.** Upon receiving notice of an I-9 inspection, the employer must post notice to all employees within 72 hours, in all languages commonly used in the workplace.

**5. Poster requirements.** E-Verify participants must display both the IDOL and federal E-Verify posters in English and Spanish in a conspicuous location.

### Enforcement and Penalties

Enforcement authority under the Act rests with the Illinois Department of Labor, the Illinois Attorney General, and private aggrieved parties. Civil penalties for willful and knowing violations are:
- **First violation:** $2,000 to $5,000
- **Subsequent violations:** $5,000 to $10,000

These penalty ranges apply per violation, creating significant aggregate exposure for employers who fail to comply with notification or anti-retaliation requirements across a workforce.

### DOJ Federal Preemption Challenge — Filed May 2025, Dismissed August 19, 2025 [HIGH confidence]

The [U.S. Department of Justice filed a federal lawsuit against Illinois in May 2025](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-files-complaint-against-illinois-encroaching-federal-immigration) (*United States v. State of Illinois*, No. 1:25-cv-04811, N.D. Ill.), alleging that the SB0508 amendments violate the Supremacy Clause of the U.S. Constitution and conflict with the federal Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA). The DOJ argued the Illinois law "imposes confusing procedural requirements, threatens employers with steep penalties, and actively discourages use of the federal E-Verify system." The DOJ simultaneously moved for a preliminary injunction. Illinois moved to dismiss.

On August 19, 2025, Judge Sharon Johnson Coleman of the Northern District of Illinois [denied the DOJ's motion for a preliminary injunction and granted Illinois's motion to dismiss](https://iptp-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/2025.08.19_Memorandum_Opinion_-_United_States_v._Illinois.pdf). The court held that the challenged provisions of the Right to Privacy in the Workplace Act are not expressly preempted by IRCA and do not intrude upon the federal government's constitutional powers in immigration and foreign affairs. The case is closed. Public Act 103-879 has been confirmed as fully valid and remains in effect. No injunctive relief was ever issued, and employer compliance obligations were uninterrupted throughout the litigation.

## Impact Assessment [MEDIUM confidence]

### Affected Employers

The guidance and the underlying SB0508 amendments affect:

- **All Illinois private-sector employers that voluntarily participate in E-Verify** — including those that adopted E-Verify as a best practice for workforce compliance. These employers must implement the full suite of new procedural obligations effective January 1, 2025.
- **Federal contractors operating in Illinois** — already required to use E-Verify under FAR, these employers must now layer on the Act's additional Illinois-specific requirements when operating in the state.
- **Employers who chose not to use E-Verify** — remain unaffected. The Act does not require E-Verify participation, and no Illinois governmental entity may mandate it.

### Industry Considerations

The practical compliance burden falls most heavily on large-scale employers with diverse workforces who voluntarily use E-Verify, particularly those in:
- **Construction and manufacturing** — historically high voluntary E-Verify adoption rates
- **Agriculture and food processing** — large workforces with varied primary languages, increasing the poster and notification translation obligations
- **Staffing and professional employer organizations (PEOs)** — which process large volumes of new hires and must operationalize the five-day TNC notification workflow

## Action Items

- **If using E-Verify:** Verify that onboarding workflows prohibit pre-screening (E-Verify may only be run post-offer); update the process to generate written TNC/discrepancy notices within five business days, in the employee's preferred language.
- **Poster compliance:** Display the IDOL Right to Privacy in the Workplace/E-Verify poster and the federal E-Verify Participation poster in English and Spanish in conspicuous locations at all Illinois workstations.
- **I-9 inspection protocol:** Establish an internal escalation procedure to trigger employee-wide notification within 72 hours of receiving any government I-9 inspection notice.
- **Anti-retaliation training:** Train HR and management that adverse employment action based solely on a TNC or discrepancy notice is prohibited under Illinois law, regardless of the federal E-Verify result.
- **Federal preemption challenge resolved:** The DOJ's preemption challenge (*United States v. Illinois*, 1:25-cv-04811) was dismissed on August 19, 2025. The court rejected federal preemption arguments; no injunctive relief was issued. Public Act 103-879 is confirmed valid and fully in force — no changes to employer obligations are anticipated from this litigation.
- **Multi-language readiness:** Identify languages commonly used in the workplace and secure certified translations of required notifications and postings.
